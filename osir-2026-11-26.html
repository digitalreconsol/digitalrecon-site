area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. DRC: Ebola cases pass 12,000; responders evacuated from Ituri fighting

Government data released on 26 November show 12,021 confirmed Bundibugyo-strain Ebola cases, including 5,904 deaths, in 8 of the country's 26 provinces; there is no approved vaccine or treatment. WHO incident manager Catherine Smallwood said about a dozen people, including two WHO staff, were evacuated to Bunia after three days trapped by fighting in Akwe between the army and Thomas Lubanga's CRP, and an aid team came under fire near Bunia on Monday. [1]

Assessment: The outbreak is accelerating while insecurity in Ituri cuts off the response, so the risk of further regional spread is high.

2. LIBYA / TURKEY: Erdoğan receives Haftar; no official readout

Erdoğan received Khalifa Haftar at the Presidential Complex on 25 November with the defence minister and MIT chief present; his office released no details. [2] Haftar's son Saddam said the talks "laid the groundwork for a new phase in relations," and Daily Sabah notes Ankara also met Dbeibah in Istanbul on 22 November. [3] No LNA retaliation over the Benghazi attack was reported in the window.

Assessment: Turkey is likely positioning itself as broker with both Libyan camps, which could weaken Moscow's hold over Haftar after the Benghazi strike.

3. SUDAN: Blue Nile drone toll revised to 19; army says it downed a drone near Kadugli

Reuters reports the rights group Emergency Lawyers now says RSF drone strikes on 24 November killed 19 civilians and wounded 20 in Damazin and Roseires, where drones targeted the dam and power facilities; the RSF did not comment and Reuters' own figures are internally inconsistent. [4] The Sudan Doctors Network gives 17 killed in Damazin, and the UN counts more than 1,630 civilians killed by drones in Sudan from January to October. [5] The army said it shot down a drone west of Kadugli early on 26 November. [6]

Assessment: Strikes on Roseires power infrastructure threaten the national grid and extend the war east toward the Ethiopian border.

4. NIGERIA: Tinubu pledges to end the northeast insurgency at Maiduguri rally

Campaigning in Maiduguri on 25 November ahead of the 16 January election, President Tinubu said "the terrorism will go" and that forces are working to rescue more than 70 schoolchildren abducted in Borno in May and June. [7]

Assessment: Northeast security is now a central campaign issue, so any major attack or failed rescue before January carries political weight.

5. SOUTH AFRICA: Johannesburg council votes Friday; ANC-ActionSA bloc set to install Mashaba

Johannesburg council elects its mayor at 1000 on 27 November; under a deal agreed in principle, ActionSA takes the Johannesburg and Tshwane mayoralties and the ANC takes Ekurhuleni. The ANC, ActionSA, EFF and MK hold 138 of 270 seats, two more than needed, and the deputy mayor post is still disputed. [8]

Assessment: A two-seat majority leaves the vote exposed to defections or a DA legal challenge.

Collection gap: No in-window reporting was verified on El Fasher, Kordofan beyond Kadugli, the Nyon dialogue, LNA retaliation, Somalia security, South Sudan, Burkina Faso, Niger or the Kenya ballot-paper judgment; IOL, France 24 and Addis Standard pages could not be opened.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: Houthis claim Ras Tanura refinery hit and strike Jizan port; missiles intercepted over Riyadh

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree called the attack on Ras Tanura, the kingdom's largest refinery, an "accurate and direct" strike that caused fires, and footage appeared to show smoke; the coalition said only that drones were intercepted over Ras Tanura and did not acknowledge damage. [9] After renewed coalition strikes on Hodeidah, coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki confirmed a projectile struck Jizan port, causing "minor damage" and no casualties, and said a drone was destroyed over Jeddah port; the coalition also said it intercepted two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and a cruise missile aimed at the western region, and Saree announced a "port-for-port" policy. [10]

Assessment: The Houthis are now carrying out their threat against Saudi Red Sea ports, and the unresolved gap between the two sides' accounts of Ras Tanura is relevant to oil markets.

2. UAE / GULF: UKMTO reports projectile exploding near a tanker 50 nm west of Dubai; Brent jumps

UKMTO said a tanker's master reported "an explosion caused by an unknown projectile" close to the vessel about 50 nautical miles west of Dubai on 26 November; there was no damage and the crew are safe. No vessel was named and no one claimed responsibility. [11][12] Brent settled up 3.5% on Wednesday and traded at $94.71 early Thursday in Asia, [13] before easing to $93.79 by 0939Z. [14]

Assessment: An incident this far inside the Gulf, well west of Hormuz, points to a widening area of risk to shipping.

3. IRAN / UNITED STATES: Bessent says Iran has "not a drop" of oil at sea; mine claims still unconfirmed

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called Iran "a sinking ship" that "doesn't have a drop of oil on the water left to sell," saying no Iranian tanker has loaded since the blockade began on 24 August; neither CENTCOM nor JMIC has publicly confirmed the IRGC's 25 November mine claims. [15] Iran International reports Tehran warned the US, Israel and Gulf states of new military capabilities, and IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebbi said the blockade is failing. [16] It also reports Kataib Hezbollah has set up new positions in western Iraq ahead of a possible renewed US-Iran war. [17]

Assessment: Washington is presenting the blockade as decisive while Tehran raises its rhetoric, and open sources show no sign of an imminent deal or a resumption of US strikes.

4. YEMEN: Heavy fighting around Taiz; NYT reports Saudi-Yemeni command friction

The Yemeni army spokesman claimed 654 strikes against the Houthis on Wednesday, while the Houthis said the coalition carried out 105 strikes on Sanaa and 10 governorates; fighting is concentrated on Taiz's western front. [10] The New York Times reports Houthi fighters are "a few dozen kilometres" from Aden, that a 15 November coalition withdrawal order sparked a Yemeni-Saudi dispute, and that Saudi Arabia then replaced its operations commander; a government spokesman called the situation "not as dire as reported." [18]

Assessment: The Aden line did not change in the window, but reported command friction raises questions about the coalition's cohesion.

5. ISRAEL: October 7 inquiry bill passes preliminary reading; Haredi draft-arrest freeze defeated

The Knesset passed a bill for a state commission of inquiry into October 7 by 65-50 in its preliminary reading; it still needs three more readings. [19] A Haredi bill to freeze arrests of draft evaders failed after Eisenkot's bloc, including Yisrael Beytenu, voted against it. [20] Yashar said on Thursday that "marathon" coalition talks with Yair Golan's Democrats would resume, ending a freeze Golan imposed on Tuesday. [21]

Assessment: The Eisenkot-led majority is using its Knesset control to legislate, but Liberman's "end of week" target for a government is likely to slip.

6. GAZA: Israel kills Hamas economy and education official

An overnight strike in Deir al-Balah killed Khaled Abu Nada and his wife, according to Palestinian media, which described him as deputy education minister. [22] Netanyahu and Katz said he held Hamas's economy and education portfolios and that about 80 Hamas operatives have been killed in the past month. [23]

Assessment: Israel's campaign against individual Hamas officials continues despite pressure to advance the Board of Peace plan.

7. IRAQ: Top court backs Baghdad's control of Kurdistan's borders and oil; Erbil calls a rally

The Federal Supreme Court ruled on Wednesday that federal forces may secure all international borders, including Kurdistan's, and oversee crossings and oil and gas fields; the KRG cabinet rejected "military language and threats." [24] The National reports a classified Iraqi report found oil smuggled through Kurdistan to Iran and Afghanistan, and Masoud Barzani called Baghdad's moves "unsound and reckless." [25] A government source said there are "absolutely no such intentions" to send troops into the region, [26] and Erbil scheduled a mass rally for Thursday morning. [27]

Assessment: The ruling gives Baghdad legal cover, but its disavowal of troop deployments points to managed escalation for now.

8. LEBANON / SYRIA: Private US aid corridor in occupied south Lebanon fuels UNIFIL succession debate

The National reports a US foundation runs aid and escorts to three besieged Christian villages inside the Israeli-occupied zone; Beirut wants a UN-endorsed force, while Israel's UN envoy rejects any international force. [28] Reuters reports Lebanon plans to open Kleiat airport in December, which Hezbollah had opposed. [29] In Syria, SOHR-sourced reporting says Israeli forces detained and later released a resident of Jubata al-Khashab in Quneitra. [30]

Assessment: Both fronts are low-intensity, and the post-UNIFIL arrangement is the main issue to watch in Lebanon.

9. PAKISTAN / AFGHANISTAN / KAZAKHSTAN: ISPR reports 12 militants killed in Balochistan; Tokayev meets Putin

ISPR said security forces killed 12 militants in four Balochistan operations between 20 and 23 November, a delayed announcement. [31] Pakistan's state radio relayed unverified claims by Afghan anti-Taliban groups of attacks in five provinces. [32] Tokayev met Putin in the Kremlin on 25 November, with both saying trade is moving toward $30 billion. [33]

Assessment: These are routine items with no sign of escalation on the Pakistan-Afghanistan border.

Collection gap: No CENTCOM, JMIC or UKMTO confirmation of the IRGC mine claims, no Iranian tanker convoy and no Saudi or Aramco damage assessment for Ras Tanura, Jizan or Jeddah were found; France 24 and Zawya pages were blocked.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE: Russia fires ballistic missiles and 113 drones overnight; Kyiv mall hit, deaths in Kharkiv

Ukraine's Air Force says Russia used Iskander-M, Zircon and S-400 missiles and 113 drones from 1800 on 25 November; 87 drones were downed or jammed and 20 locations hit. [34] A drone killed a couple in a Kharkiv high-rise, and ballistic missiles hit a shopping centre in Kyiv's Desnianskyi district, injuring seven. [34][35] A strike in Kryvyi Rih caused major power problems, and Zelensky says a deal for 48 Patriot interceptors is still unsigned by the US Defense Secretary. [34]

Assessment: The raid was routine in size, so Putin's promised retaliation for the refinery strikes has likely not yet come, and ballistic-missile interceptors remain Ukraine's main shortfall.

2. RUSSIA: Ukrainian drones reportedly hit Nevinnomyssk power plant; Moscow claims 343 downed

Stavropol Governor Vladimir Vladimirov described a "massive drone attack" on Nevinnomyssk's industrial zone, injuring two, and Astra footage suggests the 1,551 MW gas-fired power plant was the target; Ukraine has not commented. Citing Bloomberg, the Kyiv Post says Russian refining has fallen to about 2.6-2.7 million barrels a day, with at least 16 regions limiting fuel sales. [36] Russia's Defence Ministry, via TASS, claims 343 drones downed overnight, with debris damage in Nevinnomyssk, Rostov region, Voronezh and Bashkortostan. [37]

Assessment: Ukraine appears to be widening its campaign from refineries to power generation, which raises the chance of a Russian strike on Ukraine's grid in kind.

3. UNITED STATES / NATO: Trump and Rutte urge Russia not to widen the war

After their 25 November meeting in Florida, Trump and Rutte said in a joint statement they "discussed the importance of Russia avoiding any actions that intentionally, or unintentionally, expand the war" and called for a peaceful resolution "as rapidly as possible"; they also discussed defence spending and the Iran war. [38]

Assessment: The statement addresses Russian spillover into Poland and Moldova without new US commitments; the unapproved Patriot deliveries remain the real test of US support.

4. POLAND / NATO: Warsaw asks allies to consider shooting down Russian missiles over Ukraine

LB.ua, citing Bloomberg, says Poland raised the idea at a NATO ambassadors' meeting on 23 November and some allies were supportive, but no decision was taken. Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski said doing so at Kyiv's request "falls under the definition of self-defence," while Defence Minister Kosiniak-Kamysz said allies show "greater willingness to discuss" but no "determination." [39]

Assessment: A NATO decision is unlikely soon, but frontline states clearly want stronger rules than the Trump-Rutte statement offers.

5. EU / WESTERN BALKANS: Summit adopts declaration; Serbia alone in not endorsing it

EU and Western Balkans leaders adopted the Brussels Declaration on 25 November, reaffirming the membership path and saying glorification of war criminals and genocide denial conflict with European values; Serbia was the only partner absent and the only one not to endorse it. [40] Drafting of Montenegro's accession treaty has begun, with membership possible as early as 2028. [41] The declaration urges partners to align with EU sanctions on Russia. [42]

Assessment: Belgrade is isolating itself on sanctions alignment and war-crimes language during its political transition while Montenegro and Albania pull ahead.

6. EU: Leaders open budget summit; Ukraine loan faces objections

Leaders meet on 26-27 November over a EUR 1.582 trillion 2028-2034 budget including up to EUR 100 billion for Ukraine; Slovakia, the Czech Republic, Hungary and Bulgaria object to a EUR 45 billion EU borrowing plan to cover most of Ukraine's 2027 gap, and a deal is targeted for 17-18 December. [43] TASS, citing Kommersant, says the 22nd sanctions package is expected to be adopted in December. [44]

Assessment: Central European objections to the loan are the main near-term risk to Ukraine's 2027 financing.

7. RUSSIA / EUROPE: Kremlin has "nothing specific" on a European delegation to Moscow

Peskov said on 25 November there was no concrete information on a possible European delegation, noting Brussels' statement that Europeans would not hold bilateral contacts with Russia. [45] Russian state media portray France as warming to Moscow's invitation, while the EU line remains multilateral talks only; no next negotiating round is scheduled. [44]

Assessment: Moscow is likely testing whether bilateral offers can split the EU.

8. GEORGIA / RUSSIA: Lavrov welcomes Tbilisi's call for dialogue

Speaking in Gomel on 25 November, Lavrov called Georgian Foreign Minister Maka Bochorishvili's call for dialogue "a pragmatic approach" after a 19 November envoy meeting in Minsk; Bochorishvili denies the contacts mean restoring diplomatic relations. [46]

Assessment: The Georgian Dream government is slowly normalising ties with Moscow, further straining relations with the EU.

9. UKRAINE: ISW sees marginal gains for both sides

ISW's 25 November assessment reports Ukrainian advances northeast of Borova and Russian advances south of Dobropillya, the Zaporizhzhia plant's 17th loss of external power this year, and a Ukrainian strike on Savasleyka air base. It assesses proposed US security guarantees will likely be delayed or blocked. [47]

Assessment: The front is largely static and the diplomatic gap over Donbas is unchanged.

Collection gap: No in-window developments were verified for Turkey (beyond Haftar's visit), Moldova or Estonia, and no US-Russia-Ukraine talks are publicly scheduled; Civil.ge could not be opened.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. REGION / UNITED STATES: USTR proposes Section 301 "excess capacity" tariffs on 14 economies

USTR proposed tariffs on 14 partners on 25 November after an eight-month investigation: South Korea, Japan, Taiwan, the EU and Switzerland are capped at 15% including existing duties; China faces 20% while the truce holds and 40% if it collapses; Vietnam 20% with a deal or 35% without; India 17% or 25%; five Southeast and South Asian states 18%; Singapore 10%. Comments are due 5 January and hearings begin 25 January. [48][49] Taiwan's Vice Premier Cheng Li-chiun said she expects the final rate to return to "the 15 percent non-stacked rate." [50] Japan's Chief Cabinet Secretary called the plan "regrettable," [51] India said it "does not have structural excess capacity in any sector," [52] and Vietnam said the 35% rate "will not take effect immediately." [53] The Week counts 16 economies including Mexico, while Reuters says Mexico was excluded. [52][48]

Assessment: The 15% caps mostly confirm existing deals for Korea, Japan and Taiwan; the real exposure is for India, Vietnam and China if their talks stall before the January hearings.

2. SOUTH KOREA: Bank of Korea raises rate to 3.25% as markets rebound

The Bank of Korea raised its base rate 25 basis points to 3.25% by a 5-2 vote, its third hike this year, and raised its 2026 growth forecast to 3.9%; Governor Shin Hyun-song called it "both a preemptive move and a response to mounting inflationary pressures." [54] The Kospi was up 2.09% at 4,795.70 in morning trade, with a Daishin analyst saying the USTR cap "reaffirmed the existing" level. [55] The Nikkei rose 1.44% early. [56] The Taiex closed up 2.50% at 39,151.81. [57] No activation of Seoul's stabilisation fund was reported.

Assessment: Raising rates during an equity slump shows the BoK prioritising inflation over market support, leaving the unused fund as Seoul's main backstop.

3. TAIWAN: Parties begin final blitz before Saturday's vote; officials flag Chinese AI influence operations

President Lai, Premier Cho and former president Tsai are campaigning for DPP candidates until the eve of the vote, while KMT figures accuse the government of "dirty tricks" after a raid on a KMT candidate's campaign office over alleged deepfakes. [58] Taiwan flagged about 2,700 accounts to Meta, and officials say about 260 accounts spread more than 2,150 AI-generated videos mostly targeting the DPP; Meta called them "low-quality fake accounts" that had not posted about the election, and China denies interfering. [59]

Assessment: Beijing's preferred pre-election tools appear to be cheap, deniable information operations rather than large military signalling, and contested interference claims are likely after Saturday.

4. NORTH KOREA: Kim tours nuclear facilities and calls for wider tactical nuclear deployment

KCNA reported Kim Jong Un inspected nuclear material production and weapons research facilities, urged deploying tactical nuclear weapons "across multiple domains," and said any attempt to deny the North's nuclear status would be "an open declaration of war." [60] KCNA photos showed warheads and centrifuge cascades, and Kim said the North is not seeking "recognition" from anyone. [61]

Assessment: Coming the same week as the Swiss expulsion, the publicity signals Pyongyang will not trade its nuclear status for diplomatic engagement.

5. NORTH KOREA / SWITZERLAND: Bern "regrets" expulsion but keeps diplomatic relations

Switzerland's foreign ministry said it "regrets this decision" and is arranging for office head Sylvain Astier to return to the Swiss Embassy in Beijing, adding that sanctions on the Songdowon camp are independent of the office. [62] It said the closure "does not call into question diplomatic relations," with contact to run through Beijing and the DPRK embassy in Bern. [63]

Assessment: Bern is limiting the damage, while Pyongyang signals that re-engagement depends on sanctions relief.

6. CHINA / SOLOMON ISLANDS: Beijing restrained on treaty; Sogavare attacks the deal

AFP reports Beijing's line that Pacific ties are not "geopolitical contests," and that the treaty requires consultation with Australia before infrastructure or security deals with non-PIF countries. [64] Former PM Manasseh Sogavare said the treaty imposes "serious limits on our freedom to choose our own partners," and AAP reports a possible no-confidence motion early next year. [65]

Assessment: Beijing is likely to work through Solomon Islands domestic politics rather than confront Canberra openly.

7. JAPAN / CHINA: Chinese coast guard ships enter Senkaku waters and approach a Japanese fishing boat

Japan's coast guard said two Chinese vessels entered territorial waters near the Senkakus at 0742 local time on 26 November and approached a Japanese fishing boat, calling it a "violation of international law" and the fifth such approach this year. [66]

Assessment: The incident continues pressure on Japan's administrative control and reinforces Tokyo's planned maritime policy revision.

8. BANGLADESH: Dhaka police arrest 499 in 24 hours; Jamaat holds nationwide rallies

Dhaka police reported 499 arrests in 24 hours, including 49 alleged Awami League activists, and seized crude bombs and a revolver. [67] Jamaat-e-Islami called rallies in every district against alleged Awami League arson and violence. [68]

Assessment: Street politics are polarising ahead of Hasina's announced return, and clashes are likely to continue.

9. MYANMAR: Min Aung Hlaing says Myanmar will not fully comply with ICJ genocide orders

President Min Aung Hlaing said Myanmar will not "comply with requirements it considered inappropriate," according to state media, after the ICJ ordered prosecutions, Rohingya return and compensation. [69]

Assessment: Open defiance raises the likelihood of moves at the UN Security Council and in ASEAN.

Collection gap: No Taiwan defence ministry PLA counts for 25-26 November, no TAO briefing, no Kospi or Nikkei close and no confirmation of the Swiss envoy's departure were found; Japan Times, Jiji and The Diplomat were blocked.

area of responsibility

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES / MEXICO / CANADA: Section 301 tariffs proposed on 14 economies; Mexico left out "for now," Canada not investigated

USTR proposed Section 301 overcapacity tariffs on 14 of the 16 economies it investigated, with rates from 15% for the EU, Japan, Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan up to 35% for Vietnam; comments are due 5 January and hearings begin 25 January. USTR made no determination for Mexico and Norway and set no deadline, and Canada was not investigated. [70] A Mexican Economy Secretariat official said Mexican manufacturing runs at about 80% of capacity; no statement from Sheinbaum or Ebrard was found. [71] Fox Business, citing an unnamed official, says the new duties will not stack on July's forced-labour tariffs. [72]

Assessment: Mexico's exclusion is a deferral rather than an exemption, keeping its case open as leverage ahead of the Mar-a-Lago G20 and the USMCA review.

2. CANADA / UNITED STATES: Greer says talks stuck on steel, aluminium and autos but deal must "accommodate Canada"

USTR Jamieson Greer told CNBC that negotiations are stalled on steel, aluminium and autos, that "there would have to be something that accommodates Canada as well," and that Canada's waivers for Chinese steel and aluminium "give me pause." [73]

Assessment: Washington's tone has softened toward an accommodation, but the China-related waivers are now a named obstacle.

3. MEXICO: Gunmen attack police in "Chapo Isidro" territory; Mazatlán violence rises

Armed men attacked an interagency police group in Yebavito, Navolato, about 25 km west of Culiacán, on the evening of 25 November, wounding one officer, in an area El Financiero describes as controlled by Meza Flores's faction; separate shootings in Culiacán and Mazatlán killed at least three. [74] In Mazatlán, gunmen killed two women in a home invasion, and Noroeste counts 154 women murdered in Sinaloa in 2026. [75]

Assessment: Attacks on security forces in the former Meza stronghold suggest armed remnants are contesting the federal surge, while violence spreads to Mazatlán.

4. UNITED STATES: Abrego Garcia lands in Spain; judge closes the case

Kilmar Abrego Garcia landed in Madrid on 25 November; DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said he "self-deported" and "will NEVER be allowed back," and Judge Paula Xinis closed the civil case. The Justice Department's appeal on the dismissed smuggling charges is still pending. [76]

Assessment: The district-court case is closed, but the appeal keeps it alive politically.

5. UNITED STATES: Thanksgiving travel peak strains airports; aviation advisory remains in place

TSA reported waits of up to 81 minutes at Miami and expects about 17.6 million passengers between Tuesday and Monday, and the National Weather Service warned of gusts up to 50 mph on the I-95 corridor on Thanksgiving Day. AP also cites this month's FAA network failure, which caused the longest nationwide ground stop in 25 years. [77]

Assessment: Weather and system failures are the main homeland disruption risks over the holiday weekend.

6. UNITED STATES: ICE announces 115 arrests in Indiana operation

ICE said it arrested 115 people in Indiana over several weeks, 18 with final removal orders, and gives FY2026 totals of 491,000 arrests and more than 483,000 removals. [78]

Assessment: Interior enforcement continues at record pace through the holiday period.

Collection gap: No in-window reporting on the caravan's position, Iran-war funding positioning or Mexican presidential reaction to the Section 301 deferral was verified; The Hill and Washington Times pages failed to load.

area of responsibility

SOUTHCOM.

1. HAITI: Gang attack on Kenscoff farmers' market kills at least 10

Armed men attacked farmers at an informal market in Bongard, Kenscoff, before dawn on 25 November, killing at least 10 and wounding 7 with gunfire and machetes, according to police citing a local official. Police deployed with contractor Vectus Global in a helicopter-supported operation; the attack follows the announcement of a police and Gang Suppression Force base in Kenscoff. [79]

Assessment: Gangs are hitting soft civilian targets even where new bases are announced, showing the offensive is not yet protecting Kenscoff.

2. COLOMBIA: Explosive drones hit police posts in Valle del Cauca and Chocó

An explosive dropped from a drone at the police station in El Carmen, Dagua, hit a neighbouring church roof on 25 November, and a second drone attack damaged a police station in Santa Rita, Chocó; no one was hurt. [80] Defence Minister Jorge Mora called the Dagua strike a war crime; investigators have established no link to the Villavicencio bombing. [81]

Assessment: Drone harassment of rural police posts on several fronts stretches the security response alongside the Frente 39 urban campaign.

3. BRAZIL: US proposes 10% tariff on Brazilian aircraft; Lula's office calls it "unacceptable"

USTR proposed adding a 10% tariff on finished aircraft and higher out-of-quota sugar duties to its Section 301 action on Brazil, saying Brazil had made "no meaningful progress." [72] Lula's office said Brazil "will not yield to this type of threat" and did not rule out reciprocal measures. [82]

Assessment: Targeting Embraer raises the likelihood of Brazilian countermeasures before the January hearing.

4. BRAZIL: Planning minister says "there is no PEC" after fiscal meeting

Planning Minister Bruno Moretti said "there is no PEC," describing an "ongoing discussion of measures" to be sent to Lula at an "opportune moment." [83] Brasil 247, relaying O Globo, reports Lula authorised Finance Minister Durigan to advance a second fiscal package worth R$70-80 billion; figures and scope differ across reports. [84]

Assessment: The second fiscal step is being kept vague, so markets are likely to keep receiving mixed signals.

5. ECUADOR: Campaign closes ahead of 29 November local elections

Campaigning ends at 2359 on 26 November, followed by electoral silence and an alcohol ban from midday on 27 November; 13.8 million voters will choose local authorities. [85] The CNE cleared 6,795 unsentenced detainees to vote on 26 November, a lower figure than the 7,417 previously reported. [86] Noboa campaigned for ADN candidates in Cuenca and Machala. [87] Cuban state media report eight provinces under a state of exception and opposition complaints of obstacles. [88]

Assessment: Noboa's personal campaigning makes 29 November a referendum on ADN, held under states of exception.

6. VENEZUELA: Seven Ramo Verde hunger strikers stop after health deteriorates

The NGO Justicia, Encuentro y Perdón said 7 of the 36 military detainees on hunger strike ended it after a medical evaluation found conditions needing urgent specialist care. [89]

Assessment: The strike keeps prisoner releases at the centre of the US-backed dialogue.

7. GUATEMALA: Congress passes 2027 budget, cutting Arévalo's proposal by Q19.6 billion

Congress approved a Q172.4 billion budget by 117 votes after a 16-hour session, below the executive's Q192 billion proposal, cutting line ministries while raising allocations to development councils and NGOs; a deputy called it a "betrayal of the people." [90]

Assessment: Shifting funds to locally controlled councils in a pre-election year sets up a possible veto clash with Arévalo.

8. PERU: Electoral jury proclaims Jeanette Alonzo mayor of Lima

The Lima Centro electoral jury proclaimed Jeanette Alonzo of Renovación Popular mayor for 2027-2030, barring Rafael López Aliaga under the "disguised re-election" rule and seating him as first councillor. [91]

Assessment: López Aliaga's party keeps control of the council, so his dispute with the JNE is likely to continue.

9. PANAMA / CUBA: Panamanian foreign minister in Havana over seven detained nationals

Foreign Minister Javier Martínez-Acha arrived in Havana on 25 November on a visit officials tied to seven Panamanians detained since February over anti-government graffiti; Cuba's Interior Ministry claims they were paid to do it. [92]

Assessment: Panama appears to be pushing for a negotiated release or transfer.

Collection gap: No in-window Cuban grid report, no Colombian operations against Frente 39 and no account of the Ecuador detainee vote's conduct were verified; nothing significant on Nicaragua was found.
