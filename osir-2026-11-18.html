area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. SOMALIA: President accuses UAE of undermining sovereignty; Mogadishu demands federal-only dealings

In a televised address on 17 November, Hassan Sheikh Mohamud accused the UAE of signing port deals for Berbera, Bosaso and Kismayo with regional administrations, setting up forces outside the national framework, funding regions against Mogadishu and playing a major role in Israel's recognition of Somaliland. [1] The foreign ministry told Abu Dhabi on 18 November to deal only with the federal government and promised "all necessary lawful measures." [2] No UAE response was reported. Separately, the ICRC says fighting around Baidoa has displaced more than 18,000 people. [3]

Assessment: Open accusations could harden divisions with UAE-aligned Puntland and Jubaland during the election dispute.

2. SUDAN: UN verifies 2,230 civilian deaths this year, mostly from drones; army advances in West Kordofan

The UN rights office verified 2,230 civilian deaths from January to October, more than 1,630 of them from drone strikes, with Kordofan accounting for over two-thirds; it says the true toll is likely higher. [4] Amnesty documented nine RSF drone strikes on El Obeid that killed 37 civilians and called them possible war crimes. [5] Army sources told Anadolu that forces swept villages around Al-Khuway and north and east of Al-Nuhud and Abu Zabad on 17 November; the RSF did not comment. [6]

Assessment: Drones are now the main killer of civilians, and RSF drone retaliation for the army's Kordofan gains is likely.

3. DRC / KENYA: WHO says Ebola has doubled in three months; Kenya reports no community spread

The WHO Director-General reported more than 11,000 cases and 5,400 deaths across 67 health zones, with 400-500 new cases a week and two-thirds of deaths outside treatment centres. [7] These rounded figures are lower than the national institute's count cited Tuesday, likely reflecting different cut-offs. Kenya says all 135 contacts were traced and no new case has appeared since 19 October. [8]

Assessment: Late detection means a decline should not be assumed.

4. DRC: FRN rebels reportedly seize a hill in Kalehe; army accuses Rwanda over South Kivu schools

A Radio Okapi press review cites Congolese papers saying FRN rebels took Bukondo hill in Kalehe using combat drones, an FRN push in Walikale was repelled, and the army accused Rwanda's forces of turning schools and clinics near Minembwe into arms depots. [9] None of this is independently verified.

Assessment: A Kabila-aligned front is adding to the M23-Rwanda conflict in the Kivus.

5. NIGERIA: Mortar attack targets senator's campaign in Borno

Suspected Boko Haram or ISWAP fighters fired three mortar bombs at a Kukawa village on 17 November targeting Senate Chief Whip Monguno's campaign team, which escaped unhurt; the military had not commented. [10]

Assessment: Insurgents are likely to keep targeting 2027 campaign events in the North-East.

6. ETHIOPIA / ERITREA / KENYA: Border tension and displacement

DW reported before the window that Ethiopian forces have fired heavy artillery near Badme while pushing TPLF-allied forces toward Eritrea, and Crisis Group warned this could lead to direct conflict. [11] About 30,000 people have fled clashes around Moyale into Kenya's Marsabit county since 22 October. [12]

Assessment: The risk of an Ethiopia-Eritrea war remains high, though fresh front-line reporting is lacking.

7. LIBYA: Tripoli government calls parliament's moves "divisive"

The GNU rejected the House of Representatives leadership's plans to meet outside Benghazi, after the eastern government's head threatened separate administrative and financial control; the speaker has called an emergency session in Benghazi. [13]

Assessment: Institutional rivalry is undercutting US-backed unification efforts.

8. SOUTH AFRICA: ANC postpones coalition briefing

The ANC postponed its media briefing on coalition talks, citing "ongoing and extensive discussions," as the DA leads in Gauteng's three metros. [14]

Assessment: Council deals before the first sittings remain the test of GNU cohesion.

Collection gap: Nothing in-window was found on the close of Somalia's election forum, the UN Security Council vote on the Somalia missions, the Sahel, the M23 front line or South Sudan.

CENTCOM.

1. IRAN / BAHRAIN / KUWAIT: US strikes IRGC site on Qeshm after Bahrain attack; drones over Kuwait

A US military official said US forces struck an IRGC missile launch site on Qeshm Island "in self-defense" after the third drone attack on Bahrain's Isa Air Base; CENTCOM called the IRGC's claim to have hit a US radar "FALSE" and said all drones were intercepted. [15] [16] [17] An Iranian deputy governor said the strikes caused no damage to residential or infrastructure areas. [16] Kuwait's army said late on 17 November it was countering "hostile drone attacks," with no source named and no damage reported. [15] The GCC condemned "repeated" Iranian attacks on Bahrain, and the IRGC chief warned of a "devastating" response to any new attack. [18] A second reported Qeshm strike early on 18 November is unconfirmed. [18]

Assessment: This first acknowledged US kinetic response, together with drones over Kuwait, sharply raises the risk of tit-for-tat escalation on the eve of the Shenzhen summit.

2. HORMUZ: LPG carrier hit twice and abandoned; Indian seafarer missing

The Panama-flagged LPG carrier Tara Gas, first hit on 16 November, was struck again on 17 November about 8 nm north-northeast of Khasab, and Ambrey reported Iran's navy warned it would be hit again if anyone helped it. [19] India's foreign ministry said one Indian seafarer is missing and two are hospitalised. [20] Iran claimed several more tankers hit mines east of Hormuz but named no vessels, and no outlet confirms any sinking, including the claimed supertanker. [19] [15] [18] Crew figures differ between sources. [19] [20]

Assessment: Re-striking a disabled ship and threatening rescuers marks an escalation, and unproven mine claims will still deter transits.

3. YEMEN / SAUDI ARABIA: Houthis claim pipeline strikes; Saudi raids reportedly kill 17

Houthi spokesman Sarea said five drones hit Pumping Stations 8 and 9 of the East-West pipeline, causing "major fires"; Saudi Arabia has not confirmed damage and the coalition said it downed a drone heading for Riyadh. [21] [17] The Houthi-run Saba agency said Saudi strikes killed 17 people in Sanaa, Hodeidah and Saada, which is unverified. [21] The Jerusalem Post reports Houthi advances toward Khor Omairah, about 100 km from Aden. [22]

Assessment: A hit on the pipeline would threaten Saudi Arabia's main export route around Hormuz, making the Yemen war a market risk.

4. ISRAEL: Kahana elected Knesset speaker 66-52

Matan Kahana won the speakership with the Eisenkot bloc and two Arab-led parties, defeating Likud's Ofir Katz; Likud called it "an eternal disgrace." [23] [24] Kahana pledged the Knesset would "comply unconditionally with court rulings," and the bloc agreed not to move to repeal the judicial overhaul before 23 November. [23] How UTJ voted was not reported.

Assessment: The Eisenkot bloc now controls the Knesset presidium, and the fight over the judicial overhaul could start next week.

5. IRAN: Araghchi in Muscat; Trump to raise Iran in Shenzhen

Araghchi and Oman's foreign minister discussed "safeguarding" shipping on 17 November, and Qatar warned escalation poses an "extremely grave danger" to a deal. [15] [17] A senior US official said Trump "does intend to talk about Iran" at the 19 November trilateral. [25]

Assessment: The Qeshm strike makes a Hormuz deal before the summit unlikely.

6. ENERGY: Brent near $92.6; Iraq resumes exports to Turkey

Brent traded at $92.61 on Wednesday morning in Asia, down 0.57%, with WTI at $85.06 and Murban down 4.22%. [26] Iraq resumed pumping crude to Turkey at 120,000 bpd, rising toward 500,000 bpd. [27] [17]

Assessment: The supply glut caps the war premium, but confirmed pipeline damage or a sinking could reverse that quickly.

7. IRAQ: Baghdad vows to "cleanse" Jurf al-Sakhar; compromise talks continue

A government spokesman said the state will "cleanse" Jurf al-Sakhar of oil tampering as federal troops mass nearby; if the 20 November 2000 deadline passes without a deal, the current plan is a limited counter-terrorism presence rather than an assault, and Kataib Hezbollah wants the handover delayed to June 2027. [28] Kataib Hezbollah vowed to resist any army entry. [17]

Assessment: A phased, negotiated outcome is more likely than a forced entry on Friday.

8. GAZA: Board of Peace warns of ceasefire collapse

The Board of Peace told the UN Security Council the truce could collapse, saying Israel has not let the technocratic administration start work or deployed Gaza police, delaying the stabilisation force. [29] At least five Palestinians were killed on 17 November, including a six-year-old, according to medics. [30]

Assessment: This is the most authoritative warning yet that the truce is failing.

9. SYRIA: Rare attack on Israeli patrol in Deraa; about 350 villagers flee

Gunmen fired on an Israeli patrol in the Yarmouk basin, and Israeli forces answered with shelling, a helicopter strike and an incursion into Maariya, a security source told The National. [31] Syria's foreign ministry condemned "flagrant violations." [32]

Assessment: Armed resistance in the south adds a new escalation risk, while Damascus keeps to diplomacy.

[33] Taliban leader Akhundzada for the first time offered qualified opponents government posts. EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE: Russia launches 141 drones as energy strikes continue

Ukraine's Air Force said Russia launched 141 drones overnight on 18 November, including 73 jet-powered, and 126 were downed; no missiles were reported. [36] Attacks over the past day killed four people in Donetsk Oblast and one in Kharkiv Oblast, energy sites were hit in Kyiv and Mykolaiv oblasts, and a strike in Rivne Oblast cut power to more than 80 settlements. [36] Kyiv's Southern Bridge reopened one way for vehicles up to 10 tonnes, and the Northern Bridge remains closed. [37]

Assessment: Russia is hitting energy targets daily as heating demand rises.

2. CRIMEA: Kremlin vows to rebuild the bridge; Ukrainian expert sees a year or more

Peskov said the Crimean Bridge "will be restored and will be even better than before" without a timeline, and Khusnullin said the damage assessment is complete. [38] [39] A Ukrainian expert estimates the road span could take "a year or even longer" to restore. [39] Aksyonov said rail service to Taman has resumed, while Ukraine's General Staff says the rail arch is damaged. [40] A Ukrainian outlet's claim that fallen steel blocks the Kerch-Yenikale canal is unverified. [38]

Assessment: The road link is likely out for months, pushing Russian logistics onto rail, ferries and the land corridor.

3. RUSSIA: Krasnodar refineries reportedly hit; ISW records no ground advances

Ukrainian monitoring channels reported drone strikes on the Ilsky and Afipsky refineries overnight; the Kyiv Independent could not verify them. [41] ISW recorded no confirmed advances by either side on 17 November and noted Russian MoD claims of 4,305 Ukrainian drones and 16 Flamingo missiles intercepted in 48 hours, plus a strike on Zaporizhzhia's Preobrazhensky Bridge. [42] ISW says Ryabkov and other officials rejected any European role in negotiations. [42]

Assessment: The war has become a long-range infrastructure duel while the ground front is static.

4. SHENZHEN: Kyiv asks to be consulted as Putin meets Xi

Foreign Minister Sybiha said inviting Putin "would only feed his megalomania," and Zelensky said that "if Ukraine is on the agenda, we would like Ukraine to be consulted." [43] UNN, citing the FT, said the three sides had not agreed an agenda. [43] Russian MFA spokeswoman Zakharova called expectations "enormous" but said Moscow would comment only on results. [44]

Assessment: Kyiv is seeking a voice rather than rejecting the meeting outright.

5. MOLDOVA: Russian drone over Chisinau; airspace closed

A Geran-2 entered from Odesa Oblast on 17 November and a further Shahed was seen approaching Chisinau's Botanica district; all airspace closed at 1627 local and two flights diverted to Iasi. [45] ISW cites Moldova's MoD as reporting seven drones, five of which crashed. [42] Sandu said "Russia cares about neither neutrality, nor sovereignty, nor human life." [45]

Assessment: This is the third incursion in a week and the first over the capital, raising pressure on Moldova and neighbouring Romania.

6. TURKEY: European court orders release of jailed MP; opposition TV seized

The European Court of Human Rights said Can Atalay's imprisonment was "intended to silence him" and ordered his release; the ruling is not final. [46] Earlier on 17 November, police raided Sozcu TV, a state fund was named trustee and four executives were detained. [47]

Assessment: Ankara is unlikely to comply quickly, adding to friction with Europe over the rule of law.

Collection gap: Nothing in-window was found on the prisoner exchange, the next trilateral round, NATO or Baltic incidents, Georgia, the Balkans, the EU's 22nd package or Russian MoD drone figures for the night of 17-18 November.
