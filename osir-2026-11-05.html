AFRICOM.

1. SOUTH AFRICA: ANC narrowly leads DA in early municipal count

With about 12% of votes counted, Reuters reported the ANC on about 32% and the DA on 31%; a CSIR projection puts the final ANC share near 32% and the DA near 26%. The ANC won 46% in 2021. [1] About 14.5 million ballots were cast, and the IEC hopes to declare final results by the weekend. [2] By 0700Z on 5 November, 35.5% of result slips had been received. [3] A record number of hung councils is expected. [4] No significant post-poll unrest was reported.

Assessment: The ANC is likely heading for its worst municipal result, with hung metros in Gauteng and coalition bargaining from about 8 November.

2. TANZANIA: Lissu verdict due 6 November

The Citizen confirmed the High Court will deliver its judgment in Tundu Lissu's treason trial on 6 November. [5] ACT-Wazalendo leader Dorothy Semu said "Lissu's case is political and requires a political solution." [6] No new deployments, arrests or internet restrictions were verified in the window.

Assessment: The verdict is the main flashpoint in East Africa; a conviction could trigger protests and a heavy security response.

3. SUDAN: UN condemns escalating drone war on the power grid

Radio Dabanga reported the UN Secretary-General's condemnation of "escalating drone attacks by parties to the conflict," with at least 14 civilians killed in recent strikes in Kordofan and Blue Nile. Power stations in Kassala and a substation in Merowe were hit on 3 November. [7] White Nile power is rationed after a blackout. [8] Darfur24 reported more than 10 killed at a South Kordofan fuel station, possibly the same incident Dabanga counts at 4. [9] No Ethiopian reply or UN action on Sudan's complaint was found.

Assessment: Strikes far from the front are degrading services in army-held areas.

4. SOMALIA: President dismisses chief justice hearing the constitutional challenge

President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud dismissed Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf days after the court took up an opposition petition against the new constitution, and named a ruling-party associate in his place. [10] Somali Guardian also reports intensified Turkish airstrikes, citing residents. [11]

Assessment: The dismissal undercuts the "final" dialogue agreed on 3 November and could collapse the talks.

5. DRC: Army retakes Mirindano; M23 coalition threatens to quit Doha talks

Local sources say FARDC and Wazalendo forces retook Mirindano in Masisi; at least four civilians were killed. [12] Pre-window onset. The Kabila-led FRN coalition, which includes M23 leaders, said it may leave the Doha talks over a planned constitutional referendum. [13]

6. DRC: Ituri governor warns of slackening Ebola measures

Assessment: Prevention fatigue points to continued spread.

7. NIGER / ALGERIA / CHAD: Algerian FM carries letters to Niamey and N'Djamena

CENTCOM.

1. IRAN / US: Strikes still on the table; Axios says combat preparations near completion

[21] [22] CBS, citing unnamed sources, reports that Trump has asked advisers whether Defense Secretary Hegseth should go; no decision has been made. [22] No new US strike and no on-record CENTCOM statement were reported in the window.

Assessment: The administration is likely preparing for escalation while still pursuing a deal, and the midterm result has not materially lowered the chance of renewed strikes.

2. UNITED STATES: Democratic leaders promise to use war powers and funding to end the war

[22] Treasury separately sanctioned a network run by two Indian nationals that allegedly gave Iran's shadow fleet false flags and fake certifications. [22]

Assessment: Binding congressional limits only begin when the new Congress sits, so the near-term pressure is political rather than legal.

3. IRAN: Tehran dismisses any US shift and keeps the Omani channel open

Assessment: Tehran reads the midterms as a US defeat and is very unlikely to offer concessions soon, while preparing at home for possible strikes.

[24] Reuters, via CBS, said Qatar expected feedback "within 36 hours"; no response had been reported by the end of the window. [22] This reporting does not support Wednesday's unconfirmed claim that a draft had been approved.

Assessment: The Qatari track is alive but stalled on Hormuz access and sanctions relief.

Assessment: An attack this deep in the Gulf, near Qatar, extends the threat to shipping well beyond the strait itself.

INDOPACOM.

KCNA said the Missile Administration tested a new anti-aircraft missile system and that a long-range strategic cruise missile unit "flawlessly carried out a strike mission against a simulated airfield target." [54] South Korea's JCS said the missiles fired from the Sondok area "could potentially have belonged to several different missile types" and has not named them. [55] The South Korean military had earlier put the range under 300 km. [56] A Unification Ministry official linked the launches to the combined Freedom Flag air exercise. [54] No report found that North Korea has switched on its border loudspeakers.

Assessment: The airfield-strike framing points to a counter-air-base message aimed at Freedom Flag; Seoul's caution leaves open whether any missile was ballistic.

2. TAIWAN: Most PLA sorties cross the median line; coast guard presence rises

Taiwan's MND detected 21 PLA aircraft sorties, 8 navy ships and 8 "official" ships in the 24 hours to 0600 local on 5 November; 19 sorties crossed the median line into the northern, central, southwestern and eastern ADIZ. [57] The day before, 14 of 22 sorties entered only the southwestern and eastern zones, with 5 official ships. [58] Legislators unfroze 74 of 201 frozen defence budget items, including funds for over 1,900 Stinger missiles; NT$12.9 billion for follow-on submarines remains frozen. [59]

Assessment: Totals are flat, but the share of median-line crossings and rising coast guard presence indicate steady gray-zone pressure.

3. INDONESIA: About 21,000 troops moved to Jakarta amid a wiretapping case framed as possible treason

President Prabowo dismissed his chief of staff, former police chief Listyo Sigit Prabowo, who is detained with two police generals over alleged wiretapping of the president. [60] [61] The military has committed 20,980 personnel, mainly Navy and Marines, and calls the deployment "normal." [60] The Cabinet Secretary called the surveillance possible "treason." [61] AP and the Jakarta Post differ on the arrest date (1 vs 3 November). [60] [61]

Assessment: This is likely the most serious civil-military-police rupture of Prabowo's term, and it raises the risk of unrest.

8. BANGLADESH: Government tells garment workers to stop protesting

State Minister Nurul Haque Nur said the wage board had held its first meeting, so further protests were "unnecessary," and questioned whether the proposed Tk 30,000 minimum is feasible. [70] The ICJ will rule on the Gambia v. Myanmar genocide case on 19 November. [71]

SOUTHCOM.
