area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. SOMALIA: Mogadishu expels the UAE ambassador over an "open invasion"

Somalia gave the UAE ambassador 72 hours to leave and recalled its own envoy, and the information minister warned that "measures will be taken against anyone who cooperates with UAE in the invasion against Somalia." [1] Somali sources told Reuters the UAE still pays and supplies about 3,400 Somali military police and special forces around the capital. [1] The UAE foreign ministry condemned the government's "escalatory and hostile approach" and accused it of importing internal power struggles into bilateral ties. [2]

Assessment: This sharply escalates Thursday's row, and the UAE-funded units around Mogadishu are now a near-term security variable.

2. SOMALIA: Two Djiboutian AU peacekeepers killed at Beledweyne airport

Somali Guardian reports that Hawadle clan militia moved onto the Beledweyne runway on 19 November to block a plane carrying federal troops; two Djiboutian AUSSOM peacekeepers were killed and two wounded when they tried to push them back, and the aircraft aborted its landing. [3] No AU confirmation was found.

Assessment: A fatal clash between AUSSOM and clan militia adds pressure to already strained mandate and funding talks.

3. ETHIOPIA / ERITREA: Drone and artillery fire reported around Badme

Two Badme residents told Addis Standard that drone strikes and artillery fire ran from the night of 18 November into the next morning, and a foreign diplomat said Eritrean forces had reinforced from Shambiko toward Sembel while Ethiopian deployments concentrate around Geza Tegaru. [4] Neither military commented, and no new AU or IGAD statement was found. [4]

Assessment: Recurring exchanges around Badme raise the risk of a deliberate offensive rather than skirmishing.

4. SUDAN: Drone strike on South Kordofan gold mine reportedly kills at least 10

Witnesses told Radio Dabanga that a drone fired four missiles at the Bajoun mining site in Gadeer locality, killing at least 10; the site was hit in October in strikes the UN attributed to the RSF, but this attack was not attributed. [5]

Assessment: Gold sites are becoming regular targets in the Kordofan war economy as the army pushes west.

5. SOUTH AFRICA: ANC-led pact set to shut the DA out of Gauteng metros

ActionSA's Mashaba said talks with the ANC, MK, EFF and others had "effectively concluded," making him Johannesburg mayor, keeping Moya in Tshwane and giving the ANC Ekurhuleni. [6] The Sunday Times reports the ANC is demanding the Speaker and finance posts in both metros, and ActionSA's Beaumont said "nothing had been finalised." [7]

Assessment: A deal looks likely, but the post share-out is unsettled and pressure on the DA's place in the national unity government is rising.

6. MALI: Russia's Africa Corps fields Lancet loitering munitions as strike tempo doubles

Reuters, citing Conflict Insights Group and ACLED, reports Africa Corps has used the Lancet in at least 10 strikes since April, with recorded air and drone strikes rising from about 7 to nearly 16 a week and deaths from them from about 25 to over 60 a week. [8]

Assessment: Mali is a testing ground for Russian drones, and civilian casualties risk feeding jihadist recruitment.

7. DRC: Army retakes Rugezi; fighting displaces villages in Walikale

The FARDC retook Rugezi, about 15 km from Minembwe, after weeks of fighting with the FRN, described as the renamed AFC/M23 now led by Joseph Kabila. [9] Fighting on Machumbi hill displaced residents of three Walikale villages. [10] Kinshasa also condemned a plan by five European states to send migrants to Rwanda. [11]

Assessment: Fighting is spreading across both Kivus despite the ceasefire, and Kinshasa is pressing Rwanda's European partners diplomatically.

8. NIGERIA / LIBYA: Boko Haram events shift toward Lake Chad; Dbeibah signs deals in Cairo

ACLED data carried by Premium Times show Boko Haram-related events in Nigeria falling from about 95 in Q1 to about 50 in Q3 2026, while rising in Cameroon and Chad. [12] In Cairo, Sisi hosted Libya's Dbeibah, and the two sides signed memoranda on gas, refining and natural resources. [13]

Assessment: Boko Haram violence appears to be moving across borders rather than ending, and Cairo is hedging toward Tripoli as well as the east.

Collection gap: No AU or IGAD statement on Ethiopia-Eritrea, no AU confirmation of the Beledweyne deaths, nothing on Tinubu signing the state police bill and no outcome of the House of Representatives session in Benghazi were found.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. IRAQ: Security forces enter Jurf al-Sakhar under compromise

A deal reached late on 19 November set up a joint operations centre in Jurf al-Sakhar with army, federal police and energy police units plus two PMF brigades that Al Jazeera says include some Kataib Hezbollah fighters. [14] [15] Iraq's state news agency said joint forces "have begun entering," and a military spokesman said Kataib Hezbollah's brigades would be gradually reassigned, possibly to al-Qaim; the group's leader has not explicitly confirmed a withdrawal. [16] Reuters, whose witness saw the deployment, reports the handover starts on 21 November and nine senior security and oil officials have been dismissed. [17]

Assessment: Mediation averted a confrontation, but the arrangement is fragile until Kataib Hezbollah actually withdraws.

2. ISRAEL / IRAN: Netanyahu reportedly calls Iran operation "unavoidable"

Channel 13 reported that Netanyahu told a security meeting an operation against Iran is "unavoidable," and a senior officer said plans with CENTCOM are "shared and approved." [18] The Washington Post, citing four sources, reported Netanyahu asked Mossad to plan a covert Iran operation before the election without telling the IDF chief, which an Israeli official called "false." [19] Reuters reported Israel's military assessed no imminent Iranian threat, and a White House official said Trump sees economic pressure as "devastating." [19]

Assessment: Israeli pressure for renewed strikes is visible, but signals conflict and Trump's decision is decisive.

3. IRAN / US: Tehran rejects chemical-weapons claims; IRGC touts 4,500 km missiles

Trump said there is an "indication" Iran is looking at chemical or biological weapons, while officials reportedly disagree on the intelligence's strength; Iran said it will not accept "unconditional and unlimited" IAEA cooperation while being bombed. [20] Iran's foreign ministry called the claims a "fraudulent script." [21] An IRGC official claimed Iran has shown missiles with up to 4,500 km range, which is unverified. [22] A Pentagon official put the war's cost above $48 billion. [20]

Assessment: The contested WMD allegation is now central to the escalation debate.

4. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: New Houthi salvo on the south; Saudi strikes on Sanaa reported

The coalition said it shot down a Houthi ballistic missile aimed at the south, and alerts were issued for Jazan, Abha and Khamis Mushait; Houthi media reported Saudi strikes on Sanaa. [23]

Assessment: The daily cross-border exchange keeps southern Saudi energy and military sites at risk.

5. HORMUZ / ENERGY: New mine claims unverified; Brent near $92

The IRGC claimed two more supertankers hit mines, which CBS noted are "often uncorroborated." [20] Brent traded near $92.22 to $92.61, heading for a third weekly loss, with Hormuz flows reported back near pre-war levels. [24] [25]

Assessment: The market is not pricing a supply shock.

6. SHENZHEN: Declaration omits Iran

The leaders' declaration did not mention the Iran war, and AP saw no sign Trump secured help from Xi or Putin on Iran. [26]

Assessment: Shenzhen produced no diplomatic lever on Iran.

7. LEBANON: Israel strikes Hezbollah site deep in the Bekaa

The IDF struck a site in Yanta that it said Hezbollah used for missile production, about 80 km beyond the buffer zone; Maariv reported possible escalation plans including a naval blockade, which is unconfirmed. [27]

Assessment: Deeper strikes raise escalation risk as UNIFIL's year-end expiry approaches.

8. ISRAEL: Likud and Eisenkot bloc to resume unity talks on Sunday

Likud said negotiating teams will meet on 22 November, and Channel 12 says Bennett backed Eisenkot serving first in a rotation government. [28]

Assessment: The Iran-strike debate is feeding momentum toward unity talks.

9. EGYPT / GAZA / CENTRAL ASIA: Other developments

Sisi and Dbeibah signed nine agreements, with the maritime dispute on the agenda. [29] WAFA reported two Palestinians killed in Gaza on 19 November. [30] Putin arrived in Tajikistan and discussed the Russian base with Rahmon. [31] [32] The Taliban denied hosting terrorists after a UN official said ISIS-K has armed drones. [33]

Assessment: Egypt is hedging between Libya's rival camps to protect its maritime claims.

Collection gap: Nothing in-window was found on Kataib Hezbollah's formal response, new US or Iranian strikes, new UKMTO incidents, the Aden front or Syria.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE: Russia's largest strike of the week kills at least 8; Kyiv bridges and Lviv hit

Ukraine's Air Force says Russia launched 84 cruise missiles and 404 drones overnight, plus ballistic and Zircon missiles, and that 462 targets were downed or suppressed, with hits at 23 locations. [34] Kyiv reported 6 dead, at least three of them water utility workers, ballistic missiles damaged Dnipro bridges, and 2 people were killed at Lviv's Sknyliv railway station; early Kyiv counts varied from 3 to 6 dead. [35] [36] The Russian MoD called it a "massive strike" on defence, energy, transport and port targets. [37]

Assessment: This looks like the "far more painful" retaliation Putin promised, aimed at logistics through Dnipro bridges and western rail and power.

2. POLAND: Suspected airspace violation; objects fall in Lublin and Subcarpathia

Poland's Operational Command scrambled air defences at about 0330 local time and is investigating objects that fell near Rudnik nad Sanem and Chmielek, one described as "likely a drone." [38] Kyiv Post reports Rzeszów airport suspended operations; the Polish military has not confirmed the cause. [36]

Assessment: A confirmed Russian origin would bring Polish demands for NATO consultations, the second spillover scare this month.

3. RUSSIA: Putin pulls energy and Black Sea truce talks and vows a "far more painful" response

Putin said "after the strikes ... we will not negotiate" ceasefires on energy and Black Sea shipping, and that Russia's response would be "far more painful." [39] Rail traffic over the Crimean Bridge remained halted, and Sevastopol occupation authorities imposed fuel limits. [40]

Assessment: Moscow has formally taken an energy truce off the table, so strikes on energy and logistics will likely keep escalating through winter.

4. UKRAINE / EUROPE: Berlin Coalition of the Willing hears US "Article 5-like" guarantee language

DPA reports 26 leaders attended and Merz said the war ends only through diplomacy. [41] Merz set the current line of contact as the starting point for territorial talks and floated "gradual lifting of some sanctions." [42] Kushner said Trump is open to a guarantee "based on Article 5" and claimed Russia sees the model as a possible basis. [43] Macron and Zelensky called for four-way talks. [44] Citing Axios, UNN reports Trump phoned the meeting and urged Merz to call Putin, with a further round with Russia possible next week. [45]

Assessment: US and European positions are converging on guarantees, but Russia's acceptance rests only on Kushner's word.

5. UKRAINE / NETHERLANDS: Drone damages Dutch and Austrian embassies in Kyiv

A daytime drone raid on Kyiv on 19 November killed one man and damaged St Volodymyr's Cathedral, and a drone came down near the Dutch and Austrian embassies. [40] The Netherlands summoned Russia's ambassador, and the Russian embassy said it was "increasingly difficult to regard the Netherlands as a mere bystander," after reports Dutch-based Destinus missiles were used against the Crimean Bridge. [46]

Assessment: Moscow's rhetoric suggests European defence suppliers may become named political targets.

6. RUSSIA: Ukrainian drones hit Smolensk fertilizer plant

Smolensk's governor said a "civilian industrial facility" and an energy site were damaged, and the Russian MoD claimed 310 Ukrainian drones were intercepted overnight; the Dorogobuzh plant produces about 11% of Russia's ammonium nitrate. [37]

Assessment: Kyiv is widening its deep-strike campaign from refineries to inputs for explosives.

7. GEORGIA / TURKEY: Moscow says Russia-Georgia dialogue resumed; court blocks Imamoglu bid

The Russian MFA said deputy foreign ministers met in Minsk and reached "a number of practical agreements," the first such meeting since relations broke in 2008; no Georgian readout was found. [47] In Turkey, the Council of State upheld the cancellation of Imamoglu's degree, which blocks a 2028 presidential run. [48] [49]

Assessment: Georgia's step toward Moscow will sharpen friction with the EU, and Imamoglu's legal path to 2028 is effectively closed.

8. EU: Hungary lifts accession block; Euroclear sees "no viable" asset plan; China tightens drone parts controls

EU diplomats say Hungary withdrew its objection to opening the last two negotiating clusters for Ukraine and Moldova. [50] Euroclear told the European Parliament there are "no viable suggestions" yet on using the €204 billion in Russian assets it holds. [51] China's licensing controls on drone components, including Geran jet engines, took effect 18 November. [52]

Assessment: Accession is moving, but funding from Russian assets remains stuck ahead of December's European Council.

Collection gap: The ISW assessment for 19 November could not be retrieved, the Axios original could not be opened, and Crimean Bridge road status on 20 November was not independently confirmed.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. CHINA / US / RUSSIA: Trilateral issues four-point declaration; nothing on Ukraine, Iran or Taiwan

The Shenzhen trilateral produced a four-point declaration pledging to "increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products," calling on the ICC and similar bodies to respect "immunities of state officials," reaffirming that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought," and backing UN reform; the Kremlin and Xinhua published it before the White House. [53] Trump said Xi agreed Hormuz should stay open, though the declaration gives no energy mechanism. [54] AP put the meeting at about 70 minutes, and Trump took no questions. [55]

Assessment: The declaration is mostly symbolic; its main practical effect is a shared position against the ICC.

2. CHINA / US / TAIWAN: Taiwan wording still unconfirmed by Washington

No White House readout confirming or denying Xinhua's claim that Trump said the US "does not support Taiwan independence" has been found. [56] [55] An Australian academic writes that the bilateral lasted "just under 15 minutes." [57] A US expert told CNA the two were unlikely "to have agreed on anything" on Taiwan and expects no decision on the $14 billion arms package for at least a month. [58]

Assessment: The Chinese account stands unchallenged, and the arms package is the next indicator.

3. APEC: Shenzhen declaration adopted without the WTO

All 21 economies adopted the leaders' declaration, which omits the WTO for a second year and backs "market-driven" integration including a Free Trade Area of the Asia-Pacific. [59] State-sourced coverage cites a "Shenzhen Outlook" and digital-trade and connectivity plans; Vietnam chairs APEC in 2027. [60] [61]

Assessment: Beijing secured its consensus text, but the bloc still cannot agree on multilateral trade rules.

4. MYANMAR: ICJ finds Myanmar responsible for Rohingya genocide

The International Court of Justice ruled nine to six on 19 November that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in 2017 and ordered safe return, reparations and prosecutions; the president's spokeswoman called the ruling "unfair." [62]

Assessment: The ruling is likely to raise friction with ASEAN and complicate any easing of Western policy toward Naypyitaw.

5. REGIONAL MARKETS: Taiex ends worst week since 1991

The Taiex fell 6.2% on 20 November and 17.5% for the week, its worst since 1991; the Kospi fell 5.2% and the Nikkei 5.1%. [63] The sell-off followed Wall Street losses on AI-bubble fears. [64] The yen was near 159.1. [65]

Assessment: The sell-off is driven by the tech sector, but Taiwan is the most exposed market and the yen remains near Tokyo's intervention zone.

6. AUSTRALIA / CHINA: Xi hails "full turnaround"; Darwin port and beef unresolved

Xi told Albanese a "full turnaround" had been achieved in relations, but neither report describes progress on the Darwin port lease, and Australian beef still faces 55% tariffs under quota. [66] Albanese said the two sides had "worked hard to stabilise the relationship." [67]

Assessment: The political thaw continues while the two main disputes are unchanged.

7. BANGLADESH: Lockdown has mixed impact; arrests continue

Dhaka police arrested 474 people in the 24 hours to midday on 20 November, 124 reportedly linked to the Awami League, with no new deaths reported. [68] Dhaka largely operated as usual during the lockdown, with the worst disruption in Gopalganj and at least six vehicles burned. [69] Former education minister Nurul Islam Nahid was arrested. [70]

Assessment: The mass-arrest drive is likely to keep tension high.

8. KOREAS / JAPAN: Lee vows to lead on peninsula; Trump briefs Takaichi

Lee said South Korea must "take the lead" on peninsula issues and is "no longer the weak nation it was." [71] North Korean media ignored APEC, focusing on a senior aide's meeting with Putin; no launches were reported. [72] Trump briefed Takaichi by phone for about 20 minutes on the Shenzhen talks. [73]

Assessment: Pyongyang is leaning toward Moscow while Seoul and Tokyo seek to avoid being sidelined.

9. SOUTH ASIA / INDONESIA / PACIFIC: Other developments

Pakistan's climate minister arrived in New Delhi for the SCO forum, saying the trip is "not a bilateral negotiation." [74] Prabowo is expected to submit two vice-presidential nominees on 25 November, with Sufmi Dasco Ahmad backed by several parties. [75] Tonga declared a 28-day drought emergency, and the Cook Islands declared an emergency on Nassau as Cyclone Xavier approached. [76]

Assessment: El Niño emergencies across the Pacific are likely to bring aid requests to Australia and New Zealand.

Collection gap: No White House readout of the Trump-Xi meeting, Taiwan MND counts or South China Sea incident reporting was found in the window.

area of responsibility

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES: Senate Democrats split over using the 11 December deadline to force an end to the Iran war

Senators Van Hollen, Sanders, Bennet and Duckworth say any stopgap must restrict the administration from spending appropriated funds on the war, and Kaine says he will not back one that leaves war funding open. [77] Thune called the demand "not a realistic ask," and with 53 Republican seats until 3 January at least seven Democratic votes are needed to clear a filibuster. [77] Several Democrats oppose a pre-holiday shutdown, with Shaheen saying "Nobody I've talked to thinks a shutdown makes any sense"; Schumer has not said whether he would withhold votes, and Van Hollen is also challenging him for the leadership post. [77]

Assessment: A short continuing resolution into early 2027 remains the most likely outcome; a shutdown on 11 December becomes a real risk only if Schumer backs the Van Hollen bloc.

2. CANADA: Ottawa sees little US movement on tariffs; Navarro attacks "arrogance and dishonesty"

Two people at LeBlanc's 19 November briefing to the Canada-US advisory committee told Reuters that "so far the US has made no movement," though LeBlanc said "we have made progress." [78] Chief negotiator Janice Charette told MPs Canada is aiming for "the lowest rate of tariff on the narrowest basket of goods," and Trump has threatened further increases in January without a deal. [79] Separately, trade adviser Peter Navarro attacked the "arrogance and dishonesty of Ottawa on trade" and said "Canada cheats us" on lumber, dairy, steel and aluminium. [80]

Assessment: The post-midterm tone has not changed the negotiating table, and Canadian auto supply chains face the January step-up unless an interim deal comes quickly.

3. MEXICO: Caravan moves toward Tonalá under escort; counts conflict

The mainly Honduran "Dios con nosotros" caravan left Pijijiapan on the night of 19 November for Tonalá with state and National Guard escort; Uno TV put it at nearly 1,000 people. [81] Interior Minister Rosa Icela Rodríguez said it numbered 781, down from about 1,500 at Tapachula, and that the INM is urging returns. [82] No new US tariff action was found in the window.

Assessment: The caravan is shrinking and escorted rather than blocked, and the tariff threat remains rhetorical for now.

4. MEXICO: No CJNG retaliation reported after "El Topo" arrest

Infobae reported no blockades or reprisals in Jalisco after the 18 November capture of César Daniel "N," alias "El Topo," described as a leader of the CJNG's Los Deltas armed wing; he faced warrants including the 2024 abduction of journalist Jaime Barrera. [83]

Assessment: Jalisco has been quiet so far, but that does not rule out delayed retaliation or a succession fight inside Los Deltas.

5. UNITED STATES: GAO faults ICE's detention build-out

A GAO report found ICE expanding detention without cost-effectiveness reviews or a current spending plan; it bought 11 warehouses for about $1.07 billion, paid at least 50% over assessed value on nine, and now plans to sell seven. [84] One Social Circle, Georgia warehouse cost about $128.6 million against a 2023 sale price of $29.4 million, and about $111 million went to two Guantánamo facilities averaging fewer than 30 detainees a day. [84]

Assessment: The report hands the incoming Democratic majority oversight material ahead of FY27 DHS appropriations.

Collection gap: No new DHS or NTAS Iran advisory, no in-window ICE use-of-force incident and nothing on the Bahamas were found; the National Hurricane Center page still served a stale 4 November issuance.

area of responsibility

SOUTHCOM.

1. ECUADOR: Hegseth meets Noboa as AFP documents US flights to a Pacific-coast site

Hegseth and Noboa discussed expanding border security cooperation and fighting illegal mining, and Hegseth reaffirmed "steadfast support" for Noboa's crackdown. [85] AFP counted at least 20 US military flights in recent months to an isolated Pacific-coast base, with satellite imagery showing significant construction; Noboa denies a US base exists and calls the presence "temporary activities," after voters rejected lifting the foreign-base ban last year. [86]

Assessment: A de facto US operating location appears to be forming without formal basing, keeping it contentious ahead of the 29 November elections.

2. HONDURAS: Hegseth and Asfura sign cyber and border "national defense areas" agreements

At Soto Cano, the two signed a cyber-defense memorandum covering systems including the power grid and a joint declaration on national defense areas for border security; Infobae valued the package at about $13.5 million for 2026-27. [87] Hegseth proposed joint "military operations" and said the US "will not cede our own backyard," a day after alleged Cártel del Diablo members killed 12 people in Yoro. [88]

Assessment: Honduras is now the most permissive partner on the tour, and the US border defense-area model may be exported to Honduran borders.

3. HAITI: Police kill 34 in Martissant; gang leader Larose survived Salvadoran strike

Haitian police said a 19 November operation in Martissant killed at least 34 alleged gang members with no police or civilian casualties, using explosive drones supported by a contractor linked to Erik Prince. [89] AP reports that Salvadoran strikes on the homes of Jeff Larose and Lanmò San Jou killed neither man. [90] ACLED counts clashes and drone strikes up 46% in the 21 days after the Salvadoran deployment, with more than 100 killed including at least 26 civilians. [91]

Assessment: Strikes are rising while leaders survive and no force holds ground afterward, raising civilian-harm and displacement risk more than degrading gang command.

4. COLOMBIA: Government to seek Congress approval for joint operations with US troops; tenth airstrike

After meeting Hegseth, Foreign Minister Omar Bula said any decision "will have to pass through Congress," adding "we cannot lose time against crime." [92] President De la Espriella announced an air force strike on a "narcoterrorist objective" in Valle del Cauca on 18 November, his government's tenth; no results were released. [93]

Assessment: Bogotá is seeking legislative cover with uncertain votes while unilateral air operations continue.

5. GUATEMALA: 44 raids against Los Lorenzana under the state of siege

The Public Ministry confirmed 44 raids in Zacapa on 19 November with police and army support. [94] Targets included a Gualán property reportedly linked to Haroldo Lorenzana, who carries a $5 million US reward, and no arrests had been reported. [95]

Assessment: This is the first large test of the post-designation campaign; watch for high-value arrests rather than property seizures alone.

6. VENEZUELA: Fourth dialogue round to close; 1,343 TSJ applications

EFE sources say government and opposition delegations expected to close the fourth round in Caracas on 20 November, focused on political-rights and organized-crime laws. [96] The judicial nominations committee received 1,343 applications for 32 TSJ seats, and 11 NGOs called for "immediate adjustments" on transparency. [97]

Assessment: Talks are moving on legal reform and the court, but there is still no election date, and prisoner releases remain the near-term test.

7. ECUADOR: Electoral council prepares for flood disruption before 29 November vote

The CNE has identified seven polling sites with standing water and will relocate any on red alert after inspections 72, 48 and 24 hours before the vote; flooding has killed 16 people since 29 August. [98]

Assessment: Disruption looks localized to Guayas and Los Ríos, but last-minute site changes create openings for disinformation.

8. CUBA / BRAZIL: Grid deficit persists; Lula weighs finance minister

Cuba's UNE forecast 1,050 MW available against 2,800 MW demand for the 19 November peak, with 1,780 MW of affectation. [99] In Brazil, Reuters sources say Lula may name a new finance minister next week, with Piauí Governor Rafael Fonteles and Vice President Alckmin the favourites. [100]

Assessment: Rolling blackouts across most of Cuba will continue, and a Fonteles pick would reassure Brazilian markets on fiscal policy.

Collection gap: No results from Colombia's Valle del Cauca strike, no confirmation that Venezuela's TSJ candidate list was published, and no arrests from the Guatemala raids were found; Cuban grid data came through a diaspora relay of state figures.
