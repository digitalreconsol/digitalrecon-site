CENTCOM weekend roll‑up. 3–4 October 2026.

What open sources reported about the Gulf, Iran, Yemen, Iraq, the Levant, and South Asia over the weekend, with every claim cited, rated, and marked as confirmed, claimed, or in conflict.

4 Oct 2026, 2130Z (1730 ET)

key judgments

1. Hormuz stays contested. UKMTO records three tanker attacks between 2 and 4 October and seven in or near the strait since 30 September. JMIC reports a further three strikes on Saturday that UKMTO does not list. No group claimed any of them and no crew casualties were reported. Iran says the strait stays closed until its conditions are met; the US says its blockade holds. [13][14][1] Confidence: high that attacks continue, moderate on the count.

2. Diplomacy is alive but stalled. Iran says it is still reviewing the US reply to its seven-day plan and has not answered. Its foreign minister says there is no military solution, while officials restate that reopening Hormuz requires an end to the blockade and sanctions relief. [1][2][5][14] Confidence: moderate.

3. Yemen’s war has widened. The government declared a major offensive on Sunday as the Houthis cut the Taiz–Aden road. The Houthis claim strikes on Aramco sites near Riyadh and Khurais; a fire at the Riyadh refinery was witnessed, but Saudi Arabia has not stated the cause. [1][6][14] Confidence: high that escalation is real, low on any specific claim or casualty figure.

4. Iraq is in its first week without coalition troops. Kataib Hezbollah suspended operations except against aircraft that violate Iraqi airspace, while the militia coalition rejects Baghdad’s disarmament timetable. Nothing new was reported over the weekend. [8] Confidence: high on the facts, low on how long the pause lasts.

5. Governments are cushioning energy markets. The G7 agreed a coordinated 100 million barrel release of oil products over four months, and seven major exporters held November output steady on Sunday. Brent remains above $100. [1] Confidence: high.

Judgments are DRS assessments drawn from the cited reporting. Confidence reflects how well the sources support the claim, not how significant it is.

reporting

By area.

1. Gulf, Strait of Hormuz, and Red Sea

contested
  • ukmto record UKMTO’s incident list shows three attacks in the window. Warning 148, 2 Oct at 1122Z: a tanker on outbound transit in the strait struck by an unknown projectile, causing a small fire and blackout; the vessel continued. Warning 149, 2 Oct: a crude tanker struck on the port side 4 nm east of Oman; crew safe. Warning 150, issued 4 Oct with no incident time: a tanker in the strait struck, damaging its engine room; crew safe, no environmental impact. [13][3]
  • jmic new AP reports that the US Navy-overseen Joint Maritime Information Center said three tankers were struck on Saturday while transiting the strait, and that attacks on the Omani side, where the US military has been guiding vessels, continue. UKMTO lists no incident for 3 Oct. [14] sources conflict
  • count UKMTO lists seven attacks in or near the strait from 30 Sep to 4 Oct (warnings 144–150), which fits Arab News calling the 2 Oct strike the fifth within days. [4][13] An entry on the CBS live page that this edition first dated 3 Oct is the 4 Oct UKMTO notice. [1]
  • red sea new UKMTO Warning 151, 4 Oct: a tanker reported multiple explosions close to the vessel about 60 nm south of Al Mukha, near Bab el-Mandeb. Crew safe; authorities investigating. AP adds that JMIC rates the threat level there substantial. No claim. [13][14]
  • iranian coast new Iran’s state agency IRNA reported the sound of an explosion from the direction of the sea near Qeshm Island on Sunday, and Fars reported an explosion near Sirik. No cause or damage was given. [2] claim, unverified
  • us posture Secretary Hegseth said on Saturday that the US blockade is “ironclad,” that throughput is running almost at prewar levels, and that the President has options if Iran continues to “play games.” CBS posted the remarks at 11:29 PM ET on 3 Oct. [1] new On Sunday IRGC commander Ali Fadavi told state television that no US warship remains in the Gulf, the strait, the Gulf of Oman, or the northern Indian Ocean. [2] sources conflict
  • camp david CBS reports senior officials met at Camp David on 2 Oct to discuss the Iran war and the Saudi–Houthi conflict. Attendees named were Vice President Vance, Secretary Rubio, Secretary Hegseth, Special Envoy Witkoff, CIA Director Ratcliffe, and General Caine. [1] We found no public readout.
  • energy The G7 confirmed a coordinated release of 100 million barrels over four months, with diesel front-loaded within 20 days. The IEA said members had released 325 million of the 400 million barrels promised in March. [1] new On Sunday seven exporters, including Saudi Arabia, Russia, and Iraq, agreed to hold November production steady and meet again on 1 Nov, with Brent above $100. [1] AP says strait traffic has improved but remains below prewar levels. [14]

Assessment: the pattern is steady, low-cost harassment of shipping rather than a mass-casualty campaign, now reaching the southern Red Sea. This is our inference from repeated strikes with no reported deaths. Confidence: moderate. The two monitors’ counts do not reconcile.

2. Iran: diplomacy and signalling

watch
  • seven-day plan A Reuters report says Qatari mediators passed US feedback on a seven-day trust-building plan to Foreign Minister Araghchi in Doha. The plan aims to return both sides to an “enhanced version” of the June memorandum, with steps on the nuclear programme and on reopening Hormuz. An official who was not Iranian but was briefed on the talks says the disagreement is about sequencing, not components. [5]
  • 4 oct Deputy Foreign Minister Gharibabadi told IRNA that the US views are being examined and Iran will act once its final position is ready, while preparing for “any other possible scenario.” Spokesman Baqaei said reopening the strait requires an end to disruption of Iranian shipping and the lifting of sanctions. Speaker Ghalibaf told parliament the US recently sent proposals through a mediator and that Iran’s seven conditions for reopening Hormuz stand. [1][2][14] official statements
  • araghchi new Speaking to foreign ambassadors on Sunday, Araghchi said there is no military or sanctions-based solution and that any renewed military confrontation would meet a stronger response. He also denied a US claim that Iran’s UN delegation had been expelled. [1][14]
  • trump Before a Saturday rally in Ohio, the President told reporters he has “a decision” to make about Iran, and that it would come “the easy way, or the hard way.” [1]
  • oil ministry new Oil Minister Mohsen Paknejad resigned on Sunday. President Pezeshkian accepted and named Hamid Bovard, head of the National Iranian Oil Company, as caretaker. No reason was given. [2][14]
  • military rhetoric An Army spokesman said work is under way to extend missile range. A member of parliament claimed Iran’s defensive capabilities have improved “considerably” since 28 February and promised “surprises.” [2][12] claim, unverified
  • uk Iran’s foreign ministry spokesman accused Britain of complicity by giving US and Israeli forces base access. [2] CBS separately reports a 25-year-old dual UK–Iranian national was released on bail after an arrest linked to a security incident at RAF Fairford. [1] The sources we read do not connect the two.
  • economy Iran International reports the dollar passed 2.7 million rials on the free market on 4 Oct. [2]

Assessment: talks continue through intermediaries while both sides signal strength, and Iran has not yet answered. Reporting on terms is thin and mostly anonymous. Confidence: moderate on process, low on terms.

3. Yemen and Saudi Arabia

escalating
  • offensive Rashad al-Alimi, head of the presidential council, announced on Sunday 4 Oct, speaking from Saudi Arabia, the start of operations to retake all Houthi-held territory. A Saudi military spokesman stressed the kingdom’s right to take all necessary measures. [1][14] Reuters, through Outlook India, had described a Saudi-overseen offensive of possibly more than 100,000 troops, with a coastal option around Bab el-Mandeb. [7] We found no reporting that a coastal push has begun.
  • taiz new AFP security sources say the Houthis have effectively encircled Taiz by cutting the main supply route; AP says the Taiz–Aden road has been disrupted. The city remains under government control. Sources on both sides told AFP more than 70 fighters were killed on Sunday: 34 government troops and 39 Houthis. [1][14]
  • aramco claim On Saturday an AFP journalist saw flames and smoke at Aramco’s Riyadh refinery, and sources told CBS an attack took place but that the Saudis were still assessing responsibility. The Houthis claimed missile and drone strikes on oil sites near Riyadh and, in a further claim, Khurais. The coalition spokesman called the claims “misleading.” Neither Saudi authorities nor Aramco had stated the cause. [1][6] claim, unverified
  • strikes on sanaa The figure of 26 strikes on Sanaa comes from the Houthi channel Al-Masirah, relayed by AFP. [1] The Saudi-backed Yemeni military says it struck “vital targets” in Sanaa and Saada. [6][14] sources conflict
  • fighting AFP reported about 80 combatants killed on Friday near the Red Sea coast. [1] Government forces claim 700 Houthi fighters killed. Nobody has verified the figure. [6] claim, unverified
  • bab el-mandeb Reuters-derived reporting, AFP, and AP all say the Houthis seized Yemen’s Red Sea coast and the Bab el-Mandeb Strait last month. [1][7][14] What that control means on the water is not described.
  • humanitarian UNFPA reports more than 145,000 people displaced by the hostilities. [1]

Assessment: both sides are widening the war, and the Red Sea chokepoint is now part of it, as the 4 Oct incident off Al Mukha suggests. Confidence: high on direction, low on every casualty and damage figure.

4. Iraq and Syria

transition
  • withdrawal The last US forces left Erbil air base on 30 Sep. The coalition had been in Iraq since 2014 and had pulled out of most of the country from 2025. The US finished its Syria withdrawal in spring 2026. The command structure stays active from Jordan. [8]
  • kataib hezbollah Leader Ahmad al-Hamidawi said the group is suspending operations “except for targeting hostile aircraft violating the country’s airspace.” [8]
  • disarmament Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi moved the militia disarmament deadline from 30 Sep 2026 to 30 June 2027. The “Islamic Resistance in Iraq” says it rejects any timetable. [8] Earlier analysis flagged an air-defence gap and vacant defence and interior ministries. [9]
  • weekend We found no militia attack or new Baghdad statement on the timetable through the update cutoff.

Assessment: the suspension looks like a conditional pause, not disarmament. That reading is our inference, supported by the carve-out for aircraft and the rejection of the timetable. Confidence: moderate.

5. Israel and Gaza

watch
  • gaza new An Israeli strike on a vehicle on Sunday killed one Palestinian and wounded at least 11, per hospital officials; Israel said it targeted a known Hamas militant. A Turkish aid group, Deniz Feneri, said one of its volunteers was killed; Israel described him as a Hamas militant and the group’s director. A mother and daughter killed in an early-morning strike were buried at a Gaza City church on Saturday. [14] sources conflict
  • toll Gaza’s Health Ministry says Israeli strikes have killed at least 1,445 Palestinians since the ceasefire nearly a year ago; seven Israeli soldiers have been killed in that period. [14]

Assessment: steady lethal strikes under a nominal ceasefire, with the 7 October anniversary ahead. Confidence: moderate.

6. South Asia

cross-border
  • pakistan–afghanistan Pakistani air strikes overnight into 1 Oct hit Helmand and Kunar provinces. The UN mission in Afghanistan confirmed at least 10 civilians killed, most of them children, and nine injured. Pakistan says it hit militant hideouts and killed 22 militants, a figure not independently verified. The Taliban government called the strikes aggression. UNAMA has documented more than 1,100 civilian casualties from cross-border violence this year. [10]

Assessment: an active cross-border air campaign with UN-confirmed civilian harm. Confidence: high on the civilian toll, low on Pakistan’s militant figure.

reporting conflicts

Where sources disagree.

Number of tanker strikes

UKMTO lists attacks on 2 Oct (two) and 4 Oct (one), and none on 3 Oct. JMIC, via AP, says three tankers were struck on Saturday 3 Oct. Warning 150 gives no incident time, so it may be one of JMIC’s three. [13][14]

US naval presence

Hegseth says the blockade is ironclad. [1] An IRGC commander says no US warship remains in the Gulf or nearby waters. [2] AP and JMIC describe the US military guiding vessels on the Omani side. [14]

Who struck Sanaa

Saudi aircraft with 26 strikes, per the Houthi channel [1], or the Saudi-backed Yemeni military, which claimed the strikes. [6][14] Both can be true if the coalition flew the sorties for the government.

Hormuz control

AP says Iran has lost its near-complete grip on the strait and Gulf exports are near prewar levels, while strait traffic remains below them. [14] Attacks continue. [13] Both can be true, but they suggest very different risk.

gaps

What we could not see.

No JMIC primary record

JMIC’s Saturday figure comes through AP. We did not see JMIC’s own advisory, so its three strikes cannot be matched to UKMTO’s notices.

Items seen by headline only

The CNBC piece [11] was reviewed by headline only. A State Department release on the Ad-Hoc Coalition to Defeat ISIS meeting on Iraq and Syria was not reviewed, so it is not cited.

Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, Egypt, Central Asia

We found no weekend-specific reporting for Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, or Egypt that we could verify, and did not search Central Asia or the smaller Gulf states. Their absence here is a limit of this edition, not a sign of quiet.

No imagery, no social media, no Persian or Arabic sources

This edition used English-language reporting only. We did not check satellite imagery, ship-tracking data, or official Iranian, Saudi, or Houthi channels directly.

Time window

Several items come from Friday 2 Oct to give context. The update cutoff is Sunday 2130Z, so late Sunday events in the region may be missing.

next 72 hours

What to watch.

These indicators are our judgment, not predictions.

Hormuz and Red Sea

New UKMTO or JMIC notices, any claim of responsibility, an explanation for the explosions near Qeshm and Sirik, and further incidents near Bab el-Mandeb.

Iran–US channel

Iran’s formal reply on the seven-day plan, any statement from Washington after the Camp David meeting, and the President’s stated “decision.”

Yemen

Whether Taiz’s supply route is reopened, whether the offensive reaches the Red Sea coast, and Saudi or Aramco confirmation of damage.

Iraq

Any militia attack on aircraft, a Baghdad response to the rejected timetable, and further statements from Kataib Hezbollah.

Gaza

Strike tempo and crossing closures around the 7 October anniversary.
