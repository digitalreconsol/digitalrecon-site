area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. LIBYA: Russian corvette sunk at Benghazi; Zelensky claims a Ukrainian naval drone operation

Libyan military officials told AP that two explosive-laden sea drones hit the Russian corvette Merkury at Benghazi Naval Base on 22 November and at least four more were intercepted; the eastern government called it a violation of sovereignty, opened a joint investigation with Russia and asked the UN to investigate. [1] [2] Satellite imagery showed the ship mostly submerged, a Maltese-flagged merchant ship was reportedly hit off Benghazi hours later, and Libyan officials said the drones were launched from western Libya. [2] Zelensky said seven Ukrainian naval drones destroyed the corvette and a Ka-27 helicopter and hit the cargo ship Angara, which he said carried weapons. [3] The Tripoli government condemned the use of Libyan territory for proxy warfare, and Moscow had not commented. [4]

Assessment: This is the deepest Ukrainian strike yet on Russia's military presence in Africa, and it sharpens Libya's east-west split.

2. SUDAN: RSF drones kill four near Khartoum and damage a UN building; Blue Nile front opens

Drone attacks blamed on the RSF killed four people around the capital on 22 November and damaged a UN building with staff inside; a Sudanese army source alleged the drones were launched from Bahir Dar airport in Ethiopia, which is unverified. [5] An RSF and SPLM-N alliance seized several localities in Blue Nile on 20 November, and Sennar imposed a night curfew. [6] The army, via SUNA, accused the RSF of executing captured soldiers. [7] A local monitor said an army drone strike on a displacement shelter in West Kordofan on 17 November killed 16. [8]

Assessment: A Blue Nile front near the Ethiopian border now threatens Damazin and Sennar, and the Bahir Dar allegation, if substantiated, would tie Sudan's war to the Ethiopia-Eritrea standoff.

3. SOMALIA: UAE deadline passes without a confirmed departure; Turkish military chief visits

The 72-hour deadline for the UAE ambassador expired, but the UAE says it closed its Mogadishu embassy in January, and the GCC called Somalia's accusations "irresponsible." [9] Turkey's chief of general staff broke ground on a new Somali Navy headquarters with President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud on 21 November. [10] AUSSOM and the government concluded security transition planning on 23 November, with the mission's mandate expiring 31 December. [11]

Assessment: The expulsion is largely symbolic, while Turkey consolidates its place as Mogadishu's main security partner.

4. DRC / KENYA: Ebola passes 11,600 cases; Ruto meets Tshisekedi

Citing CDC data, Contagion Live reports 11,645 confirmed Ebola cases and 5,683 deaths as of 17 November, up 500 cases in a week, with 9 cases in Kenya and 1 in Nigeria. [12] Ruto flew to Oyo, Republic of Congo, on 21 November for a meeting with Tshisekedi convened by Sassou-N'Guesso. [13] Government forces repelled an FRN attack at Kautu in southern Masisi on the route into Walikale. [14]

Assessment: The outbreak is still growing about 4-5% a week, and Kenya is trying to repair ties with Kinshasa as fighting toward Walikale continues.

5. NIGERIA: Village attack in Niger State; 13 kidnapped in Zamfara

A security report seen by AFP says 11 people were killed in an attack on Luman Ba'are in Borgu on 21 November, some inside a mosque, while police confirmed 5; an aid worker reported vigilante reprisals on Fulani settlements. [15] Police said 13 people were abducted in Zamfara and two soldiers were killed in Taraba. [16] No signing of the state police bill was found.

Assessment: Reprisals in Borgu risk a new herder-farmer cycle while state police are not yet in place.

6. SOUTH AFRICA: Mashaba set for Johannesburg mayoralty as metro pact is sealed

Daily Maverick reports an ANC-ActionSA-MK-EFF pact puts Herman Mashaba on course to be Johannesburg mayor this week, after ActionSA rejected a DA offer of the Tshwane mayoralty. [17] Councils must elect office-bearers by 27 November, with Gauteng metros expected to sit Thursday. [18]

Assessment: The DA looks set to be shut out of all three Gauteng metros, straining the national unity government.

7. SOUTH SUDAN: Cattle raid in Duk County kills seven

A county commissioner said a raid on a cattle camp in Duk County on 21 November killed 7, injured 10 and took 2,351 cattle, blaming youths from Greater Pibor, whose authorities denied knowledge of the attack. [19]

Assessment: Intercommunal raiding in Jonglei continues as tensions rise over delayed elections.

Collection gap: No in-window reporting on Ethiopia-Eritrea, the Sahel, El Fasher, AU or Djiboutian reaction to the Beledweyne deaths, the status of UAE-funded Somali units, or Moscow's reaction to the Merkury loss was found.

CENTCOM.

1. BAHRAIN / IRAN: IRGC strikes Bahrain; Manama reports a civilian plant hit and two injured

[20] [21] Bahrain's Interior Ministry said a civilian industrial plant in Sitra caught fire and two people were hospitalised, one critically, and the Foreign Ministry invoked its "inherent right" under Article 51 of the UN Charter. [20] Iranian state media said a US interceptor crashed on Sitra; Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Qatar condemned the attack, and no CENTCOM statement on Hengam was found. [21] [22]

Assessment: Iran is again hitting US basing in the Gulf, and Bahrain's Article 51 language raises the chance of a direct Gulf response.

2. IRAN / STRAIT OF HORMUZ: Iran says two more supertankers hit mines east of the strait

[24] An Iranian MP claimed Iran hits about 10 "violating" tankers a day, while UKMTO has recorded 169 maritime events since 28 February. [25]

Assessment: Tehran's public line is that the strait stays below pre-war conditions, so further mine and projectile incidents are likely.

3. UNITED STATES / IRAN: Trump inclined to resume strikes "after the new year" without a deal

Trump told Fox News on 22 November he is inclined to order new strikes on Iran after the new year if no agreement is reached, calling Iran's negotiators "professional tap dancers." [26] Pezeshkian said Iran faces a "silent economic war … tougher than a war in which missiles are launched," and a deputy foreign minister called the US blockade "an act of war." [27] [21]

Assessment: Trump has in effect set a January 2027 horizon for resumed US strikes, while Tehran uses shipping attacks to pressure the blockade.

Assessment: The market shrugged off the weekend attacks, and OPEC+ and any resumed US strikes are the likelier price drivers.

5. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: Houthis claim strike on Rabigh refinery; missiles intercepted over Riyadh

The Houthis claimed an attack on Saudi Arabia's Rabigh refinery on the Red Sea coast on 22 November; Saudi authorities did not immediately comment. [30] The coalition said it intercepted three ballistic missiles fired at Riyadh on 21 November, with a Reuters witness seeing a projectile, and another aimed at the western region on 22 November. 6. YEMEN: Fighting resumes on Lahij and Taiz fronts north of Aden

[31] The army claimed 854 strikes have killed or wounded 193 Houthis, an unverified party figure. [31]

7. IRAQ: Jurf al-Sakhar handover under way, but Kataib Hezbollah still present

[40] Settlers burned vehicles in four West Bank villages on 22 November, which Eisenkot called "Jewish terrorism." [41]

10. LEBANON / PAKISTAN / AFGHANISTAN: Aoun tells Hezbollah to disarm; Mastung operations

In his independence-eve speech on 21 November, President Aoun told Hezbollah "You must hand over your weapons" and told Israel to "withdraw from every inch," naming interference by "influential parties in the Iranian regime." [42] NPR reports the Zawtar al-Gharbiyah handover to the Lebanese army remains the only test of the June framework. EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE: Weekend drone attacks kill at least 11 in Kyiv Oblast

A Russian drone strike on 22 November destroyed a section of a five-story apartment building in Dymer through all floors, and the Kyiv Independent reports 11 dead there, with a 7-year-old rescued after nearly 14 hours under rubble. [46] Ukraine's Air Force said Russia launched 186 drones into 22 November and 153 into 23 November, with no missiles. [47] [48] Zelensky said Russia used about 2,700 drones and 165 missiles this week and called US delays on Patriot transfers "particularly difficult to understand." [49] Tolls changed through the weekend, from 4 to 11.

2. POLAND: Debris identified as recently made Russian Kh-101 missiles; North Atlantic Council to meet

Lublin prosecutors identified debris at three sites as Kh-101 cruise missiles made in recent weeks at a plant in Dubna, and Reuters reported the North Atlantic Council would meet on 23 November; no Article 4 request was reported. [50] A Polish general said a Swedish Gripen under NATO command was cleared to shoot down a suspected Kh-101 on 20 November but did not fire because weather prevented identification. [49] Moldova and Finland also reported Russian airspace violations on 21 November. [50]

Assessment: The attribution settles Friday's open question, and the North Atlantic Council is the next decision point.

3. FINLAND / RUSSIA: Drone-struck Russian military supply ship towed from Finnish waters

[51] Explosions were also reported near the Baltic Fleet base at Baltiysk on 21 November, unconfirmed by Russia. [52]

Assessment: Ukraine's maritime drone campaign now reaches NATO-adjacent Baltic waters, putting Finland, Estonia and Lithuania in a risk they did not choose.

4. RUSSIA: Novoshakhtinsk refinery reportedly burning; UK to build long-range drones for Ukraine

Monitoring channels reported the Novoshakhtinsk refinery in Rostov region and a Tatarstan pumping station burning after drone attacks early on 23 November, unverified by Russian officials. [53] The Russian MoD claimed 170 Ukrainian drones downed overnight into 22 November. [47] The UK signed agreements on 21 November to build "middle strike" and long-range drones for Ukraine in Britain. [54]

5. UKRAINE: Russia targets Odesa and Dnipropetrovsk grids

6. UKRAINE / US: Zelensky has seen no written "Article 5-like" commitment

7. EU / DENMARK: Copenhagen pushes EU travel curbs on Russians after alleged assassination plot

8. TURKEY / GEORGIA: Istanbul march against media crackdown; Georgian protesters pardoned

Assessment: Ankara is extending the trustee model to opposition media, while Tbilisi's pardon is a small symbolic step.

Collection gap: The ISW assessment, the outcome of the North Atlantic Council meeting, a Russian MoD figure for 23 November, any prisoner swap, Serbia, Hungary and the Baltic states beyond the Gulf of Finland were not covered.
