area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. DRC: Ebola passes 11,000 cases and 5,387 deaths

Government figures released on 11 November show 11,042 infections and 5,387 deaths, up from 10,668 and 5,198 in WHO's 3 November data, across eight provinces. [1] [2] WHO says contact follow-up averages 86% against a 95% target. [2]

Assessment: About 370 new cases and 190 deaths in a week show transmission is not under control.

2. DRC: FRN offensive widens in South Kivu; militia leader alleges army complicity

Africanews reports intensifying clashes beyond Kalehe; Wazalendo leader and MP Willy Mishiki suggested complicity helped the FRN "outflank us." [3] A military court in Walikale sentenced 66 soldiers to death on 6 November, bringing the total since early 2025 to about 381. [4]

Assessment: Insider suspicion and mass death sentences point to falling army cohesion; this contradicts Tuesday's army claim to have halted the advance.

3. KENYA: Suspected al-Shabaab ambush kills eight in Mandera

Suspected al-Shabaab fighters ambushed a rural roads convoy at Hagarsu on 10 November, killing five engineers, two police reservists and their driver. [5]

Assessment: Al-Shabaab is again hitting infrastructure in Kenya's northeast.

4. SOMALIA: Hawadle soldiers reported leaving the front

Somali Guardian reports dozens of Hawadle-clan soldiers left positions and gathered at their clan leader's residence in Beledweyne, where he reportedly said an offensive would follow; this is single-sourced and unconfirmed. [6]

Assessment: If confirmed, clan desertions would weaken operations against al-Shabaab before the 14-18 November talks.

5. SUDAN: Army claims drone shootdowns as Kordofan push continues

The army said it shot down two drones in West Kordofan and East Darfur and has downed 29 in total; the claims are unverified. [7] UNICEF says at least 15 strikes hit power stations in October and two in three child casualties this year were caused by drones. [8]

Assessment: Drone warfare is extending the threat to civilians and infrastructure.

6. SOUTH AFRICA: Johannesburg talks set for Thursday; ActionSA courts all sides

Johannesburg coalition talks are set for 12 November, with ActionSA insisting Helen Zille stand aside; ActionSA has also met the ANC and EFF. [9] In eThekwini, ActionSA held "exploratory" meetings with MK and the ANC. [10]

Assessment: Johannesburg turns on the Zille question, and ActionSA's open options make coalitions less stable.

7. TANZANIA: Lissu files notice of appeal

Pre-window onset. Lissu's notice of appeal was filed on 9 November; no bail has been granted. [11]

Assessment: Lissu is likely to remain in custody for now.

Collection gap: Nothing in-window on the Sahel, Ethiopia-Eritrea, Libya, Somalia's Daynile or judiciary standoff, the ADF near Beni or Nigeria's INEC.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. ISRAEL: Knesset sworn in; 67 MKs force a vote to replace Speaker Ohana on 17 November

The 26th Knesset was sworn in on 10 November; Herzog urged MKs to avoid another election and called for a full 7 October inquiry. [12] Sixty-seven MKs then filed to elect a new speaker, and Ohana set the vote for Tuesday 17 November, saying he would "respect the will of the majority." [13] After Netanyahu accused Ra'am and the Joint List of seeking Israel's "destruction," Yashar cancelled scheduled talks with Likud. [14]

Assessment: Kahana will likely take the speakership on 17 November, giving the anti-Netanyahu camp control of the Knesset agenda before any government forms.

2. ISRAEL: UTJ sets draft-exemption demands; Shas stays with Netanyahu

UTJ chair Yaakov Asher listed yeshiva exemptions, Haredi education funding and the religion-state status quo as conditions: "Without that there won't be anything." Eisenkot vowed not to drop "service for all," and Shas says it still backs Netanyahu. [15] Liberman said a 61-seat coalition needs UTJ and Shas, not Arab parties. [15]

Assessment: Conscription is the main obstacle, so no quick coalition is likely.

3. QATAR / IRAN / US: Qatari PM tells G7 Doha secured "at least two weeks" for diplomacy

Diplomatic sources told the Jerusalem Post the Qatari PM told G7 ministers Doha had secured "at least two weeks" before any US attack, with "disagreement over the sequencing" of the blockade, nuclear file and frozen funds. [16] French FM Barrot said escalation is "not fully ruled out." [17] Rubio urged nations to "step up" on Hormuz security: "I raised it. I didn't get a response." [18] France and the UK want a settlement before any naval mission. [19]

Assessment: The window is an unofficial assurance; diplomacy is stalled on sequencing rather than broken.

4. IRAN: Rezaei says no enriched material will leave the country

SNSC Secretary Mohsen Rezaei called reports of talks in Qatar "a pure lie" and said Hormuz stays closed until the US meets Iran's conditions; Araghchi said it could reopen within two days if they are met. [20] [21] Rezaei told Al Mayadeen Tehran "will not allow even a single particle of enriched nuclear material to leave the country." [22]

Assessment: This directly conflicts with the US demand to remove nuclear material and is the hardest gap in the mediation.

5. STRAIT OF HORMUZ: Tanker hit off Oman; ADNOC vessel damaged

UKMTO reported a tanker hit by a projectile about 19nm northeast of Lima, Oman, on 10 November, and ADNOC said one of its vessels was damaged; it is unclear whether these are the same incident. [23] The IRGC Navy says tankers hit mines on an "unauthorised route"; reports differ on whether two or three were hit. [20] [24] CENTCOM says a US aircraft disabled a cargo ship that had called at an Iranian port. [25] JMIC keeps the threat at "severe." [23]

Assessment: Iran appears to be enforcing access through the strait, undercutting hopes of a truce.

6. ENERGY: Brent recovers to about $92.8

Brent rose 1.4% to $92.77 by 0934 GMT on 11 November after Rezaei's comments, recovering from Tuesday's two-month low. [26] [24]

Assessment: Ship attacks and Iran's nuclear line limit any further fall.

7. YEMEN / SAUDI ARABIA: Houthis claim strikes on Riyadh airport and Abqaiq

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed attacks on Riyadh airport, East-West pipeline pumping stations and Aramco's Abqaiq facility, and called the Saudi account of the pipeline attack "a blatant lie." [27] Yemen's government says its operations killed or wounded about 190 Houthi fighters. [28] The US Embassy called the Houthi siege of Taiz "the only real blockade." [29] CTP-ISW could not verify the Houthi claims. [30]

Assessment: A confirmed hit on Abqaiq would be the most serious threat to Saudi output since Hormuz closed.

8. GAZA: At least seven killed; Israel and Board of Peace split over winter aid

At least seven people were killed in Israeli strikes and fire on 10 November, including a father and son in Zeitoun. [31] The PMO says more than 91,000 tents have entered but Hamas must disarm before reconstruction; the US-backed Board of Peace called Israel's response "insufficient." [32]

Assessment: The public split over shelter supplies is a new source of friction.

9. LEBANON: UNIFIL patrol fired on

UNIFIL said "about a dozen rounds" were fired at a patrol near Wadi Jilu in Tyre district; no one was hurt. [33] G7 ministers agreed on "the need to avoid a security void" when UNIFIL's mandate ends on 31 December, and Rubio said a successor "could be a civilian mission." [33] [19]

Assessment: With seven weeks left and no agreed successor, a security gap in southern Lebanon is increasingly likely.

10. EGYPT: Lina Attalah retried; Mada Masr appeal on 25 November

Mada Masr editor Lina Attalah was due in court on 11 November for retrial on a "false news" sentence; the outlet's appeal against closure is set for 25 November, and a UN spokesperson called developments "alarming." [34] [35]

Assessment: A jail sentence would draw strong Western criticism.

11. IRAQ / SYRIA: Mandali closure confirmed; Israeli shelling near Beit Jinn

Iraqi state media say the Mandali crossing closes on 13 November, and CTP-ISW reports PM al-Zaidi threatened to arrest militia members acting for Iran. [30] A Kurdish agency reported Israeli shelling near Beit Jinn in rural Damascus. [36]

Assessment: Threats to arrest militia members raise the risk of confrontation inside Iraq.

Collection gap: No in-window reporting for Pakistan, Afghanistan, Jordan or Central Asia; no Khor Mor restart, formal US reply to Iran or G7 communiqué on Iran. France24 and Anadolu did not load.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE / RUSSIA: Abu Dhabi talks enter day two; prisoner exchange discussed, Russia raises territory

Day two opened on 11 November with a trilateral session, followed by a separate meeting with European partners; national security advisers from France, Germany, the UK and Italy plus NATO and EU officials are in Abu Dhabi. [37] Zelensky said day one discussed a 1,000-for-1,000 or 1,500-for-1,500 prisoner exchange and that "the Russians raised the issue of territories," which "is a matter for the leaders." [38] Rubio called the talks "very productive" but said nothing was "on the verge of being solved in the next 24 hours"; AP reports Russia objected to European involvement but said EU representatives could join later. [39] RT says day two focuses on territory, which Zelensky's agenda does not list. [40] No joint statement had been reported by 1000Z.

Assessment: A prisoner exchange is the most likely concrete result; pushing territory to leader level keeps a broader deal unlikely this round.

2. UKRAINE: 163 drones overnight; two missiles cross Moldova

Ukraine's Air Force says Russia launched 163 drones and a guided missile, with hits at 17 locations; a woman died in Odesa region and about 135,000 customers there lost power. [41] Moldova says two Russian Banderol missiles crossed its airspace, one toward Transnistria. [42]

Assessment: Russia is keeping up grid strikes through the talks, and spillover into Moldova recurs.

3. RUSSIA: Ukrainian drones hit Ust-Luga port; two dead in Smolensk

Russia's Defence Ministry claims 627 Ukrainian drones intercepted overnight. [43] The Leningrad governor says a drone attack started a fire at the Ust-Luga oil and grain port; two people were killed in Smolensk, and Murmansk airport suspended flights. [43] [44]

Assessment: Ukraine is pressing Russia's Baltic export infrastructure during the talks.

4. BALTICS / FINLAND: Brief drone alerts

Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia briefly issued shelter alerts over drones entering from Russia or Belarus, and Lithuania said NATO jets were scrambled; Finland briefly restricted airspace over the eastern Gulf of Finland. No injuries were reported. [45]

Assessment: Drones straying from strikes on northwest Russia are a recurring source of NATO airspace incidents.

5. UKRAINE: ISW records no advances for a second day

ISW said "neither Russian nor Ukrainian forces advanced" on 10 November and assesses the Kremlin is trying to steer the talks toward its 2021 demands for a NATO rollback. [46]

Assessment: The front appears to be waiting on diplomacy.

6. DENMARK / UKRAINE: Denmark pledges EUR 643 million in military aid

Denmark announced a DKK 4.8bn (EUR 643m) military package and EUR 20m for energy during Zelensky's Copenhagen visit; Frederiksen criticised an earlier uncoordinated Nordic call with Putin. [47]

Assessment: Nordic support continues whatever happens in Abu Dhabi, though not fully coordinated.

7. GEORGIA: Court refuses to delay enforcement of any party ban

The Constitutional Court rejected a request to delay enforcement of any ban by a month so parties could seek European Court interim measures, and only partly granted a separate evidence stage; hearings continue to 13 November. [48]

Assessment: A ruling soon after 13 November now looks likely.

8. LITHUANIA: Taiwan says no plans to rename its office

Pre-window onset. Taiwan's deputy foreign minister said "there are no plans to change the name" of the Vilnius office; Chinese diplomats are expected in Lithuania this week. [49]

Assessment: The Chinese visit is the main test of whether Vilnius can restore ties with Beijing.

Collection gap: No day-two outcome or Kremlin readout by 1000Z. France's 10 November strike turnout came only from a low-profile site with inconsistent figures and is not used. Nothing in-window on Turkey or the Caucasus beyond Georgia.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. INDONESIA: Gibran has not formally resigned; about 50 MPs back an impeachment-track motion

No formal resignation had been filed by the morning of 11 November. [50] PKS's Mardani Ali Sera said almost 50 MPs had signed a motion to express an opinion (HMP), above the minimum of 25, and Speaker Puan Maharani said the leadership will act after the sitting opens. [51] Gibran said Prabowo advised him to keep fighting in the administrative court; a government spokesman denied this. [52] The rupiah closed at Rp18,149 per dollar, down 0.48%. [53]

Assessment: Pressure is moving from calls to resign to a formal parliamentary procedure, and the palace is distancing itself from Gibran.

2. JAPAN / CHINA: Xi-Takaichi meeting at APEC appears to have fallen through

Kyodo, relayed by Seoul Economic Daily, says Japan may be the only major participant whose leader does not meet Xi, with Tokyo now seeing a brief handshake as realistic. [54] Mainichi reports Tokyo told Washington it was concerned about a possible US-China-Russia leaders' meeting. [54]

Assessment: Beijing appears set to isolate Takaichi at a summit it hosts.

3. REGIONAL MARKETS: Asian chip stocks extend gains despite hot US inflation

US October CPI rose 0.4% month on month against 0.3% expected. [55] AP reported the Taiex up 3.6%, the Nikkei up 1.3% to 61,248.59 and the Kospi up 2.4% intraday; the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.99% and the dollar was at 158.85 yen. [56]

Assessment: With US yields near 5% and the yen near 159, the risk of a pullback or currency intervention is rising.

4. CHINA / US: Trump says the FBI is "looking into" Nvidia's Huang

Trump said "I know the FBI is looking into it" and that Huang "had absolutely no idea that the person in question was a spy." [57] A shareholder suit alleges Nvidia concealed the relationship; Nvidia reports earnings on 17 November. [58] China's MFA repeated that the allegation is "nothing but smears." [57]

Assessment: A federal inquiry makes the case more likely to come up at the Trump-Xi meeting.

5. NORTH KOREA: Revised constitution names South Korea a neighbouring state

Pyongyang published its revised constitution, which drops reunification, names the South a neighbouring state alongside China and Russia without defining a boundary, and gives Kim command of nuclear forces. [59]

Assessment: Leaving the maritime boundary undefined keeps open a dispute over the Northern Limit Line.

6. TAIWAN: PLA activity stays low

An unofficial tracker of MND data lists 4 PLA aircraft, 2 crossing the median line, and 7 vessels in the 11 November report. [60] The MND site did not load, and no new Liaoning reporting was found.

Assessment: Low air activity does not mean naval pressure has eased.

7. TAIWAN / PAPUA NEW GUINEA: Taiwan begins withdrawing staff

Taiwan's foreign ministry began pulling diplomatic and technical staff out of PNG after Port Moresby gave its representative seven days to leave. [61]

Assessment: The office opened in 1990 is closing for good.

8. PHILIPPINES: Duterte trial continues with more defence witnesses

The Senate court heard more defence witnesses on unexplained wealth; an earlier defence witness confirmed a mismatch between Duterte's asset declaration and tax records. [62]

Assessment: Defence witnesses appear to be adding to the prosecution's case.

Collection gap: Taiwan MND did not load. Nothing new in-window on APEC confirmations from Beijing or Moscow, India-Pakistan, India-China, Bangladesh, Australia, the South China Sea or Myanmar. Closing index levels were not confirmed.

area of responsibility

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES: House refers Hegseth impeachment to committee, 211-204

The House voted 211-204 on 10 November to send Rep. Thomas Massie's impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary Hegseth to committee, effectively killing it; Massie was the only Republican against referral. [63] Hegseth called the effort "a farce," and Trump said he "gets an 'A' PLUS, PLUS, PLUS." [63]

Assessment: The House route against Hegseth is closed for this Congress; pressure is more likely from the Democratic majority in January.

2. UNITED STATES: Jordan elected House GOP leader; Senate Republicans keep Thune

Jim Jordan beat Steve Scalise 106-73 to become House Republican leader, with Tom Emmer staying as whip. [64] [65] Senate Republicans re-elected John Thune unopposed by secret ballot after Sen. Ron Johnson sought a delay and walked out; Rick Scott said "We need a plan." [66]

Assessment: A Trump-aligned House leader and a split Senate conference raise the risk of friction over the 11 December funding deadline.

3. UNITED STATES (MISSISSIPPI): Affidavit ballots widen Colom's lead

The Mississippi Free Press reported that affidavit ballots in Humphreys and Sharkey counties were growing Colom's lead of "more than 2,500" before the 10 November cure deadline; no new legal filings were reported. [67] No post-deadline statewide total was found; counties certify on 13 November.

Assessment: The trend favours Colom, and a post-certification challenge remains the main risk.

4. UNITED STATES (ALASKA): Peltola's lead grows to 1.8 points

After a 24,000-ballot update, Peltola leads Sullivan 49.0% to 47.2% with about 84% counted; the next release is 13 November and ranked-choice tabulation is on 18 November. [68] A conservative outlet's own simulation still shows Sullivan narrowly ahead after transfers. [69]

Assessment: Peltola is favoured, but transfers keep the 18 November tabulation open.

5. MEXICO / US: Sheinbaum says relations unchanged under Miller

Sheinbaum said "No ha habido nada que nos alerte de que algo está cambiando" after Miller became chief of staff and repeated that Mexico does not share the "terrorism" label for cartels. [70]

Assessment: Mexico is signalling continuity while ruling out unilateral US action.

6. MEXICO: 35 tonnes of suspected meth precursor seized in Sinaloa

García Harfuch said federal forces stopped a truck carrying about 35 tonnes of a chemical preliminarily identified as lead acetate on the Mazatlán-Culiacán highway; three people were detained. [71]

Assessment: The federal surge in Sinaloa is now hitting synthetic-drug supply chains.

7. CANADA: Carney's trip includes Dublin and a Xi meeting at APEC

Carney leaves on 11 November for the European Political Community summit in Dublin, the Francophonie in Phnom Penh, ASEAN in Manila and APEC in Shenzhen, where he is to meet Xi. [72]

Assessment: A Carney-Xi meeting at a summit Trump attends will draw US attention to warming Canada-China ties.

8. UNITED STATES: Judge dismisses Portland ICE lawyer-access suit

A federal judge dismissed without prejudice a suit alleging ICE blocked detainees' access to lawyers during Operation Black Rose. [73]

Assessment: A procedural win for ICE; plaintiffs can refile.

Collection gap: No post-cure statewide Mississippi total, new Trump election statements or Miller policy actions. NPR, Axios, ADN and Mississippi Today did not load.

area of responsibility

SOUTHCOM.

1. COLOMBIA: Eighth airstrike ordered; reports of US drones in Catatumbo raid

President De La Espriella announced an eighth airstrike late on 10 November against an unnamed "narcoterrorist structure," with no location or casualties given. [74] Unnamed sources told El Tiempo that US intelligence and MQ-9 Reaper drones supported the 9 November Catatumbo operation against FARC dissident Front 33; there is no official confirmation. [75] Pre-window onset: an ELN communiqué claims the 30 October strike in Apure killed Venezuelan civilians. [76]

Assessment: The pace of airstrikes is rising, and reported US involvement would mark an escalation.

2. HAITI: Bukele claims the 400 Mawozo strike

Bukele posted that Salvadoran forces "destroyed the house of Lanmo San Jou," and PM Fils-Aimé replied "same fight, same determination!" [77] Al Jazeera reports 20 killed and says Amnesty wants the deployment agreement made public; AFP puts the Salvadoran force at more than 2,000, below Tuesday's 2,500. [78] [77]

Assessment: Salvadoran forces operating outside the UN-backed force raise the risk of deconfliction failures and civilian harm.

3. ECUADOR: At least 45 killings around the end of the curfew

A press-based count records at least 45 killings from 6 to 10 November, including a mayoral candidate and a council candidate shot at a rally in Huaquillas. [79]

Assessment: Attacks on candidates point to rising political violence before the 29 November elections.

4. VENEZUELA: At least 228 apply for Supreme Court seats

At least 228 people have applied for the 32 principal TSJ seats and alternates; applications close on 11 November. [80]

Assessment: The shortlist will be the first test of the process's credibility.

5. CUBA: Felton 1 shutdown pushes deficit near 2,000 MW

The UNE forecast a 1,960 MW shortfall for the 10 November peak after Felton 1 went offline for maintenance, up from 1,795 MW. [81]

Assessment: Blackouts will likely worsen while Felton 1 is offline.

6. GUATEMALA: Congress swears in slain deputy's successor

Congress condemned the killing of Deputy Julio Portillo and three police officers and swore in his successor; no arrests have been reported. [82]

Assessment: No accountability yet adds to concern about organised crime reaching the legislature.

7. REGION: No tropical formation expected

NHC expects no tropical cyclone formation in the Atlantic or Eastern Pacific for seven days. [83]

Assessment: No hurricane threat to the AOR.

Collection gap: No outcome for Lima's 10 November transport strike, nothing in-window on Brazil, no confirmed Haiti reprisals and no details on Colombia's eighth strike.
