area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. TANZANIA: Lissu cleared of treason, convicted of incitement

A three-judge High Court panel in Dar es Salaam acquitted Chadema chairman Tundu Lissu of treason, which can carry the death penalty, and convicted him of incitement. [1] [2] [3] The court found the prosecution "failed to prove the second element of the treason charge." [4] No sentence had been reported by about 0945Z, and he can appeal. [2] Police patrolled outside the court. [1] Amnesty accused the government of "weaponising counterterrorism laws." [2]

Assessment: Dropping the capital charge eases pressure for immediate unrest, but the pending sentence keeps Lissu exposed and could still prompt Chadema mobilisation.

2. SOUTH AFRICA: ANC 31.7%, DA 25.1% with two-thirds counted; turnout about 54%

With 67.5% counted, BusinessTech has the ANC at 31.7%, the DA at 25.1%, MK at 8.5% and the EFF at 6.1%; the DA leads six metros including Johannesburg and Tshwane, and MK leads eThekwini on an early count. [5] The IEC gives preliminary turnout of about 15.9 million of 29.2 million registered, up from 45.87% in 2021; Thursday's 14.5 million did not include special votes. [6] [7] The DA has ruled out MK in eThekwini, and the final declaration is expected Sunday evening. [6]

Assessment: Gauteng metro control will likely turn on a DA-ActionSA deal that both parties are holding back on.

3. SOMALIA: Supreme Court voids the chief justice's dismissal and rules the term extension unlawful

The Supreme Court ruled President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud unlawfully extended a term it says expired in May, declared his dismissal of the chief justice "null and void," and ordered elections within 90 days. [8] [9] Former PM Hassan Ali Khaire said troops were deployed to the court; AFP could not verify this. [9] The president has called national talks for 14-18 November. [8]

Assessment: Rival claims over the judiciary and a ruling that the head of state is out of mandate raise the risk of confrontation between security forces in Mogadishu.

4. NIGERIA: Court voids Atiku's ADC candidacy

A Federal High Court judge dissolved the ADC leadership, voided Atiku Abubakar's presidential ticket and removed all ADC candidates two months before the 16 January election. [10] The ADC called it "another desperate attempt to cripple the opposition" and will appeal; the APC denied involvement. [11]

Assessment: If upheld, the ruling would leave Tinubu's main challenger without a ballot line and fuel claims of lawfare.

5. SUDAN / ETHIOPIA: Each closes the other's border consulate

Ethiopia closed Sudan's consulate in Assosa and Sudan closed Ethiopia's in Gedaref, amid Sudanese claims that the RSF launches drones from Ethiopian territory. [12] Critical Threats records 21 sets of RSF strikes on fuel and power sites since 22 October and reports unverified Ethiopia-Eritrea clashes. [13]

Assessment: The Sudan war is turning into an interstate dispute across the Horn.

6. DRC: Army retakes Mahanga and Kilongo; Kinshasa says the FRN "will not reach Kinshasa"

Government and allied forces retook two Masisi villages the FRN had held for months. [14] Spokesman Patrick Muyaya called the conflict "a Rwandan aggression." [15]

Assessment: Further fighting is more likely in the short term than renewed talks.

7. DRC: WHO adds two Bundibugyo vaccines to the Ituri trial

Oxford/Serum Institute and Moderna vaccines designed for the Bundibugyo strain will join the Bunia ring-vaccination trial. WHO counts more than 10,500 confirmed cases and 5,126 deaths, lower than the 5,300+ cited Thursday. [16]

Assessment: The vaccines are a significant step, but case counts are unlikely to fall soon.

8. BURKINA FASO / MALI: Goïta and Traoré meet in Ouagadougou

Gen. Assimi Goïta arrived for one-on-one talks with Capt. Ibrahim Traoré on counter-terrorism and AES cooperation; no agreements were reported. [17]

Assessment: The visit is mainly a show of AES unity under JNIM pressure.

Collection gap: No Lissu sentence, Chadema reaction or internet-status reporting by 1100Z. No in-window JNIM, Libya or Sudan frontline reporting. No Somali government response to the troop claims.

CENTCOM.

1. ISRAEL: Eisenkot recommended by 67 MKs; Herzog to assign the mandate on Sunday

Consultations ended with factions totalling 67 MKs, including the Joint List and Ra'am, recommending Gadi Eisenkot and 46 recommending Netanyahu; UTJ recommended no one. [18] [19] Herzog said the results "appear clear" but cited "mutual vetoes between the blocs and within the blocs themselves," and will decide on Sunday 8 November. [19] [20] Joint List MK Ahmad Tibi said the party would back a minority government from outside but would not be a "safety net." Netanyahu continues to point to possible vote tampering; the Central Election Committee says integrity was not compromised. [19]

Assessment: Eisenkot is very likely to receive the first mandate, but his 67 include Arab parties he has ruled out as partners, so they are not a working coalition.

2. ISRAEL: Report that Liberman dropped his veto on Haredi parties; party denies it

N12 reported that Avigdor Liberman has dropped his opposition to governing with Haredi parties, opening talks with UTJ. Yisrael Beytenu said: "We are not aware of any such thing. Nothing has changed." [21] An Eisenkot bloc plus UTJ would still hold only 55 seats. [21]

Assessment: If confirmed, UTJ is the most plausible route to a Jewish-majority Eisenkot coalition; treat the report as unconfirmed.

3. UNITED STATES / IRAN: Trump says a decision is coming "soon"; no order issued

Trump met his national security team on 4 and 5 November and said: "Now we will make a decision... I would rather not destroy the rest of the country if possible, but maybe I'll have to. You'll know soon enough." [22] [23] Fox, citing Axios, reported CENTCOM is finalising preparations but "has not received a new directive," is "tightening" the blockade of Iranian ports, and that the USS Theodore Roosevelt strike group will not reach the region until mid-November. [24] No US strike was reported in the window.

Assessment: The carrier's timing points to a later start for any full resumption of combat, though not a guaranteed one.

4. IRAN / QATAR: Tehran sends a nuclear proposal but sets seven preconditions

A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran sent the US a nuclear proposal via Qatar, to be discussed only after Washington meets seven demands including sanctions relief, release of frozen assets and war reparations. A US official said Washington is "reviewing" it. [25] Rezaei said: "As long as they do not execute our seven conditions, we will neither open the Strait of Hormuz nor have negotiations." [22] Omani and Iranian foreign ministers spoke again on 5 November. [26]

Assessment: Iran's preconditions run opposite to Washington's sequencing, so a quick breakthrough is unlikely.

5. IRAN / RUSSIA: Rezaei delivers a Khamenei letter to Putin

SNSC secretary Mohsen Rezaei met Putin in Moscow and delivered a written message from Mojtaba Khamenei. Rezaei said Putin "reiterated readiness" to help end the war and that they discussed post-war regional security and a gas pipeline; no Kremlin readout was found. [27]

Assessment: Tehran is signalling outside backing, but the only account of Russian commitments comes from the Iranian side.

6. HORMUZ: Iran claims three more tankers hit mines

The Iranian army said three more tankers struck mines and caught fire in "unsafe waters east of the Strait of Hormuz" on 5 November. [28] [26] None has been independently identified, and the tanker hit north of Qatar on 4 November also remains unidentified. [28]

Assessment: Iran is likely using mine claims to enforce its "authorised routes" regime; the claimed count is unverified.

7. ENERGY: Brent rebounds towards $100

Brent closed Wednesday down 8.7% at $94.62 and was near $99.94 early on 6 November, with WTI at $91.72. [29] [30] The National puts the weekly loss at about 12% (15% in its own summary). [30]

Assessment: Traders are pricing a possible strike decision; an announcement would likely push Brent above $100.

8. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: Houthis claim Dammam airport strike; coalition reports intercept near Madinah

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed a drone strike on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam; Saudi authorities did not comment. [31] [26] CTP reports Saudi air support helped government forces retake ground in al-Maqatirah. [32] No Saudi response to the reported $51 billion demand was found.

Assessment: The Houthis are escalating against Gulf aviation targets while Riyadh stays silent on their terms.

9. GAZA: Five killed; post-ceasefire toll passes 1,600

AFP and hospital sources said Israeli fire killed five people, including a 12-year-old boy east of al-Bureij. The health ministry toll since the ceasefire now exceeds 1,600. [33]

Rep. Thomas Massie will reintroduce eight impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Hegseth in the lame-duck session unless he resigns; Hegseth called it a "sham." [34] [35] Trump said Hegseth is "doing a great job." [36]

Assessment: Hegseth's position looks secure with Trump for now, though a forced House vote is possible.

11. PAKISTAN / AFGHANISTAN: Army says it foiled a TTP infiltration in Kurram

Pakistani security sources said troops blocked a TTP infiltration in Kurram's Shaluzan sector and returned "unprovoked fire" from Afghan Taliban posts. [37] No casualty figures or Afghan account were found.

Assessment: Border clashes are recurring every few days; the claims are one-sided.

3. RUSSIA: Afipsky refinery reportedly hit; Russia says it downed 142 drones

Russia's Defence Ministry said it downed 142 drones; Krasnodar authorities reported a fire at an "enterprise" in Afipsky that footage identifies as the refinery. [43] [40] The hit has not been officially confirmed. [43] ISW also recorded Ukrainian strikes on Ryazan and Oryol. [44]

4. UKRAINE: 188 engagements; ISW reports Ukrainian gain near Orikhiv

6. FRANCE: 575,000 marchers by official count, 1.6 million per CGT; 448 arrests

7. EUROPE: Hoax bomb threats disrupt about 30 flights; Baltic cable saboteurs jailed

[50] A Helsinki court jailed the Russian captain of the Fitburg for Baltic cable sabotage. [51] The Kremlin called claims it hacked supermarket freezers "absurd." [52]

Assessment: The incidents fit a pattern of low-cost disruption across European infrastructure, with attribution unresolved.

8. TURKEY: Parliament ratifies the Mecca defence treaty with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan

The assembly approved the treaty 279 to 119; it commits members to "immediate assistance" if one is attacked. [53]

Assessment: A NATO member taking on a separate Gulf defence obligation raises questions of overlapping commitments.

9. MOLDOVA: Travel ban for Transnistria leader after Putin honours him

Chisinau plans to bar Vadim Krasnoselsky from leaving the country for five years after Putin gave him the Order of Honor. [54]

Assessment: The ban adds friction as Transnistria depends on winter gas arrangements.

Collection gap: French primary outlets did not load, so final strike figures are secondary. Nothing new in-window for Georgia or Serbia. No reporting on Abu Dhabi delegations.

5. NORTH KOREA: No new launch; statements against FATF and Japan
