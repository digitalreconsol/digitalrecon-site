area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. SOUTH AFRICA: Municipal elections under way with troops deployed

Polls opened at 0700 local time with heavy early turnout reported in the Western Cape, Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal. Police and SANDF troops were deployed, and the acting police minister condemned election-related killings of party members. [1] Faulty voter-management devices in several Johannesburg wards forced manual processing, and some stations opened late. [2]

Assessment: Logistics faults are the early friction point; the highest unrest risk comes with results and coalition talks in the metros over the next 72 hours.

2. TANZANIA: Police warn of planned "violence or sabotage" around Lissu verdict

Dar es Salaam's police commander said police are monitoring people mobilising to attend court on 6 November, some allegedly planning violence, and would act "swiftly"; he presented no evidence. Twenty-four parliamentarians from six countries and the EU urged President Samia to guarantee a fair trial and rule out the death penalty. [3]

Assessment: Expect a heavy security presence and possible pre-emptive arrests; a conviction carries a high risk of protests.

3. SUDAN / ETHIOPIA: No Ethiopian reply or UN action yet

Sudan's filing says October alone saw 28 attacks with 97 drones and that an attack on displaced families at Wad el-Mahi on 9 October killed 30. No Ethiopian response or Security Council or AU action has been reported. [4] The Soufan Center says Ethiopia rejects the claims and counter-accuses the Sudanese army of backing TPLF-linked groups, and notes that the Merowe Dam supplies more than 70% of Sudan's power. [5]

Assessment: The diplomatic track is stalled pending Ethiopia's formal reply; a strike on Merowe would turn local outages into a near-national blackout.

4. SOMALIA: Opposition agrees to "final" dialogue; AU to backfill bases near Mogadishu

The opposition National Salvation Forum agreed to give the president "one final opportunity" through dialogue, with talks on the 14th. [6] An AU plan has peacekeepers taking over four bases critical to protecting Mogadishu as Ugandan troops leave, while moving out of Barawe. [7] No al-Shabaab move into vacated positions was found.

Assessment: Pulling AU troops toward Mogadishu thins the Lower Shabelle corridor, giving al-Shabaab room to probe.

5. WESTERN SAHARA: General Assembly to debate the vetoes on 10 November

The General Assembly will meet on 10 November under its veto-review procedure after the 30 October double veto, and Morocco says MINURSO should leave "in the coming days." [8] The Polisario called the outcome "unacceptable" and threatened to quit the UN process. [9]

Assessment: With no observers and the Polisario threatening to disengage, the risk of incidents along the ceasefire line is rising.

6. NIGERIA: Eight security personnel killed in Zamfara ambush

Attackers ambushed a police convoy in Tsafe, killing five police and three community guards and seizing five rifles; some officers were reportedly abducted. Several Nigerian outlets gave only the police toll of five. [10]

Assessment: Bandits can still overrun mobile police units and capture weapons that fuel further attacks.

7. BURKINA FASO: HRW documents JNIM killings at a gold-mining site

Human Rights Watch says more than 60 JNIM fighters killed 24 civilians at the Soubo mining site on 1 September and a suspected IED killed three days later. [11]

Assessment: The report adds evidence that JNIM targets artisanal mining economies and uses IEDs in the north.

Collection gap: No window reporting on Ethiopia-Eritrea, eastern DRC, Libya, Mali, Algerian troops in Niger or new Sudan grid strikes. No South African turnout data yet.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. IRAN / US: Tehran rejects US Hormuz reply; Trump "locked and loaded" but no immediate plans

Rezaei wrote that "America's response to Iran's rights and demands was not convincing" and that Hormuz would reopen only if Iran's conditions are met. [12] Trump told the FT there were no immediate strike plans: "We're locked and loaded if we need to go, but there may be no reason for it." [13] On Fox he congratulated Democrats and said "a part of it was Iran." [14] No new US strikes after the polls closed were reported, and CENTCOM has made no on-record statement on Qeshm. Al Jazeera says US media still report strike plans that could start as early as Wednesday. [12]

Assessment: Trump's remarks lower the odds of an immediate campaign but do not rule one out, and Iran's rejection keeps talks stalled.

2. IRAN: Ghalibaf says Americans "said NO TO WAR"

After Democrats won Congress, Ghalibaf wrote that Americans "said NO TO WAR" and warned Washington would "learn our NO comes with a Nightmare" if it ignored public opinion. [15]

Assessment: Tehran reads the midterm result as a constraint on Washington and pairs that message with deterrent threats.

3. QATAR / IRAN / US: Doha sees "hope"; unconfirmed report of an approved draft

Qatar's spokesman said mediators are exchanging proposals in real time: "If there were no hope, there would be no negotiations." [16] Pakistan Today, citing Al-Araby Al-Jadeed and unnamed sources, reports Washington approved a Qatari draft pairing an Iranian nuclear concession with release of frozen assets; this conflicts with Rezaei's rejection. [17] Egypt's president urged both sides to show "the greatest possible flexibility." [18]

Assessment: The mediation channel is working, but nothing on the record shows an agreed text.

4. HORMUZ / OMAN: Tanker hit off Limah; IRGC claims four tankers destroyed

UKMTO reported a tanker hit in the engine room by two projectiles about 14 nm northeast of Limah, Oman, at 1900 GMT on 3 November, with no casualties. [19] The IRGC says four tankers using an "unauthorized route" hit mines and two vessels were seized; Reuters found no confirmation. [20]

Assessment: Attacks continue at October's record pace, and the mine claims add to shipping and insurance risk whether or not they are verified.

5. ENERGY: Brent edges up to $104.42

Brent rose to $104.42 by 0510Z on Wednesday after settling on Tuesday at its lowest in nearly four weeks; Tuesday's intraday high near $107 did not hold. [21]

Assessment: The market is pricing a lower chance of a near-term strike and reacting more to talks than to shipping attacks.

6. ISRAEL: Herzog consultations open; right-wing parties to back Netanyahu

Religious Zionism, Otzma Yehudit and Shas will recommend Netanyahu, and Netanyahu said the public does not want a government dependent on Arab parties. [22] Herzog meets Religious Zionism at 1700, Shas at 1815 and Yisrael Beytenu, which backs Eisenkot, at 1915 local time today; Likud is last on 5 November. [23]

Assessment: Eisenkot is likely to get the first mandate, but his path to 61 still depends on Arab-party support that Bennett and Liberman oppose.

7. GAZA: Four killed; Israel blocks goods through Rafah

Israeli strikes killed at least four people on 4 November, and the IDF says it hit militants. [24] Israel told coordinators it would block goods through Rafah, citing smuggling. [25] Drop Site, citing the Health Ministry, gives at least 1,599 killed since the ceasefire. [26]

Assessment: Aid restrictions and Israel-backed armed groups are eroding the ceasefire alongside daily strikes.

8. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: Coalition downs drone over Makkah region

The coalition said it intercepted a drone over the Makkah region; the Houthis called this a "recycled lie" and reported 109 Saudi strikes. [27] Yemen's government claims 1,208 strikes and about 370 Houthi fighters "neutralized." [28] None of the figures is verified.

Assessment: The Saudi-Houthi war is intensifying on a track separate from the US-Iran standoff.

9. IRAQ: Baghdad works to keep factions neutral

A Coordination Framework source says the government is using intermediaries to keep factions neutral and deploying forces to areas including Basra, but doubts it can protect oil exports. The deadline for state control of faction weapons has moved to June 2027. [29]

Assessment: By its own officials' account, Baghdad may not be able to restrain the factions if strikes resume.

10. LEBANON: Israeli strikes in the south and mock raids in the north

Israeli aircraft struck near Kfar Tebnit and carried out mock raids over Akkar and Hermel; no casualties were reported. [30]

Assessment: Northern overflights fit preparation for a possible Hezbollah entry if the Iran war resumes.

11. PAKISTAN: Two police guarding polio teams killed

A gunman killed two police officers escorting polio vaccinators in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and four polio workers were abducted elsewhere. [31]

Assessment: Militant pressure in the province continues alongside border clashes with the Taliban.

Collection gap: No CENTCOM or Pentagon on-record statement. Herzog's first sessions fall after the window. Nothing found for Jordan or Central Asia.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE: Russia launches 310 drones overnight at energy targets

Ukraine's Air Force says Russia launched 310 drones, 81 of them jet-powered, and 272 were downed, with hits at 19 locations. [32] Energy infrastructure in Kirovohrad was targeted, and warming centres opened in parts of Kyiv. [33]

Assessment: A second straight night of mass strikes points to a sustained campaign to deny power before the Abu Dhabi talks.

2. UKRAINE: Komyshuvakha toll rises to 33-37; 59 missing

The acting Zaporizhzhia regional head put the toll at 37. [34] Another official said 31 bodies had been recovered, up to 38 more may be under rubble and 59 are missing, and the same report cites 33 killed. [35] Moscow says it hit military units there. [36]

Assessment: The toll is likely to rise, and reported use of FAB-3000 bombs against an aid point will drive condemnation.

3. UKRAINE: Kyiv says a nuclear-plant substation was targeted

Energy Minister Shmyhal said three drones were aimed at a substation critical to the Khmelnytskyi nuclear plant on 3 November and called it "a serious escalation"; the IAEA was notified. [37] DTEK said three of its five thermal plants went offline. [36]

Assessment: Targeting off-site power for nuclear plants is a new element of the energy campaign.

4. UKRAINE / EUROPE: Brussels talks ahead of Abu Dhabi

Ukraine, the US, NATO and European officials meet in Brussels on reconstruction on 4 November and security guarantees on 5 November. [38] Merz called the Abu Dhabi talks "the right step" but said he had "not seen any sign" of serious Russian engagement. [36] ISW reports no advances by either side on 3 November. [39]

Assessment: The 5 November guarantees session is the key coordination step before Abu Dhabi.

5. POLAND: Tusk says a possible provocation was thwarted

Tusk said border guards saw "disturbing movement" near the Kaliningrad border overnight on 2-3 November and that any incident was prevented, though he could not confirm it was a provocation. [40]

Assessment: The first reported incident since the deployment supports Warsaw's threat picture; more are likely through 8 November.

6. RUSSIA: Unity Day

Putin is presenting unity awards and laying flowers on 4 November, and his holiday address praised defending the homeland against a "cruel enemy." [41][42] No security incidents were found.

Assessment: The messaging is routine and signals no shift before Abu Dhabi.

7. FRANCE: Rail and air disruption expected on 5 November

SNCF expects regional and RER services to be disrupted, and the civil aviation authority is to order flight cuts; the Paris march route was not approved as of 3 November. [43][44]

Assessment: Expect significant rail and possibly air disruption, with confrontation risk in central Paris.

8. TÜRKİYE: European court takes up İmamoğlu detention case

Human Rights Watch and partners filed a brief to the European Court of Human Rights in İmamoğlu's detention case. [45]

Assessment: An eventual ruling would deepen friction with the Council of Europe, with no near-term effect.

Collection gap: No verified window reporting on Ukrainian strikes inside Russia, Georgia, Moldova, Spain, Serbia or NATO airspace.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. NORTH KOREA: Missiles fired toward the East Sea on Election Day

South Korea's JCS said North Korea fired "multiple" missiles from the Sondok area toward the East Sea early on 4 November, believed to have flown less than 300 km. An analyst assessed them as Hwasong-11 Ra tactical ballistic missiles. [46] JoongAng Daily calls it the fourth show of force in a week. [47]

Assessment: The launch pairs messaging to Washington with tactical-weapon demonstrations.

2. NORTH KOREA: Kim Yo Jong rules out face-to-face talks

A second Kim Yo Jong statement said North Korea has "no need to sit face to face with such unreliable and hostile persons" and that countermeasures should "set new records." [47][48]

Assessment: Summit prospects have dimmed and further tests are likely.

3. KOREAS: Loudspeakers installed but silent

NK News observers saw newly installed North Korean loudspeakers at two border sites on 4 November, but no broadcasts were heard. [49]

Assessment: Pyongyang can now start broadcasts at short notice.

4. TAIWAN: Sorties fall to 22; Taipei says over 100 Chinese vessels deployed

Taiwan's MND reported 22 PLA sorties, down from 49 the day before. Security official Joseph Wu said China has more than 100 navy and coast guard vessels across the Western Pacific, with two carriers at sea. [50] An analyst called it a "quiet exercise" with no named drill. [51] The Taipei Times says the USS Theodore Roosevelt is transiting the region. [52]

Assessment: Sustained, unannounced pressure is likely ahead of Taiwan's 28 November local elections.

5. PHILIPPINES / CHINA: Wang Yi warns Manila; Reed Bank talks discussed

Wang Yi told Foreign Secretary Lazaro that "malicious interference" by Filipino politicians was harming ties, and the two discussed resuming joint energy talks at Reed Bank. [53]

Assessment: Beijing is pairing a warning with an economic incentive while keeping up its presence at sea.

6. CHINA: Midterms "an internal matter"

The Foreign Ministry called the US midterms "an internal matter" and reaffirmed the Xi-Trump consensus. [54] No Chinese comment on the Qeshm strike was found.

Assessment: Beijing is signalling continuity ahead of APEC and the 10 November rare-earth deadline.

7. BANGLADESH: Wage board agrees to raise garment minimum wage

AFP reports the wage board agreed to a raise without setting the amount as protests reached day five. [55] Reuters reports more factories stopped work. [56] Sources differ on whether new pay takes effect in December or January. [57]

Assessment: Without a figure, unrest is likely to continue.

8. MYANMAR: US strategy signals engagement with the junta

RFA reports a State Department country strategy that points to engagement with the military government, with rare earths in view. [58]

Assessment: China will likely read this as competition over Myanmar's rare earths.

Collection gap: No window reporting on Japan, India-Pakistan, India-China, Indonesia, Australia or the Solomon Islands. MND primary pages did not load.

area of responsibility

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES: Democrats win both chambers; Trump congratulates them

AP called both the House and Senate for Democrats; as of 0130 EST Democrats had flipped seven Senate and 16 House seats without losing any. [59] Trump first wrote that he "would have won IN A LANDSLIDE" had he been on the ballot, then congratulated Democrats in a Fox call. [60] Forbes reports Xavier Becerra won California's governor race. [61]

Assessment: Democrats will control both chambers from January, and Trump's initial reaction accepts the result.

2. UNITED STATES: Talarico wins Texas; outlets differ on Senate flips

AP called Texas for Democrat James Talarico, the state's first Democratic senator since 1993. [59] Sources disagree on Maine and Kansas: Spectrum counts Maine as flipped, while Forbes lists Kansas as flipped and Maine as uncalled. [61]

Assessment: Democratic gains are broad, but the final Senate count is not yet settled.

3. UNITED STATES (ALASKA): Senate race within 300 votes; DOJ target letters

With 211 of 403 precincts reporting, Mary Peltola led Sen. Dan Sullivan by 298 votes, while the second "Dan Sullivan" had about 1.6%. Ranked-choice tabulation is set for 18 November. [62] The NYT reports DOJ sent target letters to about 20 Democrats, including Marc Elias, over recruiting the second Sullivan. [63]

Assessment: The second Sullivan's share exceeds the margin, setting up a legal fight over certification.

4. UNITED STATES: Local disruptions on Election Day

A bomb threat forced evacuation of a polling school in Gibraltar, Michigan, and police identified a suspect. [64] New Mexico's secretary of state said DOJ monitors had been "questioning voters," and a monitor in Dallas was reportedly asked to leave. [65] Reuters reports DOJ published its monitor list. [66]

Assessment: Disruptions were local and brief, and monitor friction did not halt voting.

5. MEXICO: Ballistics point to fire from above in Matamoros child death

Tamaulipas prosecutors found the 7-year-old US citizen was killed by a 7.62mm round on a downward path and requested state helicopter flight logs. [67][68]

Assessment: The evidence makes state-force responsibility more likely, which could become a US-Mexico issue.

6. MEXICO: Three detained over Culiacán fairground attack

Sinaloa police detained three men, one a minor, and seized rifles and drugs. [69]

Assessment: The arrests are a quick win amid continuing daily violence.

7. CANADA: Carney says US is "asking less and offering more"

Carney said trade talks have intensified and progress has been made, and Trump said a deal was coming soon. [70]

Assessment: A deal looks close, though no date is set.

8. TROPICS: Atlantic low shifts south of Bermuda

NHC now flags a low south of Bermuda at 40% in seven days, with no stated threat to the Bahamas. [71]

Assessment: The risk to the Bahamas and US Southeast has dropped.

Collection gap: No exact time or final count for the House call. No post-election litigation beyond Alaska and no cyber incidents found.

area of responsibility

SOUTHCOM.

1. VENEZUELA / COLOMBIA: US took part in strike on ELN camp, NYT reports

The NYT, relayed by Semana, reports four US officials confirmed US participation in a Venezuelan strike on an ELN site in Apure around 30 October. A Venezuelan NGO says at least 23 died and not all were ELN members. [72]

Assessment: US-backed cross-border strikes on the ELN are a new escalation that may provoke retaliation in Colombia.

2. VENEZUELA: US negotiating a status-of-forces agreement

The NYT reports the State Department is negotiating a SOFA with the Rodríguez government covering exercises, training and port visits; a senior official said bases are "very, very far" off. [73]

Assessment: A SOFA would formalise a lasting US military presence and could fuel nationalist backlash.

3. COLOMBIA: "Esparta" campaign launched with about 1,200 personnel

De la Espriella launched the "Esparta" campaign against the ELN and Frente 33 in Catatumbo with about 1,200 troops and police and anti-drone equipment. [74]

Assessment: Together with the Apure strike, this points to a squeeze on the ELN from both sides of the border.

4. HAITI: Salvadoran and Haitian forces push into Cul-de-Sac plain

Salvadoran troops clashed with gangs in Santo and Croix-des-Missions, with at least 12 reported killed, a figure not confirmed. [75] The UN said receiving the gang leader's letter "does not constitute an endorsement" and that most Salvadoran troops are not under the UN-backed force. [76]

Assessment: El Salvador is operating largely outside the UN framework, raising accountability risks.

5. PERU: 60-day El Niño emergency declared

The government declared a 60-day emergency over 1,380 districts in 24 departments plus Callao. [77][78] Rains have left 324 people homeless in Tumbes. [79]

Assessment: Damage in the north is likely to grow as the rainy season starts.

6. ECUADOR: Curfew arrests fall on third night

Arrests totalled 491 over the first two nights, then fell sharply on the third. [80]

Assessment: The drop suggests compliance rather than degraded criminal groups.

7. BRAZIL: Flávio Bolsonaro logs Senate attendance from the US

Flávio Bolsonaro registered his attendance remotely at a Senate session, and his lawyers asked Justice Moraes to lift the passport order and step aside. [81][82]

Assessment: He intends to keep his mandate from abroad, setting up a confrontation with the Supreme Court.

8. CUBA: Peak shortfall near 2 GW

The utility forecast a 1,966 MW peak shortfall for 3 November, with five thermal units broken down. [83]

Assessment: Evening blackouts across most of the island will continue.

9. CENTRAL AMERICA: Heavy-rain risk from a new Pacific system

NHC flags a trough off Central America with 40% development odds and heavy rain possible this weekend. [84]

Assessment: Flood and landslide risk is building on the Pacific slope.

Collection gap: No Venezuelan or Colombian government statement on the Apure strike; NYT originals did not load.
