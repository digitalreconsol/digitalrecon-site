area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. SUDAN / ETHIOPIA: Khartoum takes drone charges to the UN and AU; three more substations hit

Sudan filed complaints against Ethiopia with the UN Security Council and the AU Peace and Security Council. Its Sovereignty Council says 70 assaults with 187 drones between 15 February and 27 October killed or wounded 484 civilians and killed 176 soldiers, and names Asosa, the Kurmuk-Gizen border area and Bahir Dar as launch sites. [1] Early on 2 November drones reportedly hit substations at New Halfa and Aroma in Kassala and al-Ghazali in Northern State, the 18th grid strike in a week. The army says an RSF group surrendered in Damazin and that its commander described "Ethiopian support" to the RSF. [2] The figures are Sudanese government claims; no Ethiopian reply was found in the window.

Assessment: By going to the UN and AU, Khartoum has made the dispute formally interstate, and the strikes in Kassala widen the area at risk.

2. SOMALIA: Uganda begins immediate bilateral troop pullout; Barawe handed to the army

Uganda's military spokesman said troops deployed under bilateral arrangements are withdrawing "effective immediately," and all Ugandan forces including those with AUSSOM are to leave by the end of 2027. [3] Security of Barawe port and Shalanbood passed to the Somali army on 3 November; earlier handovers elsewhere were followed by security reversals. [4]

Assessment: Coming days after the UN mission closed, the pullout likely opens a gap in Lower Shabelle that al-Shabaab is positioned to exploit.

3. NIGERIA / DRC: Nigeria's suspected Ebola cases negative; Congo toll revised up

NCDC says the two suspects in Yola tested negative after two rounds; contacts of the Abuja index case rose from 44 to 53, and Nigeria needs 42 days without a new case to be declared free. [5] Congo's death toll is now 5,372 from 10,414 confirmed cases, which CCTV+ attributes to a retrospective data adjustment in the 1 November situation report. [6][7]

Assessment: The negative results ease concern about spread in the northeast; the higher Congo toll is a reporting correction, not a sudden surge.

4. NIGER / ALGERIA: First Algerian military-escorted convoy reaches Agadez

About 60 fuel tankers escorted by Algerian forces crossed into Niger over the weekend and reached Agadez. RT cites unconfirmed sources on possible future Algerian posts. [8] Reuters, via The New Arab, earlier reported a first troop convoy at Arlit, Algeria's first deployment abroad since 1973. [9] The two accounts may describe separate movements; Algeria has not disclosed force size.

Assessment: Algeria is moving from diplomacy to a military presence protecting fuel supply and its energy interests, shifting the balance with Mali and Morocco.

5. WESTERN SAHARA: UN details MINURSO drawdown

The UN spokesperson said about 160 military observers and 180 civilian staff will leave and about 13 sites will close, with a residual team remaining. Morocco's palace called the closure a step toward removing the dispute from the Security Council agenda. [10] Foreign Minister Bourita has said the ceasefire remains "a political and legal commitment." [11] No official Polisario statement was found.

Assessment: Without observers, incidents along the berm will likely go unverified.

6. GHANA / RUSSIA: Accra says Putin agreed to stop recruiting Ghanaians

Ghana says Putin gave the assurance to President Mahama at the Russia-Africa summit. More than 270 Ghanaians had fought for Russia by mid-August, with at least 62 killed. [12]

Assessment: Moscow is trading recruitment curbs for goodwill; compliance cannot yet be verified.

7. LIBYA / NIGER: Saddam Haftar signs cooperation protocol with Niger

Saddam Haftar signed a "strategic cooperation protocol" with Niger's prime minister in Benghazi on 1 November; terms were not disclosed. The Tripoli government signed its own military memorandum with Niger in June. [13]

Assessment: Niamey is dealing with both rival Libyan authorities while the Haftar family extends its reach along the southern corridors.

8. TANZANIA: Lissu verdict still set for 6 November

The High Court will rule on treason charges against Tundu Lissu on 6 November. [6][14] No new security measures were reported.

Assessment: The verdict is a likely flashpoint; expect security deployments in Dar es Salaam.

Collection gap: No verified window reporting on Mali, Burkina Faso, eastern DRC or ISWAP, and no Ethiopian reply to Sudan. Sudan Tribune, Anadolu and Addis Standard did not load.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. IRAN / US: US strikes Qeshm Island on the eve of the midterms

Late on 2 November US forces struck Iran's Qeshm Island. US officials told AP and CBS the strikes were "defensive" and limited and did not mark a return to major combat. [15][16] An official quoted by Axios said the IRGC fired a missile at a US aircraft and a US fighter destroyed a surface-to-air launcher; IRNA reported five explosions on Qeshm. The IRGC claims it brought down US drones, which is unverified, and CENTCOM rejected a claim that a US unmanned surface vessel was destroyed. [17] Accounts conflict on whether this is part of a series of quiet strikes. The same night Trump told Fox "there hasn't been shooting for months." [18]

Assessment: US and Iranian forces are already exchanging fire below the threshold of a declared campaign, so renewed strikes would be less of a break than official messaging suggests.

2. IRAN: Ghalibaf says Iran has replied and "the ball is in America's court"

Speaker and chief negotiator Ghalibaf told parliament Iran "has delivered a firm and rational response to the American side," and that "now the ball is in America's court to prevent war." [19] He said Iran is at "the highest level of defensive readiness" and any attack would meet an "unpredictable" response. [20] Qatari mediators flew to Tehran on 2 November to discuss a draft deal; a senior Iranian official told Reuters the sides are "a long way from a deal." [21] It is not stated whether the reply answers the Vance letter.

Assessment: Tehran is placing the next move on Washington so it can assign blame if strikes resume; its sequencing of blockade relief before nuclear talks is unchanged.

3. US / ISRAEL: Netanyahu presses for a post-midterm campaign; Trump says "No"

AP, citing regional officials, reports Netanyahu is pressing for a quick but extensive air campaign after the midterms and that Trump has not decided. [22] EA WorldView summarises reporting that the US warned Gulf allies of "brief but intense" strikes as early as 4 November. [23] Asked whether he would resume attacks after the midterms, Trump said "No." [18]

Assessment: Public US signals and private warnings point in opposite directions, so 4-5 November is the period of highest risk of a decision either way.

4. OMAN / GULF: Indian sailor killed; tanker hit off Ras al-Khafji

India says the dhow Al Jusufi was hit by an "unidentified aerial object" off Oman on 2 November, killing one of 14 Indian crew; Oman's coast guard rescued the rest. [24][25] UKMTO reported a tanker hit by two projectiles about 72 nm east of Ras al-Khafji in the northern Gulf, with crew safe. [26] Pre-window onset. Iranian state TV claims 11 tankers caught fire in nine days, and UKMTO counted a record 30 attacks in October. [16] No attacker has been named.

Assessment: Attacks are spreading beyond the strait into the Gulf of Oman and the northern Gulf, undercutting the US position that cleared routes are safe.

5. ENERGY: Brent falls 7% Monday, recovers after the Qeshm strike

Brent settled at $104.77 on 2 November, down more than 7% on deal hopes, then rose to $106.61 by 0744Z on Tuesday. [27] The National had Brent near $107 by about 0850Z. [28]

Assessment: Prices now move on each strike or talks headline and carry little premium for a US campaign on 4 November.

6. YEMEN / SAUDI ARABIA: Houthis claim 17 killed and drone attacks on Dammam airport

The Houthi health ministry says Saudi strikes killed 17 civilians over two days, and a Houthi spokesman claimed drone attacks on Dammam airport and a radar in Jizan. [29] Saudi Arabia has not confirmed the claims; the US Embassy described the airport as "operational with some delays." [30] The Yemeni government says a Houthi missile hit a health centre in Lahj, killing five. [31] None of the figures is independently verified.

Assessment: The Saudi-Houthi war now reaches Gulf-coast infrastructure, raising Red Sea shipping risk independently of the Iran track.

7. ISRAEL: Herzog sets consultation order; Liberman sets terms

Herzog will see parties from smallest to largest on 4-5 November, with Yisrael Beytenu on 4 November and UTJ and Likud on 5 November; the mandate deadline is 11 November. [32] Liberman will recommend Eisenkot but rules out any government dependent on Arab parties and will not sit with Netanyahu; UTJ may recommend no one. [33]

Assessment: Eisenkot is likely to get the first mandate, but Liberman's veto conflicts with the only realistic path to 61, so a stable coalition looks unlikely.

8. GAZA: Five killed; ministry toll since the ceasefire reaches 1,593

Israeli strikes killed four people on 2 November and a fifth died of wounds; the IDF says it hit fighters. The Hamas-run Health Ministry toll since the ceasefire is 1,593. [34]

Assessment: Steady attrition continues, and the risk of a shelter crisis before winter is rising.

9. IRAQ: Hikma says Baghdad warned of sanctions if drawn into a new war

A Hikma Movement spokesman said Iraq has received official warnings, mainly from the US, of sanctions if it is pulled into a renewed war. The claim is unverified. [35]

Assessment: Iraq's neutrality depends on militia restraint Baghdad cannot enforce, making it a likely early spillover point.

10. LEBANON: Israeli strikes and demolitions across the south

Lebanese reports describe Israeli air strikes on Kfar Tebnit and Qoussair, shelling near Khiam and house demolitions in Zawtar al-Sharqiyah on 2 November. No casualty toll was found. [36]

Assessment: Pressure is steady along the border strip with no confirmed escalation.

Collection gap: No CENTCOM or DoD on-record confirmation of the Qeshm strike. The content of Iran's response is unknown. No window reporting for Syria, Egypt, Pakistan-Afghanistan or Central Asia. WSJ, CNN and Semafor did not load.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE: Large overnight strike on power plants

Ukraine's Air Force says Russia launched 289 drones, 59 cruise missiles and ballistic missiles from 1800 on 2 November, and it downed 257 drones, 49 cruise missiles and 3 ballistic missiles, with hits at 23 locations. [37] Zelensky gave 17 ballistic missiles. [38] The Ladyzhyn, Kryvyi Rih and Dnipro thermal plants were hit. [39][40] In Kharkiv a medical facility was hit and about 200 patients evacuated; injury counts range from 25 to 73. Emergency power cuts hit Kyiv and Dnipropetrovsk, and Poland scrambled aircraft. [38][40] Zelensky had warned of the strike. [41]

Assessment: This points to a winter campaign against generation capacity while Ukraine is short of interceptors.

2. UKRAINE: Komyshuvakha aid hub strike is the deadliest in weeks

Guided bombs hit a school used for aid distribution in Komyshuvakha, Zaporizhzhia, on 2 November, with a second wave hitting a shelter where people had gathered. [42] Reported tolls range from 18 (ISW) to 29, including rescuers and police, with 20-27 missing. [43][44][38] Russia denies targeting civilians. [42] Pre-window onset.

Assessment: Kyiv is presenting the strike as Moscow's answer to the Oleshky truce, hardening its stance before talks.

3. RUSSIA: Ukraine sends 493 drones toward Moscow; Yaroslavl refinery hit

Moscow's mayor says 493 drones flew toward the capital overnight. [45] Russia's Defence Ministry claims 856 drones intercepted nationwide. Yaroslavl's governor confirmed the Slavneft-Yanos refinery was hit, and Crimea's occupation authorities introduced power cuts. [46] Ukraine and Russia each hacked the other's civil alert apps over the weekend. [43][44]

Assessment: Kyiv is matching Russia's energy campaign, and the contest now extends to cyber attacks on civil alert systems.

4. UKRAINE / RUSSIA / US: Abu Dhabi talks set for 10-11 November

US envoy Witkoff announced US-mediated working-level talks in Abu Dhabi on 10-11 November on security arrangements, de-escalation and humanitarian issues. [47] The Kremlin confirmed a delegation led by Medinsky. [48]

Assessment: Medinsky's role points to a hard Russian line, and the weekend's strikes leave little goodwill.

5. UKRAINE: Russia rejected extending the Oleshky truce

A Ukrainian official told AFP Moscow rejected an extension; Peskov called fortifying during the pause "normal practice" and said no similar missions are planned. [38] Russian drone attacks on Kherson resumed on 2 November. [49] ISW reports no advances by either side on 2 November. [44]

Assessment: The Kherson front is back to routine attacks with no sign of a repeat before Abu Dhabi.

6. POLAND: Counter-terrorism police deployed along the Kaliningrad border

Police launched a special operation in Podlasie and Warmia-Masuria after a military intelligence warning of possible sabotage, including crossings by armed groups. Police nationwide are on full readiness until 8 November. [50]

Assessment: This is a credible intelligence-driven alert during Russia's 4 November holiday period.

7. FRANCE: 5 November general strike finalised

Major unions back the strike over wages and the 2027 budget, all four rail unions have called walkouts, and the Paris march route was not approved as of 2 November. A listings site reports gathering bans in central Paris, which is uncorroborated. [51]

Assessment: Expect national transport disruption and a risk of clashes in Paris.

8. SERBIA: Police chiefs remanded for up to 30 days

A Belgrade court remanded criminal police chief Kričak and organised-crime chief Sekulović for up to 30 days; Kričak is also suspected of torture. [52] Prosecutors deny any link to the election. [53]

Assessment: The detention will be read as accountability under the new political balance.

Collection gap: Nothing found for Georgia, Moldova, Spain or Türkiye in the window, and no security measures for Russia's Unity Day.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. NORTH KOREA: Cruise missile test with a cluster warhead

KCNA says North Korea tested "strategic cruise missiles" on 2 November carrying a new "middle-sized dispersal warhead" covering 5-6 hectares, and a "vertical strike" terminal manoeuvre. [54] Euronews dates the earlier unannounced ballistic test to Sunday, while others say Saturday. [55] A South Korean report says JCS detected the 31 October launch but chose not to announce it. [56] UN sanctions do not ban cruise missile tests. [55]

Assessment: Two cluster-munition tests in three days suggest a deliberate campaign to show strike options against airfields and artillery while staying below the ballistic threshold.

2. NORTH KOREA: Kim Yo Jong rejects FBI attribution of $6bn crypto theft

Kim Yo Jong called the FBI's attribution of a more than $6bn Binance theft a "grave political provocation" and said Pyongyang has "issues to reconsider." [57] KCNA separately said Japan is becoming a "warfighting state." [58]

Assessment: Kim Yo Jong's personal response raises the dispute's profile and gives Pyongyang another reason to rebuff summit overtures.

3. TAIWAN: 49 PLA sorties, the year's single-day high

Taiwan's MND reported 49 PLA aircraft sorties, six navy ships and six official ships in 24 hours to 0600 on 3 November, with 31 aircraft entering the ADIZ. [59] Most activity was in the south, and the total exceeds the year's previous high of 42. [60] China Times speculates, without confirmation, that it is linked to the USS Theodore Roosevelt transiting the South China Sea. [61]

Assessment: The southern concentration fits a reaction to US carrier movements rather than a named exercise.

4. TAIWAN / CHINA: Taipei protests handling of APEC invitation

Foreign Minister Lin urged Beijing, as APEC host, to follow established practice after it sent Taiwan's invitation by email. [62]

Assessment: The dispute is the first Taiwan-related friction ahead of the Shenzhen summit Trump plans to attend.

5. BANGLADESH: Wage protests reach day four

Police fired tear gas in Ashulia and Mirpur, and reported closures range from 529 factories (AFP) to nearly 700 (Reuters). [63][64] More factories shut in Gazipur on Tuesday. [65] The home minister alleged owners linked to the former Awami League government are fuelling the unrest. [66]

Assessment: The government's sabotage framing risks hardening worker positions as disruption spreads.

6. SOUTH KOREA: President's passport data leaked online

The presidential office held an emergency meeting over the leak of passport details for President Lee and 167 others from a June trip, including the national security adviser. [67] The Foreign Ministry calls it a data leak, not a hack. [68]

Assessment: Whether a foreign service exploited the data during roughly five months of exposure is the key unknown.

7. MYANMAR: Further airstrikes in Rakhine; family killed in Chin State

The Arakan Army says a gyrocopter bombed a displacement camp near Kyaukpyu on 2 November, killing two. [69] In Chin State a mother, two children and their driver were shot dead by unknown attackers. [70]

Assessment: Junta air pressure on Arakan Army-held areas continues day to day; the claims are unverified.

8. SOLOMON ISLANDS: PM still in hospital; anti-defection reforms announced

RNZ reports PM Wale remains in hospital in Australia with no acting PM named, as the government announces constitutional reforms on no-confidence motions and party defections. [71][72]

Assessment: The reforms appear aimed at protecting the coalition while the PM is incapacitated.

9. SOUTH CHINA SEA: No new Manila statement; Chinese carriers reported

There was no new Philippine statement on the vessel in the Scarborough lagoon. [73] An Indonesian defence blog cites imagery showing the carrier Fujian in the northern South China Sea near the Theodore Roosevelt. [74]

Assessment: Chinese carrier activity around a US carrier transit may explain the PLA peak near Taiwan.

Collection gap: No window reporting on India-Pakistan, India-China, Indonesia, Australia, CMC appointments or the Japan defence plan. Yonhap, Taipei Times, USNI and Japan Times did not load; Taiwan figures come from media reporting MND data.

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES: Supreme Court rejects DOJ bid on California ballots

The Supreme Court rejected DOJ's emergency request on the night of 2 November to have California set aside more than 20,000 mail ballots. Judge Carter has set an expedited hearing for 13 November; the state certification deadline is 3 December. [75] AP says courts have "largely blocked" the administration's election moves. [76] The vote count is not confirmed. [77]

Assessment: California's ballots will be counted on schedule, but the dispute continues after Election Day.

2. UNITED STATES: Polls open with few problems and no federal agents

AP reported "relatively few problems" early on Election Day and none of the feared scenarios, such as immigration agents at polls; the administration says no armed federal officials will guard polls. [76] DOJ says it will publish the locations of about 1,000 monitors today. Pennsylvania says monitors have no more access than the public. [78] Indiana, Kentucky and an Arkansas county have told monitors to stay outside. [79]

Assessment: The main risk today is friction where monitors are refused entry.

3. UNITED STATES: CISA reported largely absent

The New York Times, as excerpted by Political Wire, reports CISA will be "largely absent" today after running a war room in past cycles. [80] Earlier CNN reporting described Russian AI-altered videos targeting candidates. [81] No public CISA or FBI statement or confirmed cyber incident was found.

Assessment: States and counties will be first to report any incident, which may slow attribution.

4. UNITED STATES: Alaska certification suit dismissed; DOJ appeals

Judge Sharon Gleason dismissed DOJ's suit to block certification of Alaska's Senate race if Sen. Sullivan loses, calling the relief "infeasible." DOJ filed a notice of appeal. [82]

Assessment: The appeal keeps open a federal route to contest a close result.

5. UNITED STATES: Weather and local ballot problems

AP reports flash flooding in parts of Colorado, Arizona and New Mexico. [76] Fayette County, Pennsylvania, says 308 requested mail ballots were never printed. [83]

Assessment: These are routine administration problems, but in a swing state they will likely feature in challenges.

6. MEXICO: US citizen child among six killed in Matamoros

Defensa sent 450 troops to Tamaulipas after the Gulf Cartel arrest. The governor says six died, including Aranza Adailini, a 7-year-old US citizen, whose family alleges fire from a helicopter; authorities are investigating state police crews. [84]

Assessment: The death of a US citizen child, possibly by state forces, could draw US attention during trade talks.

7. MEXICO: Gunmen fire on Culiacán fairgrounds

Attackers on a motorcycle fired on the livestock fair grounds in Culiacán on 2 November; no one was hurt. [85]

Assessment: The attack fits continuing Sinaloa cartel infighting.

8. MEXICO: Uruapan march marks a year since mayor's killing

Mayor Grecia Quiroz led about 2,500 people marking a year since Carlos Manzo was killed; more than 30 people have been arrested. [86]

Assessment: A possible run for governor makes Quiroz a focal point and potential target.

9. CANADA: Carney at the Commonwealth summit

Carney is in Antigua for the Commonwealth meeting, with trade and investment as priorities. [87]

Assessment: The trip continues Ottawa's trade diversification.

10. TROPICS: Low northeast of the Bahamas

NHC gives a low northeast of the Bahamas 30% chance of formation in seven days. [88] The Pacific low has risen to 70% and is moving toward Hawaii. [89]

Assessment: Neither system threatens NORTHCOM land areas.

Collection gap: No ruling found on the Thompson TRO stay request or Big Bend. No polling-place security incidents found through about 0900Z. Many major US outlets did not load.

SOUTHCOM.

1. COLOMBIA: Truck bomb and drones kill four in Planadas; ELN drones kill two soldiers

An explosive-laden truck, reportedly with drones, detonated near an army battalion in Planadas, Tolima, killing four including a two-month-old baby and injuring 17. [90] The army says ELN drones killed two soldiers in San José del Palmar, Chocó. [91]

2. COLOMBIA: Cúcuta security council and "Campaña Esparta"

President De la Espriella was due in Cúcuta on 3 November to review the militarisation and launch the "Esparta" campaign on the Venezuelan border. [92]

3. HAITI: Gang leader seeks UN dialogue, claims Salvadoran technicians dead

400 Mawozo leader Wilson Joseph told AP he wrote to the UN Secretary-General seeking dialogue and claimed, without evidence, that the four missing Salvadoran technicians are dead. Haiti says they were abducted. [93] Bukele has said troops would leave once the men are returned. [94]

Assessment: The overture likely aims to head off Salvadoran operations; the death claim could provoke escalation.

4. VENEZUELA: Rubio presses foreign minister on elections

Rubio met Foreign Minister Blanco and reaffirmed a three-phase plan and called for prisoner releases. [95] No election date is set. [96]

Assessment: 2027 is now Washington's working horizon for elections.

5. PERU: Red alert for Piura and Tumbes

Fujimori warned of an "extraordinary" El Niño, and the army is setting up a field hospital in Tumbes. [97] Senamhi's red alert covers more than 104,000 people, with heavier rain from 5 November. [98] A multi-region emergency decree was on the cabinet agenda. [99]

Assessment: An expanded emergency declaration is likely.

6. ECUADOR: 282 detained on second curfew night

The coastal curfew runs to 10 November; 282 people were detained on its second night. [100] Local elections are on 29 November. [101]

Assessment: The curfew is likely to be extended before the elections.

7. BRAZIL: Flávio Bolsonaro's first public appearance in Florida

Flávio Bolsonaro appeared at a church service in Florida with his brother Eduardo; no Brazilian extradition request has been reported. [102]

Assessment: An extradition or asylum dispute is the next likely flashpoint.

8. CUBA: Grid shortfall about 1,922 MW

The utility forecast a 1,922 MW peak shortfall for 2 November, with plants offline for lack of fuel and lubricant. [103]

Assessment: The deficit remains structurally severe.

Collection gap: No confirmed Salvadoran ground operations in Santo, no Brazilian government reaction on Flávio Bolsonaro, and nothing found for Central America.
