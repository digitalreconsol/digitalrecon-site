area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. LIBYA: Two naval officers die after Benghazi drone attack; LNA alleges launch from western Libya and vows retaliation

AP reports that two Libyan naval officers wounded in Sunday's Ukrainian sea-drone attack on a Russian corvette at Benghazi Naval Base died on the evening of 24 November. Two anonymous LNA-affiliated officials said an initial investigation found the drones were launched from a Ukrainian-operated base in western Libya, that at least four were intercepted and two hit the ship, and that at least 10 Libyan and Russian nationals were wounded; the LNA General Command vowed retaliation "at the appropriate time and place." [1] Tripoli's Government of National Unity called claims of any prior understanding with Ukraine "unfounded," citing a 12 September defence ministry ban on using Libyan territory for hostile acts. [2] Al Jazeera, citing local authorities, put the toll at at least 10 crew killed, which conflicts with AP's figure of at least 10 wounded. [3]

Assessment: The LNA's launch-site allegation, which the GNU denies, raises the risk that Ukraine's strike becomes an east-west Libyan flashpoint.

2. LIBYA / TURKEY: Haftar makes first visit to Turkey

Khalifa Haftar, with his sons Khaled and Belqasim, arrived in Ankara on 24 November at the Turkish government's invitation and was greeted by Chief of General Staff Selçuk Bayraktaroğlu; he was expected to meet Erdoğan. [3] Middle East Eye, citing unnamed Turkish sources, says Ankara may try to bring Haftar and Dbeibah together under a "One Libya" goal and that Erdoğan plans to visit Libya in December. No official readout was issued. [4]

Assessment: Turkey is likely positioning itself as broker between Libya's rival camps just as the Benghazi strike strains Haftar's ties with Moscow.

3. SUDAN: Drone swarm reportedly kills at least 14 civilians in Damazin; 28 women and girls reported abducted in Blue Nile

AFREEQUE, rewriting Darfur24 reporting from unnamed sources, says more than 40 suicide drones struck Damazin and Roseires on the evening of 24 November, killing 14 in Damazin's Al-Saliha area and hitting the 4th Infantry Division headquarters; in Roseires one soldier was killed and 10 civilians injured. A civil society group blamed the RSF and SPLM-N, which is unconfirmed. [5] Dabanga reports the SIHA Network documented 28 women and girls abducted while fleeing Damazin and 15 cases of sexual violence against minors near Qeisan, attributed to RSF fighters and allied militias; IOM counts about 443,494 displaced in Blue Nile. [6]

Assessment: A large drone strike on the state capital suggests the RSF-led Tasis alliance is likely moving from its border gains toward Damazin itself.

4. SUDAN / ETHIOPIA: Khartoum rejects Abiy's dialogue offer; RSF-led government and Somoud welcome it

The army-aligned government said on 25 November that Ethiopia "doesn't meet the neutrality requirements" to host Sudanese dialogue, repeated its claim that RSF drones launch from Ethiopian territory (which Addis denies) and asked the AU to exclude Ethiopia from facilitating talks. The Tasis Transitional Peace Government and Hamdok's Somoud coalition welcomed the offer. [7][8] Separately, China has sent envoys to Asmara, Addis Ababa, Djibouti and Cairo to ease Ethiopia-Eritrea tensions; no talks have been announced. [9]

Assessment: The split responses deepen the Sudan-Ethiopia rift and tie Sudan's war more closely to the Ethiopia-Eritrea standoff.

5. DRC: WHO Ebola responders trapped by fighting in Ituri; UNICEF finds violence against women rising

WHO said staff and responders had been sheltering in a hospital maternity ward in Ituri since fighting began on Saturday, and fighting and rain prevented their evacuation as of 24 November. A UNICEF survey of 1,062 women and girls found nearly 60% reported violence that began or increased since the outbreak started. [10][11] UN News and ABC cite more than 11,613 cases and 5,678 deaths, which is below the 23 November UN figure of 11,762 cases and 5,775 deaths as of 20 November; the gap is unexplained. [10][11]

Assessment: Armed conflict is now physically blocking the response at the epicentre, which raises the likelihood of further spread.

6. SOUTH AFRICA: Court sets aside Phala Phala panel report, ending the impeachment inquiry

A full bench of the Western Cape High Court ruled on 25 November that the 2022 Section 89 panel "fundamentally misconstrued its functions" and set its report aside, so the parliamentary impeachment inquiry falls away. [12][13] Ramaphosa told the NCOP he holds out no hope that the US will reverse South Africa's removal from AGOA, expected from 1 January. [14]

Assessment: The ruling removes Ramaphosa's main legal threat before the 27 November Johannesburg mayoral vote, though an appeal by opposition parties is possible.

7. NIGER / SAHEL: Boko Haram kills 11 soldiers in Diffa; Reuters documents insurgent drone spread

Niger's defence ministry, via AFP, said Boko Haram attacked a patrol near Baroua in Diffa region on Tuesday, killing 11 soldiers and wounding five; it claimed 22 attackers were "neutralised." [15] A Reuters investigation citing ACLED counted 187 JNIM drone attacks from 2023 to mid-November 2026 and found governments countered only 10 of 245 documented strikes; Ukraine denies Malian and Nigerien accusations that it backs armed groups. [16]

Assessment: Cheap commercial drones are likely eroding the Sahel juntas' and Russian forces' control of the air while Lake Chad basin losses continue.

8. SOMALIA: Cabinet approves closed-list electoral amendments during mediation

Prime Minister Hamza Abdi Barre's cabinet amended 23 articles of the National Elections Law and eight of the parties law on 24 November, introducing closed party lists and a 10% threshold for national party status, with the president still elected indirectly. Parliament must approve the changes before the Supreme Court's 5 December deadline; the opposition Salvation Forum calls the timetable "election theft." [17][18] Kaab TV reports, from unconfirmed local accounts, heavy AUSSOM Ugandan shelling around Golweyn in Lower Shabelle. [19]

Assessment: Acting alone ahead of the court deadline makes a government-opposition standoff more likely.

9. KENYA / NIGERIA / SOUTH SUDAN: Election timetables generate security friction

Kenya's IEBC says it cannot sanction early campaigning because the Attorney General missed a court deadline to legislate; a judgment on the ballot-paper tender is due 26 November. [20] In Nigeria, the opposition NDC boycotted the Inspector-General of Police's security meeting and accused APC "thugs" of attacks in five states, a partisan claim. [21] In South Sudan, the CTSAMVM chair warned its technical committee meeting "could regrettably be the last" because of funding uncertainty after elections moved to 11 February 2027, [22] and voter registration in Eastern Equatoria is lagging ahead of the 28 November deadline. [23]

Assessment: All three timetables are producing new legitimacy and security risks.

Collection gap: No in-window reporting was found on El Fasher, Kordofan, Mali, Burkina Faso, M23 or direct Ethiopia-Eritrea military activity; Sudan Tribune, Hiiraan and Addis Standard pages could not be retrieved.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. IRAN: IRGC claims two more tankers hit mines; UKMTO confirms three ships hit by "unknown projectiles"; CENTCOM reportedly says lanes are clear

The IRGC Navy said via state media that two more tankers "exploded" and caught fire early on 25 November off the strait's southern and eastern shores, and told crews to ignore "misleading statements by US officials." [24][25] It had earlier claimed a fourth tanker struck a mine on an "unauthorized route" on 24 November, with two others turning back after warnings from its "Strait of Hormuz Traffic Control Center." [26] Separately, UKMTO received a delayed report that three eastbound vessels were hit by "unknown projectiles" at about 2025 UTC on 22 November; all continued their voyages and no ships were named or attacks attributed. [27] National Security Journal reports CENTCOM rejects the mine claims and says all main lanes are clear, but paraphrases without a direct quote. [28] No IRGC mine claim has named a vessel or been independently confirmed.

Assessment: The IRGC's mine claims and the projectile attacks UKMTO has verified are separate sets of incidents, and the mine claims should be treated as unconfirmed until a ship is named.

2. UNITED STATES / IRAN: Officials reportedly debate strikes on Iran's leaders; Trump holds off and says the war is "funded" through January

Fox, summarising NBC News, reports the administration is debating strikes on senior Iranian leaders to push Tehran to end the war, with opponents warning of retaliation against US officials; a White House official said the naval blockade will continue. [29] Israel Hayom says a Treasury report on Iran's economy was one factor in Trump's decision not to resume the war for now, while strike infrastructure is kept ready. [30] Trump said the war is funded "up until really the end of January, pretty much." [31]

Assessment: Washington is likely to keep economic pressure as its main tool while holding escalation options, including leadership strikes, which makes US moves over the coming weeks hard to predict.

3. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: Houthi strike hurts 11 at Riyadh's PIF tower; coalition hits Hodeidah port; Houthis threaten Jeddah

A Houthi attack on 24 November hit the Public Investment Fund tower in Riyadh's financial district for the second time since 19 November, injuring 11, according to Saudi civil defence. [32] Qatar and Pakistan condemned the attack. Early on 25 November the coalition told civilians to leave Hodeidah port, then struck "operational capabilities" there and targets in Saada and Amran; Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said "the port of Jeddah, along with all other Saudi enemy ports along the Red Sea coast, will become targets." [33][34] Coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki said four drones were destroyed over Ras Tanura and three more elsewhere in the Eastern Province, with no casualties. [34] The US Mission to the UAE issued a regional security alert. [31]

Assessment: The exchange now targets core economic sites on both sides, and the Jeddah threat raises the risk to Red Sea shipping and to Hodeidah's role as northern Yemen's main food entry point.

4. YEMEN: Government says Houthi push on Aden is held about 60 km away

Armed forces spokesman Col. Majed al-Nuzaily said "Aden is secure" with Houthi forces about 60 km northwest of the city, and claimed troops in Al-Jawf are about 100 km from Sanaa. The Interior Ministry says 14 IRGC and 7 Hezbollah experts work with the Houthis. All figures come from government officials, a party to the war. [35]

Assessment: Neither side made a decisive move on the Aden front in the window, and the government's operational claims are likely inflated.

5. IRAN: Hardliners debate breaking the blockade by force; Brent near $90

Speaker Ghalibaf said there are "military ways to break the blockade," and IRGC-affiliated Sabereen said dozens of tankers are preparing to sail for China, which IRIB denied; unverified reports say the Supreme National Security Council halted the dispatch of 14 tankers. Inflation reached 88.7% in October. [36] IRGC chief Maj. Gen. Ahmad Vahidi said forces are ready to push enemies back "to a considerable depth." [37] Brent fell 1.4% to $90.12 at 0353 GMT on 25 November. [38]

Assessment: Economic pain is driving harder rhetoric, but the official denials suggest leaders are wary of an actual convoy confrontation for now.

6. ISRAEL: Yashar and Yisrael Beytenu sign coalition deal; Knesset fast-tracks October 7 inquiry and draft bills

Yashar and Yisrael Beytenu signed a coalition agreement on 24 November, and Liberman said Eisenkot can present a government "with a clear Jewish majority" by the end of the week, with talks with UTJ going "very nicely." [39] The Knesset Arrangements Committee voted 13-9 to fast-track seven bills, including a state inquiry into October 7, a Basic Law amendment on who may serve as prime minister and two measures suspending arrests of draft evaders. [40]

Assessment: Eisenkot looks close to a majority built around a Haredi draft deal, which points away from a minority government; the draft-arrest bills are the votes most likely to strain the bloc.

7. GAZA: Israel kills two senior Hamas internal-security officials in 24 hours

An Israeli strike on 24 November killed Naim Abu Nahl, director of Hamas internal security in Gaza. [41] Early on 25 November a strike on an apartment in Gaza City's Beach camp killed Mohammad Abuelouf, whom Reuters describes as head of the interior security service, with his wife and two children; Netanyahu said Hamas could not escape Israel's "long reach." [42] Death tolls since the October 2025 ceasefire differ: at least 1,680 per Al Jazeera and at least 1,600 per Reuters. [41][42]

Assessment: Killing Hamas security chiefs days after the Board of Peace announced a disarmament roadmap is likely to strain that process further.

8. IRAQ: PM orders federal takeover of Kurdistan oil exports and border crossings

PM al-Zaidi barred KRG crude exports without federal Oil Ministry approval, placed all crossings in the Kurdistan Region under the Border Ports Authority, gave the Energy Police control of oilfields in disputed areas, and dismissed the Kirkuk operations commander and police chief after a smuggling report. KRG PM Masrour Barzani called the orders "in no way acceptable," and the KDP and PUK jointly warned against unconstitutional steps. [43]

Assessment: Baghdad's anti-smuggling drive has moved from Iran-aligned militias to the Kurdistan Region, opening a new federal-Kurdish dispute over oil.

9. PAKISTAN: Eight TTP-linked militants killed in two Khyber Pakhtunkhwa operations

The Counter-Terrorism Department killed five militants in Badaber, Peshawar, while TTP commander Taj Muhammad escaped, and three Zargul group members were killed in Bannu. [44][45]

Assessment: The tempo of counter-TTP operations remains steady.

10. REGIONAL BRIEFS: Lebanon, Syria, Egypt-Jordan, aviation and Central Asia

Lebanese state media reported Israeli airstrikes on 24 November on villages outside the buffer zone, including Touline and Qabrikha. [46] Syrian state TV said Israeli troops detained a civilian in northern Quneitra and set up a checkpoint at Ain al-Bayda. [47] Egypt's and Jordan's foreign ministers urged full implementation of the Gaza plan and freedom of navigation in Hormuz and Bab al-Mandab. [48] Etihad's Abu Dhabi-Tel Aviv flight turned back after it "was required to change its flight path over Saudi Arabia." [49] World Bank data show Afghan goods shipped through Central Asia doubled to $210 million in April-September while fresh-fruit exports fell 88% after the Pakistan border closed. [50]

Assessment: Israeli operations in Lebanon and Syria continue at a steady pace outside the main Iran theatre.

Collection gap: No primary CENTCOM, UKMTO or JMIC confirmation of any 24-25 November mine claim, no outcome of Wednesday's Knesset votes and no Iranian response to the NBC report were found; the NBC original could not be opened.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE: Russia launches 112 drones overnight; at least three killed in Odesa and Kyiv oblasts

Ukraine's Air Force said Russia launched 112 drones overnight on 24-25 November, 66 of them Shahed types, and no missiles; 88 were shot down or suppressed, with hits at 18 locations. [51] The State Emergency Service said strikes in Odesa Oblast killed two people, including a teenager, and a drone hit a Kyiv apartment courtyard, injuring two. [52] In Bila Tserkva, a 52-year-old man was killed and a store and a Nova Poshta branch were hit. [53]

Assessment: A mid-sized drone-only raid keeps pressure on Ukraine's grid as temperatures fall toward freezing.

2. RUSSIA: Ukrainian drones reportedly set fires at two Rosneft refineries in Samara region

Reuters reported industrial sites in Syzran were hit and one person was killed in Samara city; Governor Vyacheslav Fedorishchev said 145 drones were downed over the region and told residents not to publish images. [54] bne IntelliNews, citing Astra footage analysis via the Kyiv Post, reported fires at the Syzran and Kuibyshev refineries; Russia's Defence Ministry, via Interfax, claimed 305 Ukrainian drones downed over 14 regions, which is unverified. [53] Ukraine's General Staff had not commented. [54]

Assessment: Moscow's suppression of imagery suggests the refinery damage is real, and the campaign is accelerating despite the US-declared energy ceasefire.

3. RUSSIA: Putin says Kyiv is ignoring Trump on refinery strikes and calls retaliation "inevitable"

Putin said on 24 November that "the Kyiv regime continues to attack our refining facilities," ignoring Trump's advice, and that "retaliatory measures on our part are inevitable." Reuters, citing two industry sources, reported Russian refinery throughput down about 45% since late October, with retail fuel rationing in several regions; Kyiv says it will stop energy strikes only if Moscow does. [55]

Assessment: A larger Russian strike package against Ukraine's energy system, framed as a response to Kyiv defying Washington, is likely within days.

4. UKRAINE / BALTIC: Zelensky confirms Sparta II strike; Norway urges restraint

Zelensky confirmed Ukraine struck the Russian-flagged cargo ship Sparta II, owned by a company tied to Russia's Defence Ministry, in the Gulf of Finland, saying the attack was in Russian waters but that "some drift happened." Norway's Defence Minister Tore O. Sandvik said Ukrainian action in the Baltic "must respect our need for stability in our near areas," and Zelensky said he had told officials to coordinate more closely with European partners. [56]

Assessment: Ukraine's maritime strikes are straining ties with Nordic and Baltic allies and give Moscow material for its "co-belligerent" claims against Estonia.

5. HUNGARY / UKRAINE: Magyar and Zelensky declare a "new chapter" in Uzhhorod

Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar met Zelensky in Uzhhorod on 24 November and delivered 1 billion forints of medical equipment and generators, stressing that Hungary supplies no weapons. Euronews, citing unnamed media reports, said Hungary recently withdrew its objections to the last two EU negotiating clusters for Ukraine and Moldova; this is not officially confirmed. [57]

Assessment: If confirmed, Budapest's shift removes the main veto on Ukraine's and Moldova's accession talks and could ease unanimity on the 22nd sanctions package.

6. RUSSIA / UKRAINE: ISW sees no territorial change; drone again enters Moldovan airspace

ISW assessed no territorial gains by either side on 24 November, reported that an unidentified, likely Russian drone entered Moldovan airspace on 23 November, and said Ukraine likely struck Russian aerospace reconnaissance infrastructure in Smolensk or Moscow Oblast. ISW identified the detained Murmansk governor as Andrei Chibis. [58]

Assessment: The ground front is static while the air war keeps spilling into neighbouring states.

7. SERBIA: New leadership skips the EU-Western Balkans summit

Acting president and parliamentary speaker Aleksandar Čupić cited parliamentary business for Serbia's absence from the 25 November summit in Brussels, and Belgrade is not expected to sign its declaration; Russia sanctions and Kosovo remain the sticking points. [59]

Assessment: The post-Vučić government is deferring any foreign-policy realignment, leaving its position on Russia sanctions unclear.

8. TURKEY: Council of State rejects İmamoğlu's diploma appeal

Türkiye's top administrative court upheld the cancellation of the jailed Istanbul mayor's degree, ending the ordinary appeal route; he can still go to the Constitutional Court. A degree is constitutionally required for presidential candidates. [60]

Assessment: The ruling further narrows İmamoğlu's legal path to a presidential run.

9. NATO / US: Rutte to meet Trump at Mar-a-Lago

NATO said Secretary General Mark Rutte would meet Trump and senior officials at Mar-a-Lago on 25 November; no agenda was announced. [61]

Assessment: The readout is the most likely near-term signal on US backing for eastern-flank reinforcement and on the timing of peace talks.

Collection gap: No date for US-Russia-Ukraine talks, no COREPER handling of the 22nd package and no new Polish, Estonian, Georgian or Moldovan government actions were found in the window.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. SOLOMON ISLANDS / AUSTRALIA: Treaty bars foreign bases; Honiara will not confirm the end of the China pact

Prime Ministers Matthew Wale and Anthony Albanese signed the Australia-Solomon Islands Treaty in Canberra on 25 November, naming Australia the "primary security partner" and committing to "no foreign military or security base." Outside partners are allowed only for "security needs unable to be met by PIF members," and Honiara must consult Canberra on national security and sensitive infrastructure. [62][63] Australia pledged police support, a "PALM Plus" labour pathway and a visa office in Honiara; no dollar figure was given, and Wale said implementing legislation will go to Parliament in January-February 2027. [64] Wale declined to confirm reports that Honiara will let the 2022 China policing pact lapse, citing "a non-disclosure clause in the treaty with China." [62] Opposition leader Manasseh Sogavare questioned how the treaty differs from the 2017 agreement. [64]

Assessment: The treaty is a major Australian gain on paper, but it still needs passage in a volatile parliament, and Beijing's reaction is the key unknown.

2. TAIWAN / KOREA / JAPAN: Tuesday's chip rally fully reverses; Kospi falls 6.16%

Asian markets gave back Tuesday's gains as AI-linked selling resumed after Wall Street's Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell almost 8%. [65][66] The Kospi closed down 6.16% at 4,697.51, its lowest close since 13 January, with foreigners net sellers of 1.95 trillion won and Samsung Electronics down 9.87% and SK hynix 11.05%. [67] A sell-side sidecar triggered for the fourth straight session. [68] The Taiex fell as much as 3,440 points intraday, its second-largest intraday point loss on record, and was down 4.82% at 11:47 local; AP and Al Jazeera put the intraday fall higher. [69][65][66] The Nikkei was down 3.3% at midday. [70] Korean dealers also cited expected USTR overcapacity findings covering 16 economies, including South Korea. [71] None of the Korean reports mentions the market stabilisation fund being deployed. [72]

Assessment: The relief rally has fully reversed, and Seoul's next test is whether authorities actually use the standby fund.

3. NORTH KOREA: Pyongyang expels Swiss mission head and orders the office closed within 48 hours

KCNA reported that a senior Foreign Ministry official declared the head of the Swiss office persona non grata, gave him 48 hours to leave and demanded the office close, citing Swiss sanctions on Wonsan's Songdowon children's camp over its alleged hosting of Ukrainian children taken from occupied territory. Kim Yo Jong called the sanctions an "open hostile act." The office had reopened about five weeks earlier. [73]

Assessment: Pyongyang is closing one of very few Western channels to defend its Russia ties, which points to little appetite for engagement with Europe.

4. TAIWAN: 19.58 million eligible voters for 28 November local elections; Taipei race reported tight

The Central Election Commission says 19,576,028 people are eligible for the local races and 20,003,835 for the nuclear-power referendum. [74] In Taipei, DPP legislator Puma Shen, whom Beijing labels a "diehard Taiwan independence separatist," challenges KMT incumbent Chiang Wan-an; the National Security Bureau is investigating suspected Chinese cyberattacks on his campaign. Polls conflict: a Taiwan Brain Trust poll had Shen ahead 46.3% to 42.2%, while an earlier SPRI poll had Chiang ahead 52.6% to 35.3%. [75]

Assessment: The Taipei result will be read as a test of whether Beijing's pressure on Shen helps or hurts him, and interference claims from both camps are likely to intensify through Saturday.

5. PHILIPPINES: AFP says PLA H-6 bomber flew within 45 nm of Luzon

The Armed Forces of the Philippines said a PLA Air Force H-6 bomber came within 45 nautical miles of the coast between Scarborough Shoal and Luzon; spokesman Rear Adm. Roy Vincent Trinidad said there was "no indicator" of a strike rehearsal against Manila. The article gives the distance to Metro Manila inconsistently. [76]

Assessment: Bomber flights this close to Luzon mark a step up in PLA signalling around Scarborough.

6. JAPAN / CHINA: Tokyo to revise maritime security policy with a Senkaku focus

SBS, citing Nikkei, reports Japan will revise its maritime security capability policy for the first time in four years to counter closer Chinese navy-coast guard coordination; China Coast Guard ships of 1,000 tons or more numbered 175 at end-2025 against 79 comparable Japanese patrol vessels. [77]

Assessment: Tokyo is investing in grey-zone maritime capability rather than expecting a diplomatic thaw.

7. SOUTH KOREA / UNITED STATES: Lee replaces foreign, unification and NIS chiefs; Washington reaffirms alliance

President Lee Jae Myung nominated Lim Woong-soon as foreign minister, Kang Kum-sil as NIS director and Hong Ihk-pyo as unification minister. A State Department spokesman said "the United States values the importance of close cooperation in the U.S.-ROK Alliance." The reshuffle follows friction after outgoing Unification Minister Chung Dong-young disclosed a uranium enrichment site at Kusong. [78]

Assessment: Seoul is moving to repair US ties, and its North Korea policy is unlikely to change in substance.

8. BANGLADESH: Garment minimum wage set at Tk 17,000; government says Hasina will be arrested on return

The Minimum Wage Board voted 4-2 to set the garment minimum wage at Tk 17,000 a month, up from Tk 12,500, with the workers' representative backing Tk 18,000. [79] State Minister Shama Obaed Islam said of Sheikh Hasina, "If she comes, she will be arrested and sent to jail," and the government blamed Awami League activists for violence in several districts. [80]

Assessment: The split wage vote raises the risk of garment-sector unrest ahead of Hasina's announced 16 December return.

9. VIETNAM / SOUTH CHINA SEA: China's Paracels "patriotic" cruises continue without Hanoi protest

RFA reports China's revived Paracels cruise has made eight voyages since 3 October, Hanoi has issued no specific protest, and Vietnamese and Chinese coast guards began a joint Gulf of Tonkin patrol on 22 November. [81]

Assessment: Hanoi appears to be prioritising stable ties with Beijing over public protest.

10. INDONESIA: VP nominees begin verification

ANTARA reports both VP nominees face a 14-working-day verification before the 11 December MPR vote, and Sugiono called his nomination "an honor." [82]

Assessment: No change to the expected outcome.

Collection gap: No PRC response to the treaty signing, no Taiwan defence ministry PLA counts for 24-25 November and no verified Taiex close were found; Taiwan News, Mizzima, PACNEWS and RNZ pages would not load.

area of responsibility

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES: Iran-war funding still deadlocked ahead of 11 December shutdown deadline

Bipartisan Senate talks are exploring emergency Pentagon money in exchange for a ban on new offensive operations in Iran, and Sen. Tammy Duckworth says she will oppose any bill that "does not cut off spending on this illegal war." Relaying CNN, Foreign Policy Journal says several Republicans see Schumer's stance partly as positioning for his 1 December leadership contest with Sen. Chris Van Hollen, and real negotiations are expected only after Thanksgiving. [83] Sen. Tim Kaine said a group of Democrats has finalised a funding-cutoff proposal; a University of Maryland/Ipsos poll found 80% of Democrats want the war ended now and 51% want a cutoff in the stopgap. [84] No on-record Schumer statement was found.

Assessment: Shutdown risk is rising, but no decision point comes before Congress returns on 30 November, and the 1 December leadership vote is likely to set how hard Democrats push.

2. MEXICO: Federal forces concentrated in Sinaloa as violence continues; Godoy says "Chapo Isidro" will be tried in Mexico

Security Secretary García Harfuch delivered 104 patrol vehicles in Culiacán and cited 4,200 arrests under the state strategy. [85] Defence Secretary Trevilla said about 22,000 troops, 23% of the national operational force, are in Sinaloa, while Expansión cites 12 homicides in a single day; the outlet's own figures are inconsistent. [86] Attorney General Ernestina Godoy said Meza Flores "will be tried here in the country" despite the US extradition request. [87]

Assessment: The concentration of forces has not yet reduced violence, and Godoy's statement suggests the US extradition request will not be granted quickly.

3. MEXICO: "Dios con Nosotros" caravan still held at Arriaga; it has not entered Oaxaca

About 760 migrants remained at Arriaga's sports complex on the Oaxaca border on 24 November; about 60 accepted transfer to a Chiapas work programme and about 700 want permits to work in northern cities. [88][89] El Universal reporting says officials are negotiating permits and migrants say they were promised transport to Monterrey, which is unconfirmed. [90] A second group of about 200 has been reported in Tapachula. [89]

Assessment: Mexico appears to be breaking up the caravan with jobs and permits rather than force.

4. US / MEXICO / CANADA: Section 301 tariffs expected imminently; Canada denies a deal is near

Bloomberg, via El Financiero, reports the next US tariff round will be set country by country and will not exceed rates in negotiated deals, while Mexico seeks Section 232 relief before the 14-15 December G20 at Mar-a-Lago. [91] Taipei Times says USTR's overcapacity findings covering 16 partners were still imminent on 25 November and that rates are expected to be capped at 15%. [92] USTR Jamieson Greer said a Canada deal is "fairly close" but criticised Ottawa's tariff cuts on Chinese EVs and steel; Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc said Canada is not on the verge of signing.
