case file 001 · sanctions-exposure assessment

Beijing Micropilot Flight Control Systems Co., Ltd.

A Beijing drone-engine reseller in the Shahed-136 engine lineage, designated by the United Kingdom in February 2024 and absent from US sanctions lists as of October 2026.

Original DRS analysis · public records only · prepared October 2026

bottom line

Beijing Micropilot meets the public-conduct profile of Chinese engine suppliers the US has already designated, and an allied government has designated it for that conduct. We found no US listing. This is an exposure finding, not a recommendation: whether to designate is a policy decision that turns on evidence we cannot see.

Key judgments

  1. The UK designated the company on 22 February 2024 under its Russia regime, describing it as a UAV company producing engines found in UAVs used by Russian forces in Ukraine. A director-disqualification sanction followed on 9 April 2025. [1][11][2]

    high

  2. From at least 2013 the company advertised MD550 and MD275 engines, reverse-engineered copies of the German Limbach L550 and L275 that power the Shahed-136. Archived web snapshots support this. [3]

    high

  3. An August 2025 open-source analysis, citing insider information, describes the company as a reseller rather than the manufacturer and names Xiamen Limbach as the producer. A Ukrainian General Staff weapons-research officer reported in 2023 that Russian-built Geran-2 drones had switched to MicroPilot-branded MD550 engines. [4]

    moderate

  4. The US designated Xiamen Limbach on 17 October 2024 and Mado-network figures earlier for the same engine family. Beijing Micropilot was not included in those actions. [5][6]

    high

  5. No OFAC, BIS Entity List, or State Department listing was found. On 4 October 2026 we screened the company name and its alias directly against OFAC's Sanctions List Search and the Consolidated Screening List, which carries the BIS and State Department lists, using exact and fuzzy matching. This agrees with the aggregator record and the December 2025 expert review. [9][10][2][3]

    high

DRS finds no US listing in OpenSanctions' US datasets, then confirms the absence by screening directly against OFAC's Sanctions List Search and the Consolidated Screening List. [2][9][10]

peer comparison

Firms the US has designated for the same engine family.

The point of comparison is public conduct, not guilt. Designation criteria and the evidence behind them are not public.

designated: US, EU

Xiamen Limbach Aircraft Engine

Reported manufacturer of the MD550. OFAC, 17 October 2024, EO 14024, for supplying engines to Russia's Garpiya line. [5][6]

designated: US

Mado network (Ma Jie, Qian Xi Long Trading)

Hong Kong and Iran-linked network that first marketed Limbach copies. US designations 2024 to 2025. [7]

designated: US, EU, CH

Mile Hao Xiang Technology

Yunnan maker of DLE engines found in Russian Gerbera drones. US listing 12 June 2024. [13][4]

Beijing Micropilot shares the engine lineage and the Russian end-use finding with all three, and the UK has acted on it. It differs in that public reporting casts it as a reseller, not a producer, and we found no documented shipment to Iran.

gaps and conflicts

What we could not confirm.

  • No direct Iran shipment found. The Iran link is through engine lineage and the Mado network, not a documented sale.
  • US absence is a point-in-time, name-based result. It holds as of 4 October 2026 for the names and aliases above. A listing under an unrecorded name variant, or exposure through an owner or affiliate, would not surface in a name search. Re-screen on the date of any compliance decision.
  • Ukraine listing date not shown. The register gives no date of entry. Archived captures show the entry online by February 2025; an aggregator first recorded it in May 2024. [8][2]
  • Reseller vs producer. The UK press notice says “producing”, and the list entry cites making UAV systems and components available. The reseller characterisation rests on one outlet’s insider information and is not independently corroborated. Both can be true at different times.
  • No company response located. We found no public statement from Beijing Micropilot. China’s embassy in London objected to the February 2024 UK measures as a group. [12]

Method: public sanctions lists and registers, direct screening in official US government search tools, archived web captures as cited by others, expert reporting. No paid databases, no imagery, no direct contact with any party.

This assessment describes public records and reporting about a designated entity. It states no conclusion about guilt or about what any government should do. Corrections welcome at contact.drs@digitalrecon.solutions.
