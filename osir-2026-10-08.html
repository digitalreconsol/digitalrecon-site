CENTCOM: Axios reports the Pentagon told CENTCOM to finish preparations for resuming major combat operations against Iran, with no decision yet. Assessments use ICD 203 estimative language and are DRS judgments drawn from the cited reporting.

area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. ETHIOPIA / ERITREA: Addis Ababa demands Eritrean troops withdraw after columns seen in Tigray

Witnesses told Reuters they saw dozens of Eritrean soldiers driving through Adigrat, about 25 km south of the border. Eritrea's information minister did not deny that troops had crossed [1]. On 8 Oct an Ethiopian government spokesperson said "all foreign forces operating inside our sovereign territory must withdraw immediately" [2].

Assessment: The presence of Eritrean troops in Tigray is likely though unconfirmed. A direct Ethiopia-Eritrea clash in the coming weeks is a roughly even chance.

2. DRC: Army and M23 fight around Ngungu as AU facilitators urge ceasefire

Congolese forces and allied Wazalendo militias fought AFC/M23 for a second day around Kasaki and the outskirts of Ngungu in southern Masisi [3]. The AU's panel of facilitators ended consultations in Goma on 7 Oct and called on both sides to respect the ceasefire and stay in the Doha process [4].

Assessment: Ceasefire violations in Masisi will very likely continue, and front lines are unlikely to shift significantly within 72 hours.

3. DRC / KENYA: MSF says Ebola spread in North Kivu is "out of control"

MSF warned of an "alarming surge" in North Kivu, where most new cases come from unknown transmission chains and about 75% of deaths occur in the community [5]. Kenya's first case passed temperature screening at Entebbe before flying to Nairobi, and authorities are tracing 28 contacts plus the flight's passengers and crew. The DRC outbreak has killed at least 4,148 people [6].

Assessment: Further spread along the Beni–Kampala–Nairobi corridor is likely. A second confirmed Kenyan case within 72 hours is a roughly even chance.

4. SUDAN: Drone strikes on Blue Nile state capital kill four women

Drones struck a neighborhood of Ed Damazin, killing four women, and hit a technical college and the home of the state finance minister, who called it a failed RSF assassination attempt [7].

Assessment: RSF and allied drone strikes on army-held state capitals in Blue Nile will likely continue, widening the southeastern front.

5. NIGERIA: Security forces free 20 abducted youth corps members in Imo State

A joint operation freed 20 prospective National Youth Service Corps members kidnapped a week earlier on the Owerri–Onitsha road. Police said the kidnappers' camp was reportedly mined with explosives, delaying the rescue [8].

Assessment: Kidnapping for ransom on southeastern highways will very likely persist. Mining of a criminal camp would be a notable tactical escalation if confirmed.

6. LIBYA: Haftar family reportedly ends Italian firm's highway contract

Citing Italy's Il Foglio, the Libya Observer reports that the Haftar family terminated Webuild's contract for the first phase of the coastal "Highway of Peace" in eastern Libya [9].

Assessment: The Haftar family is likely consolidating control over eastern reconstruction contracts alongside its influence over the House of Representatives.

Collection gap: no in-window reporting for Burkina Faso, Niger, Mali or Somalia.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. IRAN / US: Pentagon tells CENTCOM to complete preparations for resuming major combat operations

Axios reported that the Pentagon told US Central Command several days ago to complete preparations for resuming major combat operations against Iran. No date has been set and President Trump has not decided; one Israeli official called the chances before the US midterms "not high" [10]. Iranian state TV said an Iraqi Airways flight landed in Tehran, the first foreign airline flight since the war began, after a US sanctions waiver [11].

Assessment: Washington is likely preparing a real strike option rather than only signaling. Strikes before the midterms are unlikely unless Iran escalates first.

2. QATAR / GULF: Tanker hit by several projectiles off northern Qatar

UKMTO said a tanker was hit by several projectiles about 94 km north of Madinat ash Shamal, inside Qatar's exclusive economic zone, with casualties. No one has claimed the attack [12]. Iran International reported that Hormuz crude flows fell 27% week on week [13].

Assessment: Iranian forces were likely responsible. If confirmed, the attack would show Iran widening its target area west toward Qatari and Saudi export routes.

3. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: First deaths from Houthi strikes on Saudi airports

Saudi officials said Houthi strikes on 7 Oct killed three foreign residents, two at Abha airport and one at King Khalid International in Riyadh, and wounded 36 [14]. AFP witnesses reported several explosions in Riyadh on 8 Oct [15]. ISW assessed that the Houthis cut the last ground supply route into Taiz City [16], while Yemeni government forces claim gains in western Taiz [17].

Assessment: Houthi strikes on Saudi airports will very likely continue. The civilian deaths probably raise the chance of heavier Saudi strikes on Sanaa.

4. ISRAEL / UK: Israel closes UK consulate in East Jerusalem; London rebrands it a "mission"

As Israel's 8 Oct deadline expired, about 20 British diplomats were set to leave, while the building stays open as a mission with seven staff. Foreign Secretary Miliband said the UK does not accept Israel's right to close the consulate but will not retaliate while talks continue [18][19].

Assessment: The arrangement likely defuses the immediate crisis, though relations will likely stay strained through Israel's election.

5. ISRAEL: 7 October anniversary passes without a reported attack

Israel marked three years since 7 October with memorial ceremonies and anti-government protests, and no attack was reported on the day [20]. The IDF said it killed a Palestinian who crossed Gaza's ceasefire line [21].

Assessment: The threat window likely remains open while the US decision on Iran is pending.

6. LEBANON: UNIFIL post hit by deliberate gunfire; report of $200 million Iranian transfer to Hezbollah

UNIFIL said small-arms fire deliberately hit its position near Kfar Shouba on 7 Oct, without saying who fired [22]. Sources told Reuters Iran sent Hezbollah $200 million last month for displaced Lebanese; the State Department said the money "is not there" [23].

Assessment: Hezbollah likely retains some external financing despite the blockade, though the amount is unconfirmed.

7. PAKISTAN: Khyber Pakhtunkhwa governor says emergency rule would be imposed if needed

Governor Faisal Karim Kundi said an emergency or governor's rule would "definitely" be imposed in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa if deemed necessary to fight terrorism, and accused the PTI-led provincial government of facilitating it [24].

Assessment: Federal rule in the province is a roughly even chance in the coming weeks and would likely heighten civil-military tension.

Collection gap: no ship name or casualty count for the tanker off Qatar, and no Iranian state-media reaction to the Axios report. Syria, Egypt and Afghanistan were not covered.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE: Toll from 6–7 Oct strikes rises; new drone hit on Kyiv high-rise

The Kyiv Independent reported at least 26 killed in the 6–7 Oct attack, most in Pryluky, and its updated headline puts the toll at "at least 30 dead including 5 children" [25]. Late on 7 Oct a Russian drone hit a 12-story building in Kyiv's Darnytskyi district, injuring four [26].

Assessment: The final toll will likely rise as rubble clearance continues, and Russia will very likely keep up night drone pressure on Kyiv.

2. LITHUANIA / LATVIA: Lithuania sends troops to the Kaliningrad border after armed incursions from Belarus

Lithuania's interior minister asked for army units to support border guards on the Kaliningrad border as a preventive step [27]. The move followed incidents in which masked, armed men escorted migrants toward Latvia, threatened Latvian soldiers, then crossed in, damaged equipment and stole cameras [28].

Assessment: State-directed hybrid pressure on the Baltic-Belarus border is likely to continue, and further militarization of Baltic borders is likely.

3. BULGARIA: Security council seeks Black Sea moratorium and anti-drone buildup; rejects Article 4 for now

Bulgaria's national security council condemned the 6 Oct drone strikes, proposed a moratorium on hostilities at sea among Black Sea states and faster procurement of anti-drone systems, and judged a NATO Article 4 request counterproductive "at this stage" [29]. Turkish President Erdogan raised Black Sea navigation safety with Putin in a call on 7 Oct [30].

Assessment: Sofia is very unlikely to invoke Article 4 in the next 72 hours and will likely seek allied surveillance and anti-drone support instead.

4. RUSSIA: Ukrainian drones reportedly hit Salavat refinery and a major Yandex data center

Social media footage reportedly showed the Salavat refinery in Bashkortostan burning after an overnight attack about 1,300 km from the front [31]. A large fire was also reported at Yandex's Sasovo data center in Ryazan Oblast [32]. On 7 Oct a large blaze broke out at a pipeline and storage facility in Samara Oblast [33]. Neither side has confirmed the Salavat or Sasovo strikes.

Assessment: Ukraine is likely widening its deep-strike targets beyond energy to digital and industrial infrastructure.

5. DENMARK: Intelligence service says Russian sabotage against Danish defense firms has increased

A Danish security service analyst told Reuters that Russia has carried out "a lot more" disruption operations against Danish firms supplying Ukraine since early summer, recruiting individuals online [34].

Assessment: Russian proxy sabotage against European defense producers, especially drone makers, will very likely continue.

6. MOLDOVA: Shelters to be built at Ukrainian border crossings

Moldova's interior minister announced shelters at border crossings with Ukraine and the pullback of some border guards to Moldovan territory after repeated Russian munition incursions [35].

Assessment: Spillover from Russian strikes into Moldova and nearby NATO airspace will very likely recur.

7. EU / RUSSIA: EU ambassadors approve largest-ever Russia sanctions list

EU ambassadors unanimously approved nearly 1,600 listings targeting Russia's military-industrial complex. Foreign ministers are expected to formally adopt them on 12 Oct [36].

Assessment: Formal adoption on 12 Oct is highly likely.

Collection gap: the updated Ukraine toll is confirmed only by a headline. No in-window Caucasus or NATO airspace items found.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. SOUTH KOREA / NORTH KOREA: Lawmaker says North Korea likely buried "hundreds" of mines south of the line

An opposition lawmaker told NK News that South Korean intelligence indicates North Korea likely buried hundreds of mines near the 21 Sep blast site, and that minefields are known at six sites south of the Military Demarcation Line [37]. The UN Command will not disclose its next steps; the foreign minister listed options from a Security Council statement to no action [38].

Assessment: Seoul will likely raise the issue at the UN through a letter rather than a resolution. DMZ friction will probably stay high through the 10 Oct anniversary.

2. SOUTH KOREA / UKRAINE: Seoul recalls its ambassador to Kyiv

South Korea said on 8 Oct it will recall its ambassador to Ukraine over the disclosure of the transfer of two North Korean prisoners of war [39].

Assessment: The recall is likely to chill relations and complicate any further transfers of North Korean POWs.

3. TAIWAN: Tsai describes "sustained" Chinese pressure at Stanford; PLA activity stays light

Former President Tsai Ing-wen told a Stanford audience Taiwan faces daily cyberattacks, cognitive warfare and regular PLA operations around the island [40]. Taiwan's defense ministry tracked 3 PLA aircraft and 6 navy vessels in the 24 hours to 0600 on 8 Oct [41].

Assessment: Beijing will likely rebuke Tsai's US appearances once briefings resume. Gray-zone activity around Taiwan's 10 Oct National Day is a realistic possibility.

4. TAIWAN / US: Congress responds to alleged surveillance of President Lai's son

The FBI alleges a California woman arrested on 4 Oct surveilled President Lai's son and other Taiwan-linked people on behalf of a Chinese official. House China Select Committee Chairman John Moolenaar called it "another gross violation of our country's sovereignty" [42].

Assessment: The case will likely add to congressional pressure over China policy. Beijing will almost certainly deny involvement.

5. PHILIPPINES / SOUTH CHINA SEA: Chinese ship presence nearly doubled in September

The Philippine military counted 101 Chinese navy and coast guard ships around Thitu, Second Thomas, Sabina and Scarborough shoals in September, up from 56 in August. A spokesperson said the buildup aims to "break our people's resolve" [43].

Assessment: Chinese maritime pressure is likely to stay elevated through October, and a confrontation during the next Second Thomas Shoal resupply is likely.

6. JAPAN / CHINA: Tokyo reportedly weighs nuclear-powered submarines

Citing Kyodo and Yomiuri, Chinese state media reports that Japan's year-end security document revision is considering nuclear-powered submarines, possibly as a subject for "further research," and quotes Chinese experts warning of an "arms race" [44].

Assessment: Beijing will likely escalate criticism if the wording appears in draft documents. The report awaits confirmation from Japanese sources.

7. CHINA / RUSSIA: Patrushev visits China ahead of APEC

China's foreign ministry said Russian presidential aide Nikolai Patrushev will visit 8–13 Oct for talks with Wang Yi, framed as coordination before the November APEC summit in Shenzhen [45].

Assessment: The talks will likely cover Arctic and naval cooperation, given Patrushev's maritime portfolio.

Collection gap: no PRC foreign ministry briefing, KCNA or Rodong Sinmun items were retrieved. No India or Australia items found.

area of responsibility

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES: Hurricane Isaias forecast to strike the northern Gulf Coast late Friday or early Saturday

The National Hurricane Center's 2200 CDT 7 Oct advisory placed Isaias about 460 miles south-southwest of the mouth of the Mississippi with 70 mph winds, and expects landfall "along the U.S. northern Gulf Coast within the warning area late Friday or early Saturday." A hurricane warning runs from Ocean Springs, Mississippi, to the Bay/Gulf County line in Florida, with a storm surge warning from the mouth of the Mississippi to the Steinhatchee River [46]. The forecast discussion calls for a peak near 110 mph and surge of 5 to 7 feet from Ocean Springs to Indian Pass, including Mobile Bay [47]. By 0100 CDT 8 Oct Isaias had become a hurricane with 75 mph winds [48].

Assessment: Isaias will very likely make landfall as a Category 1 or 2 hurricane between coastal Mississippi and the western Florida Panhandle. Damaging winds and surge are almost certain around Mobile Bay, Pensacola and the Eglin-Hurlburt area.

2. UNITED STATES: Hurlburt Field orders evacuation of base housing

The commander of the 1st Special Operations Wing ordered all privatized military housing and the campground at Hurlburt Field evacuated by 1700 on 8 Oct, affecting about 1,000 residents, because expected surge will knock out water and sewage service [49].

Assessment: Other Panhandle installations, including Eglin, Tyndall and NAS Pensacola, will likely announce readiness changes, aircraft relocations or curtailed operations within 24 hours.

3. UNITED STATES: Emergency declarations across the Gulf Coast

Alabama's governor declared a state of emergency for Isaias [50]. Jackson County and the city of Gautier in Mississippi declared local emergencies [51]. Florida's declaration for 25 counties was issued on 6 Oct.

Assessment: Federal pre-landfall emergency declarations for Florida and Alabama are likely within 24 to 48 hours.

4. CUBA / US: Officials say more than 620,000 gallons of Cuba-bound biodiesel intercepted

Federal agencies announced 90 interdicted shipments of US biodiesel worth about $3 million bound for a sanctioned Cuban state fuel wholesaler, found at Port Everglades and the Port of Houston. Exporters were not named [52].

Assessment: US enforcement against fuel flows to Cuba will very likely continue, and charges against the exporters are likely in the coming weeks.

5. MEXICO: Report says Sheinbaum clashed with security chief over freezing accounts tied to Baja California figures

Infobae relays a Wall Street Journal report that President Sheinbaum told Security Secretary García Harfuch planned US sanctions were part of an effort to destabilize Mexico, before agreeing to freeze the accounts [53]. The sanctioned businesses include currency exchanges, gas stations and a hotel in Tijuana and Mexicali [54].

Assessment: The reported friction probably reflects real strain over US targeting of figures linked to the ruling party.

Collection gap: NHC Advisory 7 was not yet posted at production time. No confirmed FEMA, NORTHCOM or aircraft relocation reporting.

area of responsibility

SOUTHCOM.

1. BRAZIL: Novo party endorses Flávio Bolsonaro and vows to seek Supreme Court impeachments

The Novo party's president said it backs Flávio Bolsonaro in the 25 Oct runoff and that its senators will demand the impeachment of Supreme Court justices [55]. No new runoff poll was published in the window.

Assessment: The runoff likely remains a statistical tie. Rhetoric against the court and disinformation will very likely intensify before 25 Oct.

2. VENEZUELA: US commerce official visits Caracas

The US Embassy said Assistant Secretary of Commerce David Fogel was in Caracas on 7 Oct "advancing commercial exchange" under the administration's phased plan. No deals or sanctions changes were announced [56].

Assessment: Washington will likely keep expanding commercial engagement with the interim government without imminent sanctions relief.

3. VENEZUELA: Former judge says Maduro government held her relatives to keep Machado disqualified

A former senior Supreme Court judge said three army-officer relatives were detained in 2023 to pressure her to uphold María Corina Machado's disqualification, and two were sentenced on 1 Oct [57].

Assessment: The testimony will likely strengthen accountability efforts but is unlikely to change government conduct soon.

4. COLOMBIA: President meets US lawmakers on counternarcotics while Colombia remains decertified

President De La Espriella met US lawmakers on a joint counter-drug strategy measured by "verifiable results," as Colombia seeks to reverse its September decertification [58].

Assessment: Bogotá will likely pursue more aggressive eradication, raising friction with Indigenous and peasant movements in Cauca.

5. HAITI: UN experts name two former lawmakers over alleged gang ties

Le Nouvelliste reports that a UN Panel of Experts report alleges a former Kenscoff deputy armed youth groups and a former senator backed armed actors for political ends [59].

Assessment: The findings will likely support new UN or US sanctions designations and sharpen concerns about armed-group influence on the planned elections.

Collection gap: no SOUTHCOM strike confirmed in the window, and no verified Ecuador or Central America reporting.
