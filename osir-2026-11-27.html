area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. SOUTH AFRICA: Johannesburg council reconvenes to elect a mayor; result pending at access

Johannesburg's council reconvened on 27 November to elect a new mayor under an ANC-ActionSA-EFF arrangement, with ActionSA's Herman Mashaba expected to take the post. [1] TimesLIVE's live page showed no result as of 0819Z. [2] The Daily Sun puts the bloc at 138 seats (ANC 63, ActionSA 43, EFF 17, MK 15) against the 136 needed, with the DA on 91; an unverified IOL headline says MK is pulling out, which would leave the bloc short. [3]

Assessment: Mashaba is likely to win if MK stays in, but the bloc's two-seat margin makes it fragile and a failed or delayed vote is a realistic possibility.

2. SUDAN: Drone strike kills 11 in West Kordofan; rights group reports 14 killed in RSF attacks

A drone hit a truck at Ghubeish in West Kordofan on 26 November, killing 11 and wounding 9, according to a medical source; AFP also reports the RSF is trying to take Al-Damazin in Blue Nile. [4] A Sudanese rights group, cited by Anadolu, says at least 14 people were killed in RSF attacks in East Darfur and West Kordofan; this rests on a single source. [5]

Assessment: Fighting is intensifying on both the Kordofan and Blue Nile fronts, and civilian deaths from drone strikes are likely to keep rising.

3. MALI: JNIM threatens to blockade Tonka

AFP reports JNIM has threatened to cut off the town of Tonka in northern Mali; no deadline for the blockade has been confirmed. [6]

Assessment: JNIM is likely extending its blockade tactic to more northern towns, which would further strain supply routes the junta depends on.

4. ETHIOPIA / ERITREA: Abiy tells Eritrea to stop arming Ethiopia's enemies

Prime Minister Abiy told Eritrea to stop arming Ethiopia's enemies, said Addis Ababa seeks Red Sea access through dialogue, and referred to the air force's Su-35 fighters. [7] State agency ENA's account carries a harsher line, quoting a warning that "destruction will fall upon Eritrea." [8]

Assessment: The mix of dialogue and threat keeps the risk of an Ethiopia-Eritrea clash elevated, although no military movement was reported in the window.

5. NIGERIA: Gunmen kill 16 to 19 in Plateau State

Gunmen attacked the Tafi-Gana community in Plateau State, killing between 16 and 19 people according to a community association; police have not confirmed the toll. [9]

Assessment: Attacks on rural communities in Plateau are likely to continue and to feed into campaign arguments over security ahead of the January election.

6. SOUTH SUDAN: Kiir lifts state of emergency in four states

President Kiir lifted the state of emergency in Lakes, Warrap, Western Bahr el Ghazal and Unity; an analyst questioned what the emergency had achieved. [10]

Assessment: Lifting the emergency is unlikely by itself to reduce communal violence in these states.

7. LIBYA / TURKEY: Ankara courts both camps; Erdoğan visit expected in December

Al Jazeera's analysis says Turkey is pressing for Libyan unification after Erdoğan's meeting with Haftar, and that an Erdoğan visit to Libya is expected in December. [11]

Assessment: Turkey is likely positioning itself as the main broker between the two Libyan governments.

8. DRC: MONUSCO steps up civilian protection in Ituri

UN News reports MONUSCO has stepped up civilian protection amid fighting in Ituri, including at the Savo displacement site. [12]

Assessment: Insecurity in Ituri continues to hamper both civilian protection and the Ebola response.

Collection gap: No final Johannesburg vote result, no confirmation of MK's position, no Somalia or Sahel junta statements and no in-window Ebola case update were found.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. KUWAIT / IRAN / ITALY: Drone strike kills two Italian technicians in Kuwait; Iran denies involvement

Two Italian technicians from Rheinmetall Italia, maintaining Skyguard air-defence detection equipment, were killed on 26 November when drones struck a Kuwaiti surveillance site; a Kuwaiti army captain and a Pakistani national were also injured. [13] Italian Foreign Minister Tajani told the Senate investigators found the drone was Iranian-made, while noting Iran's denial and that proxy militias use such drones; Italy will lodge a complaint with the Iranian embassy. [14] Iran's armed forces spokesman denied any offensive operation, and Foreign Minister Araghchi called the incident "a precooked recipe for war" and offered Kuwait and Italy a joint inquiry. [15] IDF spokesman Effie Defrin told Kan that Israel could strike "with force at any moment, including on our own, without the Americans," while Trump said the US was giving Iran "every chance to make a deal." [16]

Assessment: These are the first Western deaths linked to Iran in this phase, and Israel's public talk of acting alone raises the risk of new strikes on Iran while US-Iran talks stay deadlocked.

2. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: Strike on Jazan refinery kills a worker and halts some units; coalition hits Hodeidah launcher

Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry said a "hostile aerial projectile" hit the 400,000 b/d Jazan refinery on 26 November, killing a Nepali worker, injuring others and temporarily suspending some operations; the ministry did not name the attacker. [17] Coalition spokesman Turki al-Malki blamed the Houthis and said "we destroyed a ballistic missile launcher in Hodeidah" used in the attack; air defences also intercepted two ballistic missiles toward Riyadh and two drones over the Eastern Province. [18] Shafaq, citing CNN and a shipping source, reports loading at the Ras Tanura terminal was halted after Wednesday's attack, which neither Aramco nor the Energy Ministry has confirmed. [19]

Assessment: This is the first confirmed death in the Houthi campaign against Saudi energy sites in this cycle, and two Saudi refineries have now been disrupted in 48 hours.

3. YEMEN: Coalition strikes across Houthi-held areas; ICRC-facilitated exchange of remains

Houthi media reported coalition strikes on 26 November in Hodeidah, Taiz and Hajjah that killed three people, including two children; [19] the Houthis separately claimed 19 killed over 48 hours, and the remains of 35 fighters were exchanged with ICRC facilitation, which a Houthi official called a "first practical step" toward the Muscat deal. [20] AP reports Sanaa residents described overnight explosions. [21] All casualty figures come from the Houthi side and differ from one another.

Assessment: The coalition appears to be striking well beyond Hodeidah in response to the refinery attacks, while a humanitarian channel still functions.

4. GULF / HORMUZ: Second tanker hit east of Khasab; Iran's blacklist now covers tugs

Reuters reported UKMTO's account of an outbound tanker hit by an unknown projectile on 25 November, 18 nautical miles east of Khasab, Oman, with all crew accounted for. [22] Riviera Maritime identifies the ship as the Hong Kong-flagged tanker Kun Peng, reports an engine-room fire and, citing a private security firm, one crew member severely injured; it also reports Iran's self-styled Persian Gulf Strait Authority added 26 tugs and offshore vessels to its "non-compliant" list. [23]

Assessment: Iran appears to be widening pressure from tankers to the ships that support them, raising risk for salvage and towing operators.

5. ENERGY MARKETS: Brent near $97, highest since 6 November

Brent settled at $96.57 on Thursday, its highest since 6 November, and OPEC+ is likely to keep production targets unchanged when it meets on 29 November, according to two sources. [24] Brent traded above $96 early Friday as markets weighed the Saudi refinery attacks. [25]

Assessment: The risk premium is rebuilding on Saudi outages and the Kuwait deaths, and an OPEC+ rollover would leave no supply signal to offset it.

6. IRAQ: Thousands rally in Erbil; Baghdad denies sending troops north

Reuters reports thousands rallied in Erbil and a parallel protest took place in Duhok against Baghdad's moves on Kurdistan's oil and borders, and that Kataib Hezbollah agreed to let the army enter Jurf al-Sakhar. [26] Al Jazeera reports the KRG rejected Baghdad's "military language and threats," while the government denied reports of army units moving north. [27]

Assessment: A rare united KDP-PUK front and Baghdad's softer public line on troops lower the near-term risk of armed confrontation, though Kirkuk remains the flashpoint.

7. ISRAEL: Eisenkot reaches preliminary coalition deal with Golan's Democrats

Reuters reports Yashar and the Democrats agreed a preliminary deal giving the Democrats four ministries, including public security; Golan called it "the mission of our lives." [28] The Jewish Chronicle reports the deal commits to "fully repeal the judicial overhaul," Yisrael Beytenu is expected to get defence, and Bennett postponed talks to Sunday over the Haredi draft; the mandate deadline is 6 December. [29]

Assessment: The deal is progress, but Bennett is the pivot, and a government is unlikely before next week.

8. GAZA / LEBANON / EGYPT: Ten killed in Gaza; Israel strikes Radwan Force camp in the Beqaa; Sisi hosts Qatar's emir

Hospital officials told AP that Israeli strikes and a shooting killed 10 people in Gaza on 26 November. [30] The IDF said it struck dozens of rockets and launchers at a Radwan Force training camp near Ayn al-Tina in the Beqaa, the third strike in eastern Lebanon in about two weeks. [31] Sisi told Qatar's Emir Tamim that "Gulf security is an integral part of Egypt's national security." [32]

Assessment: Israel's tempo in Gaza and the Beqaa is steady to rising while the Board of Peace process stalls.

9. PAKISTAN / AFGHANISTAN: Islamabad presses concerns over militant groups

Pakistan's special representative for Afghanistan told Dutch officials there are "a large number of terrorist groups in Afghanistan"; no new border clash was confirmed in the window. [33]

Assessment: The Pakistan-Afghanistan front remains diplomatically tense but quiet.

Collection gap: No Aramco or Energy Ministry statement on Ras Tanura, no in-window reporting on the Aden or Lahij fronts and nothing verified on Syria, Jordan, Central Asia or formal US-Iran mediation were found.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE / RUSSIA: Russia launches 155 drones; Zelensky warns a massive strike is being prepared

Ukraine's Air Force said Russia launched 155 drones overnight, of which 110 were downed, with hits recorded at 27 locations; no missiles were reported. [34] Six people were injured in combined strikes on Izium. [35] Zelensky said on 26 November that Ukraine has information Russia is preparing another massive strike, and that a prisoner exchange is being prepared. [36]

Assessment: The large missile strike Kyiv has warned of for several days has not yet come, and it remains likely within the next 72 hours.

2. RUSSIA: Ukraine hits Tula rocket-fuel plant; drones reach Leningrad oblast; Estonia raises an air alert

Kyiv Post reports Ukrainian drones struck the Yefremov synthetic rubber plant in Tula, which produces HTPB rocket fuel components; the Tula governor said four drones were downed with no damage, and Russia's defence ministry claimed 318 drones over 11 regions and Crimea. [37] Estonia issued an EE-ALARM potential air threat warning for six southern counties at 0640, lifted around 0722 to 0730, and 45 drones were reported downed over Leningrad oblast. [38] Ukraine's General Staff also claimed a Neptune strike on Marinovka airbase and hits on Transneft's Subkhankulovo hub and the Slavyansk-ECO refinery. [39] Ukrainian military intelligence claims North Korea has paused KN-23 missile and shell deliveries to Russia; this is unverified. [40]

Assessment: Ukraine's long-range campaign is reaching deeper into Russia, and drone activity near the Baltic is likely to bring more alerts in Estonia and Finland. The claimed North Korean pause is doubtful given same-day reporting of new Moscow-Pyongyang links.

3. EUROPEAN UNION: Leaders fail to agree budget or Ukraine loan; decisions pushed to December

The European Council ended without agreement on the long-term budget, with a negotiating box around EUR 1.78 trillion; the EUR 45 billion Ukraine loan was backed by 24 states, with Hungary, Slovakia and Czechia out and Bulgaria supporting non-military uses only. Both issues move to the 17-18 December summit. [41] Eurozone leaders backed Pablo Hernández de Cos to succeed Lagarde at the ECB. [42]

Assessment: Ukraine's 2027 financing remains uncertain until at least mid-December, and a deal is likely to need an arrangement that works around the three holdouts.

4. UNITED STATES / RUSSIA: Trump says he told Putin to pull back across Europe

Trump said he told Putin to "pull back all military aggression against territories throughout Europe" and called a deal a "strong possibility"; Kremlin spokesman Peskov blamed Ukraine for the lack of progress. [43] ISW assesses that security guarantees have been delayed, that Russia still treats Donbas as a precondition, and that Patriot deliveries are delayed. [44]

Assessment: A near-term settlement is unlikely while Moscow keeps its territorial precondition.

5. POLAND: No-fly zone of 30 to 40 km planned along the Ukrainian border

Poland plans a 30 to 40 km no-fly zone along its border with Ukraine after Russian airspace violations, expected to take effect in late December or January. [45]

Assessment: The zone would make it easier for Poland to engage drones that cross its border, but it is unlikely to stop incursions.

6. HUNGARY / UKRAINE: Hungary joins the coalition on abducted children

Zelensky said Hungary joined the coalition for the return of abducted Ukrainian children after a visit by Péter Magyar, bringing it to 54 countries. [46]

Assessment: This is a modest but notable shift in Budapest's tone toward Kyiv, though Hungary still opposes the EU loan.

7. TURKEY: Erdoğan completes ratification of the Mecca defence pact

Reuters reports Erdoğan completed Turkey's ratification of the Mecca defence pact with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan, which states that "an attack on one" is an attack on all; members meet in Islamabad on 27 and 28 November. [47]

Assessment: Turkey's entry gives the pact a NATO member with obligations that could, in principle, pull it into a Gulf conflict.

8. GEORGIA: Opposition rally set for 28 November

The opposition plans a rally in Tbilisi at 1900 on 28 November; a Georgian Dream MP dismissed it as an effort to show sponsors the opposition has not "died." [48]

Assessment: The rally is likely to be large but is unlikely to shift the government's position.

Collection gap: No independent confirmation of the Tula or Marinovka strikes, no Balkan reporting and no in-window detail on the prisoner exchange were found.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. TAIWAN / CHINA: 30 PLA aircraft on the eve of local elections

Taiwan's defence ministry reported 30 PLA aircraft, 26 of which crossed the median line, during a "joint combat readiness patrol," while China's defence ministry said "reunification" is unstoppable. Polls open Saturday from 0800 to 1600; in 2022 the KMT won 14 of the top posts, the DPP 5 and others 3. [49] President Lai and former President Tsai campaigned at a New Taipei rally, while the KMT's Cheng and the TPP's Huang appeared together in Tainan. [50]

Assessment: Beijing is likely to keep up military pressure through the vote and to cast a DPP setback as a rejection of Lai.

2. CHINA / UNITED STATES: Beijing "firmly opposes" Section 301 tariffs

China's commerce ministry said it "firmly opposes" the proposed Section 301 tariffs, reserves the right to respond, and urged that the US$30 billion deal take effect. [51] The foreign ministry said "tariff wars... serve no one." [52]

Assessment: China is likely to hold off on retaliation until the tariffs are finalised, while signalling it is ready to respond.

3. EAST ASIA MARKETS: Kospi up 4.34%, Nikkei up 2.11%

The Kospi rose on hopes of a larger market stabilisation fund [53] and closed up 4.34% at 4,915.68. [54] The Nikkei 225 closed up 2.11% at 58,711.11. [55]

Assessment: The rebound reflects policy support more than any easing of tariff risk, so volatility is likely to return when the Section 301 decision comes.

4. JAPAN / CHINA: Senkaku ships incident brings rival accusations

Japan and China traded accusations over a ships incident near the Senkakus. [56] China's foreign ministry asserted its claim, said it took "necessary control measures" and lodged serious representations. [52]

Assessment: Both sides are likely to keep the dispute on record without escalating beyond coast guard activity.

5. PHILIPPINES / CHINA: Beijing attacks the PH-Australia pact; Manila rejects the criticism

China's defence ministry spokesman Chen Xi criticised the Philippines-Australia defence pact and called Japan a "destroyer" of regional peace. [57] DND spokesman Andolong replied, "We do not need Beijing's permission." [58]

Assessment: Manila's widening defence ties are likely to bring further Chinese pressure in the South China Sea.

6. NORTH KOREA / RUSSIA: Kim meets Russian minister; Moscow-Wonsan flights discussed

KCNA reports Kim Jong Un met Russian minister Kozlov at the 13th intergovernmental committee and watched a Russian art troupe perform. [59] TASS-sourced reporting says the two sides are discussing flights between Moscow and Wonsan. [60] South Korea's defence ministry confirmed a North Korean defector was detained in Cheorwon while moving toward the border fence. [61]

Assessment: Expanding ties make the Ukrainian claim of a pause in North Korean arms deliveries unlikely to reflect a wider break.

7. BANGLADESH: Awami League shutdown and continued bombings; garment orders shift to India

The Awami League called a shutdown for 27 and 28 November, and 213 sabotage incidents, including 121 crude bombs, were reported from 1 to 23 November. [62] TOB.news gives the lockdown dates as 29 and 30 November, which conflicts with that, and reports bombs at a Dhaka court and Kushtia jail. [63] Reuters reports about US$254 million in garment orders have moved to India, according to factory owners. [64]

Assessment: Unrest is likely to continue, and lost export orders add economic pressure on the interim government.

8. INDIA: Congress sues Meta and the government

Reuters reports the Congress party sued Meta and the government and staged protests. [65]

Assessment: The dispute is likely to sharpen scrutiny of platform moderation in India.

9. PAKISTAN: Islamabad hosts the Makkah Defence Alliance meeting

Islamabad is hosting a two-day Makkah Defence Alliance meeting with Yemen on the agenda; some claims rest on an unnamed security source. [66]

Assessment: The meeting is likely to focus on Houthi attacks on Saudi energy sites.

10. LITHUANIA / TAIWAN: Vilnius threatens the Taiwanese office's status

Bloomberg reports Lithuania has threatened to revoke the status of the Taiwanese Representative Office to repair ties with China. [67]

Assessment: A change would be a symbolic gain for Beijing and a setback for Taipei in Europe.

11. SOLOMON ISLANDS: Sogavare demands the cost of the Canberra trip

Opposition figure Manasseh Sogavare demanded the full cost of Matthew Wale's 24-member trip to Canberra. [68]

Assessment: The row reflects domestic friction over closer ties with Australia.

Collection gap: No Taiwan defence ministry figures for the full window, no Korean government response to the Kozlov visit, and no independent confirmation of the Bangladesh shutdown dates were found.

area of responsibility

NORTHCOM.

1. MEXICO / UNITED STATES: Sheinbaum calls DEA cartel tip line "interference"

DEA Administrator Terry Cole announced tip lines for information on designated cartels, corrupt officials and money laundering, starting with the Sinaloa Cartel. Sheinbaum said on 26 November, "It's clear that this is a matter of interference," and that Meza Flores's case must be "evaluated by the Fiscalía and the Security Cabinet." [69] She also said "only the Cuban people" can decide Cuba's future. [70]

Assessment: Mexico City is publicly rejecting unilateral US enforcement steps while cooperating selectively on high-value targets, so bilateral security friction is likely to rise before the December G20.

2. MEXICO: Judge dismisses amparo against "Chapo Isidro" extradition; hearing set for 28 November

A federal court in Toluca dismissed an amparo against Meza Flores's possible transfer to the US because it was filed by a third party without his signature. The FGR says any handover depends on the treaty and "diplomatic timing," and his initial hearing is set for Saturday. [71]

Assessment: The ruling clears a procedural obstacle but does not settle whether he is prosecuted in Mexico first; new amparos with his signature are likely.

3. UNITED STATES: Progressive Democrats tie 11 December funding deadline to an Iran operations ban

Reps. Yassamin Ansari and Ro Khanna say they will oppose any funding package that does not explicitly bar offensive operations in Iran; Al Jazeera puts war spending "approaching $50bn" and cites a Reuters/Ipsos finding that 71% of Americans want the new Congress to end the war. [72]

Assessment: A short-term stopgap that pushes the Iran fight into the new Democratic-majority Senate is plausible.

4. UNITED STATES: Asylum ban and third-country deportations head to the Supreme Court

NPR reports the Supreme Court will soon take up the border asylum ban and in December will review deportations to third countries; CBP counts fewer than 99,000 southwest border apprehensions in FY2026, down 95%. [73]

Assessment: A ruling against the government would likely bring new executive measures rather than a return to earlier border flows.

5. CANADA / UNITED STATES: Poilievre plans Washington trip as talks pause for Thanksgiving

Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre will visit Washington next Thursday "to fight for tariff-free trade," coordinating with the Prime Minister's Office; Trade Minister LeBlanc says talks paused for US Thanksgiving and resume next week. [74]

Assessment: The opposition is signalling a unified "Team Canada" line rather than a parallel channel.

6. MEXICO: More bodies found in central Sinaloa

Two men were found shot dead beside Federal Highway 15 in Mocorito on 26 November, and the state prosecutor opened a double-homicide inquiry. [75]

Assessment: Factional killings continue in the Culiacán-Navolato-Mocorito corridor, and Saturday's Meza Flores hearing is a possible trigger for more.

Collection gap: No Mexican government statement on the Section 301 deferral, no caravan location update, no Bahamas reporting and no major Thanksgiving-day homeland security incident were found.

area of responsibility

SOUTHCOM.

1. COLOMBIA: Drone attack kills police auxiliary in Chocó; ELN-held hostage found dead

A drone attack on the police station in Condoto, Chocó, on 26 November killed police auxiliary Juan Alcides Murillo Palomino; authorities count 11 drone attacks in Chocó in three days, and President De la Espriella called it a "terrorist attack." [76] In Ábrego, Norte de Santander, Anyi Jácome, reportedly held by the ELN with three police officers since August, was found dead, and an assistant prosecutor was shot dead in Cauca; police captured 89 people in Valle del Cauca raids. [77]

Assessment: Armed groups are spreading drone attacks on police across the Pacific region, and more strikes on police posts in Chocó and Cauca are likely.

2. GUATEMALA: Arévalo says he will veto the 2027 budget

President Bernardo Arévalo said he will veto the Q172.4 billion budget, saying 80% of the cuts fall on public-service investment while development councils received Q12 billion; ministers were told to prepare to operate on the 2026 budget. He said, "We do not accept it and we will not allow it." [78]

Assessment: A carry-over of the 2026 budget is now the likely outcome, deepening the executive-Congress standoff before the 2027 elections.

3. ECUADOR: Detainee vote turnout 7% with no incidents; quick-count claim unconfirmed

Notimundo, relaying CNE figures, reports 523 unsentenced detainees voted in 33 prisons on 26 November, 7.05% of 7,417 eligible, with no incidents; ballots stay in military custody until the 29 November count. This eligible figure conflicts with the 6,795 reported earlier. [79] Resumen Latinoamericano, a partisan outlet, says the CNE suspended the quick count, which could not be confirmed. [80]

Assessment: Early voting went quietly, and how results are released is the main credibility risk on 29 November.

4. HAITI: Police task force operation in Martissant

Le Nouvelliste reports a police and task-force operation in Martissant overnight on 25-26 November destroyed a gang leader's house and killed "several armed individuals," a police claim with no figure given. [81]

Assessment: Operations are shifting toward holding ground, but no follow-up on the Kenscoff attack has emerged.

5. BRAZIL: Alckmin calls US aircraft tariff "out of place"; government weighs WTO action

Vice President Geraldo Alckmin called the proposed 10% US aircraft tariff "totally out of place," [82] and the presidency's communications office called it improper pressure aimed at Pix and digital trade; Brazil is weighing WTO action and reciprocity if the tariffs are confirmed. [83]

Assessment: Brasília is likely to build a legal case during the USTR comment period rather than retaliate before the rule is final.

6. CUBA: Grid deficit of 1,645 MW forecast

The UNE forecast 1,105 MW available against 2,750 MW demand at the 26 November peak, with seven thermal units out of service. [84]

Assessment: Blackouts above 1,600 MW are now routine, and new battery capacity will not close the gap soon.

7. VENEZUELA: National Assembly takes up $14 billion borrowing law

Transparencia Venezuela says a supplementary borrowing law raises the debt cap from $1.9 billion to $13.96 billion without naming projects or lenders; the opposition bench reserved its vote. [85]

Assessment: The law raises transparency and inflation concerns as the dialogue continues.

8. NICARAGUA: Laureano Ortega leads delegation to Russia, including Rosatom talks

A delegation led by sanctioned presidential son Laureano Ortega Murillo is in Russia working on a cooperation plan, including talks with Rosatom on a nuclear-medicine centre. [86]

Assessment: Managua keeps deepening ties with Moscow, sustaining US sanctions pressure.

9. PERU: Government signs gas expansion; foreign minister to be questioned on "Escudo de las Américas"

The government signed a $550 million addendum extending gas distribution to seven regions, and Foreign Minister Carlos Espá will appear before deputies on 1 December over the "Escudo de las Américas." [87]

Assessment: The 1 December hearing may shed light on regional security commitments involving the US.

Collection gap: No Kenscoff follow-up, no in-window Honduras security reporting and no Brazilian finance minister statement were found; the Venezuela prisoner figures rested on an aggregator and were not used.
