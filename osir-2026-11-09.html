area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. SOMALIA: Chief justice says he is not safe; judges shelter with former president

Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf Ahmed said he "no longer feels safe" in Mogadishu and accused President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud of threatening him; Somali Guardian says the six Supreme Court judges took refuge at former President Sharif Sheikh Ahmed's residence after their security details were withdrawn and troops took the court building. [1] Former PM Khaire says the court's operations are suspended; AFP could not verify this. [2] The defence minister had called the ruling an "Abu Dhabi agenda." [3]

Assessment: With the judiciary physically aligned with the opposition and the government calling it foreign-directed, the risk of armed confrontation before the 14-18 November talks is rising.

2. SOUTH AFRICA: IEC declares elections free and fair; ANC 30.76%, DA 24.64%

The IEC declared all 213 councils finalised, with the ANC on 30.76%, the DA 24.64%, MK 9.76%, the EFF 6.22% and ActionSA 5.67%; SAnews counts 127 hung councils, against about 70 in 2021. [4] The IEC and The Citizen put turnout at about 14.9 million (52%), lower than Friday's partial figure; the Mail & Guardian cites 15.6 million. [4] [5] [6] Only Cape Town has an outright majority; the DA leads four other metros, MK leads eThekwini and the ANC narrowly leads Mangaung. [7] Ramaphosa urged parties to negotiate in good faith. [5]

Assessment: Coalition bargaining over mayors in seven hung metros will dominate the coming weeks, with Johannesburg, Tshwane and eThekwini most volatile.

3. TANZANIA: Lissu sentenced to four years; Chadema to appeal

The High Court sentenced Tundu Lissu to four years for incitement; he has already spent 19 months in detention. [8] Chadema will appeal and seek bail. [9] The Citizen reports widening international criticism, including from the European Parliament. [10] No street protests were found in the window.

Assessment: The conviction keeps Lissu jailed without a treason flashpoint; the bail ruling is the next possible trigger.

4. DRC: FRN rebels take three South Kivu towns, threatening the RN3

Actualite.cd, citing local sources, reports FRN rebels took Bitale and Kalonge on 7 November and Bulambika in Kalehe on 8 November after army withdrawals. [11] [12] The losses threaten the RN3 road between Bukavu and Walikale.

Assessment: The FRN push undercuts Kinshasa's claims of momentum after Friday's Masisi gains.

5. SUDAN: Army claims West Kordofan gains

Military sources say the army and Joint Force took Ayyal Bakhit in West Kordofan and downed an RSF drone, the fourth in a week. [13] [14] The claims are unconfirmed.

Assessment: The army's Kordofan push is the main ground effort, but positions change hands often.

6. SUDAN: Army re-detains ICC fugitive Ahmed Haroun

Defence lawyers told AP that military authorities detained Ahmed Haroun in Kassala, possibly over a podcast about Islamist-military ties. [15]

Assessment: The arrest suggests Burhan is distancing himself from public Islamist claims of partnership; an ICC handover remains unlikely.

7. NIGERIA: INEC to consider Atiku ruling next week

INEC said it will consider the court judgment voiding the ADC leadership and Atiku's ticket at its meeting next week, after the ADC filed an appeal. [16]

Assessment: Whether INEC acts before the appeal court rules on a stay will decide whether Atiku's candidacy is in limbo.

Collection gap: Nothing verifiable in-window for the Sahel, Libya, Ethiopia-Eritrea, Darfur, the Sudan-Ethiopia dispute or Ebola. No on-record Somali presidential statement after 7 November.

CENTCOM.

1. ISRAEL: Eisenkot handed the mandate; Netanyahu calls for "unity"

Herzog announced his choice on Saturday, a day early, and formally gave Gadi Eisenkot the mandate on Sunday; he has 28 days, extendable by 14. [17] [18] [19] Eisenkot called for a "national emergency government" requiring "creative and unconventional political acts," invited Likud and Religious Zionism to join, and ruled out a repeat election. [17] [18] Netanyahu said "this is the time for unity" and "a government that relies on supporters of terror will not fight terror." [20] Ynet reports Liberman is now willing to compromise on the Haredi draft but will vote against a minority government reliant on Arab parties; he meets Eisenkot on 9 November. [21] [17]

Assessment: Eisenkot has no clear route to 61 seats without Arab or Haredi votes; the Liberman meeting and the Knesset swearing-in on 10 November are the first tests.

2. IRAN / US: Trump confirms "minor" strike on Qeshm, then calls war "highly unlikely"

Trump said on Friday evening the US hit "a little bit of an anti-aircraft device" on Qeshm Island; IRNA reported two projectiles and no casualties. [22] RFE/RL notes explosions across Hormozgan on 7 November, which Tasnim first called missile strikes and then linked to "managing shipping traffic." [23] On 8 November Trump said a return to war was unlikely if Iran signs "a very strong agreement." [24] Jerusalem Post sources say Israel and the US prepared for a possible strike over the weekend but no order came, and that Trump gave Vance "a few days" to reach a deal. [25]

Assessment: This is the first reported US strike on Iranian territory since Friday's edition, paired with a short deadline for a deal.

3. IRAN / US: CENTCOM launches "Task Force Iron Gate" and disables a blockade-runner

CENTCOM announced Task Force Iron Gate to target weapons and sanctioned goods moving across Iran's land borders and the Caspian, and US forces disabled the cargo ship M/V Huda in the Gulf of Oman. [26] [27] CENTCOM says it has redirected 147 vessels since July. [26] The IRGC called the task force "merely a wordplay." [26] The Jerusalem Post, citing other reports, says the Roosevelt strike group has left Singapore for the region; the US Navy has not confirmed this. [25]

Assessment: Iron Gate extends the pressure campaign from the sea to Iran's land and Caspian routes.

4. IRAN / US / QATAR: Araghchi tells the White House to choose; Rubio sees no breakthrough

Araghchi said it is "time for the White House" to choose and that mediators have stopped travelling since the midterms. [26] Rubio said the sides are not "on the verge of a breakthrough." [23] [26] A UAE official said "we see some progress." [27]

Assessment: There is still no formal US reply to Iran's proposal, and public positions remain far apart.

5. HORMUZ / GULF OF OMAN: More incidents; mine claims unverified

UKMTO reported a bulk carrier "involved with military forces" east of Khor Fakkan, possibly the Huda interdiction. [28] The IRGC claimed two more tankers hit mines; no maritime authority confirmed it. [23] The UAE rescued 22 crew from a burning cargo ship, and UKMTO reported a tanker fire near Khasab. [26]

Assessment: Shipping risk stays elevated even as the diplomatic language softens.

6. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: Houthi missile debris damages Aramco tower; Houthis say Saudi strike killed 33

Debris from intercepted missiles blew out windows on several floors of Aramco's Riyadh tower; the coalition said it intercepted four missiles over Riyadh and the south. [29] The Energy Ministry called the damage "minor," while AP witnesses described heavy damage. [30] The Houthis say Saudi-led strikes on a village east of Sanaa killed 33; Saudi Arabia has not commented. [31]

Assessment: Exchanges are escalating, with the first reported damage in Riyadh's financial district.

7. IRAQ: Drone strike halts Khor Mor gas field; Kurdistan loses about 2.7 GW

A drone struck LPG storage at Khor Mor late on 8 November and Dana Gas suspended production. [32] [33] The Kurdistan electricity ministry says generation fell from more than 3,700 MW to about 1,000 MW. [32] The Jerusalem Post links the attack to a Kataib Hezbollah official's remark; no group has claimed it. [34]

Assessment: This is a major infrastructure hit; suspicion falls on Iran-aligned militias, but nothing is confirmed.

8. ENERGY: Brent opens lower Monday

FinanceFeeds, citing Yahoo Finance, put Brent near $96.45 early Monday, down from Friday's $98.32 settlement; HDFC Sky had $98.2. [35] [36]

Assessment: The market is pricing lower war risk despite continuing Hormuz incidents.

9. GAZA: Two killed; ceasefire toll at 1,610

Anadolu reports two killed and 12 wounded on 8 November; the health ministry counts 1,610 killed since the ceasefire. [37]

Assessment: Low-level lethal violence continues at a steady pace.

10. SYRIA / LEBANON: Israeli raids in Quneitra; IDF reports caches near UN posts

11. PAKISTAN: TTP kills a policeman in Tank

3. RUSSIA: Ukrainian drones set fire to the Kirishi refinery

4. UKRAINE: Komyshuvakha toll rises to 55

5. UKRAINE: ISW reports Russian advances south of Dobropillia

6. FRANCE: Weekend turnout falls; new strike called for 10 November

7. SERBIA: New parliament to meet 17 November

INDOPACOM.

1. CHINA / US: Beijing extends rare-earth and tariff pauses to 10 January

MOFCOM extended by two months, to 10 January, its suspension of export controls on certain rare earths, industrial diamonds and lithium-battery technology, which was due to expire on 10 November. [56] The 24% additional tariff on US goods stays suspended for two months, with the 10% tariff kept, which Beijing said implements the Xi-Trump "consensus." [57] [58] The port-fee suspension on US-linked ships was also extended; Beijing said it would "closely follow" US plans to replace IEEPA tariffs with Section 301 tariffs. [58]

Assessment: The extension removes the 10 November cliff edge but keeps Beijing's leverage into APEC.

2. TAIWAN / REGIONAL MARKETS: Taiex rebounds about 5% after stabilisation fund authorised

Taiwan's National Stabilisation Fund authorised its tenth market-rescue mandate, with up to NT$500 billion, and regulators cut intraday short-selling caps from 30% to 3%. [59] The Taiex closed up about 1,960 points, near 41,660 to 41,680, depending on the source. [59] [60] The Kospi fell 0.8% after early gains and the Nikkei rose 2.3%. [61] Intraday figures were higher. [62]

Assessment: Taipei's intervention bought a relief rally, but volatility is likely to continue.

3. CHINA / US / RUSSIA: Trump expects to meet Xi and Putin at APEC

Trump said he expects to meet Xi and Putin on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen on 18-19 November, with "one of the meetings" including both, to discuss denuclearisation and ending the Iran war. [63] No response from Beijing or Moscow was reported.

Assessment: Treat the three-way meeting as Trump's expectation until Beijing and Moscow confirm it.

4. CHINA / US: Beijing calls Nvidia spy allegation a "smear"

AP, citing the New York Times, reports Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang told his board of a relationship with an executive US intelligence assessed was gathering information for Beijing; the board opened an independent investigation. 5. INDONESIA: Coalition parties call for treason inquiry; reshuffle expected

The Jakarta Post reports more coalition parties want investigators to decide whether the alleged wiretapping of Prabowo amounts to attempted treason. Jokowi said "let us wait and see," and an analyst expects a reshuffle targeting his loyalists. [65]

Assessment: A cabinet reshuffle in the coming days is plausible.

6. PHILIPPINES: Duterte impeachment trial resumes on one remaining article

The Senate impeachment court resumed on 9 November with only the unexplained-wealth article left; the defence opened with a Davao City assessor's official. [66]

Assessment: The outcome now rests on wealth and asset-declaration evidence.

7. JAPAN: M5.7 earthquake injures more than 20; Takaichi repeats offer to meet Kim

An M5.7 quake in western Shizuoka injured more than 20 people; JMA saw no change in the Nankai Trough but warned of possible strong quakes over two to three days. [67] PM Takaichi restated her willingness to meet Kim Jong Un on abductions. [68]

8. KOREAS: Seoul says Pyongyang-Beijing ties turning "strategic"

Senior WPK official Kim Song-nam met Wang Yi and other Chinese leaders in Beijing; Seoul's Unification Ministry said the relationship is turning "strategic." [69] No North Korean launch was reported in the window.

9. TAIWAN: PLA activity falls sharply over the weekend

Taiwan's MND reported 4 PLA aircraft, 6 navy vessels and 2 official ships in the 24 hours to 0600 on 8 November. [70]

Assessment: The lull fits a pattern of pulses rather than a sustained easing.

10. BANGLADESH: Factories reopen after wage deal; brief Savar protest

Owners and workers accepted the wage board's recommendations, effective December, though the amount was not reported; workers briefly tried to block roads in Savar on 8 November. [71] Industrial Police said no protests or clashes were reported. [72]

Assessment: Flare-ups remain possible until the wage figure is disclosed.

3. UNITED STATES (MISSISSIPPI): Colom's lead holds at 2,574

AFP reports Democrat Scott Colom leads Sen. 5. UNITED STATES: Protest after ICE agents block ambulance access at a Los Angeles hospital

SOUTHCOM.

1. COLOMBIA: Drone attack on army base kills three soldiers

Explosive drones hit a base in Santa Rosa del Sur, southern Bolívar, on 7 November, killing three soldiers and wounding 16. [87] [88] DW, citing AFP and EFE, says the Defence Ministry blamed the ELN; AP says no perpetrator had been named and that the Clan del Golfo, ELN and FARC dissidents all operate there. 2. HAITI: Boston gang leader killed; explosive-drone campaign widens

PM Fils-Aimé confirmed the death of Mathias Saintil, leader of the Viv Ansanm-affiliated Boston gang in Cité Soleil. [90] CMC puts the Salvadoran deployment at 2,500 troops, which is not confirmed elsewhere. [90]

Assessment: Decapitation strikes are speeding up, with growing civilian-casualty concerns and a near-term risk of Viv Ansanm reprisals.

3. CENTRAL AMERICA: Pacific low weakens; Costa Rica soils saturated

NHC cut the odds for the low off Nicaragua and El Salvador to 20%, from 40% on Friday, but says it may still bring heavy rain "over the next day or so"; the Atlantic outlook shows no formation in seven days. [91] Costa Rica's IMN says North and Central Pacific soils are more than 90% saturated. [92] El Salvador reported rain but no damage. [93]

Assessment: A tropical cyclone is now unlikely; the remaining risk is flooding and landslides, especially in Guanacaste.

4. ECUADOR: Curfew arrests reach 2,292; curfew ends Tuesday

The first week of the curfew in five coastal provinces produced 2,292 arrests and seizures of 216 firearms and 612 kg of drugs. [94] [95] EFE notes local elections are on 29 November; no extension was reported. [95]

Assessment: Whether the curfew is renewed before the elections is the key decision to watch.

5. CUBA: Blackouts near 1,700 MW; protest in El Cobre

The UNE forecast a 1,680 MW shortfall for 8 November after a 1,706 MW peak on 7 November, with six thermal units out. [96] Dozens marched in El Cobre chanting "Freedom for Yoel!" after the detention of an aid activist; authorities then sent food trucks. [97]

Assessment: The power deficit is stable but very large, and small protests in the east continue.

6. PERU: Tumbes floods leave 324 homeless

COEN reported 324 homeless and 808 affected in Tumbes, with red alerts for Cajamarca, Piura and Tumbes and 20 ports closed; no deaths were reported. [98]

Assessment: Saturated ground means repeated flooding on the far-north coast.

7. VENEZUELA: Machado tours quake zone; report on Cilia Flores' health

Machado toured a Caracas district hit by June's earthquakes. [99] CiberCuba, summarising a New York Times report, says Cilia Flores has lost weight in US custody and may have had a mild heart attack; a sovereign-immunity hearing is set for 17 November. [100]

Assessment: The 17 November hearing is the next legal milestone.

Collection gap: Nothing new on Lt. Pantoja, Operación Esparta, Apure casualties, Brazil, Guatemala or Honduras rain damage, or Cuban fuel deliveries.
