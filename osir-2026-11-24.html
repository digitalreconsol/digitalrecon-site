area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. LIBYA: UN calls for independent probe of Benghazi strikes; rival governments split over attribution

UNSMIL called for an "immediate, independent and transparent investigation," warning Libya "must not become a battleground for foreign conflicts." [1] Libyan military officials said at least four drones were intercepted and two hit, while Ukraine's SBU says seven naval drones were launched from a mothership near the target. [2] Eastern PM Osama Hammad asked the UN for an inquiry, including into suspicions the drones came from Ukrainian-run facilities in western Libya, and Tripoli's government said no side has the right to conduct military operations on Libyan soil. [3]

Assessment: The accusation against Tripoli risks pulling Libya's east-west rivalry into the Russia-Ukraine war and gives Moscow reason to deepen ties with Haftar.

2. SUDAN: Khartoum accuses RSF of executing officers in Blue Nile; drone kills eight in West Kordofan

The government accused the RSF of executing captured army personnel in Blue Nile, and the RSF-SPLM-N alliance claimed areas about 50 km from Ed Damazin, which the state government disputes. [4] A drone strike on a civilian vehicle near Armal in West Kordofan killed at least eight; local sources blame an army drone. [5] Anonymous sources told Darfur24 the army has sent more than 7,000 troops to the Saudi-led coalition, which is unconfirmed. [6]

Assessment: The Blue Nile front is now near enough to Ed Damazin to drive displacement, and the execution allegations may harden the army's refusal of a ceasefire.

3. DRC / SOUTH SUDAN: Ebola passes 11,700 cases; MSF suspends Yei work, closing an isolation unit

CDC data through 18 November give 11,712 confirmed Ebola cases and 5,720 deaths. [7] Fighting between the FRN and the army continues to displace villagers in Walikale. [8] In South Sudan, MSF suspended all activities in Yei River County after an abduction, taking the county's only Ebola isolation unit offline. [9] An attack on a cattle camp in Tonj East killed 23, which a county official blamed on SPLA-IO forces and an SPLA-IO faction denied. [10] A petition against moving the elections to February 2027 was filed at the Supreme Court. [11]

Assessment: Insecurity for aid workers is weakening Ebola readiness on the DRC border as the outbreak keeps growing about 3% a week.

4. NIGERIA: Four killed in attack on campaign convoy; Niger State reprisal toll put at 38 to 64

Gunmen attacked a Senate candidate's convoy in Ughelli North, Delta State, on 23 November, killing four according to the campaign, and a curfew was imposed. [12] A herders' association said 64 Fulani residents were killed in reprisal attacks in Borgu on 21-22 November, while a community leader said 38 were buried. [13]

Assessment: Pre-election violence and communal reprisal cycles are both worsening.

5. SOUTH AFRICA / SOMALIA: Johannesburg mayoral vote postponed; Mohamud expected in Kampala

Johannesburg's mayoral vote was postponed to 27 November at the DA's request, and the coalition that would make Mashaba mayor has not yet signed its agreement. [14] [15] Somalia's president was expected to travel to Kampala amid a standoff with the opposition over the electoral process. [16]

Assessment: Mashaba's election is delayed and less certain as the DA tries to break the coalition.

Collection gap: No in-window reporting on Ethiopia-Eritrea, the Sahel, Kenya, El Fasher or new Somalia-UAE developments was found; several regional sites blocked access.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. IRAN / STRAIT OF HORMUZ: IRGC claims three more tankers hit mines; UKMTO reports projectile hit off Khor Fakkan

Late on 23 November the IRGC said three "violating" tankers under US escort "suffered a severe explosion and fire after hitting naval mines," without naming the ships; CENTCOM had not responded. [17] The Business Standard said the claim "could not be independently verified." [18] Separately, UKMTO reported that an "unidentified projectile" struck a bulk carrier anchored 23 nm northeast of Khor Fakkan, UAE, causing a fire with no casualties. [19] UKMTO data cited by the Jerusalem Post show at least 22 attacks on shipping in November. [17]

Assessment: The three-tanker claim rests only on Iranian statements and may partly restate Monday's incident, while the Khor Fakkan hit shows the threat reaching UAE anchorages.

2. IRAN / US / QATAR: Tehran restates "seven conditions" as Qatar lobbies Vance and Rubio

Speaker and lead negotiator Ghalibaf told parliament there would be no negotiations with the US until Iran's seven conditions are met, and warned Iran would change "the geography of the battlefield" in response to any miscalculation. [20] The foreign ministry called the US blockade "an act of military aggression" and cited Article 51. [21] Qatar's prime minister met Vance and Rubio in Washington; AP reports Trump told Vance the administration "did not need a deal" with Iran, and mediators have proposed lifting the Hormuz disruption for limited sanctions relief. [22] Tehran summoned Qatar's chargé over a Qatari-German statement condemning Iranian attacks on Gulf states. [23]

Assessment: Public positions hardened on both sides, and Iran's protest to Doha shows strain on its main channel to Washington.

3. US / IRAN: Treasury targets shadow banking; Iran suspected in cyberattacks on ships

Treasury designated more than two dozen Iranian exchange houses, front companies and individuals, and Bessent called shadow banking "the last financial lifeline of a bankrupt regime." [24] Gulf News relays a Wall Street Journal report that US investigators are examining whether Iran was behind cyberattacks on up to 50 commercial vessels; Russia is also being considered. [23]

Assessment: Washington is pressing economically and in cyberspace rather than resuming major strikes, and the cyber attribution is preliminary.

4. YEMEN: Houthi media report 25 dead or missing in Saudi strike in Lahj

Houthi-run Saba said a Saudi strike on buses and trucks in Wadi Mabaq left 25 dead or missing; Saudi Arabia did not comment. [25] A 23 November strike on a telecommunications facility in Hodeidah killed 8 according to Sanaa health officials, though Houthi-affiliated tolls range from 4 to 17. [26] [27]

Assessment: Coalition air operations appear to be stepping up, and a Houthi retaliatory strike on Saudi territory is likely within days.

5. ISRAEL: No unity deal reported; Kan says Eisenkot preparing fast minority-government vote

At Likud's faction meeting, Netanyahu said the state's survival "depends on our unity," while Eisenkot said the gravest Iranian threat is "the 440 kilograms of enriched uranium" still not removed; Kan reports Eisenkot may announce a coalition suddenly and call a Knesset vote within a day or two, relying on Arab parties. [28] Likud sources called the unity talks "a show." [29] Votes on the 7 October inquiry, Haredi draft and premiership bills are set for Wednesday. [27]

Assessment: Pressure is building toward a minority-government vote before the 6 December mandate deadline.

6. GAZA: Board of Peace says Hamas agreed to a disarmament roadmap; UNRWA worker killed

The Board of Peace's report to the Security Council says Hamas and other factions agreed on 30 July to a 15-point disarmament roadmap but that implementation "remains limited"; Hamas has not responded. [30] Israeli strikes on 23 November killed at least three, including UNRWA worker Adel al-Helo in Jabalia. [31]

Assessment: The disarmament "agreement" is the Board's own characterisation, and lethal strikes continue at a steady rate.

7. ENERGY: Brent falls below $90, lowest since late August

Brent was down at $89.10 by 0700 GMT on 24 November, its lowest since 27 August, on expectations of a supply glut. [32]

Assessment: The market is discounting the Hormuz incidents; a confirmed hull loss or resumed US strikes would likely reverse that before the 29 November OPEC+ meeting.

8. PAKISTAN / AFGHANISTAN / SYRIA: BLA claims 20 killed; Kabul accuses Islamabad; Israeli incursions in Daraa

The BLA claimed 20 Pakistani personnel killed on 21-22 November, including 13 at a Frontier Corps camp in Nushki, which Pakistan has not confirmed. [33] Afghan FM Muttaqi accused Pakistan of a destabilisation campaign, and Islamabad rejected Taliban claims about Badakhshan. [34] Israeli forces held positions in Syria's Yarmouk basin on 22 November, part of 67 cross-border violations in a week counted by SOHR. [35] Israel also approved the return of flydubai flights to Tel Aviv. [36]

Assessment: Balochistan's tempo remains high and Afghanistan-Pakistan rhetoric is hardening.

Collection gap: No US, Bahraini or GCC response to the Bahrain strike, no UKMTO or CENTCOM confirmation of the three-tanker claim, no outcome of Monday's Likud-Yashar round, and nothing new on Rabigh, Jurf al-Sakhar, Lebanon, Egypt or Jordan were found.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. POLAND / NATO: North Atlantic Council sends AWACS; new Russian drone enters Polish airspace

At an extraordinary North Atlantic Council meeting, allies agreed to deploy an AWACS aircraft to Poland and said Russia "bears full responsibility" for the three Kh-101 missiles; other Polish requests were deferred to next week's foreign ministers' meeting, and no Article 4 request was reported. [37] A Russian drone entered Polish airspace overnight 23-24 November and was cleared for shoot-down but not engaged after turning back east. [37] [38]

Assessment: NATO chose a limited defensive response while a second incursion within days shows Russian testing continues.

2. EU / RUSSIA: Commission proposes 22nd sanctions package

The proposal includes a transaction ban and SWIFT cut-off for about 60 Russian banks, more than 70 shadow-fleet vessels, a proposed ban on Russia-insured tankers, a visa ban for Russians who fought in Ukraine and a ban on Russian teams competing under their flag. [39] [40] All 27 member states must approve it. [39]

Assessment: The shadow-fleet and tanker-insurance measures are the most likely to face pushback from shipping states.

3. UKRAINE / RUSSIA: Russia claims 699 Ukrainian drones downed, 355 aimed at Moscow; Perm refinery hit

Ukraine's Air Force said Russia launched 96 drones overnight, with hits at 13 locations. [41] Russia's Defence Ministry claimed 699 Ukrainian drones downed overnight, and Moscow's mayor said 355 flew toward the capital, restricting flights at two airports. [42] Ukraine's General Staff confirmed strikes on the Lukoil refinery in Perm, more than 1,400 km from the border, and the Kaleykino pumping station feeding the Druzhba pipeline. [43]

Assessment: If accurate, the Moscow salvo is among the largest of the war, and the strikes now reach export pipeline infrastructure.

4. RUSSIA / LIBYA: Kremlin defers to military on Merkury sinking; state media silent

Peskov said Russia had "taken note of" Zelensky's claim and that "the Defense Ministry is handling these matters," while main state channels did not report the sinking. [44] Peskov also dismissed German reports of a possible nuclear-armed satellite launch as "hardly worth taking seriously." [45] ISW assesses Russian officials reject Article 5-style guarantees for Ukraine and no new trilateral round was confirmed. [46]

Assessment: Moscow appears to be managing a domestic embarrassment rather than preparing an immediate public retaliation.

5. ESTONIA / RUSSIA: Moscow warns of "the brink of open war" over border closure

Russia summoned Estonia's chargé over Tallinn's plan to close its road crossings from 1 December, calling it a "new Iron Curtain" and accusing Estonia of letting Ukrainian drones use its airspace, which Estonia rejected. [47] Bild reports Germany sent tanks to Lithuania by rail rather than through Poland "to avoid provoking Putin," which Berlin called routine. [48]

Assessment: The airspace accusation creates a possible pretext for Russian pressure on Estonia.

6. CYPRUS / GERMANY / RUSSIA: Suspected GRU diplomats expelled; school bomb threats; Murmansk governor detained

Cyprus expelled two Russian diplomats, one described as a GRU officer, after an intelligence operation. [49] About 450 German schools and several Danish schools received emailed bomb threats believed to be hoaxes, and European services are examining possible Russian involvement in the wave. [50] Murmansk Governor Andrey Chibis was detained on embezzlement charges. [51]

Assessment: These fit the pattern of Russian hybrid activity across the EU, though the bomb threats are not attributed.

Collection gap: No in-window reporting on Moldova, Serbia or Hungary, no Russian MoD statement on the Merkury and no Russian comment on the Kh-101 attribution were found.

INDOPACOM.

1. TAIWAN / KOREA / JAPAN: Asian chip stocks surge after Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang; Taiex posts record point gain

The Taiex closed up 8.99% at 40,122.35, its largest single-day point gain on record, with TSMC up 10%, though foreign investors were net sellers and turnover was the year's lowest. [52] The Kospi rose 6.32% to 5,006.03 and the won firmed to 1,342.4 per dollar. [53] The Nikkei, reopening after a holiday, rose 3.91%, with the yen near 159.2. [54] RTTNews attributed the rally to Wall Street's Monday surge after Nvidia reinstated Huang and to oil falling on reports the US had postponed planned strikes on Iran. [55] Mainland and Hong Kong shares lagged. [56] AP reports Nvidia's board is still investigating whether Huang was targeted by a foreign spy. [57]

Assessment: The rally rests on one external trigger, and thin Taiwan turnover with continued foreign selling suggests it is fragile.

2. SOUTH KOREA: Stabilisation fund on standby after rebound

The government had not activated the market stabilisation fund by the evening of 24 November, and size and timing remain undecided; the Kospi is down 12.65% in November. [58] Seoul Economic Daily says the fund is designed at about 10.76 trillion won and would likely buy index products, with deployment possible "as early as this month." [59]

Assessment: Tuesday's rebound lowers pressure to deploy the fund, but a renewed sell-off would probably trigger it.

3. INDONESIA: Prabowo's VP nominees named as Sugiono and Andi Amran Sulaiman

MPR Speaker Muzani read out Prabowo's letter naming Coordinating Minister Sugiono and Agriculture Minister Andi Amran Sulaiman; verification began the same day, with the plenary vote set for 11 December. [60] The Jakarta Post notes Amran is a cousin of coal tycoon Andi Syamsuddin Arsyad, reportedly a major backer of Prabowo's 2024 campaign. [61]

Assessment: Both nominees are close Prabowo loyalists, so the president consolidates control over the line of succession either way.

4. SOLOMON ISLANDS / AUSTRALIA / CHINA: Honiara will not renew its 2022 security pact with China

Prime Minister Matthew Wale's government has told Beijing it will not renew the pact, which expires in April 2027, and Wale is due to sign a treaty in Canberra on 25 November; a Lowy Institute analyst called it a "massive win" for Australia, and no Chinese response was reported. [62] Foreign Minister Penny Wong was due to tell the Senate that China is "intensifying" efforts to "remake the multilateral system." [63]

Assessment: This is the most consequential Pacific alignment shift in the window, and a Chinese response and pressure on Wale's fragile majority are likely.

5. TAIWAN: Taipei calls Xinhua's account of Trump's remark "not factual"; DPP alleges election interference

The Mainland Affairs Council said preliminary checks found Xinhua's account of Trump saying the US "does not support Taiwan independence" "not factual," and urged Washington to clarify; the White House has neither confirmed nor denied it. [64] DPP lawmakers accused Beijing of subsidised flights, illegal funding, disinformation and hacking ahead of the 28 November local elections. [65] President Lai visited the Xietong No. 115 exercise in Kaohsiung. [66] Xi and Takaichi had only a brief handshake at APEC, and Japan says its Taiwan position is unchanged. [67]

Assessment: Washington's silence lets Beijing's version stand, and interference claims and grey-zone activity are likely to intensify before 28 November.

6. NORTH KOREA: Kim Yo Jong threatens Switzerland's new Pyongyang office over sanctions

Kim Yo Jong called Swiss sanctions on a Wonsan children's camp, which Bern links to "forced assimilation" of Ukrainian minors, an "open hostile act" and said ties would be "comprehensively" reconsidered. [68] Seoul will repatriate a North Korean fishing boat via the UN Command. [69]

Assessment: The threat shows Pyongyang's sensitivity over its role in the Russia-Ukraine war, cutting against its stated wish to expand foreign ties.

7. PHILIPPINES: Chinese ships in West Philippine Sea more than double; Manila rejects blanket US sourcing

The Philippine military said 48 Chinese vessels were monitored on 17-23 November, up from 19 the previous week. [70] The Palace said Marcos made "no such terms" committing to source all energy and farm imports from the US. [71] [72]

Assessment: Beijing's summit-period restraint was tactical, and Manila is resisting US trade pressure while relying on the alliance.

8. BANGLADESH / MYANMAR: Dhaka rejects Hasina's 16 December return; UK sanctions Min Aung Hlaing

A Bangladeshi minister told diplomats Hasina is "not an ordinary passenger" and would be arrested if she returns on 16 December as requested. [73] State-sourced reporting says at least four people have been killed in Awami League-linked violence. [74] The UK sanctioned Min Aung Hlaing and expelled Myanmar's defence attaché after the ICJ genocide ruling. [75] An opposition outlet reports a 22 November airstrike on a church in Karen State killed one woman. [76]

Assessment: Hasina's planned return date is a set-piece flashpoint, and Western pressure on Naypyidaw is hardening.

Collection gap: No Taiwan MND count for 24 November, no US statement on Taiwan and no Chinese response to the Solomon Islands decision were found; several regional sites could not be fetched.

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES: Senate GOP leaders signal openness to Iran-war funding limits

Thune said he was open "to anything that keeps the government funded," and Collins said she was "looking at language" to cut off Iran war funding. [77] The Daily Caller reports Senate Democrats have settled on withholding support for funding unless Trump gets congressional war authorisation, with an exception for defensive operations; Schumer and Jeffries have not stated a position. [77]

Assessment: Republican movement reduces but does not remove the risk of a shutdown on 11 December, and the Democratic leadership's position remains undefined.

2. MEXICO: "El Chapo Isidro" held at Altiplano; US extradition request confirmed

Meza Flores appeared by video before a judge on firearms and drug charges, and the decision on binding him over for trial was deferred to 28 November. [78] Excélsior, citing prosecutors' sources, says he faces five Mexican case files and that a US court has issued an arrest request for extradition. [79] Security Secretary García Harfuch said 10 soldiers were wounded, and the Defence Secretary denied foreign personnel took part. [80] Proceso cites the Defence Ministry as reporting 16 attackers killed, against the 7 reported Monday. [78]

Assessment: Mexico is prioritising domestic prosecution, making a near-term extradition unlikely.

3. MEXICO: Federal reinforcements to Sinaloa as shootings continue

The federal government is sending more than 150 vehicles, including armoured trucks, and 240 highway monitoring points to Sinaloa, alongside a 3,200-strong Navy reinforcement. [81] Gunmen in Culiacán and Mazatlán killed 3 people and wounded 4. [82] Quadratín counted 16 dead over the weekend in Michoacán from violence and accidents, including a landmine blast near Apatzingán. [83]

Assessment: The federal government is digging in for a long post-capture effort, but violence persists in Sinaloa and Tierra Caliente.

4. UNITED STATES: FBI arrests man accused of recruiting armed group to storm Adelanto ICE center

The FBI arrested a Hacienda Heights man accused of recruiting an armed group on social media to storm the Adelanto ICE center and free detainees; a search reportedly found a firearm, ammunition and body armour. [84] Separately, an advocacy network says ICE arrested 14 people in Sonoma County in the county's largest single-day action. [85] The Port of Seattle Commission asked Congress to end immigration enforcement at US airports. [86]

Assessment: Threats against ICE facilities and local political pushback are both growing.

5. MEXICO / BAHAMAS: Caravan reaches Oaxaca border; Bahamas in recession

The caravan of at least 1,000 walked from Tonalá to Arriaga on the Oaxaca border, and a migrant said immigration agents detained about 50 who went ahead; no new US tariff action was found. [87] Official data show Bahamian real GDP fell 2.7% in the first half of 2026. [88]

Assessment: Mexico is containing the caravan, and a breach of the Oaxaca line would likely revive the US tariff threat.

Collection gap: No Schumer statement, no new Canada development and no DOJ release on the Adelanto case were found; the National Hurricane Center page still served a stale 4 November issuance.

area of responsibility

SOUTHCOM.

1. COLOMBIA: Car bomb outside Meta governor's office in Villavicencio wounds up to 23

A remotely detonated car bomb exploded outside the Meta governor's office at about 2100 on 23 November; the health secretariat gave a preliminary count of 23 injured and no deaths. [89] Early counts ranged from 5 to 19. [90] [91] Investigators are examining whether Mordisco-aligned FARC dissidents carried it out in retaliation for the 21 November Mapiripán strike. [91] President De la Espriella wrote "THERE WILL BE NO DIALOGUE WITH NARCOTERRORISM." [90]

Assessment: This is a significant escalation into a departmental capital, and further urban attacks are likely.

2. VENEZUELA: Fourth dialogue round closes with eight-point accord but no prisoner releases

The government and opposition delegations closed the round on 23 November with agreements on reforming the anti-hate and organised-crime laws, broadcast concessions and a roadmap to recover gold held in Britain. [92] EFE described it as closing "with barely any progress," with no CNE renewal or prisoner releases; talks resume in December. [93]

Assessment: Slow progress on prisoners and the electoral council will keep US and opposition pressure high going into December.

3. HAITI / EL SALVADOR: Bukele offers troops an exit after three deaths

Bukele named the three soldiers killed, pledged $200,000 per family and said any soldier may leave Haiti "without fear of reprisal." [94] AP corrected its initial account that they died removing a grenade from a drone; per Bukele, the deaths were an "accidental explosion" during the mission. [95]

Assessment: The exit offer suggests strain on Salvadoran morale and will sharpen questions about the unilateral deployment.

4. ECUADOR: Noboa briefly resumes presidency to raise the minimum wage before local elections

The Assembly voted 83-60 to let Noboa resume office until the end of 23 November. [96] During that window he raised the basic wage from $482 to $500 from January 2027, six days before the 29 November vote. [97]

Assessment: Opponents are likely to call the decree electoral use of state power.

5. HONDURAS / GUATEMALA: Yoro operation continues; 11 killed in Guatemala City shootings

Congress President Zambrano said more than 1,000 police and troops remain in Yoro and cited more than 230 arrests. [98] Police said operations will run around the clock until the Cártel del Diablo leader is caught. [99] Five weekend shootings in the Guatemala City area killed 11. [100]

Assessment: Central American governments are settling into open-ended militarised postures as gang violence persists.

6. CUBA / BRAZIL / PERU: Another generating unit trips; real tests R$6; Junín aftershocks

Another thermal unit failed, and the utility projected 1,725 MW of load shedding, over 61% of peak demand. [101] Díaz-Canel launched an anti-crime exercise run from "wartime" structures. [102] The Brazilian real passed R$6 per dollar before closing at R$5.92 as Lula's finance minister choice remained open. [103] [104] Peru's geophysical institute recorded 12 aftershocks after the Junín quake. [105]

Assessment: Cuba's grid keeps deteriorating as internal security measures expand, and Brazil's markets await a fiscally credible finance pick.

Collection gap: No Venezuelan prisoner releases, no in-window Ecuador election-security incidents and no Haitian outlet reporting could be verified; the National Hurricane Center page still served a stale 4 November issuance.
