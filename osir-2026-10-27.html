area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. DRC: Ebola outbreak passes 10,000 confirmed cases

Congo's health ministry reported 10,060 confirmed cases on 27 Oct, nearly half of them fatal, in 66 health zones across eight provinces. About 80% of recent cases come from outside known contact lists, and at least half of deaths were among people never admitted to treatment. [1] Fear, misinformation and attacks on treatment centres are hampering the response. [2]

Assessment: With contact tracing failing, cases will very likely keep rising quickly, and further spread outside DRC is likely.

2. AFRICAN UNION: The 28 Oct Ebola meeting is for health ministers, not heads of state

Pre-window onset: Africa CDC is convening a virtual meeting of African health ministers on 28 Oct to prepare for a later heads-of-state summit, for which no date has been set. [3]

Assessment: The likely outcome is ministerial commitments and a summit date rather than leader-level decisions.

3. ALGERIA / NIGER: Tebboune formally orders troops to Niger

Algeria's presidency said on 26 Oct that President Tebboune ordered troops to Niger at Niamey's request to protect shared energy and economic interests; numbers and timing were not announced. A senator said the troops will guard Sonatrach oil facilities and are "not being sent to wage war." [4] Reports of the Senate vote differ: one says unanimous, [5] AFP reported 148 of 174. [6]

Assessment: The order turns the vote into a commitment; a hostile reaction from Mali and the Alliance of Sahel States is likely.

4. SUDAN: RSF drones knock out five power stations in eastern Sudan

An official told AFP that RSF drones hit five power stations in Al-Jazira, Gedaref and White Nile on 26 Oct, cutting power across large parts of all three states. [7]

Assessment: The RSF is pushing its drone campaign deep into army-held territory, which will worsen the humanitarian and economic crisis.

5. SUDAN: One year after El Fasher fell, abuses continue

Pre-window onset: Amnesty reports civilians in El Fasher still face detention, torture and killings, with fewer than 70,000 people left in a city of over a million. A Western coalition called for accountability, and the army-aligned governor's claims of 140 mass graves are unverified. [8] Human Rights Watch says the US is pushing to extend the arms embargo nationwide. [9]

Assessment: The anniversary adds pressure for a countrywide UN arms embargo.

6. SOMALIA: Major clash with al-Shabaab, with sharply conflicting claims

Somali forces and al-Shabaab clashed on 26 Oct. The defence ministry says it killed 75 militants but admits vehicle bombs damaged army vehicles; al-Shabaab claims it killed 321 troops, and neither claim is verified. The government reportedly called an emergency cabinet meeting. [10] Reports disagree on the location. [11]

Assessment: The emergency meeting and admitted losses suggest government casualties were significant.

7. NIGERIA: Army says 16 ISWAP fighters killed; Taraba toll rises to 50

The military says strikes near Lake Chad on 26 Oct killed 16 ISWAP fighters and badly wounded a local commander; these are military claims. [12] Survivors told AFP the toll from Friday's raid on Binnari, Taraba state, rose to 50 after 43 more bodies were found in a river. [13]

Assessment: The rising Taraba toll will sharpen criticism of rural security.

8. RUSSIA / AFRICA: Moscow hosts Russia-Africa summit on 28-29 Oct

Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said 48 African countries have confirmed attendance, with Burundi's president expected to co-chair. [14]

Assessment: Security and energy deals with Sahel juntas are likely to be announced.

9. KENYA / TANZANIA: Exiled Tanzanian opposition figure reportedly abducted near Nairobi

Pre-window onset: Amnesty says Twaha Mwaipaya, a Chadema organiser in exile, was seized from a taxi in Machakos County on 25 Oct and urged Kenya to locate him. [15]

Assessment: This adds to a pattern of transnational repression on Kenyan soil.

10. SOUTH AFRICA / US: Ambassador says ties are "strained"

South Africa's ambassador to Washington said relations are strained and he wants "direct dialogue," amid G20 exclusion, visa curbs and an AGOA review. [16]

Assessment: No breakthrough is in sight.

Collection gap: nothing new in the window was found on Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger security incidents, Ethiopia-Eritrea, Abyei or ground fighting in Kordofan.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. IRAN / SAUDI ARABIA / TURKEY / PAKISTAN: Tehran talks end with no deal but a pledge to keep talking

The four countries agreed on 26 Oct to continue security consultations, but announced no agreement or joint mechanism; Saudi Arabia said the talks support Qatari and Pakistani mediation between Iran and the US. [17] Iran's security council secretary called them "very good." [18] An Iranian official set seven conditions Washington must meet before new negotiations. [19][20]

Assessment: The talks produced a channel, not a breakthrough, and Iran is hardening its preconditions for talks with the US.

2. EGYPT: Parliament authorises military support for Saudi Arabia

Egypt's parliament authorised Sisi to take "all necessary measures" to help defend Saudi Arabia if Riyadh asks; no request has been announced, and Sisi says support will not include attacking Yemen. [21] The Social Democratic Party abstained. [22] A Houthi official said Egypt "declares war against the Republic of Yemen." [23]

Assessment: This is a legal step, not a deployment, but any Egyptian contribution would make Egypt a declared Houthi target, with risks to the Red Sea and Suez.

3. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: Houthi missiles aimed at Eastern Province; pipeline strike claimed

The coalition said it intercepted three ballistic missiles fired at the Eastern Province and four drones on 27 Oct, with no damage reported. [24][19] The Houthis claimed strikes on Riyadh's airport and East-West pipeline pumping stations; Saudi authorities did not immediately comment. [25]

Assessment: Houthi targeting is shifting toward oil export infrastructure; the pipeline claim is unverified.

4. ISRAEL: Election day under way with minor incidents

Polls opened at 0700 for about 7.3 million voters and close at 2200, with exit polls expected only after that. [26] Incidents by mid-morning were minor, including a burned billboard, wrecked party stalls and three arrests over alleged vote-buying. [27] Police are deployed at thousands of polling stations. [28] A pollster projects Arab turnout of 62-63%, against about 53% last time. [29]

Assessment: Voting appears orderly so far; the main risks are a late provocation and disputes over the result.

5. ISRAEL / US: CNN says Trump refused Netanyahu's push for a pre-election strike on Iran

CNN reports Netanyahu pushed for a joint strike on Iran before the election and Trump "did not give a green light"; US forces could launch a major operation within 96 hours of an order. The prime minister's office called the report "fake news." [30] Haaretz reports the Mossad chief backed the plan over his aides' objections. [31] Channel 12 reports IDF chief Zamir was the only security chief opposed and that Netanyahu is likely to fire him if re-elected. [32]

Assessment: The US is holding back an Israeli strike for now, but the risk of joint action after the vote is elevated.

6. GULF / IRAQ: Vessel on fire off al-Faw; Iran claims control of Hormuz

UKMTO reported a vessel on fire about 63 nm southeast of Iraq's al-Faw, with the cause unknown. [33] Iran's armed forces chief claimed full control of the Strait of Hormuz, contradicting US statements. [19]

Assessment: Shipping risk is spreading into the northern Gulf near Iraqi oil terminals.

7. ENERGY: Brent slips to about $114.81

Brent was $114.81 early on 27 Oct, down about 1% on the Tehran diplomacy, with Hormuz flows estimated at about 60% of normal. [34]

Assessment: Prices are essentially flat; the pipeline claim is the main upside risk.

8. LEBANON: Reuters says Hezbollah is rearming with Iranian cash

Lebanese security sources say Hezbollah received tens of millions of dollars from Iran to rebuild military capabilities, though it is not yet fully ready; Hezbollah has not said whether it would join new fighting. [35]

Assessment: Hezbollah's posture is the key indicator if strikes on Iran resume.

9. GAZA: At least five killed, including a 7-year-old

Strikes killed four people in a displacement camp in al-Mawasi and one in Gaza City; medical sources put the toll since the ceasefire at 1,559. [36]

Assessment: Strikes continue at a steady rate during the truce.

10. JORDAN / SYRIA: Jordanian airstrikes in southern Syria after Karak church attack

Pre-window onset: After gunmen killed nine people at a church funeral in Karak on 23 Oct, Jordan struck what it called militant hideouts in southern Syria. Syria said the strikes were uncoordinated, killed civilians, and closed the Nasib crossing. [37] Jordan says more than 20 ISIS militants were killed. [38]

Assessment: This opens a new Jordan-Syria friction point and disrupts a key trade route.

Collection gap: Israeli turnout figures could not be confirmed on an opened page, and no in-window Pakistan or Afghanistan items were found.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE: Large overnight strike on Kyiv's transport links

Ukraine's Air Force says Russia launched 2 ballistic missiles and 280 drones overnight into 27 Oct, downing 257, with hits at 25 locations; Kyiv's Pivdennyi Bridge was closed and the metro disrupted. [39] Zelensky gave a higher figure of 16 ballistic missiles, called it "a serious escalation" against logistics, and blamed delays in interceptor deliveries. [40] An evening strike on 26 Oct near a university dormitory killed one and injured at least 10. [41]

Assessment: Russia is concentrating on Kyiv's bridges and transport, and the gap between official missile counts is unexplained.

2. RUSSIA / US / UKRAINE: Abu Dhabi talks reported for around 10 November

Ukrainska Pravda sources say the next three-way round is planned for around 10 Nov in Abu Dhabi, with Kyiv and Washington wanting it a week earlier and Russia yet to confirm. [42] ISW also lists the talks for 10 Nov and notes Moscow still rejects any ceasefire. [43]

Assessment: The date is widely reported but not confirmed by all three sides.

3. UKRAINE: Limited Russian gains in Zaporizhzhia

ISW saw a limited Russian advance in the Hulyaipole direction but did not confirm Russian claims to have taken Obshche. [43]

Assessment: The front remains largely static.

4. SERBIA: Student List near 52.5% with 96% counted

Pre-window onset: With 8,077 of 8,392 polling stations processed, the Student List had 52.54% and the ruling SNS-led list 39.29%. [44] The new assembly must convene within 30 days of final results, and campaign leader Ilija Srdanović is seen as the likeliest prime minister. [45]

Assessment: The Student List has a clear majority; certification and the snap presidential vote due by 27 Dec are the next tests.

5. MOLDOVA / UKRAINE: Military cooperation agreement signed

Moldova's prime minister signed a military-technical cooperation agreement in Kyiv on 26 Oct. [46] The sides agreed to revive a format with Romania and expand joint border control. [47] Moldova reaffirmed "firm support" for Ukraine. [48] No Russian retaliation for last week's expulsion was found.

Assessment: Moldova is moving closer to Ukraine on defence; Russia's promised response is still pending.

6. GEORGIA: Court refuses to call Ivanishvili in party-ban case

The Constitutional Court rejected the opposition's request to call Bidzina Ivanishvili and the prime minister as witnesses; hearings run to 30 Oct. [49]

Assessment: The court's handling points toward a ruling in the ruling party's favour.

7. FRANCE: Youth unions reject concessions and keep protest dates

Student and youth unions rejected the government's education concessions as "largely insufficient" and kept protests set for 31 Oct and 5 Nov. [50] Macron called 31 Oct "high-risk," and the main unions have called a strike for 5 Nov. [51]

Assessment: The risk of disorder on 31 Oct remains high.

8. TURKEY / LIBYA: Erdoğan defends maritime deal as neighbours call it "null and void"

Erdoğan said criticism of the Turkey-Libya maritime deal "has no legitimate basis." [52] Greece, Cyprus, Egypt, Bahrain, the UAE and the EU called it "null and void." [53]

Assessment: The eastern Mediterranean dispute is sharpening; watch for naval or survey activity.

9. TURKEY / AZERBAIJAN: Erdoğan fires adviser over hijab row

Erdoğan dismissed an adviser who criticised Azerbaijan's school hijab ban, after Baku barred 31 Turkish nationals. [54]

Assessment: Ankara is putting the Baku alliance ahead of domestic religious pressure.

10. ESTONIA: Light fighter jets considered against drones and cruise missiles

Estonia's defence minister said the country may buy light jets to counter cheap Russian cruise missiles and jet drones. [55]

Assessment: This is a capability debate, not a decision.

Collection gap: nothing in the window was found on a NATO airspace incident, Germany or Serbia's certified final result.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. SOUTH KOREA / NORTH KOREA: Seoul says Pyongyang aims to finish border fortification; military plays down howitzers

Unification Minister Chung Dong-young told parliament Pyongyang has "a strong intention to complete the 'borderization' project as scheduled" and that the fix is to restore the 2018 military agreement. [56] The Joint Chiefs said the howitzers facing Gimpo are an older system, not the "new" weapons Kim Yo Jong flagged. [57]

Assessment: Seoul's measured line suggests any response to the loudspeakers would also be measured. No North Korean loudspeaker activation or missile launch was found.

2. TAIWAN: TV host's disavowal of independence becomes a campaign issue

Entertainer Kevin Tsai said he has "never supported Taiwan independence" after a PLA-linked Weibo account criticised him for attending a DPP campaign event; Taipei's KMT mayor said he will not accept "anti-democratic intimidation." [58] Chinese interference led a major newspaper's front page on 27 Oct. [59]

Assessment: More such pressure is likely before the 28 Nov local elections.

3. CHINA / JAPAN: Beijing uses Fukuda's death to rebuke Takaichi

Former Prime Minister Yasuo Fukuda died on 22 Oct. China's foreign ministry said he "held a correct view of history" and urged self-reflection by Japan's current government. [60] Global Times noted Fukuda had urged Takaichi to retract her Taiwan remarks. [61]

Assessment: There is no sign of a thaw before APEC.

4. CHINA / INDIA: Beijing defends Arunachal patrols

China said its border patrols are normal after reports of continuing face-offs in Arunachal Pradesh. [62][63] The Indian Army debunked a deepfake video claiming China had seized Arunachal areas. [64]

Assessment: Friction on the Line of Actual Control is persistent but managed.

5. PHILIPPINES: Manila puts US alliance first at ASEAN defence meetings

The defence department said the US alliance will be a priority at the ASEAN defence meetings in Cebu, which US Secretary of War Pete Hegseth will attend. [65] China would not say whether it will attend and warned against using the meetings "to seek selfish gains." [63]

Assessment: Whether China attends, and at what level, is the key signal.

6. CHINA / INDONESIA: Army counter-terrorism drill resumes after 14 years

China's defence ministry announced "Sharp Knife 2026" with Indonesia's army in Guangdong, the first since 2012. [66]

Assessment: The drill cuts against a common ASEAN position on the South China Sea.

7. INDONESIA: Haze crisis deepens

Palangka Raya deployed an "oxygen bus," and the health ministry counts about 350,000 fire-linked respiratory cases since August. [67]

Assessment: The rising health burden keeps up domestic and regional pressure.

8. MYANMAR / MALAYSIA: Kuala Lumpur to query Myanmar over detained returnees

Malaysia's foreign minister said Malaysia will ask Myanmar about reports that about 300 or more returnees remain in military detention. [68]

Assessment: The second phase of repatriation could be delayed.

9. PAPUA NEW GUINEA: MPs back Bougainville independence in debate

Two MPs publicly backed independence or criticised past military deployment during the referendum debate. [69] Parliament resumes in November for a vote. [70]

Assessment: Support is becoming more visible, but the November vote is the decision point.

10. AUSTRALIA: Marles says Timor-Leste framework completes regional network

Deputy PM Richard Marles said the Timor-Leste defence framework completes a regional network and that a Solomon Islands agreement is in progress. [69]

Assessment: The pending Solomon Islands deal is the one to watch.

Collection gap: no Taiwan defence ministry count for 27 Oct, new South China Sea incident, Japan flooding toll or APEC confirmation from Beijing or Washington was found.

area of responsibility

NORTHCOM.

1. MEXICO: Vance weakens to 45 mph; no watches

At 0900 UTC Vance had 45 mph winds about 275 mi SW of Manzanillo, with no coastal watches; 2-4 inches of rain is forecast through Thursday over Sinaloa, Durango, Nayarit and Jalisco. [71] No Atlantic storms are expected in the next seven days. [72] The rain overlaps Día de Muertos travel. [73]

Assessment: The threat is now inland flooding and mudslides.

2. UNITED STATES: Appeals court declines to fast-track bid to set aside California ballots

The Ninth Circuit declined to rule quickly on a Justice Department bid to set aside about 20,000 California ballots it says were cast by noncitizens, sending the case back to a district judge for a hearing on 27 Oct. [74]

Assessment: A sequestration order days before 3 Nov would be a major flashpoint.

3. UNITED STATES: DOJ charges 48 with election crimes

The Justice Department announced election-related charges against 48 people in five states, including noncitizen voting and false citizenship claims. [75]

Assessment: The timing reinforces the administration's noncitizen-voting narrative.

4. UNITED STATES: Big Bend wall application still pending

Pre-window onset: The Supreme Court docket shows no order; challengers' response is due on 29 Oct. [76] The government wants the halt on construction lifted. [77]

Assessment: An order is unlikely before 29 Oct.

5. MEXICO: Sheinbaum confirms repeated US requests for joint military operations

Sheinbaum said US officials have repeatedly asked for joint operations in Mexico and Mexico has refused: "never with US forces operating on national territory." [78]

Assessment: The risk of unilateral US pressure before 3 Nov is elevated.

6. MEXICO: Truckers' strike blocks highways

A national truckers' strike over tax changes and road violence began on 26 Oct, with talks set for 27 Oct. [79]

Assessment: Renewed blockades would compound holiday travel and storm disruption.

7. MEXICO: Alleged Los Rusos leader arrested in Mexicali

Pre-window onset: Baja California police arrested "El Isra," an alleged leader of a group tied to the Los Mayos faction. [80]

Assessment: Retaliatory violence along the border corridor is possible.

8. CANADA: Carney says US "coming closer to our position"

Carney described a "slight, slight intensification" of trade talks and said the US is moving toward Canada's position. [81]

Assessment: A deal is more likely after 3 Nov than before.

Collection gap: no new US domestic security incident or Guard deployment was found in the window.

SOUTHCOM.

1. BRAZIL: Bolsonaro's party recognises Lula's win; Flávio drops annulment bid

PL president Valdemar Costa Neto said "we recognise the presidential result," and three other parties did the same; allies said annulment has been ruled out and Flávio will instead seek revocation of the Lula-Alckmin ticket. [82][83] His abuse-of-power suits were filed before the runoff. [84] No protests or road blockades were reported.

Assessment: With his own party gone, Flávio's challenge is a long-shot legal fight.

2. BRAZIL / US: State Department reportedly holds back congratulations

Citing Folha, a Portuguese daily reports a US congratulatory statement was ready but held back. [85] Trump had not commented more than 12 hours after the result. [86]

Assessment: With the PL conceding, continued US silence would sharpen bilateral friction.

3. VENEZUELA: Government calls Machado's return a "show"

Acting President Delcy Rodríguez said "the show could be avoided" and would not say whether courts will act against Machado. [87] Machado's camp said she will talk if the agenda is transparent, and Rubio called her return a direct result of US-backed dialogue. [88]

Assessment: The government is tolerating Machado for now.

4. HAITI / EL SALVADOR: Troops keep arriving; gang leader claims soldiers are dead

About 450 Salvadoran troops are reported in Haiti, the remains have not been identified, and a gang leader claimed in a video that the four missing soldiers are dead. [89]

Assessment: A Salvadoran push east of Port-au-Prince is likely, with a risk of heavy fighting.

5. CUBA / UN: Embargo debate opens

Cuba presents its annual resolution against the US embargo this week; its ambassador accused Washington of changing votes "at gunpoint." [90] Cuba cites blackouts averaging over 20 hours. [91]

Assessment: A fall from last year's 165 yes votes would measure US pressure.

6. ECUADOR: Noboa says opposition is funded by organised crime

Noboa said without evidence that the Correísta opposition is funded by drug money; Revolución Ciudadana is barred from the 29 Nov elections. [92]

Assessment: The risk of protest and international criticism is rising.
