area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. SOMALIA: US reverses course and backs renewal of UN and AU mission mandates

The State Department said on 15 November it would support a one-year renewal of the mandates for the UN support office (UNSOS) and the AU mission (AUSSOM), after ending UNSOS funding in July; Mogadishu welcomed the move on 16 November. [1] Uganda, the largest troop contributor, plans full withdrawal by the end of 2027, and a diplomat told AFP the policy swings were "madness." [1] The opposition boycott of the 14-18 November election forum was not reported as resolved.

Assessment: The renewal avoids a funding gap against al-Shabaab but removes leverage on Villa Somalia during the election crisis.

2. SUDAN: Army claims gains toward Abu Zabad; rights group reports 13 civilians killed

Army spokesman Didan told Anadolu that forces took Al-Saatat, northeast of Abu Zabad on the El Obeid-Dilling road, and military sources claimed several more villages east of Al-Nuhud; the RSF did not comment. [2] The Emergency Lawyers group said an army air strike in the Abu Zabad area killed at least 13 civilians, which Al Jazeera could not verify. [3]

Assessment: The SAF is pressing a Kordofan offensive, and civilian harm claims are likely to feed RSF messaging.

3. DRC: Ebola deaths reach 5,568

The national public health institute reported 36 deaths and 60 new cases in 24 hours, bringing totals to 11,376 cases and 5,568 deaths, according to a secondary state-media report. [4]

Assessment: Transmission remains high, and insecurity in the east continues to limit the response.

4. SOUTH AFRICA: ANC-DA municipal talks strain the coalition

An analyst told EWN that disputes over mayoral posts could strain the national unity government over time but do not threaten it immediately, noting reports of DA talks with MK in some councils. [5]

Assessment: Council coalition deals before the first sittings are the near-term test of GNU cohesion.

Collection gap: Nothing in-window was found on Ethiopia-Eritrea, al-Shabaab or AFRICOM strikes, the Sahel, Nigeria, Libya, Kenya, South Sudan or the M23 front line; africom.mil did not load.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. BAHRAIN / IRAN: Third IRGC drone attack in five days; US responds with condemnation

Bahrain's air defences stopped a drone attack on 17 November; the IRGC said it targeted a US radar at Isa Air Base. [6] Secretary of State Rubio condemned the attacks in a call with Bahrain's foreign minister and restated US commitment to Gulf security. [7] CENTCOM had not commented on the latest two claims. [8]

Assessment: Iran has shifted to repeated pressure on US sites in Bahrain, and Washington is answering with diplomacy, not force, so far.

2. HORMUZ: Tanker hit off Khasab; Iranian sinking claims unverified

UKMTO said a tanker leaving the strait was hit by an "unknown projectile" north of Khasab on 16 November, starting an engine-room fire; the crew were safe. [9] [8] The IRGC navy claimed six tankers caught fire and were sinking since Sunday, which Reuters could not verify. [9] No source confirms Monday's claimed supertanker sinking. [9]

Assessment: At least one projectile attack is confirmed amid many unverifiable claims, keeping shipping risk high.

3. IRAN / PAKISTAN / OMAN: Araghchi presses Hormuz plan in Islamabad and Muscat

Araghchi met Pakistan's army chief and prime minister and said the ECO backed Iran's plan to reopen Hormuz in exchange for frozen assets, sanctions relief and an end to the US blockade; no other member confirmed that. [10] [8] He said Iran trusts Pakistan's mediation, and discussed "safeguarding the security of maritime passages" with Oman's foreign minister. [11] [12]

Assessment: Tehran is seeking regional cover for its Hormuz terms, with no sign of US engagement.

4. ENERGY: Brent falls on surplus forecasts

Brent settled at $92.44 on Monday, down about 3.6%, after an early rise to around $96 cited in Monday's edition. [13] It traded at $91.62 on Tuesday morning as IEA and Goldman Sachs surplus forecasts outweighed Gulf attacks. [14]

Assessment: Markets are looking past Iran's escalation, leaving room for a sharp move if a sinking or closure is confirmed.

5. ISRAEL: Speaker vote due; Eisenkot bloc appears to have the numbers

The Times of Israel counts 48 seats plus 19 from Arab parties for Matan Kahana against 53 for the pro-Netanyahu bloc; Kahana rejected Smotrich's conditions. [15] UTJ said it will not back Kahana but has not decided whether to oppose or abstain. [16] The vote falls after this window.

Assessment: A Kahana win would cost Netanyahu control of the Knesset agenda.

6. WEST BANK: Woman, 72, killed near Jericho; UK sanctions settlement builders

Palestinian officials say settlers killed a 72-year-old woman in Al-Duyuk Al-Tahta; the IDF says Israelis fired after their vehicle was attacked. [17] The UK sanctioned eight settlement construction firms and the group Tzav 9. [18]

Assessment: Settler violence and British pressure add to friction while Israel is between governments.

7. GAZA: Strikes and demolitions continue

A drone strike near Bureij critically wounded one person, and Israeli forces demolished homes near Deir al-Balah; the Hamas-run health ministry counts 1,650 killed since the ceasefire. [19]

Assessment: The ceasefire is eroding through daily attacks rather than full war.

8. YEMEN: Houthis reported 80 km from Aden

An analyst says Houthi forces reached Tor al-Baha after government forces left Dhubab airport, and the Houthis fired at Mecca, Riyadh and the Eastern Province. [20]

Assessment: A second maritime chokepoint is at risk while Hormuz is effectively closed.

9. IRAQ: Ruling bloc split over Jurf al-Sakhar; 20 November deadline

A security source says Kataib Hezbollah must leave Jurf al-Sakhar by 2000 on 20 November, but the Coordination Framework refused the prime minister's request to authorise sending forces in. [21]

Assessment: A phased handover is most likely, though a limited clash remains possible.

10. SYRIA / LEBANON: Israeli raids in Deraa; strike in the Bekaa

Israeli troops raided Maariya in western Deraa and shelled farmland, prompting residents to flee. [22] The IDF said it struck a Hezbollah weapons shipment in the Bekaa, with no casualties reported. [23]

Assessment: Israel continues to operate freely in southern Syria and Lebanon.

Collection gap: No speaker vote result, no Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee reporting, and nothing in-window on Pakistan-Afghanistan, Egypt, Jordan or Central Asia.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE / RUSSIA: Crimean Bridge hit again; road span collapses

Zelensky called the strike an "unprecedented operation," the seventh phase of a multi-week campaign that first hit air defence, radar and electronic-warfare assets, with drones and missiles, including a Flamingo, striking both road and rail sections. [24] Photos show a roadway span in the water; Russia's Transport Ministry reported no casualties on the bridge and suspended road and rail traffic. [25] Russia's Investigative Committee confirmed "damage to the structure" and opened a terrorism case. [26] Crimea's Russian-installed head Aksyonov said drones killed two people near Kerch. [27] [28]

Assessment: Unlike the first strike, this one appears structural and is likely to cut road logistics into Crimea for weeks.

2. UKRAINE: Russian ballistic missiles hit Kyiv's Dnipro bridges

Mayor Klitschko said the Northern and Southern bridges were hit, the metro was damaged, and water, power and heating were partly cut. [29] Explosions were also reported in Dnipro, Pavlohrad, Zaporizhzhia and Kharkiv, and three people were injured in Smila. [29] [30] Russia's MoD said it hit two Kyiv bridges and missile and drone production sites. [27] Both bridges are closed pending engineering checks. [31] No Ukrainian Air Force launch or intercept figures were found.

Assessment: Moscow is answering the bridge campaign in kind, so further strikes on Ukrainian transport are likely.

3. RUSSIA: Moscow claims record drone interceptions; figures conflict

ABC cites Russia's MoD at 1,441 drones overnight and Sobyanin at 800 aimed at Moscow, with flights restricted at all four Moscow airports. [28] i24NEWS cites the MoD at 2,049 drones in 24 hours and Sobyanin at about 600. [30] The figures are Russian state claims and are unverified.

Assessment: Whichever count is right, Ukraine has sustained mass drone raids for two nights, disrupting Russian civil aviation.

4. DIPLOMACY: Kremlin expects Ukraine on the Shenzhen agenda

Ushakov said Putin's talks with Xi in Shenzhen will cover Ukraine, a joint statement is expected and a Putin-Trump meeting is possible, though the White House schedule lists only Trump-Xi. [32] Finnish President Stubb told the FT that relying on the US to run talks is "irresponsible" and that Putin is "ready to engage with Europe." [33]

Assessment: Shenzhen is now the main venue for Ukraine diplomacy this week, while Europe weighs its own channel to Moscow.

5. EU: 113 new listings target banks, shadow fleet and e-commerce owners

The EU listed 12 individuals and 101 entities, including 49 banks and financial institutions, 27 refining, mining and gas firms, 15 shadow-fleet owners and insurers, and the founder of Wildberries. [34] [35] A 22nd package is expected before the 26-27 November summit. [34]

Assessment: Targeting payment intermediaries and marketplace owners widens pressure on Russia's domestic financial system beyond energy.

6. UKRAINE FRONT: ISW confirms only Ukrainian advances

ISW's 16 November assessment confirms Ukrainian advances near Yunakivka, Vilcha and Novoandriivka and no Russian advances, and reports continued Starshield speed limits on Ukrainian drones. [36]

Assessment: The front is broadly static, but the Starshield issue is a capability risk for Ukraine.

7. BELARUS: Snap readiness check of border units

Lukashenko ordered an unscheduled combat-readiness check of border-guard rapid-response units, according to state agency Belta; Ukraine's border service called it information pressure. [37]

Assessment: This is probably signalling, but it adds to tension on the Polish and Lithuanian borders.

Collection gap: Ukrainian Air Force figures, civilian casualty totals, Black Sea shipping, Georgia, the Baltics and the Balkans had no in-window reporting; the 11 November prisoner exchange was not reported as carried out.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. PHILIPPINES / US: Trump arrives in Manila for first in-person Quad summit in two years

Trump landed in Manila on 17 November and was due to meet the leaders of India, Japan and Australia that evening, before a state visit hosted by Marcos on 18 November and departure for Shenzhen. [38] Anadolu lists a trade deal signing with Thailand and events with ASEAN leaders. [39] The Quad session was set for about 1030Z, so its outcome falls after this window.

Assessment: Washington appears to want visible alliance reassurance before engaging Xi and Putin.

2. ASEAN: Chair passes to Singapore; format of US meeting unclear

The Philippines was due to hand the 2027 chair to Singapore on 17 November, the summit's last day, alongside the ASEAN Plus Three and East Asia summits. [40] Malaysia's state agency calls Tuesday's US event an "ASEAN-United States Special Summit," while The Star reports only a dinner with Trump. [41] [40]

Assessment: Confirmed US-ASEAN deliverables look thin.

3. REGIONAL MARKETS: Asian shares fall again before Nvidia results

Early on 17 November the Nikkei fell 1.2%, the Kospi 1% and the Taiex 0.9%, after a 2.5% Nasdaq drop and new US rules restricting remote access to advanced chips; the yen eased to 159.35. [42] Nvidia reports after this window. [42]

Assessment: Taiwan's chip-heavy market remains the most exposed to the Nvidia results and the new chip rules.

4. JAPAN / TAIWAN / RUSSIA: Russian aircraft violate Japanese airspace and pass Taiwan

Japan said two aircraft "believed to be Russian" violated its airspace off Okinoshima and called it "a serious infringement," the first such violation since September 2024; Taiwan tracked the pair off its east coast. [43] AP counts 2,401 PLA flights toward Taiwan this year and reports the $14 billion arms package remains stalled. [44]

Assessment: The flights look like Russian signalling of Pacific reach and alignment with Beijing on the eve of Shenzhen.

5. NORTH KOREA: Kim shown at ski resort; Beijing has "no information" on Trump meeting

KCNA showed Kim and family at Samjiyon on 15-16 November after Trump said "We may meet with Kim Jong-un," depending on Xi. [45] China's foreign ministry said it had "no information," and ABC notes Kim rarely flies. [46] [47]

Assessment: A Kim appearance at APEC is unlikely.

6. SOUTH KOREA / PHILIPPINES: Lee and Marcos expand defence and shipbuilding ties

Lee and Marcos agreed to deepen defence cooperation and expand shipbuilding, AI, nuclear power and space ties, with bilateral trade at a record $21.3 billion. [48]

Assessment: Seoul is locking in defence-industrial ties with Manila.

7. INDONESIA: Vice-presidential vote delayed to 9 December

The MPR moved the vote from 2 to 9 December at Prabowo's request, and he must submit two nominees by 25 November. [49]

Assessment: The delay gives Prabowo room to bargain within the coalition after APEC.

8. CAMBODIA / FRANCOPHONIE: Secretary-general vote postponed a year

Delegates deferred the vote after the DRC threatened a walkout if Rwanda's Mushikiwabo won a third term; she stays in post and Rwanda will not field a candidate next time. [50] A pro-Kigali outlet credits Senegal's President Faye with brokering the deal, and RFI says Macron proposed that both candidates withdraw. [51] [52]

Assessment: The Great Lakes conflict is now paralysing the organisation.

9. BANGLADESH: Awami League calls nationwide lockdown for 19 November

The banned Awami League plans to mobilise in 29 more districts and has called an "all-Bangladesh lockdown" on 19 November; border guards have deployed in several districts. [53]

Assessment: The risk of street clashes rises on 19 November.

10. CHINA: Shenzhen locks down for APEC

Shenzhen banned horns and grounded delivery drones as about 20 leaders arrive, barring out-of-town cars from most districts from 17 November. [54]

Assessment: Expect tight message control around the trilateral.

Collection gap: Taiwan MND daily counts, on-the-ground Manila reporting and the Quad outcome were not available; no new South China Sea or India-Pakistan developments were found.

area of responsibility

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES: Lame duck squeezed by funding deadline and Iran-war rider fight

Republicans have four legislative weeks left in the majority; Democrats say they will back a spending bill only if it cuts off funds for the Iran war, which Thune called "pretty complicated." [55] Other priorities include the NDAA and a possible $350 billion defence supplemental. [55]

Assessment: A stopgap into January is most likely, but an Iran-war rider fight raises the risk of a short lapse around 11 December.

2. UNITED STATES: ICE agents accused of pepper-spraying pregnant US citizen

A 23-year-old US citizen who monitors ICE activity says agents pepper-sprayed her, forced her to the ground and detained her outside a Modesto jail on 15 November; ICE accuses her of assault, she has not been charged and DHS had not commented. [56]

Assessment: Such incidents will feed protests and oversight demands once the new Congress sits.

3. MEXICO / CHINA: Sheinbaum meets Xi, plans meeting with Carney

Sheinbaum called her 17 November meeting with Xi a "nueva era," though China's readout carried no concrete commitments. [57] She was also scheduled to meet Carney to discuss stalled US trade talks and the CUSMA review. [58] [59]

Assessment: Both US neighbours are hedging toward Beijing while US talks stall, inviting a US response.

4. CANADA / CHINA: Carney and Xi cut tariffs and extend trade deal to 2028

China will drop its 25% tariff on Canadian frozen pork and some seafood and open pulse access, while Canada extends tariff relief to 32 more Chinese steel and aluminium items; the January arrangement now runs to 2028. [60] [61]

Assessment: Expect a sharp US response that complicates the CUSMA review.

5. MEXICO: Caravan reaches Mapastepec; new group leaves Honduras

The caravan reached Mapastepec, Chiapas, with participants putting it at almost 2,000, though about 300 left to surrender to migration authorities; an activist says a new caravan left San Pedro Sula. [62]

Assessment: Caravan growth raises the odds Trump acts on his tariff threat.

6. MEXICO: Family attacked in Nuevo León; three killed

Gunmen killed a father and two children and wounded a 12-year-old in Galeana, Nuevo León, on 16 November; there are no arrests. [63]

Assessment: Rural massacres in the northeast continue despite the government's arrest messaging.

Collection gap: No in-window reporting on Alaska, Mississippi, war-powers votes or the Sheinbaum-Carney outcome.

area of responsibility

SOUTHCOM.

1. GUATEMALA: Congress to vote on state of siege

Congress was due to vote on the 30-day state of siege in Zacapa and Izabal on 17 November after this window; ratification needs 81 votes and the Congress president says the consensus exists. [64] [65] Some parties want it extended to more departments. [64]

Assessment: Ratification is highly likely, possibly with a wider scope.

2. COLOMBIA: Drone hits Huila police station; toll-booth bombings reach seven

A drone-dropped explosive hit the El Pital police station in Huila on 16 November, lightly wounding an officer. [66] Infobae counts seven November attacks on toll stations, including car bombs in Neiva and Meta on 15 November, after a dissident commander named tolls as targets. [67]

Assessment: Dissidents are shifting to infrastructure targets, and more attacks during holiday travel are likely.

3. VENEZUELA: Talks continue through the week

The opposition negotiator said the fourth round will run all week, covering civic space, media, political disqualifications and Supreme Court renewal. [68]

Assessment: Progress hinges on lifting disqualifications, and no election date is in sight.

4. HAITI: Haitian officials praise Salvadoran deployment ahead of Bukele talks

Haiti's security minister praised the more than 2,000 Salvadoran troops; UN data attribute more than half of 4,217 deaths from January to September to security operations. [69]

Assessment: An extension of the deployment is likely, with growing scrutiny of civilian harm.

5. CUBA: Guiteras back online, deficit still about 1,730 MW

The Guiteras plant resynchronised, but the grid operator forecast a 1,730 MW peak deficit on 16 November with other units down. [70]

Assessment: A slight improvement on Monday, but national blackout risk persists.

6. ECUADOR: Election materials distributed amid Guayaquil violence

The electoral council began distributing 41,023 election packages under military custody for the 29 November vote. [71] Four shootings in Guayaquil on 15 November left three dead. [72]

Assessment: Election-period violence in Guayas will test security.

7. BRAZIL: Lula returns to name new cabinet

Lula said after meeting Spain's prime minister that he was returning home to make things "happen faster," with cabinet appointments and a disputed fiscal bill ahead. [73]

Assessment: The cabinet picks will signal his fiscal direction.

Collection gap: No new Colombian airstrikes or Haiti-El Salvador talks outcome found, and the National Hurricane Center outlook page returned a stale 7 November issuance, so no tropical status is reported.
