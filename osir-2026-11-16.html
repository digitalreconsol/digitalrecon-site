area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. SOMALIA: Election talks fail to open as opposition boycotts

The 14-18 November forum did not open after the Somali Future Council, including Puntland and Jubaland leaders, said it would join only internationally facilitated talks held outside Villa Somalia. [1] [2] A single source said two other blocs agreed in principle to attend, which one denied. [3] [1]

Assessment: The talks have stalled before starting, keeping the risk of armed clashes elevated.

2. SOMALIA: AFRICOM strike near Leego

AFRICOM announced a 12 November airstrike on al-Shabaab near Leego, Lower Shabelle, in support of the federal government, with no damage assessment. [4]

Assessment: US air support continues for the Mogadishu-Baidoa corridor even as the political crisis diverts federal forces.

3. SUDAN: Drone strikes and tribal clashes kill dozens

Darfur24 reported at least 15 civilians killed in drone strikes around Al-Nuhud, West Kordofan, over a week. [5] Rizeigat-Habbaniyah clashes in South Darfur killed at least 31. [6]

Assessment: Civilian deaths are rising with the army's West Kordofan push, and communal splits weaken the RSF.

4. DRC: Africa CDC says Ebola "still not under control"

Africa CDC said more than 80% of new cases arise outside contact lists and only $1.3 billion of $2.9 billion pledged has been released; government data put the outbreak above 11,200 cases. [7] US CDC data to 10 November show 11,145 cases and 5,437 deaths. [8]

Assessment: Cases will keep rising through year end.

5. ETHIOPIA / ERITREA: Drone and artillery exchanges near Badme

Anadolu, citing diplomatic sources, reports Ethiopian drone strikes and Eritrean artillery near Badme, with civilians fleeing. [9]

Assessment: Escalation to interstate war is plausible.

6. NIGER: Ambush near Tera kills at least 37 civilians

Gunmen ambushed villagers and their self-defence escort near Tera, Tillaberi, on 13 November; no group has claimed it. [10]

Assessment: Tillaberi remains the deadliest zone in the central Sahel.

7. NIGERIA: Zamfara governor's aide killed; Sokoto abductions

Bandits killed a senior aide to the Zamfara governor on 15 November, and gunmen abducted 22 in Sokoto. [11] The military claims airstrikes killed "scores" of attackers. [12]

Assessment: Northwest banditry is now reaching state officials.

8. SOUTH AFRICA: ANC-DA local coalition talks open

The ANC and DA opened local coalition talks, though the NEC ruled out a national pact and treats the DA as a "last resort." [13] Ramaphosa reportedly said he is "tired of the DA." [14]

Assessment: Municipal bargaining strains the national coalition but does not signal a near-term break.

Collection gap: No checked in-window reporting on Libya, South Sudan, Kenya, Mali, Burkina Faso, El Fasher or the FRN front.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. BAHRAIN / IRAN: Second IRGC drone attack in three days; Bahrain blames Tehran

Pre-window onset. CENTCOM said on 13 November that the IRGC launched two one-way attack drones toward Bahrain and that "both were shot down by U.S. forces," with "no injuries or damage," denying Iran's radar claim. [15] Blasts were heard over Manama early on 15 November as the IRGC said it sent six drones at a US radar at the Al-Riffa helicopter base; the Bahrain Defence Force said it "intercepted and destroyed" them. [16] [17] [18] Bahrain held Iran "fully responsible," while Tehran called the strikes "purely defensive." [19] Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Jordan and Egypt condemned the attack; no damage or casualties were reported and CENTCOM had not commented on the second attack. [20] [18]

Assessment: Two attacks on a US host nation in 72 hours look like deliberate Iranian pressure on Gulf states as Washington weighs further strikes.

2. HORMUZ: IRGC claims a laden supertanker sank; UKMTO raises threat to "severe"

The IRGC said tankers on an "unauthorised route" hit mines and that a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels sank; no ship was named. [21] [22] UKMTO could not link a reported attack on a Gulf tanker to the claim and said the crew were safe. [21] Reuters material carried by Fox News says UKMTO raised the regional threat level to "severe." [23] Iranian statements give one, two or three tankers. [23] [20]

Assessment: The sinking is unconfirmed, but the run of mine claims and the "severe" rating will raise insurance and transit costs.

3. ENERGY: Brent edges up after weekend attacks

Brent settled at $95.84 on Friday, a second weekly loss of about 2.5% on surplus concerns. [24] A market site put Brent at $96.28 early on Monday. [25]

Assessment: The market still weighs surplus signals over Hormuz risk; a confirmed sinking or US strike would be the likeliest trigger for a move above $98.

4. IRAN / US: Tehran says the door to talks is open as a third carrier arrives

Speaker Ghalibaf said the path to ending the conflict is open if the US accepts Iran's "logical conditions," and warned Iran would "determine the geography of battle" if strikes resume. [26] Reuters reported Trump told advisers he may hold off on further strikes absent new provocations, while Israel presses for action. [23] [27] A US official told Axios the USS Theodore Roosevelt arrived, giving three carrier groups in the region. [28]

Assessment: Both sides are signalling an escalation ceiling, but the Bahrain attacks test Trump's "no new provocations" threshold.

5. PAKISTAN / IRAN: Islamabad restates mediation role at ECO meeting

Pakistan's foreign minister said Islamabad would keep working to ease US-Iran tensions, and said better ties with Kabul depend on the Taliban acting on terrorism. [29] Araghchi said the US "chose a completely different path" and that regional states' territory "must not become a platform for attacks." [30] [29]

Assessment: Pakistan remains the main visible US-Iran channel, while Araghchi's message aims to separate Gulf hosts from Washington.

6. ISRAEL: Liberman takes defence committee chair; speaker vote set for 17 November

Liberman was elected chair of the interim Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee on 16 November and immediately sought to summon the IDF, Mossad and Shin Bet chiefs. [31] The Eisenkot bloc plans to replace Speaker Ohana with Matan Kahana on 17 November and to begin repealing judicial-overhaul laws from 18 November. [27]

Assessment: The committee now gives the incoming majority oversight of caretaker war decisions, and Tuesday's vote is the bloc's first real test.

7. GAZA: Strike kills Hamas Rafah commander; cold deaths reach 11

The IDF said a drone strike in al-Mawasi killed Rafah Brigade commander Samir Abu Younis; the strike hit a tent and wounded two others. [32] The Hamas-run health ministry reported 16 killed in 48 hours and 11 cold-related deaths. [32]

Assessment: Targeted killings continue under a stalled ceasefire as winter adds to civilian deaths.

8. WEST BANK: Settlers attack olive harvesters near Sa'ir

Dozens of masked settlers attacked Palestinian harvesters near Sa'ir on 14 November, injuring at least five according to WAFA; the IDF condemned the attack. [27]

Assessment: Harvest-season settler violence continues despite military condemnation.

9. YEMEN / SAUDI ARABIA: Houthis fire at Riyadh; government forces leave Bab el-Mandeb heights

The Houthis said they fired two ballistic missiles at Riyadh's airport and financial centre, and the coalition said it intercepted two missiles; a Houthi strike on a Lahj checkpoint killed eight. [33] Government forces withdrew from heights overlooking the Bab el-Mandeb to reinforce Aden. [33] [20]

Assessment: Houthi control of the strait's heights raises the risk to Red Sea shipping.

10. IRAQ: Prime minister sets deadline for Kataib Hezbollah to quit Jurf al-Sakhar

PM al-Zaidi sacked nine officials in an oil-smuggling probe targeting the militia. [34] Shafaq News, cited by The New Arab, reports he gave Kataib Hezbollah until the end of next week to hand over Jurf al-Sakhar, with counter-terrorism forces on alert. [35]

Assessment: This is the most direct state challenge to the militia's core enclave in years and could draw in Iran.

Collection gap: No CENTCOM statement on the 15 November attack, no independent confirmation of the tanker sinking, and no checked in-window reporting on Lebanon, Syria or Afghanistan.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE / RUSSIA / US: Talks widen to European security; no date set

Kushner said Russia agreed to include Europe and that the US is "preparing the agenda" for the next round, without a date or venue. [36] AP reports the agenda now covers wider European security arrangements; Kallas wants a ceasefire first, and Peskov said Moscow is open to contacts depending on Europe's "true intentions." [37] ISW cites a reported Russian non-paper seeking NATO pullback from post-1997 members. [38]

Assessment: A wider security negotiation is likely to slow any Ukraine-specific ceasefire.

2. UKRAINE: Zelensky says US floated NATO membership for Donbas withdrawal

Pre-window onset. Zelensky said the US for the first time raised whether Ukraine would leave Donbas if it joined NATO, with guarantees "like Articles 4 and 5" and no US troops, and said Ukrainians would decide. [39] He called Putin's Kostiantynivka proposal a bid to take the city. [38]

Assessment: The idea clashes with Moscow's core demand and is unlikely to survive in this form.

3. UKRAINE: Russian strikes kill at least 11; Odesa grid hit

Strikes killed seven and injured 80 in the 24 hours to 14 November, with Zaporizhzhia hardest hit. [40] A mass attack on Odesa Oblast cut power to about 41,000 families as four more were killed. [41] Russia launched 158 drones overnight into 16 November, of which 131 were downed or suppressed. [42] No prisoner exchange was reported in the window. [36] [37]

Assessment: Strikes of 100-160 drones a night continue to target energy.

4. RUSSIA: Ukraine's largest drone attack hits Crimean Bridge

Russia's MoD claimed 1,336 Ukrainian drones downed overnight into 16 November, its highest total, and Moscow's mayor said about 600 headed for the capital region. [43] [44] Crimea's Russian-installed head confirmed a strike on the Crimean Bridge, restricting traffic. [43] [45] Ukraine's General Staff claimed hits on refineries in Tatarstan, a helicopter plant in Bryansk and an airfield in Astrakhan. [46] [47]

Assessment: The bridge strike and Moscow salvo signal Ukrainian leverage as talks continue; Russian retaliation on energy is likely.

5. UKRAINE: Small advances on both sides

ISW confirmed small Russian advances near Lyman and Sloviansk and Ukrainian gains south of Novopavlivka, and found Russian infiltration in Kostiantynivka still unconsolidated. [38]

Assessment: The front is broadly static, with Russia's main effort on Kostiantynivka.

6. ROMANIA / BLACK SEA: Three merchant ships hit by drones off Romania

Drones struck three merchant vessels near Sulina and Sfantu Gheorghe on 13 and 14 November without casualties; Romania scrambled F-16s and is investigating the drones' origin. [48]

Assessment: Repeated strikes at the edge of NATO waters raise the chance of a Romanian Article 4 request.

7. UNITED KINGDOM: Suspected drones at RAF Lakenheath

US Air Force security forces responded to suspected drones at RAF Lakenheath on 13 November; no damage was reported and the origin is unknown. [49]

Assessment: This fits a pattern of probes against US bases in Europe.

8. EU / RUSSIA: EU forces board false-flag shadow fleet tanker

Kallas said EU forces boarded the falsely Zimbabwe-flagged MIDAG in the Mediterranean on 13 November, with new listings targeting the shadow fleet due on 16 November. [50]

Assessment: The EU is moving from listing ships to interdicting them.

9. GEORGIA: Tbilisi protests reach day 718

Protesters marked 718 consecutive days outside parliament, demanding new elections. [51]

Assessment: Protests continue at a low level without escalation.

Collection gap: ISW assessments for 13 and 15 November were unavailable, and nothing in-window was found on Moldova, Belarus or the Baltics.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. CHINA / US / RUSSIA: First Trump-Xi-Putin meeting confirmed for APEC

The White House confirmed Trump, Xi and Putin will hold their first three-way meeting on the APEC sidelines in Shenzhen, with the Iran war and nuclear arms on the agenda rather than Ukraine; an official said "We do not expect to get to any kind of agreement." [52] Reuters reports Trump meets regional leaders in Manila on 17 November and that Beijing's top priority is blocking a $14 billion Taiwan arms package Trump is reportedly holding as a "negotiating chip." [53]

Assessment: Low expectations plus a withheld arms package raise the risk that Taiwan or Korean issues are traded in Shenzhen.

2. PHILIPPINES / ASEAN: Summit opens without a South China Sea code; Myanmar exclusion kept

Marcos opened the 49th ASEAN Summit on 15 November; the foreign secretary said the South China Sea Code of Conduct is "80 per cent" done and will pass to Singapore's chairmanship. [54] [55] ASEAN will keep Myanmar out of key meetings despite Thai pressure. [56] [55] Activist groups reported about 300 protesters detained on 15 November. [54] Reported summit dates differ between 15-17 and 15-18 November. [57]

Assessment: Manila hands over the chair without its main maritime deliverable, and protests are likely to grow around Trump's arrival.

3. CHINA / ASEAN: Li Qiang praises "extraordinary" ties a day before Trump arrives

Li co-chaired the ASEAN-China summit on 16 November, citing more than $1 trillion in annual trade, and a Xinhua readout quotes him urging the bloc to "ward off interference." [58] Marcos called for disputes to be settled under international law. [59]

Assessment: Beijing is positioning itself as ASEAN's indispensable partner and framing the US presence as interference.

4. AUSTRALIA / PHILIPPINES: Defence arrangement signed; Beijing calls Manila a "troublemaker"

Philippine Defence Secretary Teodoro signed an arrangement to implement the visiting forces agreement covering maritime awareness and infrastructure, with Albanese present. [60] China's defence ministry called the Philippines "a troublemaker and a saboteur of peace" and said the PLA "remains on high alert." [61]

Assessment: The pact deepens the US-aligned network in the region, and Chinese pressure at sea is likely after the summits.

5. REGIONAL MARKETS: Taiex falls 4.1% as Nvidia fallout continues

On 16 November the Taiex fell 4.1%, the Kospi 2.8% and the Nikkei 1.6%, while the yen held near 159.64 with no reported intervention; Nvidia reports earnings on 17 November. [62]

Assessment: Nvidia's results and the yen near 160 are the near-term triggers for further volatility or Japanese intervention.

6. INDONESIA: Prabowo delays vice-presidential nominees until after his trip

Prabowo said he will name the two nominees after returning from abroad, and the MPR has formed verification and ad hoc committees for the 2 December vote; no names are confirmed. [63]

Assessment: Nominations are unlikely before about 20 November, keeping pressure on the rupiah.

7. KOREAN PENINSULA: Kim aide in Moscow; Seoul asks Beijing for help

KCNA says senior party secretary Jo Yong Won arrived in Moscow on 14 November for talks with Medvedev. [64] Seoul asked Beijing to help bring the North back to talks and said any summit with the US and Russia must not decide peninsula issues without it. [65] Rubio said Kim "just wants to survive in his regime." [66]

Assessment: Pyongyang is coordinating with Moscow before Shenzhen, and Seoul fears being bypassed.

8. JAPAN: Takaichi sets out economic agenda before Manila and Shenzhen

In a Philippine Star op-ed, Takaichi announced energy-resilience initiatives and said Japan will revise its three security strategy documents by year end, without mentioning Taiwan. [67]

Assessment: The economic framing aims to limit friction with Beijing, but the security revision is a coming flashpoint.

9. CAMBODIA: Francophonie summit opens amid Rwanda-DRC rift

Macron handed the chair to Cambodia in Phnom Penh, where leaders including Carney, Kagame and Tshisekedi gathered and choose a secretary-general on 16 November. [68] Reports conflict on whether the DRC withdrew its candidate; Kinshasa has threatened to quit if Rwanda's Mushikiwabo wins. [68] [69]

Assessment: The vote could fracture the organisation regardless of the outcome.

10. BANGLADESH / SOUTH ASIA: Awami League protests; Pakistani minister to visit India

Banned Awami League supporters clashed with police in Gopalganj on 15 November, and four members were arrested in Dhaka. [70] [71] Pakistan will send its climate minister to an SCO event in New Delhi on 20-22 November, the first such ministerial visit in about three years. [72]

Assessment: Street pressure in Bangladesh is likely to grow, while the Pakistani visit is a small thaw signal.

Collection gap: Taiwan MND daily counts for 14-16 November could not be retrieved; no North Korean launches, South China Sea incidents at sea, confirmed vice-presidential nominees or Myanmar ground reporting were found.

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES (ALASKA): Peltola's lead widens to about 11,200

Unofficial totals show Peltola at 49.94% and Sullivan at 45.96%, a margin of 11,213 and about 157 votes short of a first-round majority. [73] [74] The ranked-choice tabulation is on 18 November and certification is slated for 25 November. [73]

Assessment: Peltola is very likely to win.

2. UNITED STATES (MISSISSIPPI): All counties report; Colom leads by 7,587

The Secretary of State's count shows Colom at 49.3% and Hyde-Smith at 48.6%, a 7,587-vote margin, with about 73% of affidavit ballots accepted. [75]

Assessment: The result is settled; statewide certification is a formality.

3. UNITED STATES: Lame duck opens with funding deadline first

Congress returns facing the 11 December funding lapse, with hearings for Commerce Secretary Lutnick and Nvidia's Jensen Huang. [76]

Assessment: Funding will dominate the week.

4. UNITED STATES: New ICE detention site in Minnesota draws protests

More than 70 people held a vigil at the Appleton detention facility, a former private prison ICE bought in August, in its first weekend. [77]

Assessment: Expanding detention capacity is likely to bring more local protests.

5. MEXICO: Caravan resumes march as Sheinbaum heads to China

A caravan of about 1,200 people resumed its march from Escuintla, Chiapas. [78] Sheinbaum will meet Xi at APEC and repeated that Mexico will not use force against migrants. [79]

Assessment: The tariff threat likely stays rhetorical unless the caravan moves beyond southern Chiapas.

6. MEXICO: Félix Gallardo dies; arrest in Manzo killing; six killed in Silao

Guadalajara Cartel founder Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo died in hospital custody on 15 November. [80] Federal forces arrested a CJNG suspect in the 2025 killing of Uruapan mayor Carlos Manzo. [81] Security forces killed six alleged CJNG members in Silao, Guanajuato. [82]

Assessment: Federal pressure on the CJNG continues.

7. MEXICO: Sheinbaum seeks report on Zuany's whereabouts

Sheinbaum asked her security cabinet where former Chihuahua prosecutor Zuany is, after reports he is in the US; the outcome of his 13 November court hearing was not reported. [83]

Assessment: The CIA-in-Chihuahua affair remains a sovereignty irritant.

8. CANADA: Carney concludes Philippines trade talks

Carney announced concluded free-trade negotiations with the Philippines and pledged C$540 million to a rainforest initiative before heading to APEC. [84]

Assessment: Ottawa continues to diversify trade away from the US.

Collection gap: Nothing in-window on war-powers votes, the NDAA, border figures or Zuany's hearing.

SOUTHCOM.

1. COLOMBIA: Nine airstrikes and 69 dead in first 100 days; drone attacks spread

The Defence Ministry's 100-day report lists nine airstrikes killing 69, including the previously undisclosed 5 October Vichada strike. [85] A drone struck an army vehicle in Curumaní, Cesar, on 13 November, wounding a soldier. [86] Troops captured dissident leader "Primo Gay" in Antioquia. [87]

Assessment: Armed groups' drone attacks are spreading beyond Catatumbo, and more airstrikes are likely.

2. HAITI: Bukele shows armed robot dogs in stadium operation

Bukele posted video of rifle-mounted robot dogs in the operation to retake Sylvio Cator stadium, which he said will be a base for deeper pushes; the claims are his own. [88]

Assessment: The offensive will likely push into nearby gang-held neighbourhoods, with civilian displacement.

3. VENEZUELA: Fourth round of talks opens on prisoners and electoral reform

Government and opposition delegations opened talks in Caracas on 13 November covering 321 political prisoners counted by Foro Penal and electoral-body reform; Machado is excluded. [89] NPR reports Exxon is studying a return and Chevron plans $7 billion in investment. [90]

Assessment: Progress will likely be slow as US oil interests deepen.

4. CUBA: Peak deficit forecast at 1,860 MW

The grid operator forecast a 1,860 MW shortfall on 15 November, with Guiteras and three other units down. [91]

Assessment: The risk of a total grid collapse is high while Guiteras is out.

5. ECUADOR: Campaign under state of exception with main opposition barred

More than 51,000 candidates are campaigning for 29 November, with Revolución Ciudadana barred and Noboa on leave to campaign, according to Cuban state media. [92]

Assessment: Attacks on candidates are likely in coastal provinces.

6. PERU: Pope ends visit without incident

Pope Leo XIV was due to leave Lima for Rome on 16 November after stops in Chiclayo, Cusco and Pucallpa. [93]

Assessment: The visit ends peacefully.

7. GUATEMALA: State of siege in Zacapa and Izabal

Arévalo decreed a 30-day state of siege after attacks in Zacapa killed at least 17, including a congressman; Congress must ratify it within three days. [94] [95] The US Embassy barred its staff from both departments. [96]

Assessment: The ratification vote tests Arévalo's legislative support.

8. REGIONAL: No tropical development expected

The National Hurricane Center expects no tropical cyclone formation in the next seven days. [97] Collection gap: Nothing in-window on Colombian retaliation strikes, Haitian casualties, Brazil, Honduras or Nicaragua.
