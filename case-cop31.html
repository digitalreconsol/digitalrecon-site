case file 002 · travel threat assessment

COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye. 9–20 November 2026.

A threat assessment for people travelling to the UN climate conference: delegates, civil society, journalists, and corporate staff. Physical risk in Antalya looks moderate. The larger exposures are legal, logistical, and tied to the Iran war.

Original DRS analysis · public sources through 4 October 2026 · prepared 4 October 2026

bottom line

For most attendees, the risk of physical harm in Antalya is moderate. Three things set this event apart. First, a host agreement that puts a duty on attendees not to interfere in Türkiye’s internal affairs, set against a record of mass detentions before the July NATO summit. Second, air-travel disruption and tail risk from a US–Iran war that has no settlement. Third, a crowded field of unofficial booking and guide sites at an event the UN has already warned about for fraud. We rate overall risk moderate, rising to elevated for anyone planning public protest, advocacy outside UN-controlled zones, or reporting critical of the Turkish government.

DRS assessment. Confidence: moderate. Ratings are our judgment from the sources below, not an official finding.

what sets this event apart

Three findings that drive the rating.

1. Legal exposure for public expression

elevated
  • The host agreement, signed in June and made public on 25 September, says participants must “respect Türkiye’s laws and regulations and not to interfere in its internal affairs.” Whether conduct breaches this is “evaluated on a case-by-case basis, with close coordination between the country’s authorities and the UN climate change body.” [3]
  • The same agreement gives accredited attendees immunity for statements, writings, and actions connected to the conference, reaching beyond the UN-run Blue Zone in certain circumstances and continuing after the summit. The UN can waive it where it believes immunity would “impede the course of justice.” [3]
  • An official reportedly told lawmakers on 16 July that authorities would not “offer explicit security guarantees for public protests outside UN-controlled zones.” The Blue Zone is under UN jurisdiction; the Green Zone is under Turkish administrative authority. [5] The COP31 president-designate has said his team will “try to meet” activists’ requests. [4]
  • Precedent: before the NATO summit in Ankara on 7–8 July, overnight raids on 22–23 June detained at least 209 people, later counted at 225, including activists, lawyers, an academic, and a journalist, on terrorism-law allegations. Ankara then banned all public assemblies from 28 June to 10 July. [7][8]
  • The precedent reached Antalya. Its governorate banned protests, marches, and similar events across the province from 1 to 9 July because of the Ankara summit. [24]
  • The UK FCDO notes that insulting the President or Atatürk is a criminal offence, that it applies to online comments “no matter where you were or when you posted them,” and that penalties run from six months to three years. [10]

Rating: elevated for public expression, moderate for other attendees. Confidence: moderate. The facts are well sourced; whether the NATO pattern repeats for COP31 is our inference.

2. The Iran war and air travel

elevated disruptionlow–moderate direct threat
  • A ceasefire took effect on 8 April and a memorandum was signed on 17 June. Around 7–9 July the US struck 85 and then 90 Iranian targets, and Iran retaliated against US assets and Gulf infrastructure. Trump called the memorandum “over” on 9 July while saying talks could continue. [13][14]
  • As of 29 September, Iran had offered a seven-day plan and the US had rejected it. The Strait of Hormuz remains closed, and a vessel was struck there on 28 September. [12]
  • As of late August, nearly 3,500 flights across Europe and Türkiye had been disrupted. Services to Gulf cities remain suspended or reduced, Istanbul is the main rerouting hub, and Antalya is among airports with knock-on delays. Compensation under EC 261 is unlikely for conflict disruption. [15]
  • The UK FCDO tells travellers to prepare for cancellations and airspace closures and to avoid military facilities. [9] We found no reporting of Iranian strikes on Türkiye itself.

Rating: elevated for travel disruption, low to moderate for direct physical threat. Confidence: moderate on disruption, low on direct threat, because absence of reported attacks is not proof and our search was not exhaustive.

3. Terrorism and a high-profile gathering

moderate
  • The UK FCDO says terrorists “are likely to try to carry out attacks in Turkey,” most often in the southeast, Ankara, and Istanbul, at places visited by foreigners and at large gatherings. [10]
  • On 7 April three gunmen fired at least 24 shots in under a minute outside the Israeli consulate in Istanbul. One attacker was killed and two police officers were injured. Türkiye then detained nearly 200 alleged Islamic State suspects. [16]
  • On 23 May Türkiye and Syria detained 10 alleged ISIL members, including a man believed to be the group’s intelligence chief for Türkiye. [17]
  • COP31 is expected to draw nearly 120 heads of state and government, with leaders’ hotels kept confidential. [18] The US advisory is Level 2, advises avoiding crowded public spaces, and restricts the 10 km strip along the Syrian and Iraqi borders. It does not list Antalya as restricted. [11]

Rating: moderate. Confidence: moderate. We found no Antalya-specific 2026 incident, but we did not search Turkish-language sources.

Host-agreement duty, declined protest guarantees outside UN zones, and the NATO precedent, which included a province-wide protest ban in Antalya. Immunity for accredited attendees exists but can be waived. [3][5][7][8][24]

Air travel and regional conflict

elevateddisruptionlow–moderatedirect threat

Heavy flight disruption since spring, no settlement as of 29 September, and Gulf connections suspended or reduced. [12][15]

Terrorism

moderateconfidence: moderate

Persistent threat concentrated outside Antalya, an April attack in Istanbul, and a large leaders’ gathering as an attractive target. [10][16][18]

Crime, alcohol, and health

low–moderateconfidence: moderate

Street robbery and pickpocketing in tourist areas, drink spiking, counterfeit US currency, and methanol poisoning from illegally produced alcohol. Sexual-assault cases have been reported, many involving hotel or spa staff. The UN advises comprehensive international medical insurance. [10][1]

Fraud and information environment

moderateconfidence: moderate

UNFCCC warns of fraudulent messages that falsely claim its authority. Fake COP30 letters used a lookalike domain, unfccc-int.org, against the official @unfccc.int. [2] Independent booking sites state they are not affiliated with COP31 or UNFCCC, [20] while the presidency’s own site, cop31.tr, identifies itself as official and links to UNFCCC. [21] One hotel-market commentator reports November rates 25–40% above normal, an unverified estimate. [19]

Natural hazards

lowconfidence: low

Wildfire risk is high from April to October, so largely outside the event window. Earthquake risk is referenced by the FCDO but we did not assess it. [9]

Crowding and logistics

moderateconfidence: low

Provincial officials project 80,000 to 120,000 visitors from 196 countries, including more than 100 heads of state or government. Hotels near the venue are expected to be taken by delegations, and independent travellers may be pushed farther along the coast. Officials say more than 30,000 police, gendarmerie, health, and service staff will be deployed, with security planning coordinated with the UN Department of Safety and Security. Detailed arrangements are not public. [18][19][25]

by traveller profile

Exposure depends on what you plan to do.

low to moderate

Government and corporate delegates

Main risks are flight disruption, crowding, and fraud. Accredited attendees fall under the immunity provisions of the host agreement. [3]

elevated

Civil society and activists

Protest security was not guaranteed outside UN-controlled zones, and the NATO summit saw mass detentions and an assembly ban. A detained environmental activist received a prison sentence in July. [5][6][7]

elevated

Journalists

Media accreditation for COP31 has closed. [1] Rights groups document prosecutions of journalists. [3] NATO denied accreditation to some Turkish journalists. [8] In 2025 a BBC correspondent was expelled after detention and a Swedish journalist was detained. [23]

moderate

LGBT+ travellers

Same-sex activity is legal. Public displays of affection may attract unwanted attention, and Pride events have been banned or dispersed since 2015. The FCDO updated its LGBT+ information on 21 September. [10][9]

practical measures

What the evidence suggests doing.

General guidance drawn from government advisories and the UN. It is not legal or security advice.

  • Treat any COP31 message as unverified until checked. Official UNFCCC mail ends in @unfccc.int; query doubtful messages to secretariat@unfccc.int. [2]
  • Book through the accommodation platform UNFCCC links to, or a hotel you have verified directly. [1]
  • Apply for the free COP31 visa through Türkiye’s e-Visa system. It is required for every registered participant, including nationals who normally enter visa-free. [26]
  • Carry comprehensive international medical insurance. [1]
  • Plan for flight disruption: avoid a single Gulf-hub connection, add buffer days, and keep written airline confirmation of delay reasons. [15]
  • Decide before you travel what public expression you will take part in, and read the host-agreement terms as reported. Get advice from your organisation’s counsel if you plan activism or critical reporting. [3]
  • Avoid demonstrations and leave if one develops. Do not photograph military or official installations. [10]
  • Remember that insult laws reach online comments wherever and whenever posted. [10]
  • Drink only from licensed venues, because of methanol contamination. [10]
  • Enrol in your government’s traveller programme, such as STEP for US citizens. [11]

what would change this

Indicators to watch.

Would raise the rating

  • Talks collapsing into sustained strikes, or airspace closure notices affecting Türkiye or Gulf hubs.
  • An attack inside Türkiye claimed by or attributed to ISIS or another group, or a change to FCDO or State Department wording.
  • A ban on public assemblies in Antalya. For the NATO summit, Ankara’s ban began nine days before it opened and Antalya’s six days before. [8][24]
  • Detention sweeps of activists or journalists in late October. The Ankara raids came about two weeks before the summit. [7]

Would lower it

  • A signed US–Iran deal that reopens the Strait of Hormuz and regional airspace.
  • Published, specific protest areas with security guarantees.

The NATO timing is a single precedent and our inference. Treat it as a watch item, not a forecast.

gaps and conflicts

What we could not confirm.

  • Date conflict on the NATO crackdown. Climate Home News places it in January 2026. HRW and Amnesty give raids on 22–23 June, a ban from 28 June to 10 July, and a summit on 7–8 July. We use the rights groups’ dated documents. The 11 September article had not been corrected as of 4 October. [4][7][8]
  • Detention counts differ. HRW reports at least 209, later 225, with 178 in pretrial detention. Amnesty reports 225 with 103 remanded. The difference may reflect timing. [7][8]
  • TEMA Foundation numbers differ. SCF reports ten volunteers detained; HRW reports fourteen members. [5][7]
  • Attendance is a projection. Provincial officials gave 80,000 to 120,000 visitors on 23 September. A hotel-market commentator gives about 80,000, benchmarked against COP28. No registration count has been published. [18][19][25]
  • The host agreement text was not reviewed. Climate Home News links a copy posted by UNFCCC. Our account of its terms relies on that reporting. [3]
  • The US advisory is dated. It was issued on 9 June, before the July escalation, while the FCDO updated on 21 September. [9][11]
  • Not assessed: phone and internet controls, earthquake risk, and local transport. Turkish-language reporting was reviewed for assembly bans, detentions, and security planning only. Detailed security arrangements have not been published.

Method: government advisories, UN pages, rights-group reports, and news reporting. No paid databases, no imagery, and no contact with any party.

This assessment compiles public information for travel planning. It is not legal, medical, or security advice, and conditions can change quickly. Check current government advisories before you travel. Corrections welcome at contact.drs@digitalrecon.solutions.
