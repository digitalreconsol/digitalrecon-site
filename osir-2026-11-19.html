area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. ETHIOPIA / ERITREA: Both armies reportedly mass forces on the border

Reuters, citing ACLED and people tied to Tigray militias, reports a build-up by both armies along the border, with Ethiopian drones reportedly hitting Eritrean positions near Badme on 12 November and a clash on 13 November; Reuters could not confirm the skirmishes. [1] Eritrea's information minister dismissed Ethiopian claims as "propaganda," and Crisis Group warned the fighting "could trigger a direct conflict." [1]

Assessment: This is the strongest open-source signal yet of a possible interstate war.

2. SOMALIA / UAE: Abu Dhabi calls Mohamud the "outgoing" president

UAE adviser Anwar Gargash said the "outgoing Somali president's attack on the UAE reflects the shifting nature of his positions" and that "internal power struggles are dragging Somalia into regional disputes." [2] [3] "Outgoing" refers to a Supreme Court ruling that Mohamud's mandate has expired, which the government disputes. [3]

Assessment: By questioning Mohamud's legitimacy, the UAE has aligned itself with the Puntland and Jubaland opposition.

3. SUDAN: Army pushes toward al-Nuhud

Citing ACLED, The National reports the army and allies have retaken more than 30 villages and towns in North and West Kordofan since early November, and that RSF commander defections could leave the group vulnerable "as far as Darfur." [4]

Assessment: The advance on al-Nuhud makes RSF drone retaliation against the power grid more likely.

4. NIGERIA: State police bill reaches Tinubu; military claims ISWAP leader killed

Tinubu received the constitutional amendment creating state police after all 36 state assemblies approved it. [5] The military said airstrikes near Monguno and Arinna killed 21 ISWAP fighters, including a Shura Council member; the figures are unverified and do not reconcile. [6]

Assessment: State police would be the biggest change to Nigeria's security architecture since 1999.

5. SOUTH AFRICA: ANC, ActionSA, EFF and MK move toward metro coalition without the DA

The parties agreed on 18 November to build a common framework for Johannesburg, Tshwane, Ekurhuleni and eThekwini, though mayoral posts remain unresolved. [7] ActionSA said the DA "has never really had talks" with it. [8]

Assessment: A Gauteng deal without the DA would strain the national unity government.

6. SENEGAL: Ruling party expels President Faye

Pastef expelled President Faye and 31 others, leaving him without a parliamentary base while the party holds 130 of 165 seats; the budget standoff threatens IMF financing. [9]

Assessment: The split raises the risk of fiscal paralysis.

7. KENYA / LIBYA: Ruto pledges police accountability; HoR session outcome unreported

Ruto told Parliament that "where security officers use unlawful force… wrongdoing must be acknowledged" and cited KSh1.2 billion paid to protest victims. [10] Libya's House of Representatives called an emergency session in Benghazi after GNU-aligned forces blocked MPs from landing at Zintan; no outcome was reported by 1100Z. [11]

Assessment: Any move toward eastern autonomy would deepen Libya's split.

Collection gap: Nothing in-window was found on the Sahel, DRC fighting, new Ebola figures, the UN Security Council's AUSSOM discussions or South Sudan.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. IRAN / KUWAIT / BAHRAIN: Iran answers Qeshm strike with drones on Ali Al Salem and Bahrain

Iran's military said its drones targeted Patriot systems and launch platforms at Kuwait's Ali Al Salem air base on 18 November; Kuwait and Bahrain said they intercepted the attacks, with no damage reported. [12] [13] Army chief Hatami warned any new US action would meet a "more crushing, harsher response, without geographical limitation," and Iran claimed it destroyed a US MQ-9, which is unverified. [14] CENTCOM said no US personnel were injured this week, and the US Embassy in Bahrain issued a "heightened vigilance" alert; CENTCOM's commander met Israel's military and Mossad chiefs in Tel Aviv. [15] An unconfirmed Israel Hayom report says Trump is weighing strikes on nuclear and missile sites. [16]

Assessment: Iran is keeping up calibrated, mostly intercepted strikes, and the risk of a larger US-Israeli round has risen.

2. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: Houthi missile damages Riyadh tower housing PIF

Three sources told Reuters a Houthi missile damaged the King Abdullah Financial District tower housing the Public Investment Fund on 19 November, and a Reuters witness saw the district evacuated. [17] The coalition said initial assessment points to debris impact and that it intercepted five ballistic missiles toward the south. [18] Western officials told Reuters Riyadh expects the Houthi campaign to run into 2027. [15] No Saudi confirmation of damage to the East-West pipeline has been found.

Assessment: The Houthis are now hitting high-value economic targets in central Riyadh.

3. HORMUZ: New tanker strike off Khasab; vessel identities unclear

An unidentified projectile hit the engine room of an unnamed outbound tanker about 6 nm north of Khasab at 1645Z on 18 November, with all crew safe. [19] TASS separately reported a tanker attack 13 nm north of Khasab with one crew member missing, and accounts across incidents cannot be reconciled. [20] India says one Tara Gas seafarer remains missing as Omani search and rescue continues. [21] The US warned vessels taking Iranian cargo through ship-to-ship transfers could be seized. [22]

Assessment: Near-daily strikes off Musandam continue, and attribution remains unestablished.

4. IRAN / SHENZHEN: Trump and Xi back oil flows through Hormuz; Trump rejects outside help

Trump said he and Xi "strongly support oil flowing through the Strait of Hormuz" and told the summit "We're not going to let Iran have a nuclear weapon." [16] Before the meeting, he said the US does not need Chinese or Russian help to end the war. [23] He called the June memorandum with Iran "a waste of time" and said "I'm going to finish it off — soon." [24]

Assessment: Shenzhen produced supportive language on Hormuz but no mechanism, and Trump's rhetoric points to further escalation.

5. ISRAEL: Knesset advances three bills repealing judicial overhaul

The new majority passed preliminary readings of bills repealing the judicial selection amendment (67-47), the law weakening the attorney-general (63-47) and the broadcasting overhaul (63-49); each needs three more readings. [25] [26] Justice Minister Levin called it "hysterical and shameful." [26] Eisenkot, Bennett, Lapid, Liberman and Golan said they had made progress toward an Eisenkot-led "national emergency government," after Bennett met Netanyahu. [27] [28]

Assessment: The anti-Netanyahu majority is unwinding the overhaul before any government is formed.

6. GAZA: IDF raid beyond Yellow Line completed

The IDF said a tank-backed operation near Bureij and Nuseirat, west of the Yellow Line, to dismantle a tunnel was completed on 19 November; Hamas called it a "blatant ceasefire violation." [29] Anadolu reported demolitions north of Nuseirat and east of Maghazi. [30]

Assessment: The cross-line raid sets a precedent a day after warnings the truce could collapse.

7. IRAQ: Forces ordered into Jurf al-Sakhar before Friday deadline

Iraq's armed forces command ordered the army, federal police and PMF to take control of Jurf al-Sakhar, and Kataib Hezbollah said withdrawal "will not happen, not even over our dead bodies." [31] The prime minister postponed a Lebanon trip, citing security. [32] Reports conflict on whether a deal was reached with Quds Force chief Qaani. [33] [34]

Assessment: The risk of a clash around the 20 November deadline remains real, with a PMF swap the most-reported off-ramp.

8. ENERGY / REGIONAL: Brent near $92; Libya-Egypt talks; Pakistan raids

Brent stood at $92.20 on 19 November as Hormuz crude flows were reported back near pre-war levels and US stocks rose. [35] Treasury sanctioned a Hezbollah charity and a gold and cash network run with the Quds Force. [15] Libya's Dbeibah arrived in Cairo amid Egypt's objection to the Turkey-Libya maritime deal. [36] Pakistan's army said 22 TTP fighters were killed near the Afghan border. [37] A US deputy secretary of state discussed mining with Tajikistan's president. [38]

Assessment: Markets are treating the Gulf exchanges as contained.

Collection gap: No Iranian official sources, Saudi pipeline statement or official trilateral readout were available, and nothing in-window was found on Afghanistan.

EUCOM.

1. SHENZHEN: Trump and Putin discuss Ukraine; Zelensky calls it "a mistake"

Trump and Putin held their first in-person meeting since August 2025 before the trilateral; Ushakov said Ukraine was the main topic, that "neither side made any particularly important statements," and that Putin left in an "excellent mood." [39] [40] Reports of its length vary from 35-40 minutes to nearly an hour. [40] [41] Zelensky called the meeting without Ukraine "a mistake" and said US and Chinese trade pressure on Moscow would mean "the end of the war." [41] [42] US envoys Witkoff and Kushner are to brief Kyiv at a Coalition of the Willing summit in Berlin on 19 November with nearly 40 delegations. [43] [44]

Assessment: No Ukraine outcome had been reported by 1100Z; Berlin is the first venue for the US account of Shenzhen.

2. CRIMEA: Bridge hit for a third time; traffic suspended again

Aksyonov said traffic over the Crimean Bridge was "temporarily suspended" after a new attack just after midnight on 19 November. [45] [46] Kyiv Post reports both road and rail traffic halted, reversing reports that rail had resumed. [47]

Assessment: If rail is halted again, Russia's Kerch route is fully closed, pushing logistics onto the land corridor.

3. UKRAINE: Russia launches 98 drones and Oniks missiles; four killed in Odesa

Ukraine's Air Force said Russia launched 98 drones and Oniks missiles overnight, with 69 drones downed and impacts at 18 locations. [48] Four people were killed in central Odesa and 16 injured in Kharkiv Oblast. [49] ISW said neither side advanced on 18 November. [50]

Assessment: The Oniks launches and Odesa strike keep Black Sea ports a priority target.

4. RUSSIA: Drones hit Transneft station in Tatarstan; refining reportedly down sharply

Tatarstan authorities confirmed oil storage tanks near Almetyevsk were struck, identified by Astra as Transneft's Kaleykino pumping station. [47] Reuters' industry sources estimate refinery throughput has fallen 45% since late October, while Kyiv Post cites a lower 30% Reuters figure. [51] [47]

Assessment: Ukraine is deliberately cutting Russian fuel output while the talks proceed.

5. MOLDOVA: Russian ambassador summoned over drone incursions

Moldova summoned Russia's ambassador and issued a protest over seven drones that entered its airspace on 17 November. [52] Russia rejected the accusations, and no NATO statement was found. [53]

Assessment: Chisinau is likely to stop at diplomatic protest given its lack of counter-drone capability.

6. TURKEY: Imamoglu convicted and banned from politics

An Ankara court sentenced Istanbul Mayor Imamoglu to two years and one month and banned him from politics, pending appeal. [54]

Assessment: This is a major step toward removing the opposition's strongest challenger.

7. POLAND / EU: Reported false-flag plot; sanctions package next week

Rzeczpospolita, citing anonymous sources, reported that Poland foiled a Russian false-flag plot that could have caused mass casualties. [55] ISW assesses that Polish border deployments likely disrupted it. [50] Von der Leyen said the 22nd sanctions package will be presented "next week." [56]

Assessment: The unverified plot claim raises alert levels on the Polish and Baltic borders.

Collection gap: No White House or Chinese readout of the trilateral and nothing in-window on Georgia, the Baltics, the prisoner exchange or the Balkans was found.

INDOPACOM.

1. CHINA / US: Xi presses Trump on Taiwan; Xinhua says Trump disavowed independence

Xinhua called the Trump-Xi bilateral in Shenzhen "positive and constructive," said the two agreed to draft an action plan for a "strategically stable relationship," and reported Xi urging the US to do the "right thing" on Taiwan. [57] CNA reported that Xinhua also said Trump stated the US does not back Taiwan independence; no White House readout had been issued by 1005Z. [58] China's commerce ministry said a bilateral agriculture working group will meet "in the near future" while voicing "firm opposition" to recent US trade restrictions. [59] [60] The $14 billion Taiwan arms package remains unsigned. [61]

Assessment: The only readout is Beijing's, and the claimed Taiwan-independence statement is unverified until Washington issues its own.

2. CHINA / US / RUSSIA: First trilateral lasts about 90 minutes; no joint statement yet

TASS reported the Xi-Putin-Trump meeting ran from about 0827Z to 0957Z. [62] Xi said it had "groundbreaking significance," and Trump said the three could discuss reducing nuclear weapons and, on Ukraine, "Let's see if we can get something done." [60] Xi proposed three-way coordination on energy supply chains. [63] Ushakov said a joint statement was being drafted but might not be issued, and that Ukraine dominated a roughly 40-minute Trump-Putin meeting beforehand. [64] [60] No Trump-Kim meeting was confirmed, and no North Korean statement or launch was found in the window. [64]

Assessment: The meeting is so far more symbolic than substantive; any reference to arms control in the readouts is the key indicator.

3. BANGLADESH: Awami League lockdown begins after violence kills at least four

At least four people were killed in the two days before the banned Awami League's 19 November shutdown, with crude bombs and arson in 28 districts. [65] Crude bombs exploded in Dhaka before dawn, and Muhammad Yunus's ancestral home in Chattogram was set on fire without injuries. [66] [67] At least 37 Grameen Bank offices have been targeted since 10 November. [67] AFP reports 107 Awami League-linked arrests, while PTI cites 565 in a Dhaka drive; the figures may cover different scopes. [68] [66]

Assessment: Further deaths are likely on lockdown day, especially around Gopalganj.

4. TAIWAN: MAC plays down Taiwan's place in talks; Russian bombers enter ADIZ

Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council said "from what we know, Taiwan is not the focus" of the trilateral and had not responded to the Xinhua readout by 1005Z. [58] Two Russian Tu-95 bombers entered Taiwan's ADIZ, the second Russian entry this week, while the defence ministry counted only one PLA aircraft and two official ships. [69]

Assessment: Beijing kept military activity low around the summit while Russian flights served as a signal.

5. JAPAN: China snubs Takaichi; she meets Putin

Takaichi was placed in the back row of the APEC photo and is reportedly the only major leader without a separate Xi meeting; a Japanese official called the treatment "extremely rude." [70] She held 30 minutes of talks with Putin, the first Japan-Russia leader meeting since 2019, and has arranged a call with Trump. [70]

Assessment: The Japan-China freeze persists, and Tokyo fears being sidelined by the trilateral track.

6. APEC / AUSTRALIA: Xi pledges $20 million; Albanese-Xi talks Friday

Xi pledged $20 million for APEC cooperation and called for "open regionalism." [71] No leaders' declaration had been reported by 1100Z. Albanese meets Xi on 20 November and said the commitment to return the Darwin port lease to Australian hands stands. [72]

Assessment: Darwin port and farm-trade barriers are likely to frame the Albanese-Xi meeting.

7. REGIONAL MARKETS: Taiwan stocks lead losses; yen near 159

Reuters reported Taiwan's benchmark down 4.4% on AI-profitability worries, with the yen at 159.48 and a Fed governor floating a possible December hike. [73] AP reported the Nikkei up 0.1% and the Hang Seng down 1.9% in mid-session trading. [74]

Assessment: Taiwan tech is the main stress point, and the yen stays close to Tokyo's intervention threshold.

8. INDIA / PAKISTAN: Pakistani minister to attend Delhi SCO forum

Climate Minister Musadik Malik will lead Pakistan's delegation to the SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum in New Delhi on 20-22 November, the first visit by a Pakistani federal minister since May 2023. [75] [76] India says there is "no change in policy on Pakistan," and no bilateral meeting is scheduled. [76]

Assessment: This is low-level multilateral contact, not a resumption of talks.

9. PACIFIC: Papua New Guinea scales up drought aid

Papua New Guinea is expanding drought aid for at least 680,000 people as El Niño is forecast to peak in December. [77]

Assessment: Drought will strain Pacific governments into early 2027.

Collection gap: No US readout of either Shenzhen meeting or an APEC declaration was available by 1100Z; nothing in-window was found on the South China Sea, Indonesia's vice-presidential nominees or Myanmar.

area of responsibility

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES: Officials rule out cyberattack behind outages but say Iranian threats have risen

At the House Homeland Security hearing on 18 November, DNI Clayton said the outages "are not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack," and FBI Deputy Director Raia said there was "no indication whatsoever" of malicious cyber activity. [78] Clayton said Iranian threats to Americans "have substantially increased," while Raia called much of the dirty-bomb reporting "largely aspirational." [79] [78] Mullin's written testimony said Iran and its proxies have targeted US transportation, healthcare, water, energy and financial infrastructure since February, mostly with limited disruption. [80]

Assessment: The cyber question is settled for now, but the higher Iran threat rating will keep homeland-security pressure on Congress.

2. UNITED STATES: Shutdown risk rises as Congress leaves for Thanksgiving

Punchbowl reports that Johnson and Thune will not accept limits on Trump's Iran war powers, while Schumer and Jeffries will not back a funding deal without them; Thune called Iran restrictions "not a realistic ask." [81] Congress returns 30 November, leaving little time before the 11 December deadline. [81]

Assessment: With no movement and a lost week, the risk of a December shutdown is rising.

3. MEXICO / CANADA: Sheinbaum and Carney meet Trump in Shenzhen; no deals

Sheinbaum and Trump met for about 20 minutes; she said talks are "very far along" but gave no timeline, and neither side publicly raised the caravan tariff threat. [82] Carney also met Trump for just under 20 minutes and said a deal is possible "but we're not yet there," adding: "if the U.S. doesn't want to do the deal, we're busy." [83] [84] US tariffs on steel, aluminium and autos remain in place. [84]

Assessment: Both neighbours are signalling alternatives rather than concessions, and the caravan-linked tariff threat remains the main near-term risk for Mexico.

4. MEXICO: CJNG cell leader "El Topo" arrested in Zapopan; caravan count disputed

Federal forces arrested Los Deltas leader "El Topo," wanted for the kidnapping of a journalist, in Zapopan on 18 November, and three gunmen were killed. [85] The Navy later gave a running total of eight detainees. [86] An NGO counted 1,500 to 2,000 migrants between Escuintla and Mapastepec, more than double the official figure of 781. [87]

Assessment: CJNG retaliation against federal forces in Jalisco is a near-term risk.

5. UNITED STATES: New York challenges ICE tent facility; Alaska tabulation unseats five Republicans

Governor Hochul's administration demanded answers about an $8.7 million ICE tent annex for up to 100 women at Batavia, saying the state was not consulted. [88] Alaska's ranked-choice tabulation unseated five Republican legislators, one by three votes, and bipartisan coalitions keep both chambers. [89]

Assessment: Detention expansion is likely to generate further state-federal litigation.

Collection gap: No in-window ICE use-of-force incident was verified, no US readout of the Carney or Sheinbaum meetings was found, and nothing was collected on the Bahamas.

SOUTHCOM.

1. VENEZUELA: Hegseth makes unannounced Caracas visit and signs military cooperation deal

Hegseth met interim President Delcy Rodríguez at Miraflores on 18 November and called the visit "a reestablishment of our defense partnership" focused on counternarcotics; Rodríguez said the deal allows US training of Venezuela's armed forces. [90] Al Jazeera reports it covers training, intelligence sharing, equipment and border security, with terms unpublished. [91] The judicial nominations committee received more than 1,200 applications for Supreme Court seats and will publish a list on 20 November. [92]

Assessment: The agreement formalises US security influence in Caracas and could support operations along the Colombian border.

2. COLOMBIA: Hegseth delivers first C-130; both sides deny report of rejected joint operations

Hegseth delivered the first C-130 in Bogotá on 18 November and said Medellín will host the next Shield of the Americas summit in 2027. [93] The presidency and Hegseth denied a New York Times report that President De la Espriella rejected US airstrikes and joint ground operations. [94] [93] La Opinión reports the government will ask Congress to authorise foreign troops and that Hegseth's tour ends in Ecuador. [93]

Assessment: Because foreign troops need congressional approval, joint ground operations are not imminent.

3. HAITI: Salvadoran helicopters destroy gang leader's home

Bukele said his troops destroyed the home of Taliban gang leader Jeff Larose in Canaan on 18 November and posted video of helicopters firing on it; it is unclear whether Larose was killed, and the UN-backed Gang Suppression Force said it was not involved. [95]

Assessment: Salvadoran operations are popular but run outside the UN-backed framework, raising civilian-harm concerns.

4. GUATEMALA: 21 arrests in first three days of state of siege

The interior ministry reported 21 arrests in Zacapa and Izabal from 15 to 17 November and screened 2,500 people, but named no detainees or links to the attacks that prompted the siege. [96]

Assessment: Modest early results leave the government open to pressure.

5. ECUADOR: Flooding may force local suspensions of 29 November vote

The electoral council said seven polling sites remained flooded and voting could be suspended in specific localities. [97] The interior minister said 57,470 police will secure the election. [98]

Assessment: Flooding and violence add logistical and security risks, and local postponements are possible.

6. CUBA / BRAZIL: Grid deficit near 1,800 MW; Senate passes fiscal package

The state utility forecast 1,040 MW available against 2,800 MW of peak demand on 18 November, with up to 1,790 MW of cuts. [99] Brazil's Senate passed Lula's fiscal package of about $10 billion, which now goes to the lower house; Lula has still not named a finance minister. [100]

Assessment: Cuba's grid remains at risk of another collapse, and Brazil's market doubts will persist until a finance minister is named.

Collection gap: The National Hurricane Center page returned a stale 4 November issuance; nothing in-window was found on Venezuela's negotiating table, Peru security, Honduras or Panama.
