area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. SOMALIA: Mandate dispute turns violent as al-Shabaab exploits it

Critical Threats reports the government removed the Chief Justice after the Supreme Court ruled the president's term extension unlawful, while militias seized facilities in Beledweyne and forces loyal to a former state leader attacked Baidoa. [1] Al-Shabaab has struck a deal with some local factions as Uganda begins withdrawing about 2,000 troops. [1] Government-opposition talks are due from 14 or 15 November. [1] [2]

Assessment: Armed disputes in two member states are opening space for al-Shabaab as foreign forces draw down.

2. SUDAN: Army takes North Kordofan highway; RSF opens Blue Nile front

The army retook um Sumeima and advanced into West Kordofan, while the RSF took Sali and al Keili and retook Bakori in Blue Nile. [1] An advocacy group reports an RSF drone strike killed nine civilians in Kurmuk on 10 November. [3]

Assessment: The army has momentum in Kordofan, but the Blue Nile front could pull forces away.

3. DRC: Ebola passes 11,100 cases as attacks on responders mount

Africa CDC counted four armed attacks, 16 assaults on responders and 11 health facilities targeted, with more than 1,000 contacts lost to follow-up; government data show 11,114 cases and 5,433 deaths. [4] WHO's Director-General cited "almost 11,000" cases on 11 November. [5]

Assessment: Insecurity is now the main barrier to containment.

4. DRC: UN adds Kabila's FRN to M23 sanctions listing

The UN sanctions committee listed the FRN as an alias of the Alliance Fleuve Congo, extending the asset freeze and arms embargo; OCHA estimates more than 323,000 people were displaced in Kalehe and Kabare. [6] The army says it stopped the FRN advance at Bunyakiri. [7]

Assessment: The listing may deter some backers but does not change the balance in Kalehe.

5. ETHIOPIA: Fighting reported near the Eritrean border

An advocacy group reports artillery fire near Badme, clashes at Rama and Sheraro, and that adviser Getachew Reda accused Eritrea and Sudan of arming insurgents; its reporting is not fully verified. [3]

Assessment: The risk of an Ethiopia-Eritrea war is rising.

6. SOUTH AFRICA: ANC executive backs Ramaphosa and turns to council deals

At the special NEC meeting, no one called for Ramaphosa to resign, and members debated a DA coalition, deals with smaller parties, or opposition; councils must seat by 26 November. [8]

Assessment: Municipal coalitions are likely to be unstable, straining Gauteng's metros.

Collection gap: No verified in-window reporting on the Sahel, Nigeria, Libya or South Sudan, and no new AFRICOM strike releases confirmed.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. IRAN / BAHRAIN: IRGC claims it destroyed a US radar in Bahrain after a strike in southern Iran

The IRGC said its aerospace force destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, home of the US Fifth Fleet, in retaliation for a 12 November air attack on a telecoms tower in Sirik county, Hormozgan. [9] [10] It called the strike a "serious warning" over "aerial provocations and oil smuggling" and threatened a response "without geographical limits." [11] IRNA reported an "enemy" strike on Sirik without naming the attacker. [12] The Times of Israel calls it Iran's first such claim against US assets in a Gulf state since early September. [13] There was no US or Bahraini comment, no independent confirmation and no casualty figure by about 0940Z. [9] [10]

Assessment: If confirmed, this is the most serious direct Iran-US exchange in months; the US and Bahraini response is the key indicator for the next 24 hours.

2. US / IRAN: Trump says no deal is needed and he could strike "right now"

Trump said Washington no longer needs an agreement with Iran, told Vance to focus on domestic issues, and said five Gulf states had asked him to postpone broader attacks. [9] [14] In a radio interview he said "it's possible I would do that right now" if things went "the other way" and claimed the US "totally" controls Hormuz. [15]

Assessment: Washington has shifted from seeking a deal to relying on the blockade, with Gulf states as the main restraint; together with the radar claim, the risk of renewed US strikes has risen.

3. IRAN: Assembly of Experts calls NPT withdrawal review an "inevitable necessity"

The Assembly of Experts urged a review of withdrawal from the NPT and of defence doctrine, and a wartime economic headquarters under the Leader's office. [16] The body has no direct authority to withdraw, and the Foreign Ministry still affirms Iran's membership. [14]

Assessment: This is signalling meant to raise the cost of strikes; a parliamentary bill or Supreme National Security Council action would be the real escalation.

4. HORMUZ: IRGC claims an LNG tanker hit a mine

The IRGC navy said an LNG tanker hit a mine and exploded on 13 November while using an "unauthorised and dangerous route"; no vessel name or independent confirmation was available. [17] UKMTO issued two new warnings after a projectile hit a tanker's engine room off Ras Al Khaimah on 11 November. [18] Washington Institute data cited by Reuters count at least 10 ships hit in the first 10 days of November. [17]

Assessment: Attacks on shipping continue at a high rate despite US claims of control, and Iran appears to be enforcing its own transit rules.

5. ENERGY: Brent near $96 after Thursday's jump

Brent rose about 3.3% on Thursday after Houthi claims of attacks on Saudi energy sites, and traded at $96.04 at about 0912 GMT on Friday, still heading for a weekly fall of about 2.3%. [19] [20] US crude stocks rose 4.7 million barrels against an expected draw. [19] These prices predate the IRGC radar claim.

Assessment: The market is still pricing a contained conflict; a confirmed and answered attack on a US asset would likely push Brent toward $100.

6. ISRAEL: Liberman's committee vote delayed as Iran oversight fight grows

Likud blocked the vote to install Liberman as Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee chair, delaying it to 16 November and leaving the incoming majority without access to classified briefings. [21] Defence Minister Katz said the caretaker government needs no opposition approval for operations against Iran; Liberman said a caretaker government may not move toward war without parliamentary oversight. [22] Netanyahu briefed Eisenkot on 12 November, and Lapid said the bloc will form a government after "painful compromises." [22]

Assessment: The 16 and 17 November votes are the next chances for the Knesset to check a caretaker decision on Iran.

7. GAZA: 53 states urge winter aid as infant dies of cold

The UK, Qatar and Jordan led 53 countries and organisations at the UN in urging Israel's incoming government to admit shelter, fuel and medical supplies. [23] A nine-month-old died of hypothermia in Khan Younis; Netanyahu said Israel had allowed in 91,000 tents. [24] Palestinian media reported one killed in a strike near Shifa Hospital. [22]

Assessment: Winter conditions are making aid a diplomatic test for the next Israeli government.

8. PAKISTAN: Car bomb repelled in Mastung; army says 74 militants killed in 10 days

Police said forces repelled a vehicle-bomb attack on a post in Mastung, Balochistan, killing three attackers; two civilians died and 16 people were wounded. [25] The army said it killed 74 militants in 10 days in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan and again alleged Afghan and Indian backing. [26]

Assessment: High-tempo operations and accusations of outside backing keep Pakistan-Afghanistan tension high.

Collection gap: No in-window Lebanon or Iraq incidents confirmed; nothing new on Syria, Yemen beyond Houthi claims, Egypt, Jordan, Afghanistan or Central Asia; no US or Bahraini statement on the radar claim.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. RUSSIA / US: Putin meets Witkoff and Kushner; talks to continue with no change in terms

The Kremlin meeting began about 1229Z on 12 November with Ushakov, Medinsky and Dmitriev present. [27] Witkoff said "We agreed to another meeting" and suggested Europeans would join, but Ushakov played down any European role. [28] ISW reports Putin restated that the borders of all four annexed oblasts are the only acceptable line and offered a "humanitarian mission" in Kostiantynivka, which ISW calls performative. [29] Kyiv Post reports Putin said European troops deployed after a deal would be "legitimate military targets." [30]

Assessment: The process continues, but Moscow's terms are unchanged and its line already undercuts the US claim that Europe will join.

2. UKRAINE / US: Zelensky says Russia "accepted" the largest prisoner exchange; no numbers released

Zelensky met Witkoff and Kushner in Dublin and called the agreed swap "the biggest exchange of war prisoners," without a figure; earlier he cited talks on 1,000 or 1,500 each. [31] [32] He said Europeans will "have a voice" in next week's talks. [31]

Assessment: The final numbers expected on 12 November did not appear, and the swap's scale and European participation remain Ukrainian claims.

3. EUROPE: EPC summit backs a European seat but not a spokesperson

Leaders agreed Europe must have a voice in talks but remain split over who should represent it, and von der Leyen previewed a 22nd sanctions package for the 26-27 November EU summit. [33] [34] The Irish Times reports the UK announced sanctions on all ten major Russian oil producers. [31]

Assessment: Without an agreed envoy, Europe may struggle to use any seat Washington offers.

4. UKRAINE / US: Zelensky says Starshield service throttled again

Zelensky told the summit that new speed caps on SpaceX's Starshield service in occupied areas stop interceptors from catching jet-powered Shaheds; Trump and Hegseth deny ordering any change, and the European Commission urged SpaceX to restore service. [34] [29]

Assessment: The throttling directly weakens Ukraine's defence against jet-Shahed attacks.

5. UKRAINE: 117 drones overnight; power cut in Rivne region

Ukraine's Air Force says Russia launched 117 drones, 63 of them jet-powered, downing or suppressing 80, with hits at 24 locations. [35] [36] Parts of Rivne region lost power, a ballistic missile reportedly struck Kyiv, and one person was killed in Kherson. [35]

Assessment: Strikes on western Ukraine's energy grid continue at the same pace during the talks.

6. RUSSIA: Moscow claims 1,198 Ukrainian drones; Volgograd refinery hit

Russia's MoD claimed 1,198 drones downed overnight, its second-highest total, including 467 heading for Moscow region. [37] The Volgograd governor reported four dead in the southern industrial zone, and Kyiv Post reports a fire at Lukoil's refinery there. [38]

Assessment: Ukraine's refinery campaign matches Russia's energy strikes and gives Kyiv leverage in the talks.

7. UKRAINE: ISW assesses no confirmed advances

ISW recorded no confirmed advances on 12 November and rejects Russia's claim to hold all of Kostiantynivka, citing at least 41 observed Ukrainian positions. [29]

Assessment: The Kostiantynivka "humanitarian mission" rests on a control claim ISW disputes.

8. MOLDOVA: Unidentified drone debris found

Police found drone debris in vineyards in Ștefan Vodă district; the type and origin are unknown, a day after two Russian missiles crossed Moldovan airspace. [39]

Assessment: Spillover from Russian strikes on southern Ukraine continues.

9. GEORGIA: Kobakhidze repeats "Global War Party" claims; election bill advances

Pre-window onset. In Dublin, Kobakhidze said real people stand behind the "Global War Party" and held no reported meetings with EU leaders. [40] Election Code amendments that let the electoral commission determine results faster and abolish small recounts passed first reading 79-10. [41]

Assessment: The changes would tighten the ruling party's control over certifying results.

Collection gap: No official swap numbers, no consolidated casualty total for the overnight strikes, no Kremlin transcript, and nothing in-window on Belarus, Turkey, the Balkans or new Baltic incidents.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. INDONESIA: MPR sets 2 December vote on Gibran's successor

MPR Speaker Ahmad Muzani said a plenary on 2 December will elect and inaugurate the new vice president, adding "The sooner, the better." [42] Prabowo has 14 days from the MPR's formal request to name two nominees from the parties that backed the 2024 ticket, and minister Yusril said the president has "full authority" over the choice; no candidates have been officially named. [42]

Assessment: A short, rules-based timetable should limit uncertainty; a coalition fight over the two names is the main risk.

2. INDONESIA: Rupiah holds near its record low

The rupiah opened at Rp18,225 per dollar on 13 November and edged to Rp18,230, with the vice-presidential vacancy cited as a drag on sentiment; CNBC Indonesia puts Thursday's intraday low at Rp18,255, so Rp18,225 was the record closing level. [43] Bank Indonesia is expanding renminbi clearing arrangements. [43]

Assessment: The 2 December timetable should cap further losses, but the currency stays exposed to a surprise in the nominations.

3. UNITED STATES / TAIWAN: Nvidia's Huang resigns; Asian chip stocks steady

Nvidia said Jensen Huang resigned as CEO and director with immediate effect, naming CFO Colette Kress interim CEO; earnings remain set for 17 November. [44] Press reports tie the exit to a relationship with a former Megaspeed executive whom US intelligence reportedly judged to be collecting information for China, and say Huang has agreed to testify to the House Foreign Affairs Committee. [45] [46] The Taiex closed up 1.35% on 13 November after Nvidia fell 4.3%, while SoftBank Group dropped 5.2%. [47] [48]

Assessment: The leadership question is settled for now, but the China-espionage angle could shape US chip-export policy before APEC.

4. JAPAN: Finance minister warns of "one-sided and rapid" yen moves

Finance Minister Katayama said she was seeing one-sided, rapid moves and would coordinate with the US Treasury, declining to comment on intervention, with the dollar near 159.91 yen. [49] [48]

Assessment: Escalation to the minister and a public mention of the US Treasury make intervention above 160 likely.

5. CHINA / TAIWAN: Xi calls reunification inevitable as PLA mounts readiness patrol

Pre-window onset. Xi said reunification "must be realised" and offered "one country, two systems" without mentioning force. [50] Taiwan reported 29 PLA aircraft and warships on a joint combat readiness patrol from 12 November, and Taipei rejected the offer. [51] The previous day's count was 7 aircraft and 7 naval ships. [52]

Assessment: The speech-and-patrol pairing is standard messaging a week before the Xi-Trump meeting at APEC, and the "peaceful" framing suggests Beijing wants no crisis before the summit.

6. NORTH KOREA: Foreign minister rejects G7 denuclearization call

KCNA carried Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui's statement that the North's nuclear status "can never be a subject of Western countries' debate," amid speculation about a Trump-Kim meeting around APEC. [53] North Korea also changed its flag's proportions for the first time since 1948, without explanation. [54]

Assessment: Pyongyang is setting its nuclear status as the premise of any talks with Trump.

7. JAPAN / CHINA: Takaichi to visit China for APEC, but a Xi meeting is "very unlikely"

Takaichi travels to Manila and Shenzhen from 16 to 19 November; Beijing still refuses ministerial talks over her 2025 Taiwan remarks, and Tokyo has sought a meeting with Putin and a Quad session at ASEAN. [55]

Assessment: Any encounter with Xi in Shenzhen will be the clearest signal on China-Japan relations.

8. PHILIPPINES / MYANMAR: Lawmakers urge barring junta from Manila summit

Rep. Leila de Lima and Akbayan lawmakers filed a House resolution urging the government to keep Myanmar junta representatives out of the ASEAN summit and to enforce the Five-Point Consensus. [56]

Assessment: ASEAN is divided on Myanmar going into summit week, testing Manila's chairmanship.

9. BANGLADESH: Hasina questions 2024 uprising in Indian interview

In an IANS interview, ex-Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina said violence and extremism have grown since 2024 and asked, "Is this the Bangladesh you wanted?"; her claims are unverified. [57] No new violent incidents were confirmed in the window; the latest found was tyre-burning by Awami League activists in Chattogram on 10 November. [58]

Assessment: Hasina's messaging from India will keep supporters mobilised and strain Dhaka-New Delhi ties.

Collection gap: Taiwan's 13 November MND report, Indonesian coverage of vice-presidential candidates and several Japanese and Myanmar sources did not load; no confirmed South China Sea incident, little on India-Pakistan, and no coverage of Australia or the Pacific islands.

area of responsibility

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES (ALASKA): Peltola's lead narrows to 3,975

Sullivan took 2,836 of 5,578 newly reported votes, cutting Peltola's lead to 3,975; with 86% counted she leads 48.9% to 47.3%. [59] Ballots mailed from overseas can arrive until 18 November, the day of the ranked-choice tabulation, and the state targets certification on 25 November. [60]

Assessment: Peltola still leads but is about a point short of 50%, so ranked-choice transfers on 18 November will likely decide the race.

2. UNITED STATES: ICE reports record arrests and deportations

ICE reported 483,200 deportations in fiscal 2026, up 9%, and 474,850 arrests over 12 months, roughly double the year before; the release does not say how many had criminal records. [61]

Assessment: The figures will feed the lame-duck fight over DHS funding, and the missing criminal-history data will fuel disputes over targeting.

3. MEXICO / US: Trump threatens to double tariffs over Chiapas caravan

Trump said tariffs on Mexican goods "will be IMMEDIATELY DOUBLED" unless Mexico stops a migrant caravan that left Tapachula on Tuesday; reported sizes range from more than 1,000 to nearly 2,000. [62] Sheinbaum said Mexico will not use force and is offering migrants a safe return. [62]

Assessment: The threat adds pressure to live tariff talks, and Mexico will likely disperse the caravan through attrition and return offers.

4. MEXICO / US: State Department clears $1.075 billion Black Hawk sale

The State Department approved a possible sale of 11 UH-60M helicopters to Mexico, with deliveries expected in 2028-29; Mexico frames it around disaster relief, Washington around counter-drug airlift. [63]

Assessment: The sale shows security cooperation continuing despite the Chihuahua CIA dispute.

5. MEXICO: Army clash with suspected CJNG gunmen in Ocotlán

Two attackers were killed and a soldier and a woman wounded in Ocotlán, Jalisco; troops seized a Barrett rifle, a machine gun, grenades and an improvised armoured vehicle. [64]

Assessment: Ocotlán remains a recurring army-cartel flashpoint.

6. MEXICO: Sheinbaum to attend APEC, rules out Trump meeting in China

Sheinbaum will attend APEC in Shenzhen on 17-19 November with bilateral meetings planned with China, Canada, Australia and Vietnam; she said she would "probably" meet Trump at the December G20 instead. [65]

Assessment: A Sheinbaum-Xi meeting is likely to draw US criticism given concern over Mexico-China trade.

7. CANADA: Carney holds seven bilateral meetings at EPC Dublin

Carney met the leaders of Cyprus, Denmark, France, Greece, Ireland, Latvia and Sweden and joined a Ukraine security session, announcing no new commitments. [66]

Assessment: Substantive announcements are more likely on the Asia leg.

Collection gap: No in-window reporting on Mississippi county certification, war-powers or funding action, or Zuany's 13 November hearing; ADN and The Hill pages did not load.

area of responsibility

SOUTHCOM.

1. COLOMBIA: Drone and sniper attack on Tibú battalion kills four soldiers

Attackers dropped about 20 explosives from drones and used snipers against an army battalion at Barco La Silla, Tibú, killing at least four soldiers; EFE says five were wounded, Europa Press two. [67] [68] The ELN and FARC dissident Frente 33 contest the area, and military sources cited by Caracol Radio point to the ELN. [67] [68] President De la Espriella called the attackers "narcoterroristas." [67]

Assessment: This looks like retaliation for the recent Catatumbo strikes; further bombing in the region is likely.

2. COLOMBIA: Unannounced October bombing reported in Vichada

Anonymous military sources say an operation dropped two 400 kg bombs near a security ring around FARC dissident leader Iván Mordisco in Cumaribo, Vichada, on 5 October, killing two; community members found about 48 impacts but no bodies, and the government has not commented. [69]

Assessment: If confirmed, the strike count rises to nine and questions grow over how fully the campaign is disclosed.

3. HAITI: Police and Salvadoran forces retake national stadium

Haitian police and Salvadoran forces took the Sylvio Cator stadium on 12 November; Bukele said troops found human skulls, that the stadium will become an operating base, and told gangs to leave before "the offensive." [70]

Assessment: Salvadoran operations now look like a lasting, separate campaign, and a larger push into Port-au-Prince is likely.

4. VENEZUELA: US report details $12 billion in managed oil revenue

A heavily redacted State Department report says more than $12 billion in Venezuelan oil and mineral revenue was disbursed from January to September, mostly for currency support, with recipients unnamed. [71] Separately, Lula and acting President Delcy Rodríguez agreed to restore full diplomatic relations and review trade. [72]

Assessment: The redactions are likely to draw congressional scrutiny, while Brazil's normalisation strengthens Rodríguez regionally.

5. CUBA: Peak deficit forecast rises to 1,890 MW

The grid operator forecast a 1,890 MW shortfall against 2,700 MW of peak demand on 12 November, with Guiteras and three other units broken down and three more in maintenance. [73]

Assessment: With about 70% of peak demand unserved, island-wide blackouts will continue.

6. ECUADOR: Local campaign opens after two candidates killed

Campaigning for the 29 November elections opened on 12 November, after former Huaquillas mayor Ronald Farfán and council candidate Cristian Rugel were killed this week. [74]

Assessment: Violence and prosecutions of opposition figures are likely to dominate the campaign.

7. PERU: Pope denounces machismo; visit peaceful so far

Pope Leo XIV condemned machismo and violence against women in Lima, with no security incidents reported. [75]

Assessment: Crowds remain the main public-order concern through 16 November.

8. REGIONAL: No tropical development expected

The National Hurricane Center expects no tropical cyclone formation in the next seven days. [76] Collection gap: Venezuelan blackout protests could not be verified, the UN Haiti human rights report did not load, and nothing new was found on Guatemala or Venezuela's TSJ.
