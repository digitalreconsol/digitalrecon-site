area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. SOMALIA: AFRICOM confirms US troops left Baledogle airfield

AFRICOM's deputy commander said US personnel withdrew from Baledogle, mostly to Mogadishu, calling it "100% an economic decision" over runway repair costs; airstrikes and support continue. [1]

Assessment: The move shrinks the US training footprint outside Mogadishu as the army faces reported desertions.

2. SOMALIA: Government and opposition reportedly agree security principles before forum

Security chiefs and opposition Future Council leaders, including Sharif Sheikh Ahmed, reportedly agreed not to politicise security and that elections must rest on consensus; no joint statement was published. [2]

Assessment: This improves the chance the opposition attends the 14 November forum.

3. DRC: ADF kills at least 12 civilians in Mambasa

ADF fighters attacked four villages in Ituri's Mambasa territory on 11 November, killing at least 12 and bringing deaths in the area since Sunday to about 23. [3]

Assessment: Rising ADF violence adds pressure on the army in an Ebola-affected province.

4. DRC: Violence disrupts Ebola response

Youths burned a health centre treating Ebola patients in Lolwa, Ituri, and militia clashes halted response work around Fataki; the count stands at 11,042 cases and 5,387 deaths. [4]

Assessment: Insecurity is directly stopping response work.

5. SOUTH AFRICA: ANC executive meets on coalitions as DA meets ActionSA

The ANC NEC meets on 12 November to set its coalition approach; Lesufi, the ANC Youth League and MK oppose working with the DA, which meets ActionSA on Thursday. [5]

Assessment: A bloc excluding the DA is gaining ground across Gauteng.

6. SOUTH SUDAN: Envoys warn December election risks the peace deal

AU envoy Kikwete told the Security Council that if the 22 December vote is mishandled "you cannot contemplate what would happen"; the UN envoy said only Kiir's party shows real electoral activity. [6]

Assessment: The risk of election violence is rising.

7. KENYA: Mandera ambush follow-up; IEDs cleared

The attackers took two rifles from the reservists killed in the Mandera ambush, and Kenyan troops destroyed two IEDs in Lafey on 11 November. [7]

Assessment: Al-Shabaab IED activity on the border continues.

Collection gap: Nothing new and verified in-window on Sudan, Ethiopia-Eritrea, the Sahel, Libya, the FRN offensive or Hawadle desertions. Sudan Tribune, Addis Standard and Hiiraan did not load.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. ISRAEL / IRAN: Security cabinet orders IDF to step up preparations for possible attack on Iran

KAN reported that the security cabinet under caretaker PM Netanyahu ordered the military to intensify preparations for a possible attack on Iran, with directives that "could lead it to carry out activities that could trigger a war"; scope and timing were not disclosed. [8] [9] Eisenkot wrote to Netanyahu that "only 46 Knesset members still support your continued tenure" and warned against deploying forces without "a direct and immediate threat." [8] The Jerusalem Post reports, from a single source, tension between Netanyahu and the IDF chief over Iran. [10]

Assessment: This is the most significant escalation signal in the window: a caretaker government is preparing a unilateral option while US-Iran diplomacy is stalled.

2. IRAN / US: Ghalibaf says "ball is in the US court"; Vance channel reported, then denied

Speaker and lead negotiator Ghalibaf said Washington has not answered Iran's seven conditions and must "make and announce" a decision; Tehran has rejected moving its 60% stockpile abroad. [11] [9] The Wall Street Journal, via Times of Israel, reports Trump authorised Vance to resume talks and that Vance is weighing direct contact with Rezaei, who called reports of talks "lies and slander." [12] Rezaei said an attack on "Pickaxe Mountain" would make all US bases targets, and Trump said at Arlington he wants "total victory." [13]

Assessment: Both sides are publicly placing the next move on the other, and no formal US reply was reported.

3. HORMUZ / OMAN: New mine claim; Iran reportedly attacked navigation systems on Omani island

Iran's army said a tanker hit a mine and caught fire after entering a "prohibited area," and UKMTO logged a separate tanker incident. [14] [15] Three sources told Reuters that Iran attacked navigation systems on Oman's Didamar island; Oman did not comment. [15]

Assessment: An attack on Omani territory widens Iran's target set and could strain Muscat's neutral role.

4. ENERGY: Brent gives back Wednesday's gain

Brent settled at $92.76 on 11 November, then fell about 1.5% to $91.22 early on 12 November as Middle East output rose. [9] [14]

Assessment: A confirmed Israeli or US strike decision would reverse this quickly.

5. ISRAEL: Eisenkot bloc wins first Knesset vote and key committees

The plenum approved the bloc's Arrangements Committee 63-50, and the committee made Liberman temporary chair of Foreign Affairs and Defense; Likud accused the bloc of "cooperating with supporters of terrorism." [16] [10] UTJ MK Eliakim Stark said cooperation with any MKs, including Arab-led parties, can be legitimate. [17]

Assessment: The 63-50 vote is the first hard test of the anti-Netanyahu majority, and Liberman's post gives it oversight of Iran decisions.

6. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: Missiles intercepted over Riyadh and Mecca region; Houthis claim Yanbu strike

The coalition said it destroyed cruise missiles heading for Riyadh and drones aimed at Mecca and the south, the second day of interceptions over Riyadh. [18] The Houthis called the Mecca claim "lies" and said they struck a pumping station in Yanbu; Saudi Arabia has not confirmed it or the earlier Abqaiq claim. [18] [19] WHO counts 2,098 killed since fighting resumed. [19]

Assessment: Claimed Houthi targeting of Yanbu threatens Riyadh's Hormuz bypass.

7. GAZA: Strike on Nasser Medical Complex kills six, including a surgeon

An airstrike on surgical wards at Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis killed six people, including surgeon Dr Rezik Abushira, and wounded about 20. [20] [21] The IDF said Hamas used the complex as a command centre; Hamas called it a "war crime." [20]

Assessment: This is the most serious ceasefire incident in weeks.

8. LEBANON: Qassem calls Trump's midterm defeat "an opportunity"

Hezbollah leader Naim Qassem said "an earthquake has occurred in America" and repeated that Hezbollah will not disarm before Israel withdraws. [22]

Assessment: Hezbollah is hardening its position before UNIFIL's year-end exit.

9. PAKISTAN: Ambush kills at least 10 police in Kohat

Militants ambushed a police convoy in Kohat district on 11 November, killing at least 10 officers per AP and 11 per DPA; police blamed the TTP. [23] [24]

Assessment: Mass-casualty attacks on police keep Pakistan-Taliban relations tense.

10. EGYPT: Lina Attalah's retrial adjourned to 18 November

The court adjourned the retrial to consider a defence request to remove the presiding judge; the Mada Masr closure appeal remains set for 25 November. [25]

Assessment: Both cases stay live through late November.

Collection gap: No Saudi confirmation of Abqaiq or Yanbu claims, no named vessels for 11 November incidents, no Khor Mor restart. Nothing of note in-window for Jordan, Syria (beyond unverified state media) or Central Asia. Kataib Hezbollah statements were found but their dates were unclear.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE / RUSSIA: Abu Dhabi round ends with prisoner swap agreed in principle

Day two closed without a joint statement; Kushner called the agreed swap "the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners" and said final numbers would be set on 12 November. [26] Medinsky said talks would continue but gave no date, and questioned "in what capacity" the Europeans were present. [26] [27] ISW, citing a Russian general, says an exchange of 1,000 could take place in late November or early December. [28]

Assessment: The swap is the only concrete result; positions on territory are unchanged.

2. RUSSIA / US: Witkoff and Kushner in Moscow to meet Putin

The US envoys landed in Moscow on 12 November for talks with Putin; Reuters reports differences remain on territory and that mediators have shifted toward security guarantees and reconstruction. [29] ISW reports the Kremlin has "no vision" for a format including Europeans. [28] No readout was available by 1100Z. Zelensky is in Dublin for the European Political Community summit, where the envoys are to debrief him. [30]

Assessment: The Dublin debrief will be the first sign of whether Moscow's position moved.

3. UKRAINE: Overnight attack kills woman in Kyiv; outages in nine regions

Ukraine's Air Force says Russia launched 123 drones plus ballistic and Zircon missiles, with hits at 17 locations. [31] A drone hit a residential building in Kyiv, killing a woman and injuring seven. [32] The deputy energy minister reported outages in nine regions. [33] Russia claimed it hit a rocket factory and a missile-fuel plant. [33]

Assessment: Russia is pursuing a winter energy campaign for leverage while envoys meet Putin.

4. RUSSIA: Moscow claims 71 Ukrainian drones overnight

Russia's MoD said 71 Ukrainian drones were downed overnight, five approaching Moscow; no specific hit was reported by 1100Z. [34]

Assessment: This is far smaller than the previous night's claimed 627.

5. UKRAINE: ISW confirms Russian advance in Bilytske

ISW geolocated a Russian advance in central Bilytske, southeast of Dobropillia, and says Medinsky vowed to take "every inch" of claimed land. [28]

Assessment: Small gains on the ground do not match Russia's claims at the table.

6. LITHUANIA: NATO jets twice failed to down a drone

NATO jets made two failed attempts on a drone over northern Lithuania, and debris from a self-destructed missile was recovered in Biržai. [35] [36] The defence minister said the drones were likely Ukrainian strays; the defence chief said their origin is undetermined. [35] [36]

Assessment: The failed intercept exposes gaps in Baltic air policing against low, slow drones.

7. GEORGIA: Court removes opposition leaders from party-ban hearing

The Constitutional Court removed Nika Gvaramia and Maia Kopaleishvili after disputes over evidence; one judge called the criteria vague. Questioning of the parties is expected to run until 17 November, and a ruling is due by June 2027. [37]

Assessment: The process is adversarial and likely to favour Georgian Dream, but the timeline is longer than Wednesday's report implied.

Collection gap: No Putin meeting readout by 1100Z. A reported Chinese diplomatic visit to Lithuania could not be verified. Nothing new on Moldova or France.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. INDONESIA: Vice President Gibran resigns; impeachment-track plenary cancelled

Gibran handed his resignation letter to MPR Chair Ahmad Muzani on 12 November, and a similar letter went to Prabowo. [38] [39] Support for the parliamentary motion against him had grown to about 120 MPs from eight factions; the DPR cancelled the plenary that was to read it, and a PKB MP said it was "no longer relevant." [40] [38] [39] Al Jazeera reports he had first offered to resign on Tuesday and that Prabowo urged him to defend himself. [41]

Assessment: The resignation ends the impeachment path without a vote and makes vice-presidential succession the main political question.

2. INDONESIA: MPR sets succession timetable

Muzani declared the vice presidency vacant, and MPR leaders met Prabowo the same day; the MPR has up to 60 days to fill the post, and the president has 14 days after its request to propose two candidates. [42] [41] An MPR joint meeting is set for 13 November. [42] ABC notes the widening Prabowo-Jokowi rift. [43]

Assessment: Prabowo now picks both nominees; no names have been reported.

3. INDONESIA: Rupiah closes at a record low of Rp18,225

The rupiah closed at Rp18,225 per dollar, its weakest close on record, with analysts citing expected Fed rate hikes more than the resignation; Bank Indonesia says it is intervening in several markets. [44]

Assessment: External pressure outweighs the domestic political shock for now.

4. REGIONAL MARKETS: Asian shares fall on Nvidia turmoil; Taiex down 2.63%

AP reports Nvidia fell 8.6% after hours on reports its board was meeting over CEO Jensen Huang; the Nikkei and Hang Seng fell. [45] The Taiex closed down 2.63% at 43,091.87. [46]

Assessment: Chip-heavy Taiwan is the market most exposed to the Huang affair.

5. JAPAN: Yen nears 160; currency diplomat renews warning

The dollar reached 159.94 yen, and Vice Finance Minister Atsushi Mimura called recent moves "excessively rapid," adding "It's still too early to forget what happened this summer." [47]

Assessment: The risk of intervention is high in the coming days.

6. NORTH KOREA: Kim inspects naval base; official threatens action over allied submarines

KCNA says Kim inspected construction of a "major naval base" at Munchon on 11 November. [48] Party official Kim Jong-sik said Pyongyang would "deter all threats by action," citing nuclear-submarine plans in South Korea, Japan and Australia. [49] Seoul's unification minister called shows of force a "kind of signal" about talks. [50]

Assessment: The pairing of a naval build-up with threats raises the risk of a naval or missile demonstration.

7. TAIWAN: Defence minister says China running unannounced drills with 100+ ships

New Bloom relays Defence Minister Wellington Koo's statement that China is conducting "area access denial drills" with more than 100 naval and coast guard ships including the Liaoning group, without announcing an exercise. [51] The figure far exceeds daily MND counts, which cover a smaller area.

Assessment: Pressure is being kept below the threshold of a named exercise before Taiwan's local elections.

8. PHILIPPINES / US: Trump to meet Marcos in Manila on 17 November

The ASEAN chair's spokesperson said "there will definitely be a bilateral engagement" between Trump and Marcos, with an ASEAN-US special summit on 17 November before APEC in Shenzhen. [52]

Assessment: Manila gets a brief stop; the diplomatic weight sits in Shenzhen.

9. MYANMAR: Min Aung Hlaing to skip ASEAN summits

Human Rights Watch says Manila confirmed the junta leader will not attend the 15-17 November summits; the ICJ rules in The Gambia v. Myanmar on 19 November. [53]

Assessment: ASEAN's leader-level exclusion of the junta holds.

10. BANGLADESH: Pro-Hasina unrest turns deadly

UPI reports at least one death and reported IED blasts as Hasina supporters protested after her passport application was refused. [54] The home minister told parliament those who fled "would never return" and blamed programmes "announced from across the border." [55]

Assessment: Unrest and the government's framing toward India add strain to Dhaka-Delhi ties.

Collection gap: Taiwan MND did not load. No verified in-window South China Sea, India-Pakistan, Australia or Pacific items, and no new confirmation of a three-way APEC meeting.

area of responsibility

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES (MISSISSIPPI): Colom wins Senate seat; Hyde-Smith concedes

Networks called the race for Democrat Scott Colom on 11 November after Hinds County finished its affidavit ballots, accepting 817 of 2,426. [56] Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith phoned Colom to concede and said, "I respect the decision of Mississippi voters." [57] Colom said voters felt "betrayed" by Trump policies, including the Iran war, and ABC counts it as the eighth Senate seat Democrats have flipped. [58] [59] Reported margins vary from 3,176 with 98% counted to about 4,700 with 99%, all unofficial until counties certify on 13 November. [56] [60] [59]

Assessment: The concession and the absence of any lawsuit make a post-certification challenge unlikely.

2. UNITED STATES (ALASKA): Senate race still uncalled; judge rejects Peltola data request

No new statewide count was released; Peltola leads Sullivan 48.8% to 47.1% on first choices, and the Alaska Beacon's preliminary ranked-choice modelling shows Sullivan gaining a net 906 votes, short of overtaking her. [61] A Superior Court judge denied the Peltola campaign's request for a list of rejected absentee voters, writing that "the timing of the Campaign's request could simply not be worse." [62] The absentee arrival deadline is 13 November and the ranked-choice tabulation is on 18 November. [61]

Assessment: Peltola's lead is holding; the ruling closes one line of litigation without changing the timeline.

3. MEXICO / US: Sheinbaum orders inquiry into reported CIA activity in Chihuahua

Responding to a New York Times report that US agents worked in Chihuahua in 2025 with help from former state operations prosecutor Guillermo Zuany, Sheinbaum said cooperation must follow the Constitution and National Security Law, "and in this case it did not." [63] Arrest warrants against Zuany are in force; a journalist's claim that the CIA moved him to the US is unverified, and he is due before a federal judge on 13 November. [64] Sheinbaum also confirmed her government knew of a US warrant for federal judge Luis Benítez Alcántara, detained in Colombia. [65]

Assessment: A sovereignty dispute now overlays US-Mexico security ties as Washington's line hardens.

4. UNITED STATES: Iran war funding fight builds before 11 December deadline

Sen. Chris Van Hollen and Rep. Ro Khanna are pushing for a stopgap that bars Pentagon funds for combat against Iran, which would need 60 Senate votes; Democratic leaders have not committed to the demand. [66]

Assessment: War funding is likely to dominate lame-duck talks and raises shutdown risk on 11 December.

5. CANADA: Carney heads to Dublin, then Asian summits

Carney left for the European Political Community summit in Dublin on 12 November, where an undefined EU "associate membership" for Canada has been floated; he then goes to Phnom Penh, Manila for ASEAN and Shenzhen for APEC, including a meeting with Xi. [67]

Assessment: The trip aims to cut Canada's trade dependence on the US.

Collection gap: Nothing in-window on ICE operations, the NDAA, Hegseth committee action or Mexican cartel violence; NYT, WaPo, NBC, CNN, NPR and The Hill pages did not load.

area of responsibility

SOUTHCOM.

1. HAITI: UN says Salvadoran strike on 400 Mawozo base falls outside the GSF mandate

AFP reports the Croix-des-Bouquets strike on gang leader Wilson Joseph's headquarters left at least 20 dead according to anonymous sources, and that Joseph was not present. [68] The UN says the operation is outside the mandate and oversight of the Gang Suppression Force, since most of the roughly 2,000 Salvadoran troops are not part of it; the Dominican Republic backs the effort, and Amnesty International wants the deployment agreement published. [68]

Assessment: A parallel force outside UN oversight creates command and accountability risks, including civilian harm.

2. COLOMBIA: Eighth airstrike kills eight Clan del Golfo members, including "Negro Perea"

President De la Espriella said Operation Nehemías in rural Tarazá, Antioquia, killed eight members of a Clan del Golfo substructure, including Haminton Mosquera, alias "Negro Perea," one of ten priority targets; he called it "450 kilos de democracia en una bomba." [69] [70] It came less than 48 hours after the Catatumbo raid, for which El Tiempo reported US participation was limited to MQ-9 intelligence; no US role is reported in Nehemías and no official confirmation was found. [71]

Assessment: Strikes are now coming every few days and have widened to the Clan del Golfo; scrutiny of civilian casualties will grow.

3. PERU: Pope Leo XIV arrives in Lima

EFE reports about three million people lined the route as President Keiko Fujimori received the pope, who urged authorities to "cultivar la honestidad, la rectitud." [72] The US Embassy warns of heavy security deployments and disruption through 16 November, with stops in Chiclayo, Cajamarca, Cusco and Pucallpa. [73]

Assessment: A major public-order operation; crowd and transport incidents are the main risks.

4. ECUADOR: State of exception renewed in eight provinces before 29 November elections

Decree 532 renews the state of exception for 30 days in eight provinces, including Guayas and Pichincha, allowing warrantless searches but no curfew. [74] Noboa claims homicides fell 48% during the 1-10 November curfew, a government figure. [75]

Assessment: Emergency powers will frame the campaign, and attacks on candidates remain the main risk.

5. VENEZUELA: TSJ nomination period extended to 18 November

The National Assembly's nominations committee extended applications for Supreme Court magistrates by seven days, citing a wish for broader participation. [76]

Assessment: The committee appears to want a larger or different pool, delaying selection.

6. CUBA: Guiteras plant trips offline again

Unit 1 of the Antonio Guiteras plant went offline on 11 November with an economizer leak, while Felton 1 is down for maintenance; the grid operator forecast a 1,820 MW peak deficit before the trip. [77] Díaz-Canel acknowledged Cubans want "fewer blackouts and more food." [78]

Assessment: Deficits near or above 2,000 MW are likely in coming days.

7. GUATEMALA: Raids on trafficking groups net more than 25 arrests

Police and prosecutors carried out more than 70 raids in Alta Verapaz, Zacapa and Chiquimula against the "Los Mamás" and "Los Coyotes" groups, seizing weapons, two drones and cash. [79]

Assessment: A sizeable but routine action in the eastern trafficking corridor.

8. REGIONAL: No tropical development expected

The National Hurricane Center expects no tropical cyclone formation in the next seven days. [80] Collection gap: No official US confirmation of drone support to Colombian strikes, no US boat strikes reported, and thin coverage of Brazil, Honduras, Nicaragua and Panama; Haiti casualty figures rest on anonymous sources.
