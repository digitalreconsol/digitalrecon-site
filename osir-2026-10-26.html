area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. ALGERIA / NIGER: Parliament approves first foreign troop deployment since 1973

Both chambers of Algeria's parliament approved sending army detachments to Niger in closed sessions on 25 Oct, with the upper house voting unanimously. The units are expected to secure Niger's oil facilities, following a military cooperation protocol and Algeria's dispatch of fighter jets to help Niger counter a coup attempt in August. [1][2]

Assessment: This is a major break with Algeria's non-intervention doctrine and aligns Algiers with Niamey's junta; reactions from Mali, Russia and Morocco are likely.

2. MALI: Army finds 54 civilians dead near Ménaka

Pre-window onset: Mali's General Staff said a patrol found 54 bodies at a camp about 35 km southeast of Ménaka and evacuated nine survivors; early testimony points to Islamic State in the Greater Sahara. [3] The attack happened the previous weekend, and the victims were mainly from the Daoussahak community; a security source suggested retaliation for operations by a junta-allied militia. [4]

Assessment: IS Sahel is very likely to keep targeting communities around Ménaka along ethnic and militia lines.

3. DRC / EBOLA: Nurse killed responding to Ebola alert; cases pass 9,800

Attackers killed a nurse and badly injured another health worker responding to an Ebola alert in Bafwasende, Tshopo province, and DRC now reports more than 9,800 cases and 4,700 deaths. [5] The WHO started the "Tumaini" trial testing whether the Ervebo vaccine protects against the Bundibugyo strain. [6] WHO figures as of 20 Oct were 9,704 cases and 4,687 deaths. [7]

Assessment: Insecurity and distrust are likely to keep hampering the response just as vaccine trials begin, ahead of the Africa CDC summit on 28 Oct.

4. UGANDA: 21-day quarantine for citizens returning from DRC

Uganda ordered a 21-day quarantine, paid by travellers, for citizens returning from DRC and tightened border checks. Its health minister disputed that Nigeria's case was infected in Uganda, saying "we should speak science, not politics." [8]

Assessment: The regional response is shifting toward travel controls, against WHO advice.

5. SUDAN: UN says drones killed at least 306 civilians in three weeks

Pre-window onset: UN rights chief Volker Türk said drone strikes by both the army and the RSF killed at least 306 people from 1 to 21 Oct, including 139 in a double-tap strike in West Kordofan. [9][10] UN News still puts the Abyei toll at more than 80. [9]

Assessment: Drone strikes are now the main cause of civilian deaths in Sudan, with Kordofan and Blue Nile the hotspots.

6. NIGERIA: Gunmen kill at least 10 in Taraba; bandits abduct dozens in Zamfara

Pre-window onset: Gunmen on about 50 motorcycles attacked Binnari village in Taraba State after Friday prayers; police counted 10 bodies, survivors said at least 20, and Amnesty said 40. [11][12] In Zamfara, bandits held Kaura Namoda for more than three hours early on 24 Oct, killing seven vigilantes; 38 reported abductions are unverified. [13]

Assessment: Banditry is spreading into central-eastern Nigeria, and official tolls likely understate local losses.

7. SOMALIA: Government sends 200 vehicles against rebel-held Burhakabo

The federal government sent more than 200 military vehicles toward Burhakabo, held by forces loyal to a former Southwest state president, and al-Shabaab bombs hit vehicles on the route. [14] A former spy chief did not rule out working with al-Shabaab against the president. [15] Two US strikes before the window brought this year's total in Somalia to 90. [16]

Assessment: Fighting between federal and state forces is likely to divert troops from the campaign against al-Shabaab.

8. SENEGAL: General strike ends; talks resume

The 72-hour strike ended on 23 Oct without extension, and unions now want a meeting with the prime minister. The underlying disputes remain unresolved five days before the Youth Olympics open. [17]

Assessment: Industrial action could return if talks stall.

Collection gap: no in-window reporting was found on Burkina Faso, El Fasher or an updated Abyei toll. Sahel wire coverage was thin.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. IRAN / SAUDI ARABIA / PAKISTAN / TURKEY: Security chiefs due in Tehran for de-escalation talks

An Iranian official said Saudi Defence Minister Khalid bin Salman, Pakistani army chief Asim Munir and Turkish intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin will visit Tehran on 26 Oct, and a Saudi official said the visit is "for de-escalation." Qatar and Pakistan are mediating. [18] No outcome had been reported by the end of the window. [19]

Assessment: This is the most significant new channel of the weekend, directly addressing the Houthi campaign while the US-Iran track stalls.

2. US / IRAN: Trump says "there will not be a strike today"; Iran says messages still pass

Trump told reporters on 25 Oct "there will not be a strike today." [20] He told Fox News: "Do I finish it off, which would be very easy to do, or do I make a deal?" [21] Iran's foreign ministry said messages still reach Washington through intermediaries. [22] CENTCOM's commander said more than 3,000 ships have crossed Hormuz with US support in six months and that the lanes are clear of mines. [22][21] No US or Israeli strike on Iran or IRGC naval incident was found in the window.

Assessment: A short-term pause is likely, with the risk window moving past 27 Oct and 3 Nov.

3. IRAN / SAUDI ARABIA: Iranian officials say US personnel are targets; US curbs staff movement

Two Iranian officials, one from the IRGC, told the New York Times that Tehran is preparing to target US diplomats and personnel. [23] An Embassy Riyadh alert said ordered departure of non-emergency staff took effect on 23 Oct, and staff now need approval to use any Saudi airport. [24]

Assessment: The threat to US personnel across the region is high, from both Iran and the Houthis.

4. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: Weekend Houthi barrage; Riyadh airport partly reopens

The coalition said it intercepted ballistic and cruise missiles and drones aimed at Riyadh and Khamis Mushait, then 10 more ballistic missiles at the south; debris injured two. Two terminals at Riyadh's airport reopened, and the Houthis called it a "military target." The UN said "time is running out" for about 800,000 people in Taiz. [25] Another missile aimed at Riyadh was intercepted early on 26 Oct. [26]

Assessment: The tempo is undiminished, and civil-defence strain is spreading to western Saudi Arabia.

5. ISRAEL: IDF chief reportedly resisted Netanyahu order tied to Iran

Channel 12 reported, citing anonymous sources, an "unprecedented confrontation" in which IDF chief Eyal Zamir resisted an unspecified directive that officials feared could set off a clash with Iran. Netanyahu called the report "nonsense" and Katz said there was "no intention" to strike before the election. [27]

Assessment: Civil-military friction is at a peak on election eve; the risk of a surprise Israeli action is low but not zero.

6. ISRAEL: Final polls show deadlock before Tuesday's election

The final poll average gives the opposition 54 seats and Netanyahu's bloc 49.5, with Eisenkot's Yashar ahead of Likud. [28] The IDF announced a West Bank closure for election day, and the High Court allowed licensed firearms at polling stations. [29] Netanyahu summoned the police chief over "electoral integrity." [30]

Assessment: A deadlocked result and long coalition talks are likely, with real risks of intimidation and integrity disputes.

7. TURKEY: Istanbul meeting condemns Houthis but sets no steps on Iran

Pre-window onset: The joint statement from Turkey, Egypt, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, France, Germany, Italy and the UK condemned "appalling Houthi militia attacks" and agreed to "continue the dialogue in this format," but set out no measures on Iran or Hormuz. [31][32]

Assessment: The group has little leverage; the Tehran visit is the working channel.

8. EGYPT: Parliament votes today on military support for Saudi Arabia

Egypt's House is due to vote on 26 Oct in a closed session; approval needs two-thirds of 596 seats. [33] The Social Democratic Party told its MPs to abstain, while Wafd and Nour back the request. [34] No result was found by the end of the window.

Assessment: Passage is likely but could be close.

9. ENERGY: Brent near $114.60, down from Friday's close

Brent was about $114.60 early Monday, down $2.15 from Friday's $116.72 settlement, while Murban rose about 3% to more than $118. [35] The Houthis claim strikes on Yanbu and the Rabigh refinery, which Saudi Arabia has not confirmed. [35][36]

Assessment: The Murban premium shows how scarce barrels that avoid Hormuz have become.

10. IRAQ: IRGC drones hit Iranian Kurdish group; Baghdad shuts some Iran crossings

Iranian media reported IRGC drones struck Komala's headquarters in Iraqi Kurdistan overnight 24-25 Oct. [37] Iraq closed some crossings with Iran, and Iran objected. [38] Iraqi militias allege 171 US airspace violations since 30 Sep; this is unverified. [39]

Assessment: Iran is keeping up pressure on Kurdish groups while Baghdad quietly hardens its borders.

11. GAZA: Strike on Islamic University kills three

An Israeli strike on the Islamic University in Gaza City killed three staff, and four more people were killed elsewhere on Sunday. [40] Gaza's Health Ministry says more than 1,500 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire began. [40][22]

Assessment: Lethal strikes continue under the truce.

12. PAKISTAN: Six militants and two soldiers killed in Dera Ismail Khan

Pakistan's military said an operation in Dera Ismail Khan killed six TTP militants and two soldiers. [41]

Assessment: The TTP insurgency continues as Pakistan's army chief travels to Tehran.

Collection gap: no in-window reporting was found on Lebanon, Afghanistan-Pakistan border clashes, Central Asia or Gulf states other than Saudi Arabia.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. SERBIA: Vučić concedes defeat as Student List wins

Vučić said on Sunday night, "It is clear that we have lost these elections, the Student List has won," with turnout near 70% and no violence reported. [42] With 91.84% of stations counted, the Student List had 51.71% and Vučić's coalition 39.85%, and pollsters projected 140 of 250 seats for the students. [43] Pre-election polls had shown the ruling SNS ahead. [44]

Assessment: Vučić's party loses power for the first time since 2012; the transition and any challenge to the result are the next tests.

2. FRANCE: Nearly 1,000 arrested as 410,000 protest

The Interior Ministry counted 410,000 protesters nationwide on 24 Oct, and police arrested "nearly 1,000." Police used tear gas and water cannon in Paris and Lyon, and another protest is called for 31 Oct. [45]

Assessment: Heavy policing reduced the violence but not the turnout, and the movement shows no sign of easing.

3. UKRAINE: Strikes kill four in Dnipropetrovsk; new overnight wave

Russian drones and bombs killed four people and injured 22 in Dnipropetrovsk region overnight 24-25 Oct. [46] Zelensky said Russia used about 1,600 guided bombs, 2,300 drones and 48 missiles in the past week. [47] Overnight into 26 Oct Russia launched 140 drones and 4 missiles, with hits at 15 locations. [48]

Assessment: The campaign against energy and civilian targets will very likely continue into winter.

4. UKRAINE: Zelensky says only 24 Patriot interceptors received in three months

Zelensky said Ukraine needs about 350 Patriot interceptors a month but has received only 24 in three months, and called for "unblocking" agreed deliveries. [49] He said European partners had agreed to supply them. [47]

Assessment: The shortage raises the risk to Ukraine's grid this winter.

5. UKRAINE / RUSSIA: Local Ukrainian gains and deep strikes on defence plants

ISW reports Ukrainian forces reportedly retook three villages in the Oleksandrivka direction and struck a plant in Yelets making Iskander components and a titanium plant in Berezniki. [50] The toll from the 23 Oct strike in Oryol rose to three. [51]

Assessment: Ukraine is pairing local counterattacks with long-range strikes while the front stays largely static.

6. RUSSIA / US / UKRAINE: No date yet for Abu Dhabi talks

Pre-window onset: Peskov said on 23 Oct the talks are "planned for the first half of November in Abu Dhabi." [52][53] No specific date was announced in the window. [49]

Assessment: Unchanged since Friday.

7. MOLDOVA: Russia vows "firm response" after diplomat expelled

Pre-window onset: Moldova expelled a Russian embassy staff member after a Geran-type drone crashed near homes and an energy facility. [54] Russia called it a "hostile action" and promised "an adequate and firm response." [55]

Assessment: A tit-for-tat expulsion is likely.

8. GEORGIA: Thousands rally as opposition faces ban case

Thousands marched in Tbilisi on 25 Oct under the slogan "Georgia without Ivanishvili," and Constitutional Court hearings on banning opposition parties were due to resume on 26 Oct. [56]

Assessment: Pressure on the opposition will likely rise as the case proceeds.

9. GERMANY: Merz rejects softening the AfD "firewall"

Merz said "anyone who feels they have to vote for these people has no place in our Union." [57]

Assessment: The CDU's internal split over the AfD is widening.

Collection gap: nothing in the window was found on Turkey or new NATO or Baltic airspace incidents. One Moldova source is a low-reliability aggregator.

area of responsibility

INDOPACOM.

1. TAIWAN / CHINA: Champi injuries rise to 527; eastern China evacuates tens of thousands

Pre-window onset: Taiwan's fire department reported one dead and 527 injured, with more than 288,000 homes losing power at the peak; the storm passed the northeast coast without landfall. [58] In China, more than 100 flights were halted at Shanghai, Fujian and Zhejiang airports. [59] Shanghai reportedly evacuated about 62,400 people. [60]

Assessment: Taiwan's toll looks settled at a low level, and mainland China saw disruption rather than major damage.

2. JAPAN / SOUTH KOREA: Champi injures people in Okinawa, then weakens near Kyushu

Injuries were reported on Miyakojima and Yonaguni, about 13,000 households lost power, and Okinawa requested Self-Defense Forces disaster relief. [61] Iriomote recorded 567 mm of rain in 48 hours. [62] The storm was forecast to become extratropical near Kagoshima on 26 Oct, with prolonged heavy rain possible. [63] Jeju was under a typhoon warning on 25 Oct. [64]

Assessment: The main threat on 26 Oct is flooding and landslides in western and central Japan.

3. NORTH KOREA: Pyongyang tells UN that Seoul fabricated mine blast; howitzer seen at border

North Korea's UN representative said in a letter that Seoul fabricated the 21 Sept mine incident and warned of "self-defence measures." A Reuters witness saw an artillery piece and three armoured vehicles with guns pointed south. [65]

Assessment: Openly displaying artillery during border work raises the risk of an incident.

4. SOUTH KOREA: North's loudspeakers not yet on; Seoul has proposed military talks

A defence ministry official said "it has not even been decided whether the broadcasts will resume, let alone when," and Pyongyang has not answered Seoul's proposal for military talks. [66] Border residents urged both sides not to restart broadcasts. [67]

Assessment: Activation this week is plausible, since Pyongyang's own deadline is the end of October.

5. NORTH KOREA / CHINA: Kim Jong Un makes surprise visit to Dandong

Kim crossed the new Yalu River bridge into Dandong on 24 Oct, his first trip to China in a year, and met Politburo Standing Committee member Wang Huning. [68] Seoul's government confirmed the visit. [69]

Assessment: The visit signals Pyongyang's tilt toward Beijing before APEC.

6. TAIWAN / CHINA: PLA activity resumes after typhoon

Taiwan's defence ministry reported 3 PLA aircraft, one crossing the median line, 6 navy ships and 9 official ships in the 24 hours to 0600 on 26 Oct. [70]

Assessment: This is a low-level return to routine; the high official-ship count is worth tracking.

7. PHILIPPINES: Trilateral drills with Japan and US end; no Chinese countermeasures confirmed

The 22nd Philippines-Japan-US maritime cooperative activity ended on 24 Oct with no reported Chinese response. [71] A Filipino-Chinese chamber of commerce urged both governments to "talk, don't post." [72] No source in the window confirmed or denied the reported Chinese countermeasures.

Assessment: The row remains rhetorical; a PLA "combat readiness patrol" would be the usual next step.

8. RUSSIA / CHINA / US: Kremlin says preparations have begun for an APEC trilateral

Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said Moscow, Beijing and Washington are "in principle ready" for a trilateral meeting at APEC in Shenzhen, and that Russia is ready to resume talks with Ukraine after 4 Nov. [73] China and the US have not confirmed. [74]

Assessment: This rests on Russian statements only.

9. INDONESIA: Students protest Kalimantan haze

Hundreds of students protested the haze in Palangkaraya, demanding it be declared a national disaster. [75] A rule-of-law index lowered Indonesia's ranking to 75th of 143. [76]

Assessment: The haze is becoming both a domestic protest issue and a regional irritant.

10. THAILAND / CAMBODIA: Bangkok rejects Cambodia's claim to border temples

Thailand's culture minister rejected Cambodia's demand to return artefacts and said two border temples belong to Thailand. [77]

Assessment: The heritage dispute keeps nationalist friction alive alongside maritime confidence-building.

Collection gap: nothing new in the window was found on Bougainville, India-Pakistan, Australia or South China Sea incidents. A report of shells from Myanmar landing in Thailand is unconfirmed.

NORTHCOM.

1. MEXICO: Vance weakens to a tropical storm; heavy rain later this week

At 0300 CST Vance had 65 mph winds about 260 mi SSW of Manzanillo, with no watches or warnings; 2-4 inches of rain is forecast for Sinaloa and western Nayarit, Jalisco and Durango. [78] Wind shear and dry air rule out strengthening, and the system should approach mainland Mexico later this week as it weakens. [79] No Atlantic storms are expected in the next seven days. [80]

Assessment: Flooding and mudslides in western Mexico are now the main hazard.

2. MEXICO: Ex-Chihuahua prosecutor ordered to stand trial for treason in CIA case

A federal judge ordered César Jáuregui to stand trial for treason early on 25 Oct and kept him in detention; his lawyer will file an amparo. [81] The charge carries 5 to 40 years, and prosecutors have two months to complete their investigation. [82]

Assessment: The CIA-presence issue will likely remain an irritant in US-Mexico security relations during the USMCA review.

3. MEXICO: Navy kills cartel figure "El Meny" in Sonora

A Navy-led operation killed Francisco Manuel Romero Carranza, alleged No. 2 of a group linked to Los Chapitos, and detained four. [83] The account rests on the government's statement. [84]

Assessment: Retaliatory violence in Sonora is possible.

4. UNITED STATES: FBI arrests suspect in attempted firebombing of Atlanta ICE office

The FBI arrested a 31-year-old man on 23 Oct on federal arson charges over an incendiary device thrown at the ICE office on 20 Oct. [85][86]

Assessment: The administration will likely cite the attack in pre-midterm security messaging.

5. UNITED STATES: Washington counties accept plainclothes Guard observers at ballot boxes

Five Washington counties accepted the governor's offer of unarmed, plainclothes National Guard members to observe ballot drop boxes for 3 Nov. [87] Spokane County declined, and the state Republican chair called the offer "absurd." [88]

Assessment: This state role is likely to blur debate over troops near polls.

6. CANADA: Trump says trade war will end "pretty soon"

Trump said at a Michigan rally that the dispute with Canada would be settled "pretty soon," after announcing he would lift some tariffs while adding others. [89] Carney will host an EU summit in Montréal on 29-30 Oct. [90]

Assessment: Tariff de-escalation before the midterms is possible, while Ottawa hedges toward Europe.

Collection gap: no Supreme Court order on the Big Bend wall or appeal ruling on the voter-probe injunction was found. Wainwright updates could not be retrieved.

area of responsibility

SOUTHCOM.

1. BRAZIL: Lula wins fourth term; Flávio Bolsonaro rejects result

Lula won about 51.8% with all votes counted, after a Supreme Court justice extended voting by two hours over bus disruptions. [91] Flávio Bolsonaro said it is "impossible to recognise the result" and that he will seek annulment. [92] The electoral court called his claim that party monitors were removed false. [93] The OAS mission urged calm, and several regional leaders congratulated Lula. [94]

Assessment: A formal challenge and street mobilisation are likely; the voting extension gives it a procedural hook, but no evidence of fraud has been presented.

2. BRAZIL / US: Trump questions Lula's win

Trump said Brazil had "just applied the Joe Biden playbook," offering no evidence. [95] He had not formally endorsed Flávio. [96]

Assessment: The remarks give international cover to the challenge and will likely strain US-Brazil relations further.

3. VENEZUELA: Machado returns from exile

María Corina Machado reappeared in Venezuela on 25 Oct for the first time since December. Acting President Delcy Rodríguez remains in office with no election scheduled. [97]

Assessment: Her return sharpens pressure for an election date and raises the risk of confrontation.

4. HAITI / EL SALVADOR: Bukele says remains handed over are not the missing soldiers

Bukele said remains handed to Salvadoran teams at Haiti's airport are "not our men," saying they included a child. Four bodies were recovered but not identified. [98]

Assessment: The soldiers' fate is unresolved; confirmed deaths would likely trigger a larger operation.

5. COLOMBIA: Cauca airstrike toll rises to 10, including two ELN commanders

The government said 10 ELN members died in the Cauca strike, including two commanders, and vowed to intensify the offensive. [99]

Assessment: ELN retaliation in Cauca and Valle is likely.

6. ECUADOR: Luisa González jailed pending trial

Pre-window onset: A judge ordered pretrial detention for González, who was moved to a prison in Chimborazo; Human Rights Watch is following the case "with concern." [100][101]

Assessment: Her detention deepens polarisation ahead of November's local elections.

7. CUBA: Eastern grid collapse blacks out Santiago and Guantánamo

A line failure on 25 Oct disconnected Santiago de Cuba and Guantánamo, the second eastern collapse this month. [102] Cuba will keep summer time into 2027 to save power. [103]

Assessment: Worsening grid fragility before the 28 Oct UN vote raises the risk of local unrest.

8. PERU: Lima-Callao state of emergency extended 60 days

The government extended the state of emergency in Lima and Callao for 60 days from 28 Oct. [104]

Assessment: This continues the militarised anti-crime posture.
