area of responsibility

AFRICOM.

1. RUSSIA / AFRICA: Summit closes with Moscow Declaration; no arms or basing deals reported

At his closing press conference, Putin called the summit a success. It adopted a Moscow Declaration backing a "multipolar world order" and a 2027-2029 partnership action plan. Putin put trade at $27bn and said Rosatom is building 28 of the 32 nuclear units it has under construction abroad. [1][2] Counts of attending leaders conflict: Russian outlets say 21, AFP says 22 of 48 delegations. AFP also reports Russia plans an embassy in Liberia. [3]

Assessment: The outcome documents are framework texts. No public reporting confirms new arms deals or a Port Sudan basing agreement from the summit.

2. SUDAN / ETHIOPIA: Khartoum says Ethiopia has launched 162 drone attacks from its territory

Sudanese officials said 162 drone attacks launched from bases at Assosa and Bahir Dar have killed more than 400 civilians and 102 military personnel, using Turkish and Chinese drones. The defence minister said more than 20 countries support the RSF. [4] The claims are unverified, and Ethiopia has previously rejected such allegations.

Assessment: Khartoum is building a formal case against Addis Ababa just as Russia arms Ethiopia, raising the risk of a wider confrontation.

3. AFRICA: Ministers keep borders open as Ebola spreads

More than 40 health ministers agreed at an Africa CDC and WHO meeting to keep borders open while monitoring them. [5] Africa CDC gives 10,172 confirmed cases and more than 4,932 deaths across the DRC, Uganda, Kenya and Nigeria, with 10 more countries at risk; its director said "we are not winning so far". [6] The Mongbwalu treatment centre in Ituri discharged its last confirmed patients. [7]

Assessment: With no approved vaccine or treatment for the Bundibugyo strain, spread to the at-risk states is the main near-term risk.

4. DRC / RWANDA: US sanctions Rwanda's Defence Ministry

The US Treasury sanctioned Rwanda's Ministry of Defence and a savings bank it says moved tens of millions of dollars and gold for M23, naming Kabila's FRN as the successor to M23's political alliance. [8] Kinshasa welcomed the move; Rwanda's foreign minister denied responsibility. [9]

Assessment: The sanctions add financial pressure on Kigali but are unlikely to change the military situation in the Kivus soon.

5. TANZANIA: Election anniversary passes quietly under heavy police presence

Dar es Salaam was quiet on 29 Oct with a heavy police presence; no protests, arrests or internet disruption were reported. The US ambassador said the 2025 killings "will not be swept under the rug". Africanews says Lissu remains on hunger strike, which conflicts with earlier reports that it ended. [10]

Assessment: Pressure shifts to the 6 Nov Lissu verdict as the next likely flashpoint.

6. SOMALIA: Casualty claims for the Leego ambush diverge further

Al-Shabaab now claims 413 soldiers killed; a Reuters source cited more than 140 casualties and federal sources "several dozen" dead, while the government reports only militant losses. [11]

Assessment: The government's silence on its losses points to a worsening position for federal forces in Bay.

7. ALGERIA / NIGER: Deployment authorised, no troops across yet

Algeria's Senate approved deploying troops to the former French base at Madama, the first foreign deployment since 1973, but a Nigerien source said none had crossed. [12] Algeria already has fighter and tanker aircraft in Niger. [11]

Assessment: The first ground movement toward Madama is the indicator to watch; Mali and Haftar are likely to react.

8. LIBYA: Central bank headquarters raided

Libya's central bank said unidentified people raided its Tripoli headquarters hours after the eastern parliament accepted the governor's resignation in principle, and blamed the defence ministry, which guards the building. [13]

Assessment: A renewed east-west fight over the bank, as in 2024, would threaten oil revenue.

9. SUDAN: Yale imagery shows El Fasher devastated a year after RSF takeover

A Yale satellite study found eight of El Fasher's nine major markets inactive, no normal function at 19 health facilities, and a 75 km trench and berm around the city. [14] MSF asked the RSF to remove commercial sites and Starlink networks from Zalingei Teaching Hospital. [15]

Assessment: The evidence strengthens the accountability case against the RSF, while humanitarian access still depends on RSF commanders.

10. NIGERIA: Unverified Ansaru claim for killing of Katsina cleric

Ansaru claimed in an unverified video the 26 Oct killing of a cleric and an imam in Katsina State. [16] Police arrested 14 after attacks on ruling-party supporters travelling to a campaign launch. [17]

Assessment: A confirmed Ansaru claim would mark notable jihadist activity in the bandit-dominated northwest as 2027 campaign violence begins.

Collection gap: no Putin-Burhan readout or Sahel bilateral outcomes were found. Nothing new was found on Mali, Burkina Faso, Ethiopia-Eritrea or fighting in the eastern DRC.

area of responsibility

CENTCOM.

1. ISRAEL: Final results unchanged; Eisenkot expected to get first mandate

The Central Elections Committee's final results leave seats unchanged: Likud 27, Yashar 21, B'Yachad 12, Joint List 11, Democrats 8, Ra'am 8. Turnout was 77.9%. Results go to President Herzog on 4 Nov and the Knesset is sworn in on 10 Nov. [18] Both blocs expect Eisenkot to get the first chance to form a government; Haredi parties signalled they would negotiate with him, and unity-government contacts are reported. [19] Lapid said the opposition "won on points" but a government is not guaranteed. [20] Eisenkot said the swearing-in will not be delayed "by a single centimetre". [21]

Assessment: Neither bloc has a 61-seat path without crossing a red line, so drawn-out talks with a unity government or repeat election as live outcomes are likely.

2. ISRAEL: Shin Bet calls its funding inquiry "limited"

Shin Bet said its inquiry rests on its own intelligence, has not established foreign interference, and should "not be used to discredit a community that voted democratically". Police arrested four more vote-buying suspects. Any challenge to the results must be filed within 14 days. [22]

Assessment: The probe now looks more likely to yield individual bribery cases than grounds to overturn the result.

3. IRAN / US: Strike options readied; Rezaei seen as best channel

Fox News reports the military is preparing strike options; asked about striking Iran, Trump said "anything's possible", and CENTCOM said more than 50,000 US service members are in the region. Sen. Chris Murphy called a briefed plan to restart the war "insane", and Speaker Ghalibaf restated Iran's "seven conditions". [23] The Washington Post reports US intelligence sees security council chief Rezaei as the best chance of avoiding renewed fighting; he has not replied to Vance's letter. [24] Trump has described the outreach as about humanitarian aid. [25]

Assessment: Public signals point toward strikes after 3 Nov unless Rezaei replies; a response over the weekend is the key indicator.

4. IRAN / HORMUZ: Tanker hit by unknown projectile; CENTCOM rejects mine claims

UKMTO reported an outbound tanker struck by an unknown projectile about 25 nm northeast of Khasab and later on fire, with no attribution. [26] A dhow caught fire and sank north of Khasab with crew evacuated. CENTCOM called Iran's mine claims "FALSE" and said the main routes are clear; the IRGC told it to "prove" it and claimed to have captured a US drone. [23] Maritime officials say there is no evidence of mines. [25]

Assessment: Whatever the method, attacks on outbound traffic near Khasab continue, keeping transit risk high.

5. SAUDI ARABIA / YEMEN: Missiles intercepted over Riyadh; Houthis claim six strikes

Saudi air defences intercepted two missiles over Riyadh; debris lightly injured one person. [27][28] The Houthis claimed hits on Ras Tanura, Abqaiq, Rabigh and three airports, all unverified. [29] The coalition said it downed a drone over the Mecca region, which the Houthis deny. [30]

Assessment: The campaign against Saudi energy and aviation targets continues, and the Mecca accusation strengthens the case in Riyadh and Cairo for escalation.

6. YEMEN: WHO counts 1,677 killed since August

WHO counts at least 1,677 killed and 7,395 injured since August, with casualties running at about 93 a day in late October; OCHA reports more than 281,000 displaced, over half in Taiz. Human Rights Watch accuses the Houthis of cutting off supplies to Taiz city. [31]

Assessment: The casualty rate is accelerating, and Taiz is the humanitarian centre of gravity.

7. ENERGY: Brent near $116 on December contract expiry

Brent for December traded at about $116 early Friday, with January at about $112. The December contract expires Friday, so Monday's headline price will be about $4 lower without any real easing. [32]

Assessment: Oil is set for a fourth monthly gain; the contract roll may look like a fall when it is not.

8. GAZA: Drone strike kills four aid monitors in al-Mawasi

An Israeli drone strike killed four Economy Ministry employees monitoring aid trucks in al-Mawasi and wounded more than 30. The Health Ministry reported 6 deaths in 24 hours. [33]

Assessment: Low-level strikes and the medical-evacuation bottleneck continue unchanged.

9. SYRIA / JORDAN: Accounts of Jordanian strikes still conflict

Jordan says its strikes were coordinated with Damascus and killed more than 20 ISIL fighters; Damascus says there was "no prior coordination" and reports civilian deaths, including a grandmother and three grandchildren. [34]

Assessment: The joint committee and crossing reopening suggest both sides want to contain the dispute.

10. PAKISTAN / AFGHANISTAN: Islamabad calls Taliban account "fabricated"

Pakistan's information ministry rejected the Taliban account of the Angoor Adda clashes as "fabricated". [35]

Assessment: Tit-for-tat border fighting continues with no sign of de-escalation.

Collection gap: nothing new was found on an Egyptian deployment, an Aramco statement, Iraqi militias, Lebanon or Central Asia.

area of responsibility

EUCOM.

1. UKRAINE: Dnipro station strike kills at least 14; 27 dead nationwide in a day

Pre-window onset: a Russian jet-powered drone hit a platform at Dnipro station where a train was waiting on the morning of 29 Oct. [36][37] The toll rose to at least 14, with 14 injured; Peskov called Kyiv's drone attack on Moscow "very strange". [38] Over the day 27 were killed and 128 injured nationwide; the Kyiv Independent puts the Dnipro toll at 15. [39] A missile killed two teenage girls in Chernihiv. [40]

Assessment: The attacks continue a pattern of escalating Russian strikes on rail targets; no source explicitly calls them retaliation for the Moscow raid.

2. UKRAINE: Overnight attack of 113 drones and missiles

Ukraine's Air Force says Russia launched ballistic missiles, four guided missiles and 113 drones overnight; 99 were downed, with hits at 17 locations. [41] About 350,000 people in Dnipropetrovsk Oblast lost power. [39]

Assessment: The high share of jet-powered drones keeps hit rates up on energy and rail targets.

3. UKRAINE: Oleshky evacuation begins

Both sides said demining is complete and the convoy is ready. [42] Ukraine's ombudsman said 25 buses and ambulances were prepared. [43] Fewer than 2,000 residents remain, and some fear "filtration" on the Russian side. [44] Reports differ on whether the ceasefire ends Sunday or late on 1 Nov.

Assessment: The evacuation is a rare working Russia-Ukraine-US mechanism, but its effect on wider talks is limited given the continuing strikes.

4. RUSSIA / EUROPE: Putin says he does not want war with Europe

Putin said "we do not want any war with Europe" and called the Finnish and Norwegian leaders' call "the right thing to do". [1][45] ISW assesses that the Kremlin aims to split Europe. [46]

Assessment: Moscow is using the Nordic outreach to widen European divisions ahead of the expected Abu Dhabi talks.

5. UKRAINE: ISW reports Ukrainian advance near Novopavlivka; Russian infiltration at Dobropillya disputed

ISW says Ukrainian forces advanced south of Novopavlivka and that Russian forces may have infiltrated the southern edge of Dobropillya, a claim Ukrainian sources dispute. [46]

Assessment: Infiltration toward Dobropillya is the main operational threat to watch.

6. BOSNIA AND HERZEGOVINA: EUFOR helicopter crash kills four

An Italian CH-47 Chinook on the EU's Operation Althea crashed near Brčko; EUFOR says four crew died and 11 were injured. The cause is under investigation. [47]

Assessment: Nothing suggests hostile action, but the crash may be used in disputes over the mission's future.

7. SPAIN: Urda train crash toll revised to 69

Investigators revised the toll to 69, and about 50 remain in hospital. [48] The transport minister said a computer failure stopped six trains and controllers then let them resume without speed limits. [49]

Assessment: The control centre is now at the centre of the investigation, adding pressure on the government four weeks before an election.

8. FRANCE: Paris bans protests as interior minister warns of "very high" risk

Protest routes for 31 Oct were rejected and undeclared gatherings are banned around key Paris sites over the weekend; the interior minister warned of a "very high" risk of urban violence. [50]

Assessment: The bans raise the chance of unauthorised gatherings and clashes on 31 Oct ahead of the 5 Nov national strike.

9. UKRAINE / US: Allies doubt Patriots will arrive before the midterms

NATO sources doubt more PAC-3 interceptors will reach Ukraine before 3 Nov, pending approval from Hegseth; one said Trump is using "as much leverage as he has". [51]

Assessment: A gap in interceptors would leave Ukraine more exposed to ballistic strikes.

10. TÜRKİYE: Erdoğan hosts Turkic states summit on Republic Day

Erdoğan hosted Organization of Turkic States leaders in Ankara and said they would make the coming period the "Turkish Century". [52]

Assessment: Ankara is pushing to lead a Turkic bloc across the Caucasus and Central Asia.

Collection gap: no NATO airspace incident, Serbian certification, Georgia or Moldova item was found in the window.

INDOPACOM.

1. CHINA: Plenum expels 33 officials; military commission hollowed out

The plenum expelled 33 officials, including former Politburo members Zhang Youxia and Ma Xingrui. Attendance was 279 of 376, the highest absence rate of the post-Mao era. Only Xi and Zhang Shengmin remain from the 2022 Central Military Commission, and Zhang Shengmin was raised to the Politburo. [53] The 21st Party Congress is set for late 2027. [54] No new commission members or Taiwan wording were reported.

Assessment: The high command has been gutted and is now led by a discipline enforcer; the unfilled seats remain a gap.

2. CHINA / PHILIPPINES: "Combat readiness" patrols around Scarborough Shoal

The PLA Southern Theater Command said its forces carried out combat readiness patrols around Scarborough Shoal, and the coast guard ran patrols the same day, calling them a "necessary countermeasure" to Philippine "provocative acts". [55]

Assessment: Beijing has moved its response to the ASEAN row into military signalling at the most sensitive flashpoint; an incident in the coming days is likely.

3. NORTH KOREA: KCNA presents launch as a "Sniper" rocket battalion demonstration

KCNA said Kim watched a demonstration including the first public firing of a 600mm "Sniper" rocket launcher battalion, suicide drones and cruise missiles. [56] Kim called for "absolute superiority" in drone technology. [57]

Assessment: The account confirms the KN-25 assessment and presents it as an operational battalion system.

4. SOUTH KOREA: Intelligence service and military deny loudspeaker broadcasts began

South Korea's intelligence service told lawmakers resident reports of North Korean broadcasts were "not true", and the military said frontline units heard none. [58] Deepfake images of DMZ activity are circulating. [59] The national security adviser said Seoul would "respond firmly" to border breaches. [60]

Assessment: The North's end-of-October deadline falls on 31 Oct; no activation is confirmed.

5. TAIWAN / CHINA: Beijing calls Liaoning deployment routine

Taiwan's map shows 34 warships among the 53 vessels, with the Liaoning group east of the Philippines. China's defence ministry called the exercise annual training "not directed at any particular country". [61]

Assessment: The deployment spans the first island chain as the PLA steps up at Scarborough, though Beijing frames it as routine.

6. JAPAN / US: Details of AMRAAM factory and SM-3 deal

Hegseth and Koizumi agreed to build an AMRAAM factory in Japan and share data to increase SM-3 interceptor output; Koizumi called it "groundbreaking". [62]

Assessment: The deal ties Japan further into the US munitions supply chain.

7. MYANMAR: Junta and envoy give different accounts of talks

Envoy Lazaro met the junta's foreign minister; junta media said talks focused on restoring Myanmar's ASEAN role, while Lazaro's release stressed humanitarian corridors. She was told Suu Kyi "is doing well". [63] Myanmar Now reports the military has retaken key routes and towns. [64]

Assessment: The junta is pressing for restored ASEAN status while gaining ground, shrinking ASEAN's leverage.

8. SOLOMON ISLANDS: PM Wale asks for prayers; condition undisclosed

Wale asked the public to keep him "in your prayers" while recovering in Australia; no acting prime minister has been named. [65]

Assessment: Continuity of government is uncertain in a country where Chinese and Australian influence compete.

9. PACIFIC: US warns of cartels expanding into the Pacific

After the first US-Pacific police ministers' conference, a US official warned cartels and "narcoterrorist" groups are expanding into the Pacific. [66]

Assessment: Washington is framing Pacific policing around transnational crime, where Chinese police cooperation is also active.

10. INDIA: Rajnath offers ASEAN counter-terrorism expertise

At the ADMM-Plus, Rajnath Singh cited Operation Sindoor and said India's navy acts "without coercion or unilateral actions". [67]

Assessment: India is positioning itself with ASEAN states that have South China Sea disputes.

Collection gap: no Taiwan defence ministry daily count, Philippine response to the Scarborough patrols or Trump-Xi APEC confirmation was found.

NORTHCOM.

1. UNITED STATES: DOJ files notice of appeal on California ballots

DOJ filed a notice of appeal and said it would "immediately seek an emergency order from the Ninth Circuit". [68] No emergency motion or Supreme Court filing was confirmed by the cutoff. Newsmax reports Los Angeles County found only 7,035 of the flagged records had county addresses. [69]

Assessment: An emergency filing before 3 Nov is likely, but relief from the Ninth Circuit looks unlikely.

2. UNITED STATES: Iowa says DOJ plans monitors in all 50 states; FBI unease reported

Iowa's secretary of state said DOJ plans about 1,000 monitors in all 50 states and that they cannot direct operations. [70] HuffPost, relaying anonymous sources, says FBI agents fear being ordered to seize ballots; DOJ called that "fearmongering". [71]

Assessment: The federal monitoring footprint is unusually large and is the main friction point with states going into 3 Nov.

3. CANADA: First national security strategy in over 20 years

Ottawa's strategy covers war, AI, espionage and foreign interference, names eight economic sectors for protection and promises new intelligence legislation within a year. Officials called frontier AI possibly "one of the greatest threats facing Canadians". [72]

Assessment: This is a framework without funding; next week's budget is the first concrete marker.

4. MEXICO: Roadblocks in Matamoros after arrest of Gulf Cartel leader's son

Army special forces arrested the son of Gulf Cartel leader "El Contador", followed by blockades across Matamoros and Río Bravo. No casualties were reported. [73]

Assessment: Short-term disruption at border crossings is possible.

5. MEXICO / US: US Mission claims 95% fall in border apprehensions

The US Mission said border apprehensions fell 95% in 12 months and cited 96 extraditions, without methodology. [74]

Assessment: A positive message four days before the midterms, sitting alongside continuing tension over designations.

6. UNITED STATES: Big Bend challengers file Supreme Court response

Challengers asked the Supreme Court to leave in place the order halting wall construction in Big Bend. [75]

Assessment: Briefing is complete and a ruling can come at any time.

7. MEXICO: Vance dissipates

NHC issued its final advisory on the remnants of Vance; rain may cause flooding in Sinaloa and the US Southwest. [76] No new Atlantic systems are expected. [77]

Assessment: No new tropical threat is forecast for the AORs.

8. MEXICO: Sinaloa interim governor removes Rocha Moya's daughter from welfare post

The interim governor replaced the daughter of Governor Rocha Moya, who is on leave after US accusations, as head of the state family-welfare agency. [78]

Assessment: The state government is distancing itself from the Rocha family as US cases proceed.

Collection gap: no verified threat or incident at a polling place was found, and a viral claim of Guard mobilisation in all 50 states could not be verified.

area of responsibility

SOUTHCOM.

1. VENEZUELA: Machado backs US roadmap but questions oil deal

At her first Caracas press conference, Machado backed Washington's sequence of a new Supreme Court, a new electoral council and a 2027 vote, asked for all three at once, and said the US-backed oil deal "no refleja los niveles de transparencia" Venezuelans want. [79] Acting President Rodríguez said "Habrá elecciones" without a date and said more than 1,000 people have been freed under the amnesty law. [80]

Assessment: Machado is pressing Washington's timeline while questioning the oil deal, and Rodríguez is competing for credit on reform.

2. HAITI: About 2,000 Salvadoran troops in country

A Haitian official told Reuters about 2,000 Salvadoran soldiers were in Haiti, capped at 2,500; Bukele said El Salvador pays 90% of the cost, and Pentagon C-130s helped move them. [81] The four missing technicians are still missing, and MSF has suspended some services in Port-au-Prince. [82]

Assessment: A large force with no clear mandate or UN cover raises the risk of escalation and civilian harm.

3. BRAZIL: Flávio Bolsonaro cancels Miami press conference; no warrant

Flávio's Miami press conference was cancelled; a US official told SBT the US would not want him presenting himself as Brazil's de facto president. [83] The Federal Police say he is "não é considerado foragido" and no arrest warrant exists. [84] Gazeta do Povo reports he is preparing an asylum claim. [85] A regional outlet reports US records show a parole classification rather than a visa, which is unconfirmed. [86]

Assessment: Washington appears to be limiting how he can present himself, and an arrest warrant is legally constrained while he holds his Senate seat.

4. COLOMBIA: Cúcuta militarised after border violence

The president ordered the temporary militarisation of Cúcuta, with at least 500 special forces deployed and 300 more due. [87] An ELN-attributed drone attack in Catatumbo injured three civilians. [88]

Assessment: Urban militarisation is becoming the government's standard response as ELN-dissident fighting spills into the border city.

5. CUBA: Expelled diplomat returns; Díaz-Canel calls vote a "victory"

The Cuban diplomat expelled after a scuffle at the UN returned to Havana. [89] Díaz-Canel called the reduced UN vote a "victory" while admitting hospitals run "without fuel, without transportation, without medication". [90]

Assessment: Havana is presenting a weakening position as a win, and friction with Washington remains high.

6. PERU / EL SALVADOR: Fujimori and Bukele sign four agreements

Fujimori and Bukele signed a strategic partnership and three other agreements, and Fujimori invited Bukele to Peru. [91] After touring CECOT, she said Peru will build a high-security prison on the Salvadoran model. [92]

Assessment: Peru is formally adopting Bukele-style prison policy, which is likely to draw rights criticism.

7. ECUADOR: First Quito march draws about 100

About 100 people marched in Quito demanding the release of former candidate Luisa González; more dates are set before the 29 Nov local elections. [93]

Assessment: A low-intensity start; the November dates are the better test.

Collection gap: no Venezuelan government response to Machado, Haitian statement on Salvadoran operations or Brazilian court decision was found.
